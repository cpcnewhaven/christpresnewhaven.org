A Connectional Model for Reaching Greater New Haven

 

Our Theology:

As Presbyterians, we do not believe in the "independent" church. Rather we believe in a church that is connected as reflecting our organic union with Christ as our heavenly center. As discovered in the New Covenant context itself, we can speak of "one" church consisting of multiple worship centers. Consequently, to the degree that we can genuinely function as "one" with respect to our ministry of word, sacrament, and pastoral care is the degree to which we experience an authentically connected church. This has been articulated by Edmund Clowney in the following manner:

The scriptural model in view is the "city-church"… these are seen as unified churches under one presbyterial government but including a number of congregations… They are members of the church of the city…

… a different concept of "the church of God which is at Corinth." Corinth is one place of manifestation (of the heavenly church manifest on earth) for God has "many people" in that city, but the church is not the church of Stephanas (16:15), or of Paul, Peter, or Apollos (1:12) or of Corinth. It is the church of God; therefore it includes those who are called to be saints and they are addressed with "all that call upon the name of our Lord Jesus in every place."

The organic concept of the church that appears in the New Testament… presents a more theological, Christ-centered, spiritual view of the church as defined not by one earthly hierarchial center nor by many earthly congregational centers, but by a heavenly center that requires multiform earthly manifestations. Earthly assemblies do not define but manifest the nature and the center of the church.

Within a denomination-- those who share one common confession of "one lord, one faith, etc."-- we strive to include all types of people from all walks of life so as to better approximate the great "inclusion of the nations" commission given to the church. We want a strategy that will maximize BOTH the missiological advantage of "targeting" so as to bring the gospel to every "ethnos" or (nation) and the ecumenical advantage of confessionalism so as to have genuine Christian unity as defined by our having a common Lord and faith. We believe connectionalism.is the biblical answer to these two aspects of the great commission. And whatever else connectionalism might be, it ought to mean something with respect to the three marks of the church so as to represent an authentic expression of "one body" of Christ in Greater New Haven. A collegial model for "church" as represented by many worship centers that are connected diaconally, instructionally and governmentally would best express this even as was the case in the NT context.

 

Our Demographic Reality:

  • While our congregation is rich in international diversity, we share in common much more than "one Lord, one faith, etc." For instance, all of our people are more or less "achiever" in social ethic. And with some notable exceptions, our congregation shares a higher learning sociology as well. This explains why our congregation (more or less) consists of those associated with Yale if living in the city and then those families living in the edge cities who themselves came from some similar academic context given their personal development and values. But where are the labor and survival types of people-- where do they live and what kind of ministry would relate best to their world so as to make evangelical/reformed disciples of Christ? Where are the indigenous ethic categories of people that are reflective of a truly "New Haven" church?
  • With respect to the type of person we are presently reaching (achiever/educated types) who are attracted to our present style of ministry, where do they live if not at Yale and how does this impact their decision about what church to attend?. I.e. With respect to those families who would consider greater New Haven their long-term home AND who would be attracted to our present style of ministry-- they live in regions more hospitable to their values relative to their children's education in the edge towns. This makes it all the more difficult to reach the very people that our style of ministry would most readily reach given our present location in the city. Yet to move our "mother" congregation to any one of the edge cities would significantly reduce our impact to the world AND our impact in the other edge cities. In other words, given a single congregation model, our present strategy is preferable over any other single location strategy since it provides greatest access to the greatest number of people. (Center city in relation to the various edge cities makes it "closer" to a greater number of people. Also, the fact that more edge city dwellers have access to transportation than those living in the city and/or associated with the University.)

  • The recent US census data as summarized in the Dec. 2 issue of the New Haven Register tells the story. It explains why in almost 3 years we have had so few long-term achiever/education type families visit our church. (those who actually plan to "settle down" in our region.) For the fact remains that CT is decreasing in population(.4%)-- STILL! (We will lose a congressional seat this year) More to the point, the city of New Haven has decreased at 4% over the past 6 years. Whereas some of that decrease has been the result of suburban flight, it is significant to note the direction-- the eastern shoreline. Whereas places like Madison(4%), Guilford (6%) and North Branford (6%) have increased in population, the western shoreline is decreasing in pop.-- Orange -3%, Derby at about -2%, West Haven at -3.5%. (One interesting note is that Bethany to the north, has increased by 3%-- perhaps mostly due to its previous low density pop and indicative of the Cheshire, Middletown overflow growth) This is the demographic realities that we must face head on if we are going to reach our vision for a thriving and growing church for the glory of Christ in our region. This in a region that is both historically antagonistic to the evangelical faith, a region without people looking for a new church, a region where liberal and Roman religion enjoys a significant resource advantage in comparison to a new church such as ours. What all this means is as follows:

If a large number of families did visit and then decided not to stay, we would want to ask, "what about our current strategy is presenting a problem?" But this is clearly not the case. Moreover, those indigenous families who have visited but have not stayed have almost always stated as their primary if not exclusive reason that church location was the reason. Now if we think about this, some haunting realities come into focus. For a generally "good" percentage of people to stick who have visited a church is said to be around the 30% figure. This means that to grow by 3 indigenous families every year-- we would need to average about 10 visiting families. Yet this hasn't happened in five years! Moreover, even as the above statistics illustrate we have seen a significant number of families in our church move out from the city into the edge towns, if not out of the region all together. The towns these people have moved to are again illustrated by the statistics-- the eastern shoreline. It's all about demographics! The point of all this is to take notice if we were to begin another service-- both in so far as where we would hold it and yet in such a manner as to not destroy the center city service.

Theology and Demographics Together Toward a Theological Vision:

1. Theologically, we can envision a multiple service church reaching many different types of people yet within one faith, one financial plan, one shared leadership structure, one training center, shared youth group, etc so as to best approximate a Biblical vision for confessional connectionalism.

2. If we were to reach the same type of person we are currently reaching but those who do not live in the city, we would want to begin a similar style service in a place representing the most potential for new family visits. Statistically, this would direct us to the Eastern Shoreline or Northern towns. To the north, we have less human resources and would be closer to an existing reformed/Presbyterian church (OP) than with the eastern shoreline. This directs us to target the eastern shoreline for our second, similar style, worship service. (As it happens our main "evangelical" rival, the "E" Free church has already begun a second church in the Madison area to match it's current Branford location.)

3. IF we were to reach a different type of "ethnos" (demographically speaking) living in the downtown city, we would need to network through whatever current contacts we have and consider a different philosophy of ministry (especially worship style)-- but so as to remain theologically driven (albeit demographically sensitive). Perhaps the Dwight Hall community (Hinkson's) or Fair Haven community (Cousar's) would be good places to start. This would also be true for reaching different types of people living in the surrounding edge towns-- perhaps Milford, West Haven.

 

Our Vision in Summary:

We envision a church in greater New Haven that transcends all human distinction while effectively reaching all categories of people. Recognizing that some categories of people are better reached as from within a given demographic context, we will plant multiple worshipping congregations in greater New Haven. Yet wanting to avoid sectarianism in the church, we will maintain one government, one center city training and study center, one budget even one pastoral team so in effect to be one Christ Presbyterian Church of Greater New Haven consisting of multiple worship centers. Our study center would become a kind of "hub" for administrative and training purposes. This, we believe, will better approximate New Testament context and its corresponding theology of the church in conformity to the connectional and organic aspects of the one holy catholic church:

 
    • Greater potential to reach those pockets of people that are not being reached by a single demographic and/or location church..

• Greater potential for the organic unity of the church to be expressed in more authentic and personal shepherding, servant teams, pastoral care and diaconal sharing, across demographic categories.

• greater potential to develop genuine trust so as to reduce the "politics of suspicion" that inevitably flow out of the lack of real, personal relationships across demographic lines in the church.

    • greater potential for an equitable and frugal stewardship of human and fiscal resources through the cooperative use of administrative, pastoral and even diaconal ministries.

 

Our Goals:

1. To target the Spring of '99 for starting a second worship service in downtown New Haven area (Dwight Community). The service would meet on Saturday evenings. The service would maintain each of the five weekly elements of worship that we practice in our present service but would be significantly changed in both "form" and "circumstances." (I.e. a roughly 15 to 20 minute sermon, hymns that remain God centered but with guitar and in "folk" vs. "classical" tunes. A summer mission project will be established in the Summer of 2000 that will bring the gospel to the community in a wholistic and more visible way.

2. To target the Spring of ' 00 for starting a third service to meet on Sunday a.m. This service would more or less be exactly the same as the center city service using the same bulletin, same preacher, etc. This plan has already been tentatively approved by the session and requires a minimum of start up money and human resources (relatively speaking).
