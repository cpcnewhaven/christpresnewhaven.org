PASTORAL VISITATION

One aspect of our strategy for Christian discipleship is that the pastor be committed to a schedule of regular pastoral visitations. Pastoral visitation has been faithfully utilized throughout the church's history by some of the most Godly shepherds of God's flocks. (See Richard Baxter's, The Reformed Pastor for example) Yet more significantly, the genesis of pastoral visitation is derived from Scripture. In Acts 20:18-20, we have a record of Paul's pastoral ministry of visitation when he writes that You yourselves know how I lived among you all the time from the first day that I set foot in Asia, serving the Lord with all humility and with tears and with trials which befell me through the plots of the Jews; how I did not shrink from declaring to you anything that was profitable and teaching you in public and from house to house...

Not only did Paul administer the Word of God publicly, but he also performed that ministry "house to house" in order to minister the Word to the particular needs of individuals and households. No doubt, through such visits, Paul also knew better the strengths and weakness of believers at Ephesus such that he was more able to instruct the people and pray for them. The preaching/teaching ministry of the pastor has since been understood to be one that consists of both public preaching and private instruction.

Pastoral visitation, then, is part of the ministry of God's Word. Its purpose is not for the minister and the people to "get to know one another better" in the social sense but rather in a ministerial sense. Pastoral visitation will enable the pastor to carry the discipleship ministry of our church to you in a more personal way. Moreover, it will enable him to pray more precisely for you during the week and plan sermons that are more sensitive to your particular needs. On a more general note, while pastoral visitation will not replace the need for pastoral counseling on given occasions, it has often functioned to prevent some needs from ever developing into full grown problems. These, then, are the reasons why we think that pastoral visitation ought to be a vital element of our discipleship strategy at CPC.

The plan is for the pastor to visit every communicant member in the church, and, when practical, their non-communicant children as well, in some regular fashion. (Once per year) Each visit will consist in spending about 15-20 minutes with each individual (although this is flexible). This should usually be enough time. When more time is desired, another visit could be planned for a later date. Or, if there is the need for some further information or instruction, the pastor might follow up the visit with some suggested readings or a pastoral letter. In anticipation of each visit, you could use the following questions to begin thinking about some of the areas that might be helpful to discuss. Please don't feel at all threatened by the questions. They are simply guides to the kinds of things that might help in the discipleship ministry of God's word and prayer.

1. In what specific area/areas has your understanding of the Christian faith and life grown since our last visit?
2. In what specific area/areas of your life have you seen some evidence of growth in holiness?
3. In what specific area/areas of your life are you disappointed in the lack of evidence of growth in holiness?
4. In what specific area could the pastor provide instruction which would be of help to you? (As I mentioned, this might lead to another visit or my sending you something to read)
5. (If applicable) Are there any particular needs pertaining to the spiritual health and/or happiness of your family that you would want to discuss.
6. (If applicable) Are you presently pleased with your family devotions and the training of your children in the Christian faith? Is there a way I can be of assistance?
7. In addition to the above areas, are there any specific areas that you would want the pastor to pray for on a weekly basis?

Tuesday afternoons and evenings have been designated as the most suitable time for pastoral visitations in lieu of other pastoral commitments. Fran Onofrio has graciously volunteered to handle the scheduling. She will initiate a contact with each member regarding a suitable time to meet.
