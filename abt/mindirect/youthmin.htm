Youth Ministry at CPC

 

Summer camps, roller coasters at Six Flags over New England, fun times with friends… ask any youth and these are the things that make life great!  And yet, we all know that life today for kids can be like that favorite roller coaster, filled with the ups and downs, turns and jerks of self-discovery—and most especially—the discovery of God and his infinite glory and grace!  Wouldn’t it be great if there were a ministry that combined fun and positive socialization with the opportunity to discover the transforming power of God’s grace?  This our goal with the youth at CPC!  

 

We believe the transition from “covenant child” to “adult Christian” is not so much a transition from “non-Christian” to “Christian.”  Rather, it is the transition from “unconfirmed faith” to a “confirmed faith—or perhaps better, from a faith that is assumed as passed down by even one believing parent, to a faith that is owned.  The passage begins with entrance into the church through Christian baptism, even as it culminates in a personal profession of faith and admission to the Lord’s Table as an adult Christian.  The passage is NOT so much defined by a child becoming more and more holy (although this is in some ways happening), but in a child becoming more and more aware of how unholy we/they are as to be enabled by God’s grace to turn away from “self-righteousness” to “faith righteousness” in Christ.  Toward this end, we have implemented the following “strategy” for child discipleship that stresses a close cooperative between parents and the church. 

 

CPC Sunday School

 

We have tried to develope a very thoughtful and comprehensive approach towards providing our children with deep and enduring roots in the Christian faith.  Our curriculum represents both a deductive and an inductive approach. E.g. By the time a child leaves the home, they would have been trained in 1) a comprehensive study of the Bible, in all its various periods of redemptive history (inductive via Sunday School) 2) a systematic study and review of the more essential teachings of the classic/evangelical Christian faith (deductive via a weekly children’s assembly and the review of the Westminster Shorter Catechism, culminating with a full year study in the Westminster Confession of Faith in Jr. High), and 3) all within the context of a practical and personal knowledge of Christ as experienced within the loving community of a Christ-centered Church.  (A more comprehensive outline of our teaching strategy may be obtained from the church upon request.)   In High School, the curriculum focuses more on issues pertaining to Christian faith and practice in the present context of living “in the world but not of the world.”  E.g. Topics include “Faith in the context of religious pluralism”, Calling, Vocation and Discerning God’s Will”, Whole Life “Gospel Transformation”, Christiann apologetics, etc.

 

Mom’s Fellowship Group and Children’s Story Hour (Mid-week)

 

On  a weekly basis during the school year, mothers of pre-school children meet for prayer and Bible Study as children play, sing and hear bible stories.

 

Children Choir

 

A fun musical experience for children ages 4 and up.  Children typically rehearse on Sundays after church, and perform at various special services.  See also “Music at CPC” for additional musical experiences aimed at children.

 

“Student Partnership” and Monthly Youth Meeting

This involves a college student mentoring program with High School students, combined with a monthly “youth group” led by the college students under supervision of the college minister at CPC.

 

Summer Theology Camp for High School

Believe it or not, a “theology camp” that is a “great hit” for kids in the 9th thru 12th grade years!  Over the period of three years the camp combines fun activities with theological and historical discovery--  including an annual road trip to “Puritan New England” ( Boston, Providence, Princeton—and of course New Haven and the legacy of Jonathan Edwards!).  Using the “Socratic method” the pastor becomes the “skeptic” as students seek to convince him of the Christian faith using J.I Packers “A Concise Theology.”

 

Summer “Terrific Thursdays”:

These are daytime excursions to various local sites for parents with young children for the purpose of fun and fellowship.
