An Appeal From our pastor to support “Hope For New Haven”

 

The spirit of the Lord GOD is upon me, because the LORD has anointed me; he has sent me to bring good news to the poor, to bind up the brokenhearted, to proclaim liberty to the captives, and release to the prisoners; to proclaim the year of the LORD’s favor, and the day of vengeance of our God; to comfort all who mourn…

Isaiah 61:1-2

 

With these words, Isaiah longingly envisioned the ministry of the Messiah who was to be God’s anointed servant.  Centuries later, Jesus chose to introduce his ministry on earth with these very same words (Luke 4:17ff).  And when John the Baptist needed confirmation that his imminent death for the sake of Christ was not in vain, Jesus assured him saying, the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news brought to them (Luke 7:22). 

 

To be sure, the test of Christ’s authenticity as the Messiah was related to his ministry to the “poor.” The same standard was assumed by Paul in his apostolic ministry, even as he once reminded the Galatians that the counsel of Jerusalem “asked only one thing of me, that I remember the poor” of which Paul further noted that he “was eager to do so” (Gal.2:10).  James applied the same standard to the New Testament church as well (James 2).  

 

All of this raises an important question: who are the “poor” today that need Christ’s attention as through His people? In the Bible one can quickly identify the use of “poor” in two ways--  one related to the “outward” person and the other related to the “inward” person—or a poverty of body and soul. In the first case, a person is deprived of some basic outward need as related to his/her physical, emotional or social wellbeing.  In the second case, a person is deprived of a basic inward need of righteousness as related to his/her wellbeing with God. And while the two types of poverty ought never to be confused, neither can either of them be falsely minimized or ignored in the name of Christ. This is because God knows us not in our parts, but as whole persons made in His image.  So too, we know God’s love ultimately in a holistic restoration of body and soul, even if not completely until the next life.

 

But it goes even deeper than this!   For at the heart of both kinds of poverty is an ethic of hopelessness, or in the words of Isaiah, “brokenheartedness.” That is, poverty is NOT  merely the lack of some  outward or inward need, poverty is the lack of options which renders one HOPELESS. It is the married couple that has lost hope in finding peace and happiness in their marriage.  It is the child that lacks hope in “the system” such as to drop out of school.  It is the teenager that has lost hope in tomorrow such as to waste his/her talents on immediate gratification today.  It is the man who has lost hope of dignity such as to beg.  It is the parent that has lost hope in pulling it together such as to abuse the one that makes them feel guilty for it.  It is the neighborhood that has lost hope of being a “haven in a heartless world.”  It is the sinner that has lost hope of forgiveness.

 

For this reason, I hope you will consider supporting Hope for New Haven.  As a subsidiary organization of Christ Presbyterian Church, its purpose is ultimately to bring the hope of Christ to bear especially upon the “outward” needs of our city, even as this will enable God’s people to have an authentic witness to the hope of Christ in relation to their inward need as well.   For  the purpose of making a donation, you may send a check to “Hope For New Haven” at 135 Whitney Ave., New Haven CT 06510.  For more information, please call 203-865-HOPE (4673).  Please help it be said in New Haven about Christ:

 

In his name the nations will HOPE!

Mathew 12:21
