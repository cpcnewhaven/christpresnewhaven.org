<P ALIGN=left>
<B>Bringing HOPE to our city</B>
</P>

<P ALIGN=left>
When John the Baptist needed confirmation that his imminent death for the sake of Christ was not in vain, Jesus once assured John the Baptist of the authenticity of his messianic ministry by saying, <I>the blind receive their sight, the lame walk, the lepers are cleansed, the deaf hear, the dead are raised, and the poor have good news brought to them</I> (Luke 7:22).  And when Paul was asked by the counsel of Jerusalem to "remember the poor," he reported that he "was eager to do so" (Gal.2:10).  James applied the same standard of authenticity to the New Testament church as well (James 2).
</P>

<P ALIGN=left>
Who are the "poor" today?  The Bible speaks of the poor in two ways-- one related to the "outward" person and the other related to the "inward" person—or, a poverty of body and soul.  In the first case, a person is deprived of some basic <I>outward</I> need as related to his/her physical, emotional or social wellbeing.  In the second case, a person is deprived of a basic <I>inward</I> need of righteousness as related to his/her wellbeing with God. And while the two types of poverty ought never to be confused, neither can either of them be falsely minimized or ignored in the name of Christ..
</P> 

<P ALIGN=left>
At the heart of both kinds of poverty is an ethic of hopelessness.  <I>Poverty is not merely the lack of some outward or inward resource, poverty is the lack of options which renders one HOPELESS and in need of Christ’s help!</I>  It is the married couple that has lost hope in finding peace and happiness in their marriage.  It is the child that lacks hope in "the system" such as to drop out of school.  It is the teenager that has lost hope in tomorrow such as to waste his/her talents on immediate gratification today.  It is the man who has lost hope of dignity such as to beg.  It is the parent that has lost hope in pulling it together such as to abuse the one that causes them to feel guilt for it.  It is the neighborhood that has lost hope of being a "haven in a heartless world."  It is the sinner that has lost hope of forgiveness. 
</P> 
 
<P ALIGN=left>
For this reason, CPC organized <I>Hope for New Haven</I>, a faith-based, non-profit subsidiary organization of CPC that is a collaborative project with other likeminded churches in New Haven.   Its purpose is to bring the hope of Christ to bear especially upon the "outward" needs of our city, even as this will enable God’s people to have an authentic witness to the hope of Christ in relation to their inward need as well.  Currently, the hope of Christ is being offered through a Christian counseling center, a Christian daycare center, a jobs training program, an after school tutorial program for children in the city, an educational coop and a music education program.  Many other initiatives are also envisioned in connection with neighborhood revitalization.
</P> 

<P ALIGN=left>
Let it be said in New Haven: <I>In his name the nations will <B>HOPE</B>!</I> (Mathew 12:21)
</P>
