21 Cor. 1:21 For since in the wisdom of God the world through its wisdom did not {come to} know God, God was well-pleased through the foolishness of the message preached to save those who believe.

 

 

1. The distinction between natural revelation and special revelation is here taught.  In all that is made (nature) God has revealed a sufficient amount of Himself to hold people properly accountable to recognize him as God and to serve Him.  However, God has not revealed in creation a knowledge of salvation in Christ.  Moreover, God has not in natural creation accomplished that "special" and "new" creation that enables people to discern themselves as sinners and God's grace for sinners such as to embrace it by repentance and faith. (see effectual calling) This knowledge has been revealed specially, linguistically, through prophetic and inscripturated words.  This "disposition" is accomplished through effectual calling in so far as people are "reborn" to a new disposition such as to have spiritual discernment.

 

Illustration:  If you threw a brick through your neighbor’s front window, you might very well know that he would probably be unhappy (possibly even angry) even without asking him.  Apart from any conversation with him about bricks and windows, you know that you will be without excuse if you throw the brick through the window.  What you don’t know is what he will do about it; whether he would be willing to forgive you; and, if so, under what circumstances.  Without “special” revelation, you do not know how to be reconciled to him, or whether reconciliation is even possible. And more even than this, the jury is still out as to whether or not you would even want to be reconciled vs. turn and run.

As Christians we cannot begin speculating about knowledge by itself.  We cannot ask how we know without at the same time asking what we know.... to say that we do not need to ask about the nature of reality when we ask about the nature of knowledge is not to be neutral but is in effect to exclude the Christian answer to the question of knowledge.   (p.,32, 33)

Two Theories:

1. God is the final court of appeal

2. Man is the final court of appeal

 

Sin will reveal itself in the field of knowledge in the fact that man makes himself the ultimate court of appeal in the matter of all interpretation... Man has declared his autonomy as over against God.  (p.35)

 

When we say that sin is ethical we do not mean, however, that sin involved only the will of man and not also his intellect.  Sin involved every aspect of man's personality.  All of man's reactions in every relation in which God had set him were ethical and not merely intellectual; the intellectual itself is ethical.   (p.46)

 

(See Calvin, 1.5.11-12)

(See Calvin, 1.1.1; 1.3.1; 1.5.1; 1.5.9)

 

God is man's ultimate environment and this environment is completely interpretative of man who is to know himself.   (p.42)

Because man's knowledge of God is logically more fundamental than man's knowledge of the universe, we may be indifferent to the question of temporal priority... for this reason we cannot know ourselves in any true sense unless we know God.  (p.42)

 

Our argument for the objectivity of knowledge with respect to the universe can never be complete and satisfactory unless we bring in the relation of both the object and the subject of knowledge to God.  (p.43)

4. What is "presuppositionalism?"

This is, in the last analysis, the question as to what are one's ultimate presuppositions.  When man became a sinner he made of himself instead of God the ultimate or final reference point.  And it is precisely this presupposition, as it controls without exception all forms of non-Christian philosophy, that must be brought into question.  If this presupposition is left unquestioned in any field all the facts and arguments presented to the unbeliever will be made over by him according to his pattern.  The sinner has cemented colored glasses to his eyes which he cannot remove.  And all is yellow to the jaundiced eye.  There can be no intelligible reasoning unless those who reason together understand what they mean by their words.  (p.77)

 An unqualified evidentialism presupposes the ultimacy of this world such that the knowledge of God is in some sense derived from our knowledge of this world, Pressupositionalism presupposes the ultimacy of God such that the knowledge of this world is derived from our knowledge of God
