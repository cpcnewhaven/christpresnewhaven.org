The doctrine of the Trinity was indeed implicit in the teachings of both the apostolic church and the sub-apostolic antenicene church.  It is therefore a false conclusion as made by the Jehovah Witnesses that the testimony of the Bible and of history makes clear that the Trinity was unknown throughout Biblical times and for several centuries thereafter.

 

I. An Apostolic Doctrine

 

J.N.D. Kelly, Early Christian Creeds

The Trinitarianism of the New Testament is rarely explicit; but the frequency with which the triadic schema recurs suggests that this pattern was implicit in Christian theology from the start.  (p.12, emphases mine)

Explicit Trinitarian confessions are few and far between; where they do occur, little can be built upon them.  The two most commonly cited are St Paul’s prayer at the end of 2 Corinthians…and the baptismal command put by St Matthew into the mouth of the risen Lord. …These are not the only examples, however, of such formulae in the New Testament, although preoccupation with them has sometimes caused others which, while perhaps less obvious, are in reality no less significant to be overlooked.  Among these may be reckoned I Cor. 6,11; I Cor. 12,4f.,; 2 Cor. 1, 21f., I Thess. 5, 18f.…I Pet. 1, 2.…A host of other passages stamped with the same lineaments might be quoted.  In all of them there is no trace of fixity so far as their wording is concerned, and none of them constitutes a creed in any ordinary sense of the term.  Nevertheless the Trinitarian ground-plan obtrudes itself obstinately throughout, and its presence is all the more striking because more often than not there is nothing in the context to necessitate it.  The impression inevitably conveyed is that the conception of the threefold manifestation of the Godhead was embedded deeply in Christian thinking from the start, and provided a ready-to-hand mold in which the ideas of the apostolic writers took shape.  If Trinitarian creeds are rare, the Trinitarian pattern which was to dominate all later creeds was already part and parcel of the Christian tradition of doctrine.  (p. 22-23, emphases mine)

A Doctrine of the Antenicene Fathers

 

The church’s understanding of doctrine (especially specific doctrines) continues  to develop until Christ returns.  Thus, the doctrine of justification by faith was more fully developed and understood during and after the Reformation than before.  Thus, it is no argument against a doctrinal refinement  that the refinement occurs later in the church’s history.

Those attributes relative to God "that emphasis his personal nature"

L. Berkof

 

I.e. The attributes of God we can know in a real sense, albeit not exhaustively.  The Attributes of God that find a corollary in humanity, albeit not perfectly.

 

I. The Communication of God in the Imago Dei that we might know him ( Natural Evidence)

All humankind tries  to live as if what is true ultimately? (6 scenarios)

1. When things happen-- do you try to find meaning in them beyond their immediate value? 

2. If someone was dying-- do you think it worth your time and effort, even sacrificial effort, to save them?

3. When you make decisions about right and wrong-- do you look for underlying standards that are standard for all situations (or that are absolute)? 

4. When changes happen-- do you look for progress in them? Why?

5. When someone close to you dies-- do you treat the body with reverence or as a piece of dirt?

6. When we suffer, do we look for the "lesson" or "good" that comes from it?

 

II. The Communication of  God in the Bible that we may know him. (Special Evidence)

 

A. God is Incomprehensible: To know him is not then to comprehend him!

Rom.11:33-36, Job 11:7-9, 26:14, Psalms 145:1-3

 

B. God is Sovereign-- this represents the purpose of the Triune God as absolute and unconditional, independent of the whole finite creation, and originating solely in the eternal counsel of His will.  He appoints the course of nature and directs the course of history down to the minutest details.

Acts 17:24-31(also Gen.14:19, Ex.18:11, Dt.10:14,17, Ps.22:26, 47:2,3,7,8, Ps.50:10-12, Ps.145:11-13, Jer.27:5, Luke 1:53, Rev. 19:6)

 

1. The Sovereign Wisdom of God

1 Corinthians 1:20-25,  Rom.16:2

 

2. The Sovereign Will of God (He is Free)

Job 11:10, 33:13,k Prov. 21:1, Isa 10:15, 29:16, Rom. 9:15-18, 20-21, etc.

 

3. The Sovereign Power of God

Job9:12, Ps115:3, Jer.32:17, Mt 19:26, Luke 1:37, Heb.1:3, 1Cor.1:24…

 

C. God Is Good --  the fundamental idea is that He is in every way all that He as God should be and therefore answers perfectly[ to the ideal expressed in the word "God" as perhaps summarized under the three fold categories of creation, holiness and love.

Mark 10:18, Ps.36:9

 

1. The Goodness of God in Creation--that perfection of God which prompts Him to deal bountifully and kindly with all his creatures.

Gen.1:26-27, 2:7 , Psalms 33:6-9 and Nehemiah 9:6  (see also John 1:1-4)

 

2. The Goodness of God In Holiness --It is not correct to think of holiness primarily as a moral or religious quality, as is generally done.  Its fundamental idea is that of a position of relationship existing between God and some person or thing.  Berkof

Exodus 15:11, Isaiah 6:3, Psalm 99:1-5

a. Holiness manifest in Righteousness

Ezra 9:15, Neh.9:8, Ps.119:137, 145:17, Jer.12:1, Lam.1:18, Dan.9:14, John 17:25, 2Tim.4:8, 1John2:29, 3:7, Rev. 16:5

 

b. Holiness manifest in Justice

Isa. 33:22, James 4:12, Dt. 4:8, Ps.99:4, Rom. 1:32

 

c. Holiness manifest in Wrath

Dt. 32:16, Joshua 7:1, Hebrews 12:29, Ezk.5:13,

 

3. The Goodness of God in Love--… that perfection of God by which He is eternally moved to self-communication…. And since God is absolutely good in Himself…He loves his rational creatures for His own sake… He loves in them Himself, His virtues, His work and His gifts. He does not even withdraw His love completely from the sinner in the present sinful state, though the latter's sin is an abomination to Him… Berkof

John 3:16, Mt.5:44-45, John 16:27, rom.5:8,k 1 John 3:1

 

1) Grace--In the general sense, it is "Favor shown to another" such that between person to person, it need not necessarily be "undeserved." Grace from God to humanity is always favor undeserved if but for the relation of creature to Creator, not to mention then the addition of sin.  As such, the grace of God is the source of All blessings, not least of which are the spiritual blessings bestowed upon sinners.

Gen.33:8,10,18, 39:4, 47:25, Ruth 2:2, 1Sam.1:18…, Eph.1:6,7, 2:7-9, Tit.2:11, 3:4-7, Rom. 3:24, 2 Cor.8:9

 

2) Mercy--"The goodness of God's love shown to those who are in misery or distress, irrespective of their own worth.  In God's mercy He reveals himself as a compassionate god who pities those who are in misery and is ever ready to relieve their distress." Berkof

1Tim.1:2, 2Tim.1:1, Titus 1:4, Dt. 5:19, Ps.57:10, 86:5, 1Chron.16:34, 2Chron.7:6, Ps.136, Ezra 3:11

 

3) Longsuffering--  "long of face", It is that aspect of the goodness of God's love wherein God bears with the evil in spite of their long continued disobedience. Thus the forbearance of God!

Ex.34:6, Ps.86:15, Rom 2:4, 9:22, 1Pet.3:20, 2Pet.3:15

 

IV. The Sovereign Goodness of God's Holy-Love All Satisfied  by Christ

Rom.3:21-26, Heb.9

 

The cross is the only place where the loving, forgiving merciful God is revealed in such a way that we perceive that his holiness and his love are equally infinite...it consists in the combination of inflexible righteousness, with its penalties, and transcendent love.

                                                                                                Emil Brunner,  The Mediator

 

In the cross of Christ, God's justice and love are simultaneously revealed.

                                                                                                                G.C. Berkouwer

 

In a marvelous and divine way He loved us even when he hated us.               

                                                                                                                 John Calvin.
