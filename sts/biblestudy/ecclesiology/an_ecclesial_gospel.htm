Introduction:

Membership Vows, Baptism, Elder Installation, deacons, a church activities fair, denominationalism–thing that most of us put over in a margin if at all as related to the gospel… perhaps even an apologetic footnote… what’s all this hallibalou about anyway?

And if you happen to attend a church on "moving up" Sunday or perhaps some other ecclesiastical event, you are thinking, here comes the "church" sermon–the only thing worse being the "money sermon"–and you are thinking… man, did I choose a bad Sunday…. You can almost hear the "clicks"–people turning off the pastor…

Why, because most of us, if we are honest, have concluded that Christianity is not really about "church" – religion is a private thing, we might say, or for the more evangelical types, it is said abit differently, how Christianity is really about a personal relationship with Jesus Christ.. (with an emphasis on "personal")

In short, there is a huge question mark beside the word "church"–let me illustrate?

A person is asked to say something about how they came to embrace Christ as their great Redeemer. Sheepishly, the person begins to apologize since their "story" is something less than extravagant-- at least as it concerns a modern and individualistic kind of extravaganza. The embarrassed Christian can't account for a spiritualized version of "I pulled myself up by the bootstraps" kind of testimony so familiar to the American expectation. Rather the believer can only offer the rather boring (so it seems) story of how faithful parents together with a faithful church "parented" him or her to Christ with no major bumps along the way. The Christian therefore declines an invitation to tell of God's faithfulness through his/her church and family with words something like, "there's nothing really to tell about."

Do you hear the question, the "church question" that is? (get response)

Here again, we see an indication of the question as to the church as an essential element of God today. It is revealed in how many today evaluate discipleship or conversion as strangely more authentic when less influenced by the church. Take for example the Gallup Poll taken in 1978 and the findings of Robert Bellah's Habits of the Heart, Individualism and Commitment in American Life. For in relation to religious institutions, Bellah found that individualism in America had resulted in a tragic reversal in how one understands his or her faith in relation to the religious community. As summarized by Bellah, " For American's, the traditional relationship between the individual and the religious community is to some degree reversed. On the basis of our interviews, we are not surprised to learn that a 1978 Gallup poll found that 80 percent of Americans agreed that an individual should arrive at his or her own religious beliefs independent of any church or synagogue. From the traditional point of view, this is a strange statement-- it is precisely within the church or synagogue that one comes to one's religious beliefs-- but to many Americans, it is the Gallup finding that is normal."

We see then in the Bellah summation what some have described as one of the most prominent legacies of modernity-- a legacy that has more or less been disdainful of all social parenting as through institutions of any kind, the religious institutions proving no exception. Influenced by the modern notion of individualism, a person's faith as nurtured from childhood in a Christian home in cooperation with the faithful ministry of a local church is suspiciously less authentic in comparison to the testimonials that are so often celebrated in a Christian conference. But how does this compare to the Biblical pattern of passing down the faith from generation to generation-- a pattern that was celebrated by Paul with respect to Timothy? (2 Tim. 1:5) Even tradition itself in the nurture of faith is discounted. But again, how does this square with the expectation of scripture? (1 Cor. 11:2, 2 Thess. 2:15)

    Argument summrized:

    An Addendum:

    Reasons for Why We Believe the Church Visible Is an Essential Element of the Gospel--Briefly Summarized

    1. A "Presence" Theology of Salvation Requires A Visible Church:

    Nothing short of a gospel that mediates a regulated and divinely appointed presence will sufficiently satisfy an authentic gospel. The "full gospel" is defined in Ephesians where "Christ fills all in all." And clearly what is not meant here is the filling by an individualized Holy Spirit, but rather that kind of filling that is explicitly stated in the passage, "for the church which is his body…" (Eph.1:22, see also Eph.2:19-22 and then Eph.4:10ff) This church made visible in its sacramental worship, confession and pastoral oversight. (see 1 Tim.1-3)

    Furthermore, it can be shown in redemptive-history that ever since Adam and his posterity were excommunicated from "before the face of God" (Gen.3:8) until the elect are reunited back into God's immediate presence (as told in Revelations 21:3-4 using language derived from Leviticus 26:9ff), God has in a provisional way mediated his saving presence as through divinely appointed sacramental rituals, governments and confessions in every redemptive era. King David certainly understood the omnipresence of God in the world, yet he still longed for that saving presence that was mediated through the ordinances and worship of the old covenant. (Pss. 84:2) Orthodoxy in the Old Testament era was described in terms like "dwelling place", "living God", "tabernacle" even as covenants where initiated and sealed through rites whereby God manifested his presence to his people.. The New Covenant, although under new apostolic forms, proves no exception as promised by Christ in Matthew 28:18-20, " I am with you until the end of the age."

    2. Precedent in Redemptive History:

    There was never a time when God did not mediate his means of grace through visible ordinances as authorized by God through special revelation. An "unregulated" ministry of the word, sacraments and government is practically unthinkable after even a cursory review of the teachings of God throughout each of the covenants

    ·Adam-Abraham – Patriarchal system (whereby God's grace was mediated through the prophetic, priestly, and kingly aspects of the patriarch).

    ·Mosaic – Whereby God's grace was mediated through the prophetic, priestly, and kingly aspects of the temple system.

    ·New Covenant – Whereby God's grace is mediated through the prophetic, priestly, and kingly aspects of the apostolic system with Christ as the cornerstone (Eph. 1:22, 2:20, 4:10—11ff, 1 Tim. 3:15).

    3. The Biblical Application of Christology:

    Good ecclesiology (doctrine of the church) is good Christology (doctrine of Christ) applied. (This is ultimately why good ecclesiology is to the honor of Christ himself.) Christ's offices of prophet, priest, and king are presently mediated through the Holy Spirit as he works through human agency (Eph. 4:10—11). The system whereby an authorized human agency is expressed has been instituted by Christ (Mat. 16:18) upon the foundation established by the apostles (Eph. 2:20) whereby Christ as Head fills (mediates His presence) in His corporate body, the Church (Eph.1:22, 4:10—11).

    Christ's office of prophet is presently mediated through the ministry of preaching; His office of priest is presently mediated through the ministry of the sacraments in worship; and His office of king is presently mediated through the ministry of government – all three of which are the defining marks of Christ's visible church. (Notice how Paul addresses each aspect in his charge to Timothy: apostolic doctrine, 1 Tim. 1; apostolic worship, 1 Tim. 2; apostolic government, 1 Tim. 3). He concludes that these instructions are how "it is necessary to conduct oneself in the household of God, the church of the living God, the pillar and bulwark of the truth."

    4. The Implication of the Great Commission (Mat. 28:19—20):

    The Great Commission was given not to the crowds, but to those to whom God entrusted the task of building His church. The one command – "to make disciples" – is qualified with "by baptizing them" and "teaching them to obey." These qualifications correspond to the three offices of Christ insofar as his priestly role is mediated through sacramental entrance into a sacramental community ("baptizing them"), his prophetic role mediated through "teaching," and his kingly role mediated through an accountability unto obedience. When this commission is coupled with the foundation built by the apostles, it is clear that there was an authorized means by which these three aspects of "disciple-ship" were to be accomplished.

    5. The Positive Institution by Christ of a Visible Church (Mat. 16:18ff):

    That Christ instituted the "church" by His own command is clear to all. That the "church" in Mat. 16:18 is, in fact, visible with real "keys" (indicating the power to admit or demit people into its society) is likewise evident by the simple fact that what is ratified in heaven is something that is actually happening on "earth." The language of "binding and loosing" as applied to the use of the "keys" is further clarified in Mat.18:17—18 to be the exercise of church government to declare them as outside the means of grace ("Let such a one be to you as a Gentile or tax collector"), later illustrated by the New Covenant context of the gospel as in 1 Cor. 5:1—5.

    6. The Church Is Said to Be the Pillar and Bulwark of the Truth, Not Individuals (1 Tim. 3:15):

    The "truth" is also described by Paul as "sound doctrine" (2 Tim. 4:3, Titus 1:9, 2:1), "the teaching" (1 Tim. 6:3), "the things which you have heard from me" (2 Tim. 2:2), "sound words" (1 Tim. 6:3), and "the faith" (1 Tim. 3:9) – all of which comprise "the gospel" (1 Thess. 2:8, 2 Tim. 1:8,10). According to Paul, this "truth" is entrusted not to individuals but to the "assembly" or "church" (1 Tim. 3:15). This is the same "church" that Paul has instructed pertaining to doctrine, worship, and govern-ment (chapters 1, 2, and 3, respectively). Whereas individuals may or may not be in some relation to "the truth," the assembly is authorized to declare the truth as a standard to be applied in the administration of sacraments and discipline.

    7. By Apostolic Succession, the Church Is Authorized in its Ministry:

    The language of "appoint" as applied to the offices of ministry is clearly used in the pastoral Epistles. This is significant since the pastoral Epistles were written as in-structions for the church as it was to continue on after the apostolic era (Titus 1:5). The appointment of "officers" (however one may name them) in the church is accompanied by the "laying of hands" first by the apostles (Acts 8:18) and later by the "presbytery" (1 Tim. 4:14, KJV), indicating an authorization that is ultimately traced back to Christ insofar as he authorized the apostles to do this (Mat. 16:18, Eph. 2:20). Therefore, where we do not hold to a human succession traced through human lineage as the Roman church does, we do recognize a lineage that has been handed down through "presbyteries," or that "corporate body" called the "church" in 1 Tim. 3:15, which is the guardian of the Truth.

    8. Affirmation Throughout Church History:

    One of the things "believed" in the Apostles Creed is "I believe in the one holy catholic church." That this "church" refers to that that church that includes the visible, assembled church of every age and every place throughout redemptive history and in heaven is further illustrated by the various affirmations to this throughout church history. So, for instance,

    · Thirty-nine Articles: Article XIX, of the Church (Of The Episcopal Tradition) – The visible Church of Christ is a congregation of faithful men, in which the pure word of God is preached and the sacraments be duly ministered according to Christ's ordinance in all those things that of necessity are requisite to the same. As the Church of Jerusalem, Alexandria, and Antioch have erred: so also the Church of Rome hath erred, not only in their living and manner of ceremonies, but also in matters of faith.
