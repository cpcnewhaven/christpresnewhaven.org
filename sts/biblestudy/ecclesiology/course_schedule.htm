Evangelical and Reformed Ecclesiology and Polity

Course Instructor: Rev. Preston Graham Jr.

 

Course Summary:

It has been said, "neither a theology without a Church, any more than a Church without a theology, fulfills all the conditions of a pure gospel." The first objective of this course will be to discern the truth of this statement by constructing an Ecclesial Theology out of a Redemptive Historical analysis of the Bible. It will be discovered how redemptive history is not merely about the history of a saving message but of a saving presence as regarding a sacramental conception of the gospel within a living fellowship. Therefore, the student will discover that "the Bible is not more a book of Theology, technically so called, than a book of ecclesiastical history."

The second objective of the course will be to thoughtfully engage the issues and topics relevant to ministry in the contemporary church within the classic Scoto–American tradition. The prophetic, priestly and kingly aspects of Christ acting in and through the church will be applied to various ecclesial issues including: church vocation and polity (including issues regarding the vocation of women in ministry), discipline and pastoral care, covenantal worship and liturgy, subscription, mercy and diaconal ministry, ordination, church unity and ecumenism, church planting and evangelism.

As relevant to a denominational polity course, the student will notice that the first objective pertains to the ecclesial assumptions that are typical of a Presbyterian "Preface to the Book of Church Order." The second objective pertains to the various topics covered in the "Form of Government, Rules of Discipline and Directory For The Worship of God" sections. The Course is designed to meet over the period of ten weeks, one class per week lasting approximately 3 hours each. Each class will consisting of a one hour lecture followed by a 1 1/2 hour class discussion relevant to the class lecture and required readings. Some classes will involve clergy/church planters residing in New England.

Annotated bibliography on supplemental readings to be determined by student in consultation with instructor. An annotated bibliography prepared by student on supplemental readings will be due by the end of the course.

  1. Skim Denominational Book of Church Order of students choice
  2. Writing Assignment: Each student will be required to prepare EITHER:

1) a single 15-20 page research essay on a topic relevant to Ecclesial theology as determined by the student in consultation with instructor. Topic could range from a Biblical Theology of "Presence," a Theology of Church Vocation, Confessionalism, Ecclesial Ethics, etc.)

OR,

2) The student may elect to write two, 5-7 page papers on various issues in Practical Ecclesiology on topics pertaining to a practical function of the church and/or pastoral ministry. Such papers will be broken into three sections: 1)Theological Issues and 2) Strategic Issues, 3) "Philosophy of Ministry." Topics would include such things as "Marriage and Divorce," Death and Dying, Church Planting, Ecclesial Evangelism, etc. (All of which would then be driven to develop a ministry plan as related to the above three sections).

E. Participation in Class Discussion: Each class will include at least one hour of discussion where the student is expected to integrate the readings with the topic.
