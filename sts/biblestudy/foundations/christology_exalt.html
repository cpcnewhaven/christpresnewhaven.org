I. Resurrection of Christ

Citadel of the Christian Faith, for on this one point, the Christian faith stands or falls.

1 Cor. 15:14, if Christ has not been raised, then our preaching is in vain and your faith is in vain.

An Historic Event

1 Cor. 15:6-Then he appeared to more than 500 brothers and sisters at one time, most of whom are still alive, though some have fallen asleep.

--These people proclaimed the resurrection to the hazard of their own lives.

 

II. Ascension

"That act of the God-man by which he brought to an end his post-resurrection appearances to his disciples, was finally parted from them as to his physical presence, and passed into the other world, to remain there until his second advent."

Luke 25:51, While he was blessing [the disciples], he withdrew from them and was carried up into heaven.

Mark 16:19, So then the Lord Jesus, after he had spoken to [the disciples], was taken up into heaven and sat down at the right hand of God.

 

III. Session

A "sitting" on the throne of the universe, thereby signifying sovereignty (I Kings 22:19), holiness (Psalm 47:8), and majesty (Isaiah 6:1-4). The Old Testament portrays Yahweh "sitting" on this throne, and the Messiah is invited to occupy the position of honor at his right hand (Psalm 110:1). In the New Testament, Jesus Christ, as this long-expected Anointed One, is exalted by God the Father to this position.

Acts 3:32-33, This Jesus God raised up...being therefore exalted at the right hand of God.

Colossians 3:1, ...seek the things that are above, where Christ is, seated at the right hand of God.

1 Peter 3:22, ...Jesus Christ, who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers made subject to him.

 

This section of the Creed represents the culmination of the earthly ministry of Jesus. His life and death could not have its full effect until he ascended to the presence of his Father, to whom he presented his finished work of atonement. The resurrection assures us of Christ's victorious triumph over the grave, and the efficacy of his work as mediator. The ascension displays that his work is perfected. From his throne, Christ continues his work as prophet, priest, and king through his church. Though his work is finished, Christ is progressively consummating his finished work of redemption.

IV. Implications of Glorification:

A. The central doctrine pertaining to Christology.

1 Tim.3:16, He was revealed in flesh, vindicated by the Spirit.

Crucial Point: As long as Christ remained is under the spell of death, His Messianic identity as the one whom would fulfill the eternal throne of David was seriously jeopardized.

 

B. The central doctrine pertaining to Soteriology (Salvation)

1 Cor. 15:17, If Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins.

Heb. 7:23, The former priests were many in number because they were prevented by death from continuing in office, but he holds his priesthood permanently because he continues forever... he always lives to made intercession for them.

Rom.4:25, raised for our justification

Crucial Point: As long as he remained in a state of death, the righteous character of his work , the efficacy of his obedience unto death remained in question, in fact, implicitly denied.. God's acceptance of sacrifice remains in question. Resurrection represents the removal of Guilty verdict and God's affirmation of the work of 2nd Adam.

 

C. The central doctrine pertaining to Sanctification. (Personal Holiness)

Rom 8:11, If the Spirit of Him who raised Jesus from the dead dwells in you, he who raised Christ from the dead will give life to your mortal bodies also through the Spirit that dwells in you.

Rom 6:4, Just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.

Crucial Point: Christ resurrection is the bases for our living as new creatures. As long as Christ remained dead, the old nature enslaved to sin continues to live and the power of the reigning Christ through the Holy spirit is not available

 

D. The central doctrine pertaining to eschatology. (The End Time)

1 Cor. 15:20, 22, Christ has been raised from the dead, the first fruits of hose who have died... for as all died in Adam, so all will be made alive in Christ.

Crucial Point: Our hope in eternal life after death is based upon Christ's resurrection. "O Death, were is your sting!" As long as Christ remained dead, death is still triumphant, and the grave remains sealed shut!

Addendum on Miracles:

 

A. How to understand the function of "miracles"

Deuteronomy 13:1ff, If a prophet, or one who foretells by dreams, appears among you and announces to you a miraculous sign or wonder, and is the sign or wonder of which he has spoken takes place, and he says, "Let us follow other gods," (gods you have not known) "and let us worship them", you must not heed the words of those prophets or those who divine by dreams... (See Mt.24:24 & Mk.13:22)

Mark 16:20, "And they went out and proclaimed the good news everywhere, while the Lord worked with them and confirmed the message by the signs that accompanied it."

Acts 2:22 "You that are Israelites, listen to what I have to say: Jesus of Nazareth, a man attested to you by God with deeds of power, wonders, and signs that God did through him among you, as you yourselves know--

Heb. 2:3 how can we escape if we neglect so great a salvation? It was declared at first through the Lord, and it was attested to us by those who heard him, 4 while God added his testimony by signs and wonders and various miracles, and by gifts of the Holy Spirit, distributed according to his will.

 

J. Calvin: "Miracles strengthen the authority of God's messengers. Now these very numerous and remarkable miracles which he relates are so many confirmations of the law that he has delivered and of the doctrine that He has published." (Institutes, 1.8.5)

 

Implications:

1. Can't separate the message from the "signs" (miracles) that authenticate the messenger. Thus, we can't separate the teaching of Jesus from his activity as an exorcist, healer and worker of signs and wonders. He was not just an enlightened humanitarian.

2. The Christian faith IS a faith vindicated by miracles which did happen in time.

3. The miracle ITSELF is not alone sufficient to vindicate a message. The message itself must be consistent to prior revelations already verified by God. (Dt. 13)

 

B. How to define a "miracle":

Two categories of miracles: (p.17ff, C. Brown, History and Faith)

1. "Coincidence concept": No apparent violation of the laws of nature but a conjunction of circumstances that is so unexpected, improbable and beneficial to at least suggest a supernatural ordering of circumstances. (I.e. A unique directing of Providence given a certain set of circumstances)

2. "Violations of the law of nature" : It is impossible and yet happened.

D. Hume: "A transgression of a law of nature by a particular volition of the Deity or by the interposition of some invisible agent." (I.e. super natural)

 

Note: # 1 is no less divine than # 2.

 

C. How to defend Miracles:

Experience proves this or that, depending on the preconceptions we bring to it.
