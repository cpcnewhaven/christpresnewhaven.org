Addendum: WORSHIP AND THE LORD'S SUPPER

The Nature of the Lord's Supper as a Sacrament:

There are many theological assumptions concerning the nature and constitution of the Lord's Supper that need to be parsed out. These theological assumptions serve as the theological grounds for much of what ought to be reflected in the worship service itself. These issues are clearly represented in the four characteristics of a any sacrament as summarized by James Bannerman. (The Church of Christ, Vol. 2, (1869), rpt.1960, Banner of Truth Trust)

1. Divine Appointment:

"The first mark or characteristic of a Sacrament is that it be a Divine institute appointed by Christ for His Church." James Bannerman

Luke 22:19, "Do this in remembrance of Me"

Matthew 26:26-28

1 Cor.11:26

1 Cor. 11:23-26

Act 2:42

"There is abundant evidence to prove that the Lord's Supper was no temporary ordinance, destined to pass away with the first merging of the Jewish into the Christian Church; but, on the contrary, was intended to be an abiding appointment for the use of its members." (James Bannerman Vol. 2, p.131)

By Implication:

"It is the prerogative of Christ, as king and head of the Church to institute religious ordinances; and we are not at liberty to add to, or diminish from his appointments. Robert Shaw, Exposition of the Confession of Faith, (Philadelphia: Presbyterian Board of Publication; 1847) p. 318

2. Sign:

The next mark of a sacramental ordinance is "that they be sensible and outward signs of spiritual truths; and this mark applies to the ordinance of the Lord's Supper." (Bannerman, Vol.2, p.132)

Matthew 26:17-30

1 Cor. 11:22-26

1 Cor.10:17

The bread representing Christ's crucified body,-- the wine poured out, the shed blood,-- the eating and drinking of them, the participation in Christ's blessings to nourish the soul and make it glad,-- the "one bread" and "one cup", the communion of Christ with His people, and of them with each other,-- all these are no dumb or dark signs, but speaking and expressive of what it is intended to commemorate. This obvious characteristic of a sacramental ordinance, then, is most clearly seen in the Lord's supper, that it is an outward and sensible sign of an inward and spiritual truth. (James Bannerman, Vol.2, p.134)

As a sign we are encouraged to remember the death of Christ as a substitutionary sacrifice for our sins. The Lord's supper is not a sacrifice itself (the catholic belief of transubstantiation), but a commemorative ordinance and therefore a memorial of the great atonement and offering up of Christ on the Cross. In other words, as a covenantal meal we are reminded that the benefits of the new covenant are the forgiveness of sins (Mt.26:28-29). Furthermore, we are reminded that the expense of the new covenant is the sacrifice of Christ's body and blood. And finally, we are reminded that the duration of the new covenant is "until he comes."

Parenthetical Insert Concerning Roman Catholic practice and Evangelical over Reaction:

One of the unfortunate over reactions to the Romanish doctrine of transubstantiation by many in evangelical Protestant traditions is to minimize the sacramental element in the Lord's supper altogether. It is certainly true that the Roman Catholic doctrine concerning the Lord's Supper is in effect the "superstitious idolatry and adoration of the elements." John Calvin, Institutes of the Christian Religion, Vol. 2 (Philadelphia: Westminster Press; 1960) Book 4, Chapt. 7, pp.1411) Yet to see in the Lord's supper merely a memorial is also to demean the language of institution attributed to the Lord's Supper as well.

Two Key Passages of Scripture Pertaining to Two false teachings in the Roman Church:

Hebrews 9:26-28; 10:10-14

The Roman Catholic understanding of "Transubstantiation" is that the "substance of bread and wine is changed into Christ's real body and blood leaving only the appearances of taste and so on of bread and wine." ( Phillip St. Romain, Catholic Answers..., p,34) According to the Roman catholic doctrine, though the body of Christ remains in heaven, it is also present not in one place on earth only, but in a thousand places, where ever the Mass is attended. . The once and for all substitutionary sacrifice of Christ work for atonement is therefore usurped by the multiple sacrifices of Christ's body wherever the priest has pronounced the words of institution. We beleive this to be in direct contradiction to the teachings of Hebrews 9:26, 28 and 10:10,14 where the emphasis upon the offering of the body of Jesus Christ as being "once for all" is clearly asserted.

Heb.4:14, 7:8,24

"The existence of an earthly priesthood as a standing ordinance of the Christian Church is inconsistent with the one office of Christ as the Priest and Mediator of His people... The office which He sustains and discharges in heaven is His own incommunicable office, which none save Himself has either the right or the power to discharge. Christ is the one priest that has made the sacrifice and offered it to God for the sins of many,-- there was none that could share with Him in that mighty and mysterious work. Christ is the one Priest to stand between God and a sinful world, there was none but the Son that could undertake so to approach unto the Most High. Christ is the one Priest to dispense unto men throughout all ages the blessings of redemption and grace,-- there is none equal to the task but He "in whom dwelleth all the fullness of the Godhead bodily. (Bannerman, p.169)

The Evangelical Over-reaction:

Yet in reaction to the Romanish doctrine, the tendency has been to devalue the Lord's supper as merely a commemoration ceremony. In effect, this over reaction treats the Lord's Supper as a sign only with no spiritual realty as a seal. This second extreme has negated the "koinonia" (communion) language associated with the Lord's Supper.

3. Seal:

The third mark of a sacramental ordinance is "that they are the seals of a federal (representative) transaction between the believer and Christ through means of the ordinance; and this mark is applicable to the Lord's Supper." (Bannerman, p.135)

a. Communion with God:

1 Cor.10:15- "the bread which we break, is it not the communion ("koinonia") of the body of Christ?"

The koinonia--the communion, (or participation, or interchange, or mutual fellowship) of the blood of Christ and the body of Christ--- cannot possibly be understood as the mere signs of the body and blood, without a very violent experiment practiced on the language of the apostle. And if "fellowship" does not refer to the outward symbol, it can only refer to the spiritual blessings represented in the ordinance-- to Christ Himself present after a spiritual manner in the Sacrament, and giving himself to the believer, while the believer gives himself to Christ, so as to establish a true "koinonia", or fellowship, or communion between them. (James Bannerman, Church of Christ, Vol.2, p. 139)

As a seal, it is a means of grace which presupposes the existence of saving grace whereby Christ comes to God's chosen children to impart yet higher and further blessings much like the grace which comes through the preaching of God's Word. The Lord's Supper becomes a spiritual transaction between the believer and Christ resulting in the promotion of spiritual nourishment and growth for believers. In both the preaching of the Word and in the enactment of that Word through participating in the Lord's Supper, the grace of God in Christ comes through faith by means of the moving of the Holy Spirit after the sovereign will of the Father to those who are His children. The Lord's Supper is therefore more than a sign which signifies a spiritual reality, it is a seal in which a real transaction is taking place with real spritual benefits being given.

b. Communion with God's people:

The Lord's supper is also used by scripture to promote the communion with others in the new covenant. In so far as we partake of one bread we affirm our union with one another in Christ. This is depicted also in worship when the congregation joins together in unison to confess our common belief as Christ's holy people. We therefore conclude the Lord's Supper with our unison recitation of a Christian creed in order to verbally confess our common faith even as it has been enacted by partaking of the Lord's supper together.

1 Cor.10:17- "because there is one bread, we who are many are one body, for we all partake of the one bread."

"this ordinance is very expressive of the communion of saints, and has a powerful tendency to cherish it." (Shaw, p.321)

4. Means of Grace:

"The fourth and last mark "is, that it is a means (instrument) of grace; and this mark also applies to the ordinance of the Lord's Supper." (Bannerman, p.140)

1 Cor. 11:26- "... you proclaim the Lord's death..."

1 Cor. 10:16

And finally, another use of the Lord's supper according to Scripture is to join with the Word in proclaiming Christ. In this manner, the Lord's supper, even by its exclusion of those who are not professing believers in Christ, tends to be evangelistic in its proclamation of Christ. "It presupposes, indeed, the existence of a saving grace on the part of the participator in the ordinance." (Bannerman, p.140)

Means of Grace when received by Faith:

"It is not after a corporal or carnal manner; that is, it is not by the mouth or as ordinary food is received. It is by Faith and therefore by the soul... If the reception is not by the mouth but by faith, then the thing received is not the material body and blood, but the body and blood as a sacrifice, their sacrificial virtue. Hence, the body and blood of Christ are received... which are their symbols and the organs of communication, as elsewhere the Word is that organ." (C. Hodge quoted by Bannerman, p.158)

The Means of Administering the Lord's Supper:

Having asserted a high view of the Lord's Supper and its intended ends, several other theological truths can now be explicated concerning the proper means of observing the Lord's supper as regulated by Scripture.

1. Perpetual Observance:

1 Cor.11:26- "for as often as you eat you show the Lord's death "till He comes"

The sacrament of the Lord's Supper is to be observed in the Church to the end of the world and the return of Christ to claim his own. At that time, we sup with the Lord in a heavenly reality so much so that we will enjoy immediate and physical presence.

2. Those who should not participate: ("fencing the table")

1 Cor.11:27-29,- "examine himself"

Every participant of the sacrament of the Lord's Supper should possess some good evidence of his/her being in a gracious state. Therefore, as required in 1 Cor.11:27-29, the participant should "examine himself" lest he/she should "eat and drink judgment to themselves."

The meaning of "examine him(her)self:

This command in scripture does not mean that we are to examine ourselves, and if we find that we are sinners, to not participate in the Lord's supper. Quite the contrary, the Lord's supper is for sinners in need of God's forgiving grace. Rather, the examination is to determine if there is some known sin that we do not wish to have God's help in turning away from it and his gracious forgiveness being applied to it. In other words, its one thing to be convicted of sin and willing to put ourselves under the means made available to us by Christ to turn away from it. (Eager submission to the preaching of God's word, the help, nurture and discipline of Christ's government in His church , etc.) It is yet another thing altogether to be convicted of sin yet be rebellious in our holding to it and not wishing for Christ through the government of his church to assist him/her in repentance. Therefore, we examine ourselves asking God to convict us of any unconfessed and unrepentant sin. In so far as we are willing to confess our sins asking for God forgiveness and help toward repentance, we ought to eagerly participate in the meal provided by Christ for our spiritual nourishment.

Those Excluded from the Lord's table:

From the implications of 1 Cor.11:27-29, the Lord's table is excluded to three classes of people:

(a) Infants and those unable to "examine yourself."

The first class of people excluded from the Lord's table are those in a state of ignorance such that the requirement to "examine himself" is not possible.

"to infants, or to any who are grossly ignorant of the fundamental principles of the Christian religion, whatever may be their age, all individuals who are absolutely incapable of that examination which the divine command explicitly enjoins." (Ashbel Green, Lectures on the Shorter Catechism of the Presbyterian Church in the United States of America, Vol. II, (Presbyterian n Board of Pub.: Philadelphia, 1841) p. 402

This would include those children who had not yet considered themselves sinners and trusted in Christ for his/her salvation. Indeed, part of the requirements for participation consist in a practical knowledge of the spiritual significance of the broken bread and the wine poured forth. This is one of the major purposes for the Christian "catechism" in church history. We encourage parents to use the Westminster Shorter Catechism as a guide toward helping their children have a credible profession of their personal faith in Christ.

(b) Those not a member of some gospel believing Church:

In that the sacrament of the Lord's supper is intended to promote and reflect the common unity of the body of Christ in the assembled form (1 Cor.10:16), the person who is not an active member in Christ's church cannot examine him/herself with respect to those sins explicit in 1 Cor. 11:17-17-22. It is important to note that Paul's instructions are to those who "assemble as a church". (1 Cor. 11:18) For this reason, the Presbyterian Book of Church order invites all those who "are communicants in good standing in any evangelical church" to participate and therefore excluding those who are not.

(c) Unrepentant sinners excommunicated by Church:

The final group of people excluded are those that through either self-examination or by the censure of Church officers have been found to be immoral or scandalous and in a state of unrepentant and therefore should be prohibited to participate in the Lord's supper in an unworthy manner. (1 Cor.11:27) This in effect is the meaning of "ex-communication".

(See also 1 Cor. 5:3-13, "remove the wicked man from among yourselves."

3. Frequent Communion:

Acts 2:42, 20:7,

1 Cor.11:20ff.

The Lord's Supper should be celebrated frequently as was the Apostolic practice. The Biblical evidence as recorded in Acts 2:42 suggest that the Lord's supper was practiced every time the church gathered for worship. Moreover, it is affirmed that the Lord's supper was in effect that climatic purpose for which the early Christians were to assemble.

"thus it became the unvarying rule that no meeting of the church should take place without the Word, prayers, partaking of the supper and alms giving. That this was the established order among the Corinthians also, we can safely infer from Paul.(1 Cor.11:20)" (Calvin, Institutes, Vol. 2p. 1422)

Weekly observance was promoted by Luther, Calvin and the early Puritans. In that the ordinance is regulated by divine law as stated above, the Lord's supper is best served when practiced every week as one element of worship.

4. Administered by the visible Church only:

Acts 2:38-42

1 Cor. 11:18

The practice of the Lord's Supper is to be administered in a proper ecclesiastical context as observed in Acts 2 and 1 Cor. 11. First, the Acts 2 and 1 Cor. 11 passages reveal that the Lord's supper is to be administered as one of five elements of worship. Second, The instructions given in 1 Cor. 11 would be irrelevant outside of an established church context. For instance, the power given to the officers of the church to ex-communicate would have little or no meaning if communion could be administered in any setting other than a church constituted by its elected and properly trained officers. Finally, the above passages are clearly speaking of the visible church as gathered together for worship. Among other things, these observations prevents the Lord's supper from being administered in any context other than the worship service of a visible and gathered church. (I.e. private communion, family communion, etc.)

WCF 25.3:

"Unto this catholic visible church, Christ hath given the ministry, oracles, and ordinances of God for the gathering and perfecting of the saints."
