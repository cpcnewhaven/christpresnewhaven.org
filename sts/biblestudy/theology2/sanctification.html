Illustration: Bill Bright's "Spirit Filled Life" (A variation of the "victorious life" view noted above, see Bright's The Secret: How to Live with Purpose and Power, 1989 or his Have You Made the Wonderful Discovery of the Spirit-Filled Life? , 1966))

In Bright's words, "although all Christians are indwelt by the Holy Spirit, not all Christians are filled by the Holy Spirit." (thus a distinction being made between two experiences or perhaps "states") By "filled" Bright means "to be controlled and empowered by Him rather than by your own motivations and desires."

Response:

1. A contradiction to the doctrine of effectual calling?

Our response will be to ask, what else is a Christian if not characterized by this (see above)? How else was a person enabled to embrace Christ if not by the "filling" of the Holy Spirit even as defined by Bright? That is to say that we will argue that conversion was just as much a "filling" as is sanctification so as to mean that Bright's distinction between "indwelling" and "filling" is arbitrary at best. As will be demonstrated , this reveals the Armenian assumptions right off, Bright's understanding of salvation to be somehow the work of human will rather than God's sovereign will.

2. Denies or underplays the eschatological aspect of the "now and not yet" dynamic of the Kingdom of God such as to correlate with a "more and more" sanctification that is progressive.

Idea of Gradual sanctification is lost so as to require some "higher" reality/experience, at man's ultimate control. Notice then our idea of sanctification as presented below. First, the necessary connection between "effectual calling"... "sanctification" such that all Christians, if they are Christian, will manifest themselves by their good works. Second, the "more and more" language to depict the ongoing process of sanctification which explains why some Christians are more "filled" (if this means controlled by the Holy Spirit) than others, but not indicating the need for a new experience. Third, the possibility that Christians will stumble, but are never "dead" in any true sense.

3. No exegetical warrant for "three" states (unregenerate, regenerate [indwelt) and regenerate/carnal [not filled]

See 1 Cor. 2:14-16

Paul gives us his spiritual taxonomy in 1 Cor. 2:14-16 consisting of the "spiritual" and "unspiritual", merely as a way to speak of those effectually called and those not effectually called by the Holy Spirit such as they are enabled by God to embrace Christ. His point then in 1 Cor. 3:1-3 (Bright's passage for a third "carnal" category) is to say that they are acting like unspiritual men, "men of the flesh" (thus Bright's word "carnal") Bright takes this to mean that those to whom he is speaking is now a third category, but this clearly does violation to immediate context. Nothing in these verses state that these people are a different category from the "unspiritual" person just described by Paul.

4. Work of Holy Spirit is never "dormant" so as to render the Holy Spirit under subjetioin to human will. Whereas Bright has rightly recognized that the Christian life is a struggle (Gal.5:17), we would argue against his view concerning human "choosing" and "yielding" that effects the Holy Spirit's control in one's life.

5. The view subjects the Holy Spirit so as to under the power of the person-- a "human centered" position such as could result in "pride."

10. Is sanctification perfected in this life? (1John 1:10 "If we say that we have not sinned, we make Him a liar, and His word is not in us.")

LQ#78

Whence ariseth the imperfection of sanctification in believers?

The imperfection of sanctification in believers ariseth from the remnants of sin abiding in every part of them, and the perpetual lustings of the flesh against the spirit; whereby they are often foiled with temptations,and fall into many sins, are hindered in all their spiritual services, and their best works are imperfect and defiled in the sight of God.

II. Historical considerations: "Perfectionism"

A. Pelagius (v. Augustine)

1. Pelagius: In that God could not commission that which could not be obeyed, Pelagius asked, "how can we possibly refuse to accept that a man can be without sin, when we are ready to admit that nothing impossible has either been forbidden or commanded?"("On the Possibility of Not Sinning" p.169) For scriptural support, Pelagius utilized the command in Leviticus 19:2, "You shall be holy for I the Lord your God am holy." And also, Pelagius used from the teaching of Christ Matthew 5:48, "You must be perfect as your heavenly Father is Perfect." And finally, he employed the teachings of Paul in Philippians 2:14-15, "Do all things... that you may be blameless and innocent, as the children of God."(Ibid. p.169)

2. Augustine: Augustine spoke of perseverance rather than perfection in this life. He notes that "the perseverance by which we persevere in Christ even to the end is the gift of God."("On the Gift of Perseverance" p.526 For Augustine, "no one lives in this corruptible body, however righteous he may be, without sins of some kind."(Ibid p. 527 Therefore, the evidence of true election in Christ was not perfection but rather perseverance until death.

11. A "passive/active" paradigm of sanctification:

A review of scripture suggest that we can in fact "rest" even as we are commanded to be holy so as to indicate a kind of "striving." So, it is an "act of God's free grace" according to #35 of our Shorter catechism , yet we are commanded to "be holy" so as to present our bodies a living and holy sacrifice acceptable to God" (Heb.12:1). How is this? It seems that the answer is to remain under and within the "means of grace."

Eph. 5:25 ...just as Christ also loved the church and gave Himself for her, 26 that He might sanctify and cleanse her with the washing of water by the word,

I.e. Whereas only God can make us new as through an "infusion of a new principle of life"(C. Hodge, Systematic Theology, 221), we can in fact, out of our new affections, remain in his love so to speak by remainin under his presence as under the influence of prayer, ministry of word and sacraments and corporate discipline. Whereas God makes us new, his "means" is mediated to us through the means of grace. We "rest" according to Hebrews even as we consider how then to stimulate one another to love and good deeds as this leads to corporate worship for example (see Heb.12:19ff).

Heb. 4:11 ¶ Let us therefore be diligent to enter that rest, lest anyone fall according to the same example of disobedience.

Thus Eph. 4.

4:11 And He Himself gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists, and some pastors and teachers, 12 for the equipping of the saints, for the work of ministry, for the edifying of the body of Christ, 13 till we all come to the unity of the faith and of the knowledge of the Son of God, to a perfect man, to the measure of the stature of the fullness of Christ; 14 that we should no longer be children, tossed to and fro and carried about with every wind of doctrine, by the trickery of men, in the cunning craftiness of deceitful plotting, 15 but, speaking the truth in love, may grow up in all things into Him who is the head--Christ-- 16 from whom the whole body, joined and knit together by what every joint supplies, according to the effective working by which every part does its share, causes growth of the body for the edifying of itself in love.

All this then to say that our sanctification is directly related to our "union" with Christ even as this is both "inside" union as by the Holy Spirit and "outside" union as by the "means of grace." We are who Christ is-- in so far as Christ is in us-- this more and more as we draw near to glory! In this sense, we are "mortified" with Christ even as we are being raised with Christ, both not yet fully but later completely and perfectly. So then, the benediction of every believer is Christ's presence which sanctifies.

1Th. 5:23 ¶ Now may the God of peace Himself sanctify you completely; and may your whole spirit, soul, and body be preserved blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ.

Heb. 13:20 ¶ Now may the God of peace who brought up our Lord Jesus from the dead, that great Shepherd of the sheep, through the blood of the everlasting covenant, 21 make you complete in every good work to do His will, working in you what is well pleasing in His sight, through Jesus Christ, to whom be glory forever and ever. Amen.

Our focus then is not on ourselves so as to be sanctified more and more, but on Christ. Christ's work for justification by death and resurrection is acting out in us now through santification. Personal holiness then is a very real and active aspect of "christ crucified." The Lord's table placed as it where in corporate worship as surrounded by the other means of grace perfectly mediates this-- in that it both directs the Christian to their justification in Christ even while while providing the Christian with renewal and a call to committment "in Christ."
