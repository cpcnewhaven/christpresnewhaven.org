The Biblical Vision For Church Planting

 

If the reader is convinced that the "Church is essential for the Gospel," then by implication church planting is the means and ends of the Great Commission just as church multiplication is the result of the Great Commission. Therefore, the present concern is to unveil the Biblical "vision" for church planting as this flows from the simple proposition: "the church is essential for the gospel." The rationale for this proposition has been the subject of this booklet and will not be reproduced here. What will be discerned is the unique and special provision made in scripture for church planting as this was instituted after the apostles under the office of "evangelist" albeit clearly anticipated by the work of the apostles themselves.

It is true that many people today tend to identify the role of the "evangelist" with primarily that of itinerant preaching and one on one evangelism. In this, the "evangelist" is either a kind of traveling "orator" of the gospel and/or everyone that is a Christian who can talk more or less. In the first understanding of "evangelist," some kind of Biblical qualifications or "standards" may or may not be required so as to be "ordained" by some "sending agency. The second understanding of "evangelist" is merely a Christian who assumes the responsibility to "preach the gospel" so to speak. In both cases, the Great Commission is consequently not essentially about church planting but rather personal evangelism and merely the publication of a message. The gospel, as such, is but a theology without a church!

What we have seen in this booklet is that the gospel is more than a message, it is the establishment of a mediatorial body, the church, whereby the "full" gospel of Christ's tri-fold offices as Mediator is participated in through the preaching, worship and discipline of the church. What will be suggested here is that a distinction needs to be made between the Biblical role and duty of individual Christians to become "witnesses" and the Biblical institution of an office of "evangelist" with the specific role of church planting.

That the "office" of evangelist even exists as one of the foundations laid for the post-apostolic period is evident in Ephesians 4:11, "And He Himself (ascended Christ) gave some to be apostles, some prophets, some evangelists and some pastors-teachers." Notice carefully the use of "some" so as to introduce four offices here (the latter office is described as "pastor and teacher" after the typical Pauline pattern of redundancy). In Ephesians 2:20, the first two offices are listed as relative to the "foundation stage" of building the church upon the cornerstone of Christ. In that this "foundation stage" has been accomplished, these two offices have ceased as with the cessation of apostolic canonization.

What then do we make of the latter two offices in Ephesians 4:11? If but by the logic inherent in the order, it is possible to discern a "role" differentiation corresponding to "church planting" (evangelist) and "church pastoring-teaching." This is further supported by roles assigned to evangelists Timothy in Ephesus and Titus in Crete to "do the work of an evangelist" (2 Tim.4:1-5) so as to include preaching the gospel and appointing elders in regions unreached with gospel believing churches. While the offices of "evangelist" and "pastor-teacher" may differ in "roles" they would both be distinguished from the "ruling elder" of 1 Tim 4:17 as one who "teaches." Consequently, both the evangelist and pastor-teacher would need to be Biblically qualified so as to "instruct the brethren" in the "words of faith and sound doctrine" as a "minister of Jesus Christ." (1 Tim.4:6, see also vs.16) Yet they are different in that the "evangelist" is particularly called to establish/organize churches whereas the pastor-teacher would serve in established/organized churches. This is evident if but for the simple fact that in the Pasatoral epistles, instructions are given not only pertaining to the "theology" of the gospel, but also regarding the "organization" of the gospel. The "evangelists" were to be concerned with establishing a pattern of sound doctrine, worship and government! As will be shown below, the historical narrative in Acts makes it clear that "church planting" was of primary importance to the apostles and their successors.

What these observations indicate is a Biblical "vision" for church planting established for the post-apostolic age by the apostles and written into the apostolic foundation itself. In other words, it was carefully secured within the foundation laid by the apostles that the "filling" ministry of Christ through the Holy Spirit (Eph.4:10) in the present age (after the "foundational" work of the apostles and prophets) is accomplished through the church which is either being "pastored" or "planted" by those who are later described by Paul as the "elders who teach" as distinguished from the "elders who rule." Perhaps to the modern reader the distinction is subtle seeming even to be "reading into" scripture. But a study of the historical narratives in Acts indicates that just as we see the preaching ministry of "pastors-teachers" anticipated by the apostles preaching, so too we see the church planting role of the "evangelist" anticipated by the apostles church planting. Theirs was an integrated ministry of word, sacrament and government!

As paradigmatic of the apostles ministry, Paul did not consider his apostolic work completed when converts were made. Rather he made it his business to finish the task by appointed elders in all the places where he harvested. (Act.14:23) The ultimate object of his labors was new churches. While we clearly see the "preaching" aspect of the apostles in Acts 2:41, 47, 4:4, 5:14, 6:7, 8:4-7-- we come to this striking observation in Acts 9:31, "Then the churches throughout all Judea, Galilee, and Samaria had peace and were edified." In other words, as summary to the work of the apostles, what we see is that church planting had been co-extensive with preaching. Numerous churches were the result of the apostles preaching-- not just private converts! The "evangelism" of the apostles was more than spreading the gospel message, it was also about the multiplication of gospel churches. Again, we see that the gospel is no less a "theology without a church" than a "church without a theology."

As this Biblical vision for church planting is established into our time, there is no Biblical reason to limit the "pattern of sound words" (2 Tim.1:13, Titus 2:7) spoken of by Paul for us to maintain so as to consist merely of words about justification, conversion, etc. Rather the "pattern of sound words" ought also to include words about such things as elders qualifications, deacons qualifications, directions for worship and so on. Samuel Miller out of Princeton said it this way,

"it is plain from the word of God, as well as from uniform experience that the government of the Church is a matter of great importance: that the form as well as the administration of that government is more vitally connected with the peace, purity and edification of the Church than many Christians appear to believe; and, of consequence, that it is no small part of fidelity to our Master in heaven to "hold fast" to a form of ecclesiastical order as well as the "form of sound words" which He has delivered to the saints." Samuel Miller, The Ruling Elder

Likewise, having given instructions for worship (2:1-15); and government (3:1-13) in 1 Timothy, Paul does not then say "these are preferable when expedient" but rather this is "how one ought" to conduct oneself in the household of God 1 Tim. 3:14-15). The point being that Paul evidently felt that the gospel included instructions concerning the planting of a church. To reduce the gospel so as to not include church planting is to no longer be the "full" gospel.

Conclusion

Biblically speaking, the gospel in the present age will spread to the degree that there is a church planting movement. In our drive to accomplish the great commission, we would certainly want to be self-conscious in our desire to preach the apostolic gospel message of salvation in Christ. But we would also want to be self-conscious in organizing apostolic worship and apostolic government as these are part of Christ's salvation. In other words, we are not merely preaching a gospel to individuals, but to those being called to form an "assembly" of God's people so as to be under the visible rule of God and gathered together for the worship of God. (Heb.10:22) Moreover, our purpose in planting churches is not just to preach justification, but to also preach sanctification, doxology and benediction-- things which require the assembling together. And of course this is what church planting is all about.

In that our commission is to take church planting to "all nations," we will necessarily want to think strategically about how exactly to do this. Perhaps one way to think of church planting is to be theologically conservative while being strategically progressive. This is not to say that strategy is unrelated to theology-- far be it! Rather even as our strategy ought to first be theological driven, these strategies will also need to consider the demographic realities of "nation." This will lead to an evangelism that is fully committed to "old" apostolic principles but fully committed to "new" and modern strategies as within the elements of worship, government and doctrine established by the foundation work of the apostles.

Finally, it exceeds the purpose here to give a specific strategy for church planting. At the very least, a strategy would be driven by the following five values.

1. A Church Planting Method That Is Self-Consciously Christ Centered

Nothing to compel, nothing to repel save Christ in all that we say and do-- What kind of a church would we have to be so that Christ is given the highest honor, the most esteemed priority and the most respected authority such that in "everything He might come to have first place?"

1 Cor.2:1-3, I came to you, brothers and sisters, I did not come proclaiming the mystery of God to you in lofty words or wisdom. for decided to know nothing among you except Jesus Christ, and Him crucified.

Colossians 1:18, He is the head of the body, the church; He is the beginning,... so that He might come to have first place in everything.

2. Church Planting That Balances Theological Reformation With Personal Revival

Without an emphasis upon revival, "reformation" becomes sterile doctrinalism. Without an emphasis on reformation, "revival" becomes shallow experientialism characterized by misinformed zeal. Only an emphasis on reformation and revival together can accomplish the Great Commission with Biblical integrity and personal sincerity that brings glory to God.

1Tim. 4:16 Pay close attention to yourself and to your teaching; continue in these things, for in doing this you will save both yourself and your hearers.

Titus 2:7 Show yourself in all respects a model of good works, and in your teaching show integrity, gravity,

3 A Church Planting Philosophy That Is Both Conservative And Progressive

We should be looking back while looking forward. We understand that true conservatism is an adherence to well established principles, not merely adherence to old measures/programs. What kind of methods will be consistent with our theology while also relevant to our "nation."

2Th. 2:15 So then, brothers and sisters, stand firm and hold fast to the traditions that you were taught by us, either by word of mouth or by our letter.

Matt. 28:19 Go therefore and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit,

 

4 A Communion with "No Distinctions"

We desire to challenge all human prejudices and strategies that create barriers in our strategies to reach all "human" categories-- for in Christ, there is no national, racial, sexual or financial distinctions. We desire to be a church movement for all peoples whose shared interest is exclusively "one Lord." This will necessarily mean being careful to plant churches concerned for the gospel and not "culture wars." Having a culture agenda will necessarily result in making distinctions along political, ethnic or economic lines. The church then becomes the promoter of a certain political, macro-economic, sociological and anthropological theory, rather than the gospel of Christ which is a "kingdom not of this world." This will mean a ministry that preaches Biblical themes in Biblical proportions lest personal-pet interests sabbatoge a healthy church planting movement. A healthy church planting movement for the "nations" will consequently have not time for "fourth of July" style sermons and preoccupation with a culture war agenda-- albeit left or right! Yet another way of saying this is that a church "for the nations" will necessarily want to be gospel centered rather than "nation" centered.

Rom. 10:12 For there is no distinction between Jew and Greek; the same Lord is Lord of all and is generous to all who call on him.

James 2:4 have you not made distinctions among yourselves, and become judges with evil thoughts?

Gal. 3:28 There is no longer Jew or Greek, there is no longer slave or free, there is no longer male and female; for all of you are one in Christ Jesus.

4 A Church Planting Plan To Accomplish The Great Commission Through Church Planting

This is NOT redundant! The church is an essential element of the Gospel such that there is no better way to make disciples than to plant healthy, God-centered church. How can our stratgy not only be about the planting of a healthy church but also a healthy church planting movement.

1Tim.3:15 if I am delayed, you may know how one ought to behave in the household of God, which is the church of the living God, the pillar and bulwark of the truth.
