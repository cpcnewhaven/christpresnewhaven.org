Marriage and Divorce

A Biblical Analysis

by Preston Graham, Jr.

 

 

I. The Sacred Institution of Marriage:

A. Marriage as initiated/instituted by God.  It is a Divine Institution!

Genesis 2:18, And the Lord said, "It is not good that the man should be alone; I will make him a helper as his partner.

Purpose of Marriage:

a. To satisfy the imago dei,  I.e. to be an aspect of the created image of the Triune God in relation together. (Gen.1:26, So God created humans in His image, in the image of God He created them male and female he created them.)

b. Complement for the sake of the creation mandate to pro-create and subdue creation for the glory of God. (vocational complementariness--Gen.1:28, God blessed them and God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the earth and subdue it, and have dominion over the fish..)

c. Companionship (Gen.2:18, It is not good for man to be alone)

d. To promote holiness and honor

1Ths. 4:1   Finally, brothers and sisters, we ask and urge you in the Lord Jesus that, as you learned from us how you ought to live and to please God (as, in fact, you are doing), you should do so more and more.   2 For you know what instructions we gave you through the Lord Jesus.   3 For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from fornication;   4 that each one of you know how to take a wife for himself in holiness and honor

 

B. Marriage as  a Covenantal Institution

1. Genesis 2:24, Therefore, a man leaves his father and his mother and cleaves to his wife, and they become one flesh

a. cleaving used descriptively means to bind together,  unity, cling, or join with. 

b. But it is important to see the covenantal language in this word! This is not just descriptive language,  but also language that places this relationship into a covenant context of binding vows!  For instance, when Scripture says that a man shall "cleave" to his wife (Gen.2:24), it is using a covenant term used elsewhere to express the way the Israelites were to cleave with affection and loyalty to the Lord. (Dt.10:20, 11:22, 13:4, Josh.22:5, etc.) 

 

2. Genesis 2:23, bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh

Likewise, when Adam made his solemn affirmation that "this is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh"  it was not some inane observation as if God needed to be informed concerning Eve's origins.  Rather these words constituted a solemn oath of covenant faithfulness-- an abbreviated way of saying "may this woman be to me as my own body-- and because of this, should I ever fail to love and care for her as my own body, thereby breaking the covenant bond between us, I hereby solemnly invite God to make it hurt me like bones and flesh being wrenched out of my body."   That this statement was a covenantal oath is further demonstrated by the use of the phrase "bone of my bones" in other similar oath taking ceremonies in the Ancient Near East as depicted in Genesis 29:14. (See also the use of this formula in Judges 9:2, 2 Samuel 19:12-13)  This formula then informs the NT teaching that husband are to love their wives as their own bodies. (Eph.5:21-33)

 

3. Mal.2:14-15, The Lord has been witness between you and the wife of your youth to whom you have been faithless, although she is your companion and your wife by covenant.)

As is true for all covenant makings, there is always the taking of vows in the presence of witnesses.  In this case, the vow of the marriage covenant is said to be witnessed by God himself.  The covenantal aspect of marriage is often used in Scripture as an analogy of God's covenant relationship with Israel and in the New Testament of Christ's relationship with the Church.  Marriage served to provide people with a living paradigm depicting the redemptive relationship between God and his bride-people. (Hosea 2:19-20, Ezekiel 16:8, Ephesians 5:21-33, Matthew 19:4-6)  Like the divine-human covenantal relationship, the marriage covenant between man and woman was intended by God to be loving, loyal and permanent.

 

C. Marriage is Recognized as Sacred by Christ

In Matthew 19:1-9, both the original design of marriage and its covenantal nature is recounted by Christ in response to the then popular laxity with respect to divorce.  Christ's point, in vs. 4 is to reiterate that what God has joined together, let no one separate.  As will be explained below, Christ's only provision for giving a certificate of divorce is when in effect an innocent party as been defacto divorced already. 

 

II. Understanding the Place of Sexual Union in Marriage:

 

Sexual union in marriage may be compared to a "sacrament" in the church.  It both initiates and consummates the realities represented by Christian union in Christ.  The language of Scripture is such that one can as readily speak of marriage in terms of this "sacrament"  of sexual union as one can also speak of church membership in terms of the communion table.  As will be noted, sexual union (as just described--both in its act and that which the act initiates and consummates) IS marriage and so too, a violation of sexual union IS divorce.  Therefore, the central element to the valid contraction of marriage is sexual union whereby the "two become one flesh." 

1 Cor. 15 Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? Should I therefore take the members of Christ and make them members of a prostitute? Never!  16 Do you not know that whoever is united to a prostitute becomes one body with her? For it is said, “The two shall be one flesh.”  17 But anyone united to the Lord becomes one spirit with him.  18 Shun fornication! 

a. This centrality of sexual union for marriage is especially illustrated by Scripture in cases where sexual union is shown to obligate marriage except in cases where the Father of the woman refuses to give his daughter to the man.  (Exodus 22:16ff, Dt.22:28-29)

b. Even betrothal (what we describe as "engaged to be married") is a pledge of cleaving so as at times a betrothed woman is called a man's wife. (Gen.29:21, Dt.22:23-24, Mt.1:18-24)  For the record, according to 1 Thess.4:4-5, Lev.19:20-22, Dt.21:10-14, betrothal should precede sexual union.

The point is that by definition of marriage, sexual union is central to the biblical definition of it-- not because marriage is only about sexual union, but because sexual union is representative of the unity that results from loving commitment and loyalty.  So the scripture clearly does not limit the purpose of marriage to sexual union, but assumes that sexual union is the inner sanctum of marriage which represents the various other covenantal purposes of marriage.  One could say that sexual union both obligates and consummate marriage.  So to, in marriage, sexual union promote loving loyalty  and is the result of loving loyalty.

Furthermore,  one could say from scripture that there really is no such thing as "premarital sex."  Sex is by definition marital (or in the case of adultery, a marital sin so as to  pervert it.).  Or to put it differently, "premarital sex" is like a pre-wedding marriage-- it even obligates the consummation of a marriage except when the woman's father prevents it by way of protection. (see above) 

 

Note: In that marriage is recognized by its sexual union, one would expect that any later allowance for the functional termination  of a marriage would be described in terms that pertain to sexual immorality, perhaps though broadly understood. (see below)

 

III. God's attitude toward Divorce

A. Malachi 2:14-16 - God hates divorce

Mal. 2:14 You ask, “Why does he not?” Because the LORD was a witness between you and the wife of your youth, to whom you have been faithless, though she is your companion and your wife by covenant.  15 Did not one God make her? Both flesh and spirit are his. And what does the one God desire? Godly offspring. So look to yourselves, and do not let anyone be faithless to the wife of his youth.  16 For I hate divorce, says the LORD, the God of Israel, and covering one's garment with violence, says the LORD of hosts. So take heed to yourselves and do not be faithless.

 

Why?

Because divorce represents a radical breaking of a covenant relationship to which He was it's primary witness. Divorce is shown to first violate God in our transgression against His honor.

 

 

Observation:  Far from just hearing in Malachi some terrifying harshness in our Lord regarding divorce, we should be glad that God hates divorce.  For Him to hate divorce  is to declare in the starkest possible way that He is on our side when marriage gets difficult-- a glorious message of hope for troubled marriages.  He stands ready to help his children avoid what He hates.

 

B.  God allows for divorce by way of a reluctant provision so as to restrain evil against innocent parties.  (Matthew 19:3-9, Mark 10:2-11)

Matt. 19:3  Some Pharisees came to him, and to test him they asked, “Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife for any cause?”  4 He answered, “Have you not read that the one who made them at the beginning ‘made them male and female,’  5 and said, ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh’?  6 So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate.”  7 They said to him, “Why then did Moses command us to give a certificate of dismissal and to divorce her?”  8 He said to them, “It was because you were so hard-hearted that Moses allowed you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so.  9 And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for fornication, and marries another commits adultery.”

 

Observations:

 

a. Divorce is NOT instituted by God, only sadly recognized as then to demand legal protections in some limited cases.

In Dt.22:19-20, 24:1-4,, Lev.21:7, 14; 22:13 and Nu.30:9, the Mosaic law takes the custom of divorce for granted, it does not institute divorce, but regulates it to limit and preclude its abuse.  In cases where divorce is regulated in the OT, it is only applied specifically to cases involving sexual immorality.  God allows divorce only as a common grace provision to restrain the evil of fornication (see explanation below) against the righteous/innocent party.  

 

b. As Christ clearly stated, it is only provisional in response to the hardness of hearts. (Mt. 19:8, Mark 10:5)

See also Matthew 5:32,   Luke 16:18, 1 Cor.7: 10-11.

 

*c. The limited condition for divorce allowed by God is for instances of "fornication" only.  

Mt. 19:9 And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for fornication...

 

Fornication is used in scripture to describe acts that violate the sexual union between a man and a woman in marriage.  ("Fornication: Grk. porneia, trans. of Heb. word anaz)

porneia defined:   Any act against sexual ("one flesh") union (and what this in tern represents regarding “covenantal cleaving” in marriage including  willful desertion whereby a spouse is either violated or defrauded of his/her exclusive conjugal rights.

Further Explanation:

1 Cor.7:2-15:    In an impressive way, Paul insists on sexual mutuality in marriage in this passage. 

a. 1 Cor.7:5-6, Do not  deprive one another except perhaps by agreement for a set time to devote yourselves  to prayer, and then come together again so that Satan may not tempt you. 

There is no "separation" recognized in Scripture apart from the legal, valid divorce.  The Greek term "separate" is used synonomously to mean "divorce."  The modern practice of "separation" is in direct violation of the scriptural provision for divorce.  The "concession" Paul gives in 1 Cor.7:5-6 is the permission given for this extremely limited kind of separation for the brief period of concentrated prayer.  Note then,  how Paul phrases it in terms of a limited refraining from sexual union. 

 

b. 1 Cor.7:15, Yet if the unbelieving partner separates, let it be so, in such a case the brother or the sister is not bound.

Paul is not adding here to the conditions of divorce specified by Christ's clarification of the Mosaic law regarding the limited conditions of fornication.  Rather,   Paul is merely demonstrating that desertion does in fact violate the "one flesh" union that is  insisted upon in 1 Cor. 7:5-6. (see above) Though often overlooked, it is crucial to see that "fornication" as a comprehensive term for sexual immorality does have for an attested meaning a specific reference to willful desertion-- an explicitly sexual sin in that it entails the defrauding of a spouse conjugal rights. 

(See for examples, Judges 19:2 [note that the second clause is in apposition to the first], Exodus 21:10-11, Hosea 2:2 and Jeremiah 2:2-32 (the later two passages specifically single out the sin of Israel's desertion as an additional ground justifying God's contemplated "divorce" of His wife-people.)

 

c. By good and necessary inference, it can be demonstrated that perhaps several other instances could likewise fall under Paul's understanding of "desertion" as a sub-category of fornication.  For instance, since life precedes marriage, the marriage is therefore conditioned upon the viability of those living within it.  Therefore, it can be argued that in such cases as spousal abuse, the innocent party has by necessity of life the need for separation.  This separation would be a "desertion" category of fornication since the effect is that sexual union is prevented due to threat of life.  Likewise, one could argue that perhaps a person who has violated the civil law so as to be put in jail, (justly) has likewise violated the "one flesh" contract in marriage so as to fulfill the conditions of fornication as in the case of desertion. (However I am less confident of this inference and the various implications.)

Summary and Basic Conclusions:

 

I.                     God Desires that Marriage be preserved and healed until such time as divorce is necessary. 

II.                   For the sake of both the sanctify of the marriage institution and the protection of the innocent, there are two grounds for divorce (some would say one ground with two expressions…)

1.       Adultery:  When one partner gives themselves emotionally AND physically (sexually) to another outside of marriage and is unwilling to repent…(see below)

2.       Desertion: When one party is “abandoned” by another such that while perhaps “legally” “married” they are no longer married “functionally” in any real sense… I.e.

                                                               i.      When one party physically leaves and is no longer committed to fulfilling their marriage vows of giving love and support… (not to be confused with when a party is physically absent due to a providentially necessary situation outside of their control…

                                                             ii.      When one party threatens the life and health of another in some real and tangible way. E.g. not merely “severe emotional stress” but a condition where the first right of a person to physical life and health is maliciously threatened by another… e.g. physical abuse, or reasonable threat to such…

                                                           iii.      When one party abandons the other both emotionally AND sexually.  E.g. Unwilling to have sexual relations with partner.

 

Advice to the “innocent” party in determining what to do when it is believed they have grounds for divorce. 

 

I.                     Read and discuss this paper (or one like it) that spells out the Biblical teachings concerning Marriage and Divorce with his/her Christian pastor.  

II.                   Ask marriage partner to do the same if a “Christian” and if not, to at least read the paper  to understand where the Christian partner is “coming from.”

III.                 If supposed “guilty” partner is willing to a) confess the sin of adultery and/or desertion as defined biblically,  b) if person is willing to get spiritual and/or medical and/or psychological help as a couple  (and perhaps do what is necessary to stabilize the situation when there is a threat involved—e.g. temporary separation, etc), then the “innocent” party should proceed to walk by faith trusting that God can do the impossible and seek all and every means of help to see the marriage restored, trusting that eventually God can heal  damaged emotions, etc.. 

IV.                If the “guilty” party is not willing to confess the sin(s) of desertion and/or adultery as defined by the Bible, then  the “innocent” party should work with his/her church to discern whether or not there are Biblical grounds for divorce and proceed with legal protection, since divorce will protect the innocent persons’ rights as a Christian and person made in the image of God. 

 

V. Scriptural advice for those who have troubled marriages:

 

1. There must first be a macro-reorientation concerning the nature of love in marriage. 

Too often, marriage has become a means of self-love and gratification rather than a means of service to God and society as a covenant institution.  We have too often raped marriage of its sacredness and Biblical intention by a misunderstanding of marital love itself.  As noted by Robert Bellah,

Marriage becomes a  context for expressive individualism, or a "life style enclave... for in believing in love, we American's believe in the self.  The love that must hold us together is rooted in the vicissitudes of our subjectivity.... Love is redefined as self-knowledge and self-realization

 

Biblical love is a commitment to another, fundamentally, and his/her best interest.  Biblical love assumes that our own needs are met by Christ.  Therefore, we are exhorted in Ephesians 5:1-2 as immediately followed by instructions regarding marital relations. 

Therefore be imitators of God, as beloved children,  2 and live in love, as Christ loved us and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God...

 

Note also that this passage indicates that acts of love is an obligation to God, and can be done regardless of emotion. In the unbiblical model, love is passive, it just happens.  In the biblical model, love is active, it comes out of our volition in submission to God.  This has been noted by Jay Adams in the following way:

 

There is a widespread notion that romantic love is necessary to form a strong, biblical marriage.  While nothing that I say must be understood to denigrate the idea of romantic love itself as pursued biblical, nevertheless, the idea that such love is ground for marriage is unbiblical... the Bible nowhere makes romantic love a ground or basis for marriage... Marriage is not based on an existing emotion, but on the promise that one will give love to his or her spouse throughout their lives together.                                                                                      (Jay Adams, p.30, Solving Marriage Problems)

 

2. Grace as prerequisite to a good (as God defines good) and happy marriage:

Ephesians 4:22 You were taught to put away your former way of life, your old self, corrupt and deluded by its lusts,  23 and to be renewed in the spirit of your minds,  24 and to clothe yourselves with the new self, created according to the likeness of God in true righteousness and holiness. 25  So then, putting away falsehood... 29 Let no evil talk come out of your mouths, but only what is useful for building up, as there is need, so that your words may give grace to those who hear.  30 And do not grieve the Holy Spirit of God, with which you were marked with a seal for the day of redemption.  31 Put away from you all bitterness and wrath and anger and wrangling and slander, together with all malice,  32 and be kind to one another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, as God in Christ has forgiven you.  

 

Observation:

Reconciliation and marital contentment is the product of two people being transformed by the power of the gospel.  That is to say that the gospel is the prerequisite to a happy marriage.  And among other attributes that flows from the gospel, one of the foremost is the attribute of forgiveness-- a kind of forgiveness that is not determined by the "forgivability" of our partner, however conceived, but in the reality of our being forgiven by Christ.  For it is true, that you will never be asked to forgive your partner, the amount of times, and with the same degree of offensiveness, that God has in Christ first forgiven you.  Therefore, happy marriages are the result of two godly people.  And godly people forgive others as they have been forgiven by Christ.

 

3. Each Partner acts as unto the Lord in marriage, not as unto their marriage partner:

Eph. 5:22  Wives, be subject to your husbands as you are to the Lord.  23 For the husband is the head of the wife just as Christ is the head of the church, the body of which he is the Savior.  24 Just as the church is subject to Christ, so also wives ought to be, in everything, to their husbands.

Eph. 5:25  Husbands, love your wives, just as Christ loved the church and gave himself up for her,  26 in order to make her holy by cleansing her with the washing of water by the word,  27 so as to present the church to himself in splendor, without a spot or wrinkle or anything of the kind--yes, so that she may be holy and without blemish.  28 In the same way, husbands should love their wives as they do their own bodies. He who loves his wife loves himself.  29 For no one ever hates his own body, but he nourishes and tenderly cares for it, just as Christ does for the church,  30 because we are members of his body.  31 “For this reason a man will leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two will become one flesh.”  32 This is a great mystery, and I am applying it to Christ and the church.  33 Each of you, however, should love his wife as himself, and a wife should respect her husband.

 

Observation:

No condition is put upon these commands that are relative to ones marriage partner.  Wives respect husbands, not because of who the husband is, but because of God's design for men and women in relation together as a reflection of his relation to the church.  Husbands love wives not because of who the wife is, but because of God's design for men and women in relation together as a reflection of his relation to the church.  Clearly, then, God knows something about the way he made men and women so as to make these commands. As noted above, good marriages are the product of godly interactions between marriage partners.  If  a partner waits for the other to be somehow worthy of said respect and love, the opposite will be the effect.  Respectful wives tend to make loving husbands, and loving husbands tend to produce respectful wives.  Each partner must look to  themselves so as to be found godly before God.  The good news is that while you may never have a perfect marriage (humanly speaking) nothing about your spouse necessarily prevents you from being a godly spouse.  Respect or love is both something you give  of your will, it is less about the way you feel and more about the way you act.  While we can't turn on emotion like a faucet, we can turn on love and respect, if we understand these terms as acts of the will under Christ's control. 

 

4. Final General Exhortation from Scripture:

Proverbs 16:25, there is a way that seems right to a man, but in the end it leads to death.

Proverbs 21:30,  there is no wisdom, no insight, no plan that can succeed against the Lord.

 

Observation:

We live in a time where the "way that seems right to a person" is that divorce and separation is the answer to marriage problems.  Perhaps it is this fundamental license that most needs to be challenged by those who follow after Christ.  We must reaffirm that our ultimate purpose is not self-fulfillment, but rather to be remade into the image of God so as to both glorify HIM and to enjoy HIM.  Perhaps God intends your marriage to accomplish this-- and perhaps this will mean that your marriage is the means through which God will discipline you as one of his children in whom He loves.  We must, if we are followers of Christ, be willing to submit to this plan of God, so as to be remade into his image by the lessons learned in our difficult marriages.  And on the way, perhaps we will also discover a plan for marriage that will succeed, at least as it makes us a more holy people-- more fit and able to glorify God and enjoy Him, both in this life and in the life to come. For it is true, that we will only ultimately know of the "success" of our lives here in this life by the value of life that awaits us with the Lord in heaven.  Lest we forget, we are all Christ's bride, being made ready for Him and the glorious union that will be in the life to come.   Might our marriages serve this end first, and only secondarily our more temporal ends.
