Does Morality Matter?

Our nation has been set in a tailspin as of late-- and over what? Foreign Affairs? Iraq?, Economics? Education? While these things are certainly in the news, the tailspin has been all about morality! So to ask, does morality matter, given the state of the nation as of late, is like asking does air matter? IT MATTERS! It bothers most people that our highest national official may be guilty of dishonesty, power exploitation and adultery. But then, should it matter? But before I say more, let me make it very clear that this is not a "partisan" piece of op-ed. Nor does it assume anything what so ever about the validity of the various allegations that have swept our nation concerning our President. Quite frankly, it would have been better for the allegations to have been contained so as to be less "gossipy." But as it were, the whole fiasko has raised questions in the minds of many as to the relation between the private and public life. For how many times in the past week have people asked the question, "does it really concern us what the President does in his private life?" Stated in general terms, "should a person's character even be an issue as related to our public leaders? The church should speak!

First, let me answer it in a way closer to home-- would it matter to you that your elders or pastor were dishonest in their private lives? More bluntly, would you trust the sermon I preach next week if you discovered that I was cheating on my wife and attempting to cover it up? Now think about this-- does my cheating make me any less capable as a Greek scholar, or any less cogent in my logic, or any less capable as a strategist, etc.? I can hear the mental agitation's already-- "well..... no... not exactly" sheepishly mumbled..."BUT," you say, "I don't think a deceptive person who is cheating on his wife would be someone I could listen to each Sunday." But why? I think it is really quite simple. Capabilities is one thing, but trustworthiness is yet another. A persons character, while perhaps having no direct impact upon his/her "skills," may in fact alter the application of those skills indirectly. The fact is, if I will be driven by personal gain and pleasure in my private life, what's to say that I won't allow personal gain and pleasure to alter my public life-- abilities aside. Leadership is a moral category-- so that to be a good leader is to see and do the good thing for those he/she serves, regardless of personal consequence.

And so, the Bible rightly tells the young leader, "take heed to yourself and to your teaching... for by so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers." (1Tim.4:16) Notice then carefully, that a person's character does in fact relate to a persons leadership-- in this case even his teaching. This is further clarified in the context where Paul explains that the beginning of heresy in the church started not with faulty exegesis, but with a faulty or "seared conscience" (4:2). In other words, our leadership, even our teaching, is somewhat related to who we are as people! (at least up to a point) A person's teaching/leadership my in fact be altered after the desire to soothe a guilty conscience as with rationalizations and perhaps unconsciously having a distorted perspective. Therefore, contrary to popular opinion, the Bible treats a person as a whole, indivisible person so that a persons soul includes a persons motives, character and actions as they are all integrated together as "one person" rather than many different persons. So find a person who has faulty character in his private life-- it is only a matter of time before this will show itself up in his public life. What a person does (privately and/or publicly) is in fact related to what a person IS! To say it more bluntly, if I found it to my personal advantage to cheat on my wife, why would I not then be lead by personal advantage in my public ministry so as to say or lead in ways less driven by conviction and more driven by private ambition? This is why, under the Old covenant church context, when Moses was instructed to find national leaders and judges for Israel, He was instructed to find men that were "wise, discerning and reputable (Deut.1:15)-- this last characteristic being the same as the New Testament language of "above reproach" as it where.

So what do we conclude from the present fiasko, regardless of the Presidents guilt or innocence. Simply this-- character should and does matter! How much character is enough for public office? This would take longer than we have here-- but suffice it to say that just as the leader of a "special grace" institution like the church ought to be exemplary in the special graces, so too a leader of the common grace institution of state ought then to be exemplary in the common graces. This then would not require a person to be a "Christian" to lead our country-- but it would require certain things like honesty, conviction, trustworthy, etc. In other words, if our nation has been built on the separation of church and state-- woe be the nation that separates the state of a person from his/her leadership in the state!
