IT’S THE END OF THE WORLD AS WE KNOW IT… AND I FEEL FINE…

And so goes the rock band "REM." Could they have ever anticipated the "Y2K" madness that has come upon us. And would they still "feel fine?" And what about you? Have you caught the Y2K madness yet and if so–are you worried or are you fine?

We have all heard about the great catastrophes expected at the turn of the millennium. Books with titles like "Time Bomb 2000" and "The Millennium Bug" or web pages like Dr. Ed Yardeni’s "Y2K Reporter" or the "Year 2000 Information Center" and any one of Gary North’s Y2K Links and Forums–they all tell of the now infamous computer bug setting off a chain of events that will shut down everything from electricity to food distribution or that will unwittingly explode bombs or send missiles flying. And if the Y2K bug doesn’t get us, more and more seem to be saying that the Y2K hysteria will! A recent documentary chronicled the increased pilgrimages of various religious groups to the "holy land" in the anticipation of the great war of Armageddon. It was noted that if the war doesn’t happen, the disappointment itself will set off a whole set of reactions that itself might stir up a war between the various factions. This has led to the need for a significant increase in policing, not to mention the various other adjustments aimed at accommodating these apocalyptic treks. Or what will happen if even 10% of American’s take their money out of the banks or stock market in fear of some computer mal-function that would prevent access to hard earned cash? And so, before even the times squire ritual had been completed, people were planning for the big event of the turn of the millennium.

Of course, apocalyptic fantasies are nothing new. But in the atomic-age of Christian culture with internet access, such visions are all the more boisterous. So then, whether forced upon us by the Y2K madness or some other "end of the world" calculations relative to the turn of the millennium, the rock ban "U2" does raise an important question–should we feel fine? Is there anything particularly significant about Y2K from a religious perspective?

First, let me say that I am of course no expert on computer viruses or the various economic, political or military ramifications that might be directly and/or indirectly related to the Y2K. Needless to say–level headed, moderate and sober-minded analysis is always the better part of wisdom. At present, I am still at a wait and see kind of posture as more information and realistic expectations begin to emerge. But as one who is more or less competent to speak on the issue of eschatology which concerns what the Bible teaches about the "last days," I think it my pastoral duty to put all this Y2K madness in perspective.

That the Bible teaches we are presently in the last days is clear. That this ought to concern us is clearly the case as in 2 Timothy 3:1,You must understand this, that in the last days distressing times will come. Not only did Paul understand that the last days would bring certain "tribulations" as noted by Christ (Mt.24), but that the last days should make it all the more pressing that the church both guard and preach the gospel with diligence and resolve. It is true, that we should live with an expectancy that "life as we know it" could end at any time. We should therefore cultivate that perspective that sinks roots into eternal things less the things of this world. We should cultivate a love for heaven and heavenly things in preparation for our eternal home, even as we pass through this age as strangers so to speak.

However, that the "last" day is in any way related to any one or more calendar event or technological development is clearly not the case. We know that it will come like a thief in the night, that people will be going on with life as usual and that until then, the kingdom of God is both here and yet not fully here, both demonstrating the reign of God in Christ and the regulated reign of Satan for a time. In short we live in a time when both the "millennium" reign of God is concurrent with the "seven year" "tribulation" — both numeric symbols depicting God’s perfect will and timing as concerning the intensity and longevity of reign and ruin with respect to these last days. We read Revelations this way since the new covenant interprets similar kinds of symbols in Daniel in a similar manner as well–less "literalistically and more symbolically of great theological themes. We therefore live with a heavenly perspective and even anticipation even as we plan NOT as if it’s THE END OF THE WORLD AS WE NO IT but as if the world will go on as we know it.

In short, the "last" days spoken of in scripture is not related to the calendar as we know it or any one or another technological or political event. Rather the "last" days is in reference to that era in succession to a whole series of eras that are defined by the activity of God in redemptive history. We could say that the Mosaic era is the "second to last era", the Abrahamic era the third to last era, etc. etc. Again, what this means is that nothing is presently happening any more than what was happening during Paul’s day that ought to make your worried. What should worry you is if you have not embraced Christ as your savior such that the present tribulations are but only the initial deposit of even worse tribulations to come in the next and final age. But if you have embraced Christ, then even the present tribulations are but the loving discipline of God preparing you for a home so glorious that even the great apocalyptic symbols given to us in Revelations are but pale replica in comparison. And then, even as now in a provisional since, we WILL BE FINE!
