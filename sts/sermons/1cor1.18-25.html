1Cor.1:18-25
Wisdom that is Foolish, and Foolishness that is Wise

Introduction:
Some say that "beauty is in the eye of the beholder,"-- is wisdom like that? According to Paul, it
is-- not because wisdom itself is relative, (nor for that matter am I saying that beauty is relative) but because of something intrinsic to the human condition. And so, we have here what is perhaps one of the most fascinating teachings in all of the bible-- yet another Christian paradox... How it is that "wisdom" can be "foolish" and "foolishness" can be "wisdom." I hope you will listen very carefully to this text-- because we learn that there is a foolishness that leads to salvation and a wisdom that leads to destruction.

Exposition
Again, I am never tired of amazement concerning the rhetorical skills of Paul-- here we have one of the best examples. Paul brilliantly connects phrases with various gramatical conjunctions so as to carefully lead the reader into the logic of his argument...
Paying careful attention to Paul's gramatical strategy, clearly vs. 18 is the point which is then forcefully argued in vs. 19-24 and restated by way of conclusion in vs. 25.

I. 18, Paul"s Primary thesis:
18) For the word concerning the cross,

  • on the one hand to those who are on the road to destruction is foolishness
  • but to those who are being saved it is the power of God!

A. the word (logos)-- can be used to signify the "wisdom" of God, , (note for instance 1 Cor.12:7-11.) Logos is also Grk. word whic trans. the Hebrew rbd --often used loosely to decribe the mind of God... Note also the "logos" became flesh"-- or the mind of God became flesh--
Here-- it is the word with respect to the cross-- I.e. the wisdom of God was revealed in the Messiah and perfected at "the cross"
Which sets us the contrast---

B. The contrast: itself is carefully worded so as to eccentuate two contrasting responses to the "mind of God as revealed in the Gospel of the Cross-
lit-- "men... de"one the one hand... but on the other hand...
(again in vs. 23 where the logic is restated, men.. de)

Significantly, the Contrast is between "those on the road to destruction" vs. "those being saved"
I.e. The issues for Paul are far from academic-- but tragically paradoxical--

1. To those on the road to destruction, the word with respect tot he cross is considered foolish(lit) Moria)
(used in verb form in vs. 20, used again in vs. 23, again in vs. 24) 4 x's used here) from which our "moron" is derived
Idea: they snear at it, they see it as non-practical, as somehow unrelated to their lives, -- this even as they are walking head on into destruction!
Same verb form used in Rom. 1:... (see below)
Rom. 1:22 Claiming to be wise, they became fools;

1Cor. 2:14 Those who are unspiritual do not receive the gifts of God's Spirit, for they are foolishness to them, and they are unable to understand them because they are spiritually discerned.

1Cor. 3:19 For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. For it is written,
2. For those who are being saved-- the wisdom of God according to the gospel is power

here-- the power to save...

Logos= creative and salvific power-- God's revelation is also God's power to accomplish his will!

II. 19-20, Paul grounds his thesis in his understanding of Isaiah 29.

A. Paul cites Isa.29:14,
I will destroy the wisdom of those who are wise and the discernment (understanding) of those who are discerning I will anul (disregard). (Is.29:14)

B. What that text means.
13 Therefore the Lord said: "Inasmuch as these people draw near with their mouths And honor Me with their lips, But have removed their hearts far from Me, And their fear toward Me is taught by the commandment of men, 14 Therefore, behold, I will again do a marvelous work Among this people, A marvelous work and a wonder; For the wisdom of their wise men shall perish, And the understanding of their prudent men shall be hidden.".

Isaiah spoke of a coming day of judgment, in which there would be a reversal of the present circumstances.-- A day when... those whose hearts are far from God and the fear of God has been subsumed under the worldly wisdom of human teaching, and even enjoy worldly acclaim for it--... when they will be humbled by destruction while those whose hearts are near to God and who fear Him, they will enjoy salvation. Moreover, not only will the worldly wise be destroyed, but...

18 In that day the deaf shall hear the words of the book, And the eyes of the blind shall see out of obscurity and out of darkness. 19 The humble also shall increase their joy in the Lord, And the poor among men shall rejoice In the Holy One of Israel..

Among those reversals is that the worldly "wise men" and the "prudent" will lose their wisdom and their understanding, and will be humbled in their destruction-- but the humble will be given wisdom...

Humble= blind=those willing to forsake their independence and follow God!

C. Paul believes Isa. 29:14 is being fulfilled in the present,
vs. 20: (then) where is the wise? Where is the wise man (scribe)? Where is the debater in this age?
--(Implied answer-the worldly wise man is being humbled by the ironic wisdom of the cross!)
And so, Paul says,
Has not God made foolish the wisdom of the world? (expect the answer "yes")
I.e. The snears will turn against those who snear!

III. 21-24, Paul discusses two realities that flow from his thesis.

A. First reality: Although the wisdom of God through preaching the gospel is not going to be acknowledged by the world, it still pleases God!
Since with respect to the wisdom of God the world did not acknowledge (understand) according to the wisdom of God, God was pleased through the wisdom according to preaching (vs.17) to save those who believe.
Further explanation:
22) Because even the Jews seek signs and Greeks seek wisdom,

B. Second Reality: Because it in fact does please God to save those who believe through preaching, Paul and his co-labors are committed to preaching the gospel recognizing the sober consequences of this decision.
(23) but we preach Christ who was crucified--
Consequences:
a. for some-- it will mean ridicule
lit: on the one hand, a scandal to Jews foolishness to the Greeks (nations)
b. for others
24) but to those who are called, it is the power of God!

Clearly this language indicates that not all are "called"-- Paul's preaching is then not to satisfy those who are not called, in fact, he knows he will be rediculed by those who are not called-- rather Paul's ambition is to please God even as he preaches for those who are called. This was in fact the motivation for the great evangelist George Whitefield-- who said to his friend Charles Wesley that his preaching is motivated by the knowledge that he preaches to the "called ones" or the "elect'-- Called ones-- both Jews and Greeks,

Note that Paul knows that preaching will not be well-received, and he knows that people want something else.

Note also that Paul recognizes that God's wisdom can accomplish what human wisdom cannot; and God's apparent weakness can accomplish what human might cannot.

Paul Conclusion: Restatement of vs. 18

25) Therefore, the foolishness of God is wiser than man and the weakness of God is stronger than man ('s strength)

Mainpoint: Do not be surprised when the very wisdom of God as revealed in the Gospel is counted as foolishness by those who are on the road to destruction! And be encouraged that the what is perhaps considered to be foolish to the world is the way to salvation.

I.e. Isaiah looked forward to a day when there would be a great reversal of fortunes; a day when the deaf would hear great things, when the blind would see marvelous things, when the humble would rejoice; when, however the wisdom of the wise would perish and the understanding of the prudent would come to nothing.

Paul believed that day began when Christ was raised from the dead; and Paul further believed that in the preaching of the gospel this great reversal was manifested. Some would find in it the greatest wisdom and the greatest power, whereas others would find in it only weakness and folly.

Application: What do we learn about wisdom
To expect that God's wisdom will be foolish to the world
Implications:

How will the world "snear" at the wisdom of God.
Where is the wisdom of God revealed but by his "logos" and ultimately at the logos of God!
Some obvious examples:

World says it's foolish to forego liesure for spiritual training on Sunday
World says it's foolish to put your character above the acqusistion of wealth
World says its foolish to forego sexual pleasure for marital fidelity
Ultimate: World says its foolish to believe in God! Nowhere better illustrated in the great NT scholar but modernist

Example: Rudolph Bultmann
one cannot use electric light and radio, call upon modern medicine in case of illness, and at the same time believe in the world of spirits and miracles of the New Testament.

Here we have our modern epitath-- blinded by progress, modern people are enlightened to spiritual regress!

Children: Story about the coolest kid who is now the greatest failure... Why? because often to be "cool" you will need to do things that are considered "cool" but are foolish-- beware!

Indeed, the 1st century Gentiles and Jews thought the cross to be foolish, 2Oth century Gentiles and Jews think it to be old fashioned and outdated

-words like political correctness, scientific plausibility, new age and new hermeneutic find the "logos" of God that is powerful unto salvation to be irrelevant.
Indeed-- what we ought most to fear about post-modernism is how the cross seemingly has become irrelevant... this is how the wisdom of God is now made to be "foolish" -- not so much in the objections to wisdom's truthfulness, but by making wisdom irrelevant!

But what is at the bottom of post-modernity, and ultimatly the wisdom of the world?

Listen to the "cliches" to know. Indeed, we are surrounded by cliches. (according to James Hunter) Cliche's emerge naturally in a context where cultural norms become socially authorized. Therefore, cliches are often invoked authoritatively, as though their meanings were self-evident by people who seem to know what they are talking about-- which is why they are dangerous-- cliche's do violence to nuance, subtlety, and qualification. Indeed they are often a signal to our culture's impatience with serioius thought.

A Post-Modern Cliche:
Arises out of Individualism-- Judge For Yourself
Bottom line: It is foolish to trust! To submit! To be humble! Authority of any kind is viewed as increasingly obsolete and perhaps even tyranical!

This was in fact the temptation in Gen. 3-- not to become an atheist, but to become "independent" of God so as to "Judge for thyself" what is the meaning of life, etc.

Be careful Christian-- you may not be exempt!
Peter Berger's The Heretical Imperative--- the social imperative that we are to live and act as heretics-- as somehow outside of an authorized authority, tradition, etc. Here-- heretic doesn't mean so much what we believe, but that I stand outside whatever institution which claims authorization...
-- the distinctive feature of our day is that there is no longer a generally acknowledged "plausibility structure"-- i.e. virtue is in choosing for ones self rather than in giving a benefit of doubt to a given tradition, and working within a conversation with that tradition... i.e. we are all expected to be "heretics" since to have a plausibility structure is considered "foolishness".

1. As an unbeliever-- you took the temptation to judge for your self between right and wrong (meaning of the temptation at the Garden of Eden)
Core-- authority-- who will direct my life. Anti-Authority is at the bottom of all worldly wisdom!
If you are not a believer this morning-- do not be surprized if the Christian life at first appears to be "foolish"-- and the utlimate worldly foolishness is to submit your will to the will of another-- even if it happens to be your God and Creator-- but remember, it is the "foolishness of God" that is wisdom unto salvation. It is the wisdom of the world that is the road to destruction. (and don't we see it in the world around us-- even as they snear at our Sunday antic-- their families are crumbling-- the "counselor" is increasingly popular--why? Could it be for the wisdom of this world-- to "judge for theyself" rather than to surrender my independence from God so as to live under his will?...

2. As a christian, it would be a terrible mistake to think that you are not impacted by the heretical imperative... for isn't it true today-- that you are expected to develop your christian mind and life independent of an authorized ministry, etc.
Even within Evangelical Christianity- we have often even institutionalized independency, a spirit of wanting to be outside of accountability with that institution which Christ himself established so as to protect us against the worldly wisdom of anti-authoritarianism...

Bellah concerning religion said it this way:

For Americans, the traditional relationship between the individual and the religious community is to some degree reversed-- 80% of Americans agreed that "an indiviual sho9uld arrive at his or her own religious beliefs independent of any churches or synagogues." From the traditional point of view, this is a strange statement-- it is precisely within the church or synagogue that one comes to one's own religious beliefs...

Now if something in you finds the idea that your personal faith is somehow LESS than personal -- that it is accountable to and in diologue with some authorized community-- then yours is the wisdom of the worldly wise in modern terms-- you have enjoined what Peter Berger has described as the cultural imperative to in fact think and act as a heretic-- as one who stands outside of authority-- even that authority that God instituted in his church, giving it sanction when he said-- and the gates of hell will not prevail against her.

But is this the wisdom of God? No
At no time in the history of redemption was there not an authorized assembly whereby God made his will known to and for his people. Whereas the prophets stood against the errors of their time, they always stood within the authority of the assembly of God throughout the ages-- prophets were in fact covenant executors-- whose mission was to maintain the teachings of their authorized traditions. So to, the reformers stood not against institutional authority, but rather what they believed to be an un-authorized authority in so far as the Roman movement had left the "catholic church" so to speak.

How then ought we to balance "freedom of conscience" on the one hand, and living within and in accountability to Christ's church on the other... Note the confesion on this point:

WCF 20.
.2 God alone is Lord of the conscience, and hath left it free from the doctrines and commandments of men, which are, in anything, contrary to his Word; or beside it, if matters of faith, or worship. So that, to believe such doctrines, or to obey such commands, out of conscience, is to betray true liberty of conscience: and the requiring of an implicit faith, and an absolute and blind obedience, is to destroy liberty of conscience, and reason also.

20.4 And because the powers which God hath ordained, and the liberty which Christ hath purchased, are not intended by God to destroy, but mutually to uphold and preserve one another, they who, upon pretense of Christian liberty, shall oppose any lawful power, or the lawful exercise of it, whether it be civil or ecclesiastical, resist the ordinance of God. And,for their publishing of such opinions, or maintaining of such practices, as are contrary to the light of nature, or to the known principles of Christianity (whether concerning faith, worship, or conversation), or to the power of godliness; or, such erroneous opinions or practices, as either in their own nature, or in the manner of publishing or maintaining them, are destructive to the external peace and order which Christ hath established in the church, they may lawfully be called to account, and proceeded against, by the censures of the church.

Conclusion:
Perhaps more dangerous than the worlds wisdom about marriage, wealth, etc, is the world orientation to life that says "question authority-- live outside and independent of tradition, etc.
How many Christian's have been robbed of Christian joys and maturity because of an independent attitude...

Do you want to be wise?
Do not be surprized if this will mean your willingness to honestly reconsider many of your attitudes and assumptions in light of scripture.
Is it "wise" according to scripture to form your faith outside of an authorized community?
Is it wise according to scripture to shun all things that offend?

Bottom line:
1. Never appeal to the world by its own standards. Challenge the world's standards as the very essence of your appeal. the first step of true conversion is submitting ourselves to God's evaluation, rather than submitting Him to ours.

2. Expect God's wisdom to appear foolish and backwards, because it will not conform to the world's standards. Is. 55:8 "For My thoughts are not your thoughts, Nor are your ways My ways,'' says the Lord. 9 "For as the heavens are higher than the earth, So are My ways higher than your ways, And My thoughts than your thoughts..

And especially-- don't be surprized that the cross of Christ--even as we are for a time expected to bear it with him-- will be foolishness to the world
For the word concerning the cross, on the one hand to those who are on the road to destruction is foolishness but to those who are being saved it is the power of God!

Who is Wise? The person who will make God their wisdom--
