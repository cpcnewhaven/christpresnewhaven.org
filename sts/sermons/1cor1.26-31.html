1 Cor. 1:26-31
Christ is the Wisdom of God

Introduction:
Listen to the things people boast in, and perhaps you will discover what people trust in for life and happiness.

What do I mean by "boast"? Not necessarily "brag" but rather what a person thinks about and talks about so as to express what they find confidence in... it is what makes a person feel secure and fulfilled when he/she lays down in bed at nights-- or better what makes a person feel insecure and uneasy when he/she lays down in bed at nights...

In this passage, we discover that many of the things people "boast" in are in fact irrelevant to God and are of little real consequence to what really gives a person assurance.
Read 1 Cor.1:26-31

Exposition:
vs. 26:Notice the for-- The passage follows the previous logic of Paul's argument-- that God's wisdom is foolishness to those on the road to destruction and the worlds wisdom is foolishness to those who are being saved. Paul now asks his readers to observe their own calling into salvation so as to demonstrate his point--and how it is that God Frustrates Human Wisdom by Divine Wisdom.

A. vs. 24: The demographic's of those called by God into Salvation
look at (consider, behold) your calling bothers,
I.e. vs. 24, called ones = the scripture often describes Christians this way-- as those God called in a special "effecacious way" into salvaton-- those in whom God calls in this special way, are those who are justified according to Rom.8:29-30.-- those who believe and put their confidence in Christ as the power and wisdom of God... who were in fact given faith so as to be saved by the wisdom of God that is revealed in Christ

-- here the request is to consider who it is that has in fact been called by God.

(Evidently, Paul knew that the constituency of the 1st century church would in fact vindicate Paul's observation that God is no respector of human standards and worldly wisdom-- but acts upon his own standards and wisdom that is shown to be true and power unto salvation.

and so... vs. 26: that not many are wise according to the flesh (human standards), not many powerful (according to human standards), not many of noble birth (according to human standards)

I.e. Relative to the people that are exalted and made popular-- not many of them are called according to God's wisdom!
Hmm!

Today-- who are the people that are written about in the papers, who are interviewed on TV
Sports heroes
Rich and Famous
Celebrities
Politicians
etc.

Paul's point is NOT to say that no one who is a "celebrity" or is "learned" in worldy terms are in fact Christians. Nor is the intention of this passage to disparage such learning or achievements... It is all together besides Paul's point here. And in so far as we are willing to subdue all things to Christ, including human achievement and learning, these are things which might well be included in Paul's later exhortation to "render all thoughts into subjection to Christ."
Rrather Paul's point is to say that that these things-- things exalted in human standards, perhaps things which make a person popular or powerful in human socieity-- are themselves IRRELEVANT to a persons eternal calling-- to the ones who have in fact discovered the wisdom of God! God is no respector of human distinctions in his eternal distinguishing between those whom are called into adoption as God's children vs. those who are not.

B. vs.27-29: The purpose of this peculiar demographic observation:
I.e. Why are "not many wise" (celebraties) according to human standards choosen by God?

1. First note: again that this IS God's choice
choosen-- stated in the first of three clauses (vs. 27), and then assumed as the verb for the following two clauses
I.e. God planned redemptive history this way for a pupose-- why?
2. vs. 27-28: God's calling "frustrates" human wisdom.
-- In order to shame those who trust in human wisdom...
shame- kataiscu/nhØ
-- dishonor, disgrace, disfigure
-- put to shame, be humiliated
-- disappoint, frustrate (of which I take to be the present usage)
I.e. Paul is saying that God orders redemption the way he does so as to frustrate human confidence in its own wisdom. To humiliate human standards that are used to replace God's standards
3. Important to put this into the broader context of Redemptive History-- starting with the Fall.

Gen 3-- the temptation was not to become an aetheist, but to be like God in wisdom
Last week-- to judge for thyself what is right and wrong-- to rise up independant of God so as to live life-- it was the temptation to follow human wisdom rather than the wisdom of God.
Redemptive history-- as seen in the flood, the towar of Babel, the dilivering over of Israel to their idols, etc, etc-- God has "frustrated" or "put to shame" all attempts to "judge for thyself what is right or wrong" independent of God's judgments! When "everyman did what is right in his own eyes" God frustrated human plans and judgments-- especially as realized in redemption!


Paul here proclaims the gospel of Jesus Christ-- fully consistent with redemptive history.
Namely, that God's redemptive plan is such that he chooses people that are not necessarily wise after human standards so as to "shame" those who are in fact trusting in human wisdom!

-- this is powerfully driven home by Paul with a rhetorical (argumentive) repetition:

- note a series of three clauses, each with two parts
a. statement concerning who God chose to have salvation by the wisdom of God
b. statement as to the purpose-- (or maybe even result, depending on how one reads the ina clauses)
lit: 27) but the foolish (mwra») with respect to the world God chose
in order to shame the wise,
and the weak with respect to the world (God chose)
in order to shame the strong
28) and the low class with respect to the world and the dispised God chose, things that are not (the non-existant),
in order to bring to nothing things that are ( do exist)
(I.E. those who are nothing much in the world are great to God, and vice versa)
-- this purpose restated and futher clarified, vs. 29:
so that noone may boast human standards in the presence of God...

vs. 31, in order that just as it is written, let the one who boast, boast in the Lord.

Jer. 9:23 Thus says the LORD: Do not let the wise boast in their wisdom, do not let the mighty boast in their might, do not let the wealthy boast in their wealth; 24 but let those who boast boast in this, that they understand and know me, that I am the LORD; I act with steadfast love, justice, and righteousness in the earth, for in these things I delight, says the LORD.


boast-- have confidence in-- familiar Pauline way of speaking about our assurance for salvation...
Note, for instance Rom.

Rom. 5:11 But more than that, we even boast in God through our Lord Jesus Christ, through whom we have now received reconciliation.
here, a paraphrase of Jer.9, but also note:
2Cor. 10:17 "Let the one who boasts, boast in the Lord."
Pss. 34:2 My soul makes its boast in the LORD; let the humble hear and be glad.
2Cor. 1:12 Indeed, this is our boast, the testimony of our conscience: we have behaved in the world with frankness and godly sincerity, not by earthly wisdom but by the grace of God--and all the more toward you.


II. The ultimate "boast" or confidence for salvation:

30) but from Him you exist in Jesus Christ, who (became for you (dative of purpose) the wisdom from God, rightousness and sanctification and redemption,

To Have Confidence in God's wisdom is ultimately to have confidence in Christ-- who is the wisdom from God
Christ himself embodies exactly what Paul has been saying-- and is in fact the ultimate point of what Paul has been seeking to proclaim

For where else, according to Paul do we better see the weakness and foolishness according to human standards but is the power of God unto Salvation-- than in the one born in a feeding trough, one having no place even to lay his head-- whose worldly fame was counted as nothing (hardly even mentioned in secular histories of that time), one who ends his mission by dying a criminals death-- than in Jesus Christ... the foolishness of man, but the wisdom of God!
That the Christ would accomplish what all the human wisdom in the world could never accomplish so as to reconcile humanity back to God-- who would teach truths that not only lead us to the forgiveness of sins, but of life restored of meaning and happiness and purpose--
Indeed, Christ is the wisdom from God-- in whom we exist as those called by God into salvation-- in whom there is life and not death-- in whom there is sanctification and reconciliation!

Christ is the Ultimate Wisdom of God-- this is the point Paul has been leading us to all along!
Paul is here following a long tradition which associated the "wisdom of God" with the person God would send to be the Messiah.
Listen for instance to a proverb that Rabbinic tradition interpreted to be about the Christ as he is personified by wisdom in Proverbs 8:22-23

Prov. 8:1 Does not wisdom call, and does not understanding raise her voice? 2 On the heights, beside the way, at the crossroads she takes her stand; 3 beside the gates in front of the town, at the entrance of the portals she cries out: 4 "To you, O people, I call, and my cry is to all that live. ... 6 Hear, for I will speak noble things, and from my lips will come what is right; 7 for my mouth will utter truth; wickedness is an abomination to my lips. 8 All the words of my mouth are righteous; there is nothing twisted or crooked in them. 9 They are all straight to one who understands and right to those who find knowledge. 10 Take my instruction instead of silver, and knowledge rather than choice gold; 11 for wisdom is better than jewels, and all that you may desire cannot compare with her. 12 I, wisdom, live with prudence, and I attain knowledge and discretion. 13 The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil. Pride and arrogance and the way of evil and perverted speech I hate. 14 I have good advice and sound wisdom; I have insight, I have strength. 15 By me kings reign, and rulers decree what is just; 16 by me rulers rule, and nobles, all who govern rightly. 17 I love those who love me, and those who seek me diligently find me. 18 Riches and honor are with me, enduring wealth and prosperity. 19 My fruit is better than gold, even fine gold, and my yield than choice silver. 20 I walk in the way of righteousness, along the paths of justice, 21 endowing with wealth those who love me, and filling their treasuries. 22 The LORD created me at the beginning of his work, the first of his acts of long ago. 23 Ages ago I was set up, at the first, before the beginning of the earth. 24 When there were no depths I was brought forth, when there were no springs abounding with water. 25 Before the mountains had been shaped, before the hills, I was brought forth-- 26 when he had not yet made earth and fields, or the world's first bits of soil. 27 When he established the heavens, I was there, when he drew a circle on the face of the deep, 28 when he made firm the skies above, when he established the fountains of the deep, 29 when he assigned to the sea its limit, so that the waters might not transgress his command, when he marked out the foundations of the earth, 30 then I was beside him, like a master worker; and I was daily his delight, rejoicing before him always, 31 rejoicing in his inhabited world and delighting in the human race.

Mainpoint: In Christ, the wisdom of God frustrates human wisdom bringing salvation to all who are called according to Christ.

Which suggests two question by inference:
1. How is Christ the Wisdom of God for us? That is, why would we " boast" or put our confidence in Christ so as to "boast in the Lord"
(J. Owens "Of Communion with the Son Jesus Christ"-- Digression II, (p. 79, Vol.2) "all solid wisdom laid up in Christ..." )

  • Christ is wisdom in his crucifiction:
    Where else is the foolishness according to human standards but is the wisdom of God more evident than in Christ's crucifiction?
    • As he is crucified, so he is the wisdom of God; that is, all that wisdom which God layeth forth for the discovery and manifestaton of himself, and for the saving of sinners, which makes foolish all the wisdom of the world-- that is all in Christ crucified; held out in him, by him, and to be obtained only from him. (Owens)

    The wisdom of God at the crucifiction accomplished the one and for atonement for sin. Christ, both man and God, endured the shame and guilt of our sin so as to satisfy divine justice-- this was the wisdom of God, but foolishness to the world.

  • Christ is wisdom in his humility not only as he satisfied God's justice, but also satisfied God's standard of rightousness so that "his righteousness" could be accredited to "our righteousness" through faith-- and this standard of rightousness is ultimately satisfied when in humility, a full surrender and submission to God is accomplished as was expected of Adam and Eve in the garden... and this is what Christ did!
    This is the ultimate in wisdom and of course the ultimate in foolishness according to the world-- that we would humbly "trust not in ourselves, but in God"
    Again, this was the point of Gen 3-- that rather than live in humble submission to God, we wanted to be "like God" so as to judge for ourselves what is right and wrong!
    Therefore, Christ became our wisdom when he satisfied our requirement of submission to God for us!
    It was Christ, who at the pinnicle of his suffering said, "not my will by thine will be done"
    It was Christ who said that he came not to do his own will but the will of his father...
    • Owens
      For he is not only said to be "the wisdom of God", but also to be "made unto us wisdom"-- He is made, not be creation, but ordination and appointment, wisdom unto us; not only by teaching us wisdom, as he is the great prophet of his church, but also because by the knowing of him we become acquainted with the wisdom of God-- which is our wisdom. The sum of what is contended for is asserted in terms stated in Col.2:3, "In him are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge."
  • Christ is our wisdom in his teachings and commands... The sum of all true wisdom and knowledge which Christ taught may be reduced to these three heads--

1) the knowledge of God-- his nature and properties
2) The knowledge of ourselves in reference to the will of God concerning us
3) Skill to walk in communion with God... as after the commands spoken by Christ and revealed throught he apostolic doctrines of holy Scripture...

There, at the final judgment, Christ will be revealed as the ultimate in wisdom-- because he is the ultimate in submitting his will to the fathers
