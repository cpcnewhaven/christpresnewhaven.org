Exposition:
What do we learn about Solomon's "wisdom" from this passage? And what does this tell us about wisdom for us today?

I. vs. 29: The Ultimate Source of Solomon's Wisdom: A Gift from God

(29) And GOD gave wisdom (hacam) to Solomon and very great insight (bina) and breadth of understanding as vast as the sand onthe beach.
and God... Wisdom is derived from God and His character
gave-- i.e. the action is attributed to God-- while Solomon is the human "wiseman", God is the ultimate wisdom from whom all true wisdom is derived!

How?

1. God's character unto wisdom is in a general way revealed through creation itself---

Job28.20, 23-- by wisdom, God # the clouds, Job 38.37-- by wisdom he founded the earth--
Pss. 19: 1 The heavens are telling the glory of God; and the firmament proclaims his handiwork. 2 Day to day pours forth speech, and night to night declares knowledge. 3 There is no speech, nor are there words; their voice is not heard; 4 yet their voice goes out through all the earth, and their words to the end of the world. 2. God's character wisdom is also made know to us in a special and applied way through his commands:

Pss. 19:7 The law of the LORD is perfect, reviving the soul; the decrees of the LORD are sure, making wise the simple; 8 the precepts of the LORD are right, rejoicing the heart; the commandment of the LORD is clear, enlightening the eyes; 9 the fear of the LORD is pure, enduring forever; the ordinances of the LORD are true and righteous altogether. 10 More to be desired are they than gold, even much fine gold; sweeter also than honey, and drippings of the honeycomb. 11 Moreover by them is your servant warned; in keeping them there is great reward.


II. God's gift of wisdom to Solomon is related to His call to Solomon as ruler over Israel: -- it is granted to respond to a genuine life circumstance or calling:

1Kings 3:7 And now, O LORD my God, you have made your servant king in place of my father David, although I am only a little child; I do not know how to go out or come in. 8 And your servant is in the midst of the people whom you have chosen, a great people, so numerous they cannot be numbered or counted. 9 Give your servant therefore an understanding mind to govern your people, able to discern between good and evil; for who can govern this your great people?"


Solomon's gift of Wisdom is put into the context of his calling-- not an abstract wish to be smart-- but like his father David, and like the Messiah in whom he ultimately foreshadows, Solomon request for wisdom was in order to fulfill God's call for him to rule Israel!

I.e. God's gift of Wisdom to Solomon was not a speculative or theoretical "knowledge," but the wisdom to accomplish God's calling in his life-- to rule well over Israel...

What exactly then did God give to Solomon?

"OT wisdom is the teaching of a personal God who is holy and just and who expects those who know him to exhibit his character in the many practical affairs of life. This perfect blend of the revealed will of a holy God with the practical human experiences of life is distinct from the theoretical and speculative-- it is always practical and related to a persons calling-- therefore, the word is used in such cases as:

skill in making garments Ex28.3
craftsmanship in metal work Ex31.3, 6
execution of battle tactics Is10.13
required of government leaders and heads of state for administration Dt.34.9, 2S.14.20 (both pagan and Israel leaders Ezk28.4-5.)


Therefore, "the essential idea of hakam represents a manner of thinking and attitude concerning life's experiences; including matters of general interest and basic morality. These concerns relate to prudence in secular affairs, skills in the arts, moral sensitivity, and experience in the ways of the Lord."

It shouldn't surprise us then that wisdom is even framed with terms that are common to everyday life!

IV. vs. 32: God's Gift of Wisdom to Solomon was put into a common literary form or structure for easy access by those who wanted it.

(32) He spoke (composed) three thousand proverbs and his songs are a thousand and five.
Again, this was not uncommon for the ancient near eastern manners.

Spoke- "dabar"-- the composition of wisdom by Solomon was first of all "spoken" or oral.. and then put into writing by the scribes of his court or family... as was common to the ancient near east...
Mesopotamia- evidence of a "school" in ancient Sumeria where scribes copied out the various literary works including proverbs as early as third mill. B.C.
14th century- Counsels of Wisdom-- moral exhortations given by an important figure to his "son" concerning guarded speech, avoiding disputes, etc. -- very familiar to Proverbs
Egypt-- Sebayit ("teaching") is the closest parallel to the material in Proverbs-- about a dozen most of them associated with the royal or government figures following a set form: "The beginning of the instruction which x made for his son y"-- Ideals are expounded very similar to Prob. 14:17, 29; 15:18; 22:24-25; 29:22.
Noteworthy is the "Instruction of Amenemopet" which is similar to Prov.22:17-24:22-- some scholars even suggest that the Proverbs passage is dependent upon the Egyptian text.
Behind these literary documents are believed to be the oral traditions...
a. Family or tribe--
b. Court school- professional bureaucratic training, more formal for the kings court of counselors
Note several "King sayings" such as Prov.25:1

2. proverbs and songs

(32) He spoke (composed) three thousand proverbs and his songs are a thousand and five.

Two literary forms that were suitable for oral transmission

1. "proverbs" or "maxims"
lvm-- saying, proverb, wisdom saying...
- a short pithy saying which assumes the status of truth
-- or synonymous with an extend parable (hence the frequent LXX translation parobole (Ezk.17:2, )
-- or can refer to an extended didactic discourse (Prov. 1:8-19)
-- or "byword" (Ps44.14, 69.11, Jer24.9, Exk14.8, etc) so as, in these cases, to represent some kind of doom that has or will come upon Israel.

Point: To make wisdom memorable and accessible through oral tradition
"in the Old Testament, the "proverb" had a clearly recognizable purpose: that of quickening an apprehension of the real as distinct from the wished for... of compelling the hearer or reader to form a judgment on himself, his situation or his conduct... this usage comes to its finest expression in the parables of Jesus."
2. "songs" Poetry-- again to make wisdom memorable and clear:
Most common literary from of Hebrew poetry was the use of

Parallelisms:
Most significantly, parallelism-- normally a two line composition in parallelism and based upon experience.

Note therefore: if one line is obscure, it is often clarified by the second line.
Synonymous- the second line repeats the idea of the first line (Prov.Ps.51:1)
Antithetical- both lines say the same thing but by the first line in contrast to the second line. (Proverbs 10-15 is characterized by this, for example 10:1)
Synthetic- first line creates a sense of expectation which is completed in the second or third line... (Job23:10)

Point:
VonRad
"perception takes place precisely in and with the poetic conception... actuality is elevated to a dimension of truth, the validity of which can be recognized by all."... the experiences of reality which confronted her could be appropriately presented only in artistic form..."
Today... only rarely does one still meet men for whom a remembered stock of proverbs is more than a rhetorical flourish, whose life and thought are so rooted in such aids to living that they serve as indispensable signposts in making decision large or small. The question is obvious enough, whether we moderns have not lost, with the disappearance of the maxim, a whole dimension of specific knowledge about the world. "

V.vs. 30, 34: God's Gift of Wisdom to Solomon was greater than the wisdom of the nations so as to make them listen.
This seems to be the climax to this passage...

(30) With the result that (or "in order that" Jussive ) the wisdom of Solomon was great, more than the wisdom of all the people of the east, more than all the wisdom of Egypt. (31) He was more wise than all people, more than Ethan the Exrahite, and Heman, Calcol and Darda, children of Mahol and his repetition(name) was to all the surrounding nations.
34) so that (jussive) they came from all the nations to listen to the wisdom of Solomon, they came from all the kings of the earth who heard of his wisdom.


Whereas the form of Solomon's wisdom would have very much been "in the world", its substance was "not of this world"---

Ultimate purpose was to so adorn the wisdom of God that all the nations would seek after it!
Here-- we know that the nations were attracted to God by the wisdom God gave to Solomon

1Kings 10:24 The whole earth sought the presence of Solomon to hear his wisdom, which God had put into his mind.


Mainpoint: The Wisdom of God to Solomon, as the revealed character of God applied to the practical callings of everyday life, is greater than all other wisdom in the world!

1. Workahalic
Workaholics think about work even when they are not on the job. They are intense, energetic, competitive, and driven. Workaholics prefer work to leisure, and fear failure, boredom, and laziness. They are incapable of setting limits to their work or of saying "no". They do not delegate will, and they demand a lot from both themselves and others. Finally, although workaholism is often discussed as though it were a disease, most workaholics are satisfied and content with their lives, though their lifestyle does produce problems for people who have to live with them.
