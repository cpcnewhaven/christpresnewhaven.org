1 Peter 3:1-6

Daughters of Sarah

Introduction:

Perhaps you have heard of the society called "The Daughters of the Revolution"--

1. They must be the direct descendents of someone who participated in the Revolutionary war.

2. They must be sympathetic to the cause represented by the war-- i.e. American Independence...

Or perhaps you have heard of the Daughters of the Union or the Daughters of the Confederacy...

Women-- this sermon is mostly for you-- especially those of you who are wives or would be wives (I don't choose the topic, God has by this passage)-- and the question this passage will raise is this-- whose daughter are you with respect to your being a wife... How would it be to be a daughter of Sarah-- perhaps the greatest women of redemptive history second only to Mary... for from Sarah's womb came Isaac and out of her influence came the nation of Israel so as to eventually include all the nations in Christ.

(No disclaimer needed-- whereas the man some of you know as Preston is certainly unqualified to speak to wives-- perhaps given his personal interest in the topic-- the preacher is qualified in so far as he sticks to the text and lets it speak as from God to women... )

Exposition:

I. How to Win an Unbelieving Husband to Christ

A.That this is Peter's primary point is clear both from the context and the first verse of this passage...

1. Context: You will remember from the context that Peter is concerned that Christians conduct themselves in their various social institutions so that "they (unbelievers) may see your good deeds and glorify God on the day of visitation" (2:10) I.e. that by our good way of life-- people will respond to the gospel upon hearing it preached to them! (By this, by their witnessing to the gospel by their lifestyles, Christians are compared to the mission of Israel as a royal priesthood in so far as the church gathers people into the priestly, kingly and prophetic presence of God...

-- You will also recall then that Peter applies this great commission-- to be witnesses to the gospel by our good conduct-- to varioius social instutitions-- he is especially concerned that Christian respect and subordinate themselves under the authorities that be.

-- Finally, Peter's interest in authority seems to be motivated by the reality that the power gatekeepers of his day tended to be apposed to the Christian faith-- So that 1st century believers where often met with the quandry-- what to do if those in authority where unbelievers-- and Peter's advice everytime has been in essence-- respect positions of authority as from God, regardless of the person who holds the position.-- 2:13-- "Live in subordination, for the Lord's sake, to every human institution (of authority)...

vs. 2: 13-177-- applies this to civil authority

vs. 2: 18-25-- applies this to the work place

3:1-6-- applies this to marriage.

So we must keep in mind Peter's concern here-- it is to equip his readers to be good witnesses-- his interest is to win the lost to Christ-- whether it be in the work place, civil government and now, the family...

2. First verse:

-- so begins with Likewise -- clearly relating his exhortation to wives to the context just summarized...

--wives be subordinate to you own huband, in order that even if some disbelieve the word, they will be gained through the lifestyle of the wife without a word.

Notice:

1. own husbands (again in vs. 5)-- Peter deliberately includes a special word in the Grk to emphasis that his instructions apply only to a wife in relation to her husband-- "lit-- her own man" (as apposed to any other man...)

2. Command "live in subordination to..." Peter assumes here the biblical teaching concerning authority in the home-- (we have already devoted a whole sermon to the husband/wife role-- that while the scriptures nowhere teach that women are necessarily the lesser in intellect, spirituality, morality, even physiologically-- and whereas a women is everybit the equal with respect to authority to a man outside of a home-- the scriptures does teach that within the home, the wife is of lesser authority than the husband so that she is to "subordinate" by way of function (role) herself to the husband in marriage-- i.e. as a way of life, live so as to respect the authority invested in the husband by God-- trusting in God's purposes as good an not evil, in his divine ordering of social relations...

2. Notice esp. Peter's application though-- he is still concerned to "win" unbelievers and in this case, the unbelieving husband.

Purpose clause-- in order that you may "win" them...

I.e. the word means to "gain" as in add them to the number who are believers...

Matt. 18:15 ¶ "Moreover if your brother sins against you, go and tell him his fault between you and him alone. If he hears you, you have gained your brother.

Phil. 3:8 Yet indeed I also count all things loss for the excellence of the knowledge of Christ Jesus my Lord, for whom I have suffered the loss of all things, and count them as rubbish, that I may gain Christ.

Key Description-- unbelief (trans. disobedient) husbands

the idea is unbelief-- those who stumble over the gospel in disbelief and whose corresponding manner of living is after the pattern of unbelief... (relation between what we believe and the way we live-- this is the deeply held committment of Peter-- so that Peter will then reverse this relationship so as to make the way we lilve the greatest apologetic to gaining believers... ) (see below on "conduct" and "evangelism" )

Implications: Wives-- this verse could be applied to two kinds of situations

1. Husbands who are in the state of disbelief and the corresponding values and behaviors that come with that

2. Husbands, while believers, who at times act out of unbelief... I.e. the Christian husband whose behavior is not based upon Christian values or belief so as to put the wife in a situation synonomous with the wife married to an unbeliever...

John 3:36 "He who believes in the Son has everlasting life; and he who does not believe the Son shall not see life, but the wrath of God abides on him.''

Acts 14:2 But the unbelieving Jews stirred up the Gentiles and poisoned their minds against the brethren.

Acts 19:9 But when some were hardened and did not believe, but spoke evil of the Way before the multitude, he departed from them and withdrew the disciples, reasoning daily in the school of Tyrannus.

1Pet. 2:8 and "A stone of stumbling And a rock of offense.'' They stumble, being disobedient to the word, to which they also were appointed.

1Pet. 3:20 who formerly were disobedient, when once the Divine longsuffering waited in the days of Noah, while the ark was being prepared, in which a few, that is, eight souls, were saved through water.

1Pet. 4:17 For the time has come for judgment to begin at the house of God; and if it begins with us first, what will be the end of those who do not obey the gospel of God?

How then does the belieiving wife "win" a husband who is either an unbeliever or even a believer acting out of unbelief?

3. without a word--

I.e. the idea of a "word"-- can refer to "teaching, exhortation, even correction... it implies the role/position of authority so as to make one person the student under the authority of the teacher...

Here then-- Peter's point is not that the women ought never to share her faith with her husband-- but rather she should do so in such manner as not to violate her divinely instituted role as wife-- she should share her faith so as to honor the role her husband has as her authority in the home,

Without a word-- here mean, "bluntly"-- ":without nagging" without posturing herself so as to become his superior, etc...

Notice Peter's alternative then-- (instead of winning them by "word" (correction, exhortation, etc), win them by TWO BASIC QUALITIES- namely

1. when they see your respect (with respect to authority)

2. When they see your and purity (with resepect to a godly lifestyle)

Interesting explanation:

1. On the one hand-- whatever you do, do not violate the authority of your husband-- respect in your manner of relating to him...

2. Yet on the other hand-- do not violate your Christian concience-- purity... I.e. submit to his authority up to the point where it clearly causes you to personal violate the clear teachings of Christ.

This is a tension that every women married to an unbeliever understand all too well. (for that matter, everyone who must subordinate themselves to unbelieving authority--whether in civil relations, work relations and here family relations... (we will return to this later in our application)

In the first century, a wife was expected to profess the religion of her husband. IF the husband adopted the Christian Faith, his spous would have to do so also. But if the wife because a Christian, here husband would consider her unfaithful to him and his pagan religion. This caused great tension in the home in the case where women professed Christian faith and the husband did not. Peter therefore counsels these women so as to respect the institution of marriage that God has ordained including the authority that is granted to the husband, and yet in such manner as to remain faithful to God. Peter seems by this passage to understand the prediccament of Christian women whose husband refuse to listen to the gospel.

II. A Description of the daughters of Sarah--How to be the daughter of Sarah

A transition is then made by Peter so as to support his teaching to women in vs. 1-2... by a reference to redemptive history... and especially Sarah.

As we read in Gen 20, Sarah is a good example

1. Because of all women, she is perhaps the most significant in redemptive history-- from her womb came the promise of salvation as through the line of Abraham, as the mother of Isaac, she is the mother of the promise to gather all nations into God-- a promise ultimately fulfilled through the work of Christ...

2. Sarah is also a good example in that she understood what it meant to subordinate herself to an act of unbelief-- when Abraham, in fear, caved into his fear by subjecting his wife to a pagan authority...

And so, Peter wants wives, everywhere, to consider themselves the daughters of Sarah-- as by their relation to their husbands...

What makes a women the "daughter of Sarah" -- the text further clarifies and hightlights the two qualities, refered to already in vs. 1-2

1. That she lives respecting the authority of her husband,

2. That she lives a pure and godly life...

3) Do not exist (imperative "to be" with strong negative... ) outwardly-- elaborately arranging the hair, wearing gold (jewelry) or fine cloths, 4) but rather (in strong contrast), let it be the inner (outwardly hidden) heart of the person with the imperishable of a gentle (yielding) and quiet spirit which is before God precious. 5) for in this way also formerly the holy women who hope in God, adorned themselves in subordination to their own husband 6) as Sarah obeyed Abraham when calling him "lord"-- who you have become a child when you do good and are not afraid with no fear.

1. With respect to purity-- Peter draws a very sharp contrast between outward adornment and inward adornment...

3) Do not exist (imperative "to be" with strong negative... ) outwardly-- elaborately arranging the hair, wearing gold (jewelry) or fine cloths,

4) but rather (in strong contrast), let it be the inner (outwardly hidden) heart of the person with the imperishable of a gentle (yielding) and quiet spirit which is before God precious

Peter objects to the excesses of make-up and dress that were common among the wealthy ladies in the church and socieity of that day.See also--

1Tim. 2:9 in like manner also, that the women adorn themselves in modest apparel, with propriety and moderation, not with braided hair or gold or pearls or costly clothing, 10 but, which is proper for women professing godliness, with good works.

That women were prone to trust and even gain their identity in their outward beauty was clearly an issue in the 1st century.

JND Kelly comments, "The elaboration in hair-styles, make-up, dress and personal jewellery in the first and second centuries is eloquently attested by the literature and art of that period."

Therefore, Peter clarifies what true godliness is and is not!

Observation:

1. It seems that one aspect of unbelief as applied to the the social relations of men and women is to REDUCE the relationship to merely that of a sexual attraction-- so then, women are reduced to mere sexual objects (and men also)...

Peter seems then to advise women not to play into this reduction! Peter, inspired by God, puts his finger on a profound irony it would seem-- in that while Peter is teaching women to subordinate themselves to their husbands (and certainly this would include Paul's teaching concerning conjugal relations between a wife and a husband)-- he elevates women so as to be more than merely an outward person or an object of sex--

As was the case in the 1st century-- so too today-- a lower view of women reduces a women to merely an outward person... women often accomodate to this reduction of women so that women trust in their outward adornment less their inner adornment... adornments that often adorn her body less her person!

As within the context of Peter's point-- perhaps he is pointing out that women, who perhaps unconciously have accomodated themselves to a reduced view of women after the pattern of unbelief-- would then trust in their sexuality as adorned outwardly rather than their spirituality as adorned inwardly...

I think then that the curious placement of these instructions is very profound--

Peter's point then is that while saying nothing about a spouses obligations to conjugal relations, nor perhaps about a person wanting to be neat and handsome-- Peter does in effect say, don't reduce yourself or put your trust in being a sexual object so as to win an unbelieving spouse to Christ...

I.e. A daughter of Sarah is that her identiy is located in her being a godly person, not a sexual attraction!

While nothing here seems to prevent a women from looking nice-- it does clearly suggest that a preoccupation with hair, jewlrey and close suggest a false idenity-- one that perhaps unconciously has caved into a reduced view of womenhood given the values of unbelief!

And lest we see this as merely an eccentric teaching of Peter, we have already seen Paul's teaching against women being preoccupied with outward appearance, and so also it was a condemnation against worldly women by the prophets...

Is. 3:13 ¶ The Lord stands up to plead, And stands to judge the people. ...the Lord says: "Because the daughters of Zion are haughty, And walk with outstretched necks And wanton eyes, Walking and mincing as they go, Making a jingling with their feet, 17 Therefore the Lord will strike with a scab The crown of the head of the daughters of Zion, And the Lord will uncover their secret parts.'' 18 In that day the Lord will take away the finery: The jingling anklets, the scarves, and the crescents; 19 The pendants, the bracelets, and the veils; 20 The headdresses, the leg ornaments, and the headbands; The perfume boxes, the charms, 21 and the rings; The nose jewels, 22 the festal apparel, and the mantles; The outer garments, the purses, 23 and the mirrors; The fine linen, the turbans, and the robes.

2. With respect to living under authority, wives "adorn themselves in subordination to their own husband--6) as Sarah obeyed Abraham when calling him "lord"-- who you have become a child when you do good and are not afraid with no fear.

Why does Peter say, "without fear"-- Like Sarah-- it is a position of social "weakness" to be in subordination to another-- so Peter acknowledges this and encourages wives, like Sarah in Gen 20, to trust in God to protect her as within her role of subordination to her husband-- in effect, not to take things into her own hands but to let God vindicate her, and perserve her after his soverign and good will.

Peter is not saying anything that every godly women understand-- that it takes more courage, not less, more spiritual and moral strength, not less, more spiritual maturity, not less-- to live in subordination to a husband, one who all to often reveals spiritual and moral weakness.

WIVES, DON'T EVER THINK THAT SUBORDINATION IS A COP OUT-- QUITE THE CONTRARY, it is a cop-out every time you coop out ouf your husbands authority-- IT TAKES LESS COURAGE AND LESS STRENGH AND REVEALS LESS SPIRITUAL MATURITY TO TAKE THINGS INTO ONES ONE HANDS AND TO DISRESPECT YOUR HUSBAND WHEN HE DOESN'T SEEM TO ACT OR THINK AS YOU THINK HE SHOULD!

TO THE NUMBER-- EVERY GODLY WOMEN, NOTED FOR THEIR GODLY CHARACTER, MATURITY AND COURAGE THROUGHOUT REDEMPTIVE AND CHURCH HISTORY ARE WOMEN WHO FOUND THAT THEIR TRUSTING IN GOD RESULTED IN THEIR BEING ABLE TO SUBORDINATE THEMSELVES TO THE HUSBAND.

PETER RIGHTLY SAYS "WITHOUT FEAR" SO AS TO ILLUSTRATE THE COURAGE AND GODLY TRUST THAT IS INVOLVED IN SUBORDINATING ONESELF TO AUTHORITY AS UNTO GOD..

Mainpoint: Wives would be the "daughers of Sarah" so as to enjoy God's approval are those who relate to their husbands as with respect for their authority and who conduct themself with godly purity.

Application:

1. Daughters of Sarah-- Wives married to unbelievers

There is the seeming tension of being "Caught in the Middle"- (I say seeming in that in so far as this passage even exists, it affirms that you are within God's plan so as to be be instructed for it... )

But all the same--

The tension you feel is clearly noted by our passage-- that tension between respecting the mariage institutioin as given the authority of husbands on the one hand, and the call to purity and lifestyle holiness as given their Christian discipleship on the other.

The delimna you face living in a home whose financial plan or time management is under the authority of a person who doesn't apply biblical values to all this.

What do do when your husband wants to take him along on Sunday morning to this or that place

What to do when you husband wants to raise your children with values that are contrary to Biblical values.

What to do when you are told to subordinate yourself to your husband, yet to live pure and godly life.

 

And then there is your lonliness--

-- perhaps your husband does attend church with you... that lonliness that you feel when as you are feeling so excited about what your hearing in a sermon and seeing that your husband is dropping off to sleep for lack of interest.

-- and if your husband doesn't attend church the loniliess of sitting alone in church while your husband is at home working in the yard

-- the lonliness you feel everytime the sermon targets couples or when you hear teaching about family worship, etc.

-- or the well meaning church friend who keeps suggesting ways for you to get your husband to church, knowing the arguments you have had so many times with your husband about church. and you think to yourself-- if they only understood...

You are both, caught in the middle and lonly--

This passage then applys to you in several ways.

1. By its very existance-- it reminds you that you are not ultimately alone-- God recognizes your predicament!

When you feel that you are somehow outside of the Christian world-- remember that God took the time to talk especially to you in his word to the church-- he considered you every bit as important as say that couple with the Christian marriage!

2. The passage also gives you some good counsel-- so as to in effect affirm your tension... I..e being caught in the middle can be viewed as a calling!

By the command of scripture itself-- it recognizes your tension such as to opt out of the tension would be in effet to opt out of your calling (repeat)

I.e. As in many other aspects of the Christian life-- sometimes "tension" is a sign of faithfulness, not faithlessness.

I.e. the tension of being in the world an dnot of the world

the tension of wanting to sin yet wanting not to sin.

etc.

The tension of wanting to be spared from suffering yet to share in the sufferings of christ...

So here, your tension is acknowledged by God-- and such is in an odd way endorsed--

Perhaps then, you should be encouraged as long as your remain in the tension...

Sure-- you could cave into your husbands patterns of unbelief so as to be with him on SUnday morning, but then, your witness would be reduced in so far as your purity has been reduced.

Notice youwould cave in either by

1. skipping church on Sunday so as to be with him

2. going to that church that doesn't preach the gospel so as to be with him.

 

Sure-- you could cave into your desire to usurp his authority by nagging him about religion-- and yet, your witness would be reduced in so far as your respect for him is reduced.

 

So don't miss the profundity of this passage-- your being caught in the middle is ordained by God, given the fact that your husband is an unbeliever...

Principle-- respect authority up to the point that it contradicts purity...

Note the "before God"--- again, do as unto the Lord-- gentle and quiet as before God-- respecting the authority invested in husband, not because of the person your husband is per se, but for the sake of God who is your ultimate authority...

Practically three tips as to how to live within the tension God has ordained for you:

1. Recognize the common grace goodness of your marriage even inspite of your husband unbelief.

2. Subordinate yourself to your husband up to the point that it causes YOU (not him) to violate your Christian conscience against the clear teachings of scripture..

Get help from your church to discern if the scripture does in fact command something of you verses something being a good thing but not commanded.

Example-- Sunday worship vs. Weds. prayer...

 

Note--- this applies also to Wives with believing husbands who are acting out of unbelief (a contradiction-- maybe, esp. if this is a pattern) yet at any given moment of a marriage, one spouse may feel that his/her believing spouse is acting out of the old self rather than the new self...

Again, at the point where your husband is acting out of unbelief, you will feel many of the same emotions of the women married to an unbeliever..

I.e. When that husband spends money like an unbeliever, or spends time like an unbeliever, or seems to act like an unbeliever--

you at that moment will feel "caught in the middle" and even lonly--

when you see other christian couples living more godly lifestyles, you will feel isolated perhaps from them

etc.

And then, the advice for you is the same...

I suspect that many believing wives married to believing husbands could learn many lessons about how to relate to their husbands from the wives married to unbelievers...

I.e. If Peter here exhorts wives to respect their unbelieving husbands, all the more ought you to be able to respect your believing husbands when they act out of unbelief... as without a word but rather in your godly conduct...

 

2. Parents of Daughers of Sarah--

-- think long and hard before you let your daughters play with jewelry-- there is a sociology of knowlege-- a kind of value and idendity transferab le from things to people-- what a child plays with does transfer values... You must understand that when a child plays, a child forms his/her identity-- it is role playing that creates values and expectations about adult life--

what is your child's vision for adulthood-- is it outward or inward... is it more or less prepared to hold to this passage concerning mature, Christian womenhood...

I know that what I am saying isn't popular-- perhaps you have fond memories of such role playing when you were a girl such that it seems innocent-- but how do you know that some of your own struggles with idendity and clearly a world that struggles with identity in outward things is not relatd to such seemingly innocent role play as a child.

(see Dobson's stuff on self-image)

where do you want your daughters identity-- what kind of person are you trying to build when you build your child-- is it a person whose identity and values relfect the innder person of the heart whos characterisitics are chaste, pure, respectful kind of beauty-- beutiful in spirit and character

Or-- a person whose identity is in the outward appearance..

Think of it this way-- will the way you raise your daughter make it easier or harder to conform to this passage..

4. All of us--

We should all be impressed with the courage and maturity that it takes to live so as to both respect Divinely instituted authority yet so as to be godly...

1. We need to pray more and work harder I not isolating those in that situation-- encourage them, pray for them, and yet-- they would all say-- include them in a one of the gang.

2. We all might see in them something of Christ-- who rather than caving into his fear-- subjected himself to every human insitution, and yet so as not to neglect his purity-- with a yeilding gentleness, christ was courageous, without a word, Christ was led as to slaughter to the cross.

Think of it, even as some among us encouraged to win their unbeleiving husbands as without a word-- so Christ, in order to save the world, submitted himself to the human authority as without a word--

Wives-- this is your example

All christians-- this is the basis of your salvation...
