B. Abraham & Sarah

1 Peter 3:5 For in this manner, in former times, the holy women who trusted in God also adorned themselves, being submissive to their own husbands, 6 as Sarah obeyed Abraham, calling him lord, whose daughters you are if you do good and are not afraid with any terror.

Since this passage raised the issue of Abraham and Sarah-- in what way was Sarah "weaker"? (Gen.20)

Upon review-- it was Sarah that submitted to her husband who out of fear, lied about Sarah being his wife, callilng her his sister-- and so as to perserve his life, gave her to King Abimelech-- And yet it was God who intervened so as to protect Sarah...

So what was Sarah's weakness?

Was it here spiritual/moral condition-- quite the contrary-- Abraham seems to have been the weaker person in Gen 20...

Was it her weaker intellect?

(well I suppose it's possible that one might question her intelligence to marry such a man-- but this is hardly the point of the story, and of course Abraham was shown to be a rightous man... )

Was it here weaker frame-- either psychologically or physcially--- No!

So what Sarah's weakness-- it was her social position as in subordination to her husband-- I.e. as regards the social relationship in her marriage-- she was under Abraham's authority-- the "weaker" position as far as social dynamics are concerned.. She was powerless as the one who was not with authority..

Peter's point--God protected the powerless, in the case, the powerless who are under the authority even of a person who acted shamefully!

C. Adam & Eve

Note-- before fall, no evidence that husband acted as within a government role of "authority" over the woman.

Prior to the fall, their was marriage, but we have no indication that "authority" was instituted so as to have a superior/inferior authority in the relationship.

This doesn't mean that authority is sinful-- rather that it is one aspect of that common grace that God instituted after the fall so as to minimize the ill effects of the fall. I.e. Notice what is the course and what was God's common grace...

Gen. 3:16 To the woman he said, "I will greatly increase your pangs in childbearing; in pain you shall bring forth children, yet your desire shall be for your husband, and he shall rule over you."

Was this a curse or a provision that God committed himself to in order to restore peace and holiness in marriage?

• Note the "yet" which puts the second clause in contrast to the first clause regarding the "curse."

• Note also the instruction under the New Covenant concerning spousal relations. (Eph.5, Col. 3, 1Peter 2) -- always viewed as a positive...

• Note finally that "rule" is a benevolent term in the Bible-- a concept that implies that the "governor" extends his rule so as to benefit and bless those under his jurisdiction.

Heb.15:17, Obey your leaders and sub-ordinate (submit) yourselves to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls and will give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with sighing-- for that would be harmful to you.

Principle: God has chosen to commit Himself to working through the co-ordinating roles of which the wife is in a "sub-ordinating" relation to the "husband ordinate." This is an aspect not of the curse, but of a provisional solution in a less that perfect world-- a solution that will be obsolete in heaven according to Christ. (Matt. 22:30) This solution pertains to the "social" aspects of a relationship and should not be falsely interpreted to imply anything about the relative value of the man or women. We know that both man and women are derived from God equally and are therefore co-equeal in worth and persons.

(We can observe the same principle working itself out in the Trinity. Surely Christ is "co-equal" with God, yet Christ sub-ordinated himself so as to be under the "government" of God the Father.)

So then 1 Tim

1Tim. 2:12 I permit no woman to teach or to have authority over a man; she is to keep silent. 13 For Adam was formed first, then Eve; 14 and Adam was not deceived, but the woman was deceived and became a transgressor. 15 Yet she will be saved through childbearing, provided they continue in faith and love and holiness, with modesty.

Paul's point here seems to be that women are to live as under the authority men in the institution of the church and family, not because of anything intrinsicly weaker in women-- but that

1. it was intrinsic to the order of creation

2. it was a provisional response of grace to the problem of sin in the world... Again, the instititution of authority was not the curse, it was a provision so as to restrain evil...

Thus--Makes sense of Jesus's teaching that in heaven, there will be no marriage instituion as we know it-- so as to have subordinating relations... one to another. I.e all human forms of government are provision so as to provisionally mediate God's rule in a fallen world waiting for the immediate rule of Christ

In heaven-- no state, all kingdom under the direct rule of Christ

In heaven-- no church-- all one kingdom under the direct rule of Christ

In heaven -- no family-- all one kingdom under the direct rule of Christ

So, in heaven, the principle of imatatio dei-- was in place- no law per se as humankind was following after god as he was imediate in thier presence.

Now-- law and human government as institution by God so as to mediate God's ruling presence in the absence of his immediate presence

Heaven-- return to the immediate presence and the absence of mediated rule and government...

So, while in heaven I believe we will know each other as in some continuity with our realtions on earth-- we will not live under our present system of human authority. -- the president will no longer be your president, though you will know him as the one who was your president...

My point-- as far as I can tell-- there is no place in redemptive history that ascribes "weakness" to women other than as applied to the social contract as between an inferior/superior authority! (not person)

So, then, as in the case of last weeks passage-- the employee was to live in subjection to his employer, not because of anything intrinsic to the person, but as to the position as instiuted by God. Trusting that God would bless him and his purpose for him as within that instition...

weaker = subordinate authority-- that's all!

Applications:

So, does the Bible teach that women are from venice and men from Mars--

1. Only if the difference is relative to

a-- reproductive/biological differences as attributed to the respective roles of mother/father

b. -- relative positions of authority so as to give the women a position of lesser authority relative to man.

This passage recognizes only that men and women are different as to their roles or "functions" as within the provision of government that god has instituted so as to mediate his protecting, ruling presence in a fallen world..

Ie.. the Bible assumes what is obvious from common grace-- that there are some physicial differences, yet seems to account from these differences in a way that is more related to our reproductive differences than anything else--

I think way too much is probably made of our difference otherwise--

Whereas in your particular marriage, you may discover that the victorian ideal plays out in your differences, there are too many instances where perhaps this would cause problems.
