Working Together, Part 2-- Younger Christian's to their Elders

Introduction:

Very common for Christians today to draw a distinction between "essentials" and "non-essentials" and then to infer from this that any fact, doctrine or teaching that belongs to the "non-essential" class must be a matter of little importance.

Essentials-- that there is a God, that all people are sinners, that God sent his eternal son who died upon the cross to make atonment for the guilty and that whoseover believe on the Lord Jesus Christ shall be saved. -- these are then the "first principles" as it where of faith, the "alphebet of the Christian system...." And no doubt, even if we were to no nothing but this Christian alphebet, we would be the more happy and blessed for it...

BUT-- if then it follows that all other truths of revelation are "unimportant" because they happen not to be of the "essential" class, if would follow that the Word of God itself, in even the large majority of its teaching would be unimportant-- since by far the greatest portion of biblical revelation is occupied with such matters as would not be considered "essential"

And so, let a person persuade him/herself that importance attaches only to what he/she pleases to call "essential" then they would practically set aside all except a very small part of the Scriptures-- and if such a thing doesn't in fact mutilate the Bible, it stigmatises much of it as trivial.

Witherow-- "Revelation is all gold for preciousness and purity, but the very touch of such a principle would transmute the most of it into dross"

Today, we consider a proposition, even exhortation, that has conveniently been classified by some as "non-essential" and therefore unimportant-- it is a proposition that if taken seriously would raise such questions as

1. Is there a "christian government" that I am to be within its jurisdiction? If so, what kind of government is the kind that scripture injoins, and what are the qualifications of its governors?

2. Is membership in a local church important?

3. What does it mean to live in subjection to elders?

These are all questions that have been stigmatized as "trivial", but are all the same as derived from this passage of scritpure, precious even as gold since it is revelation as from God himself!

These are questions that when addressed by church leaders, have often accused of being "power hungry", divisive, turf grabing... But why?-- is it because the Bible doesn't itself raise the issue such as to need clarification and study-- or is it perhaps the convenient tool which renders some portions of scritpure as "dross."

And so we consider the proposition even exhortation of 1 Peter 5:5...

The answer to both the issues raised for the elder and the issues raised for the younger christian is humility.

The humble leaders would not be given to sordid gain, they would not take advantage of the people by overpowering them, nor would they allow personal interest to keep them from the dirty and hard work of oversight...

Samuel Millers letter to congregation:

Every consideration which has been urged to show the importance and duties belonging to the office of Ruling elders ought to remind you of the important duties which you owe to them.

1. Remember that they are ecclesiastical rulers, rulers of your own choice (yes) but by no means coming to you in virture of mere human authority, but in the name and by the appointment of the great head of the Church, and are ministers for your good. Therefore, in all your views and treatment of them, then, recognize this character-- Obey them in the Lord

Examples of how you may make their job easier and more happy.

- Esteem them very highly in love for thier works sake

(love the office that they fill)

- follow them with daily prayers that God would bless them and make them a blessing. (perhaps this is more important than you think-- to live in subjection to the elders is to pray for them)

- Bear in mind the arduousness of their duties and the difficulties with which they have to contend.

(they are tempted, more than you will ever know, to not "get involved", which is what precipitatd Peters exhortation to them to not be compelled, but voluntarily watch the floock... do then what you can to be motivated to do it by themselves.

Ways-- thank them, let them know when their teaching has been useful, etc)

- Make allowance for their infirmities and be not unreasonable in your expectations of them. (they are but human asked to do what is in itself bigge than they)

- Do not expect them to know what you are unwilling to tell them.

- Be careful to assume that they are not already in process of acting on some matter

- Your elders will sometimes be called-- God gratnt that may seldom occur!- but they will sometimes be called to the painful exercise of discipline. Be not offended by them for the performance of this duty. Rather, be careful not to make the already bitterness of their official task more bitter by demonstrating a resentful temper, or by indulging in reproachful language in return for their fidelity. Surely the nature of the duty is sufficiently self-denying and distressing without rendering it more so by unfreindly treatment. Receive their private warnings and admonitions with candor and affectionate submission.

- Treat their public acts, however contrary to your wishes, with respect and reverence.

(Their is always more to dicision than are readily known)

-When your elders visit your families for the purpose of becoming acquainted with them, and of aiding the pastor in ascertaining the spiritual state of the flock, remember that it is not officious intrusion. It is nothing more than their duty. Receive them not as if you suspected them of having come as "spies" or "intruders" but with respect and cordiality. convince the by your treatment that you are glad to see them that you wish to encourage them in promoting the best interests of the Church

In short, every good Christian ought to feel himself bound in conscience and in honor as well as in duty to his Lord, to strengthen the hands and encourage the heart of the spiritual ruler who evidently seeks in the fear of God to promote the purity and edification ofthe church. Make their job easy knowing that they will give an account of their work among you to the chief shepherd himself. It is enough that this is so without always having to explaim themselves to a sceptical congregation.

Summary of congregations duties to elders: We must beware of the general scepticism that permeates our world regarding authority. there seems to be an automatic bias agaisnt authority which must be challenged in the case of the church. These are your men, selected by you to represent and lead you. WE must trust them!
