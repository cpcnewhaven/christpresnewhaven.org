Mt. 27:27-44
Christ Endured Our Shame

Introduction:
Our creed describes several distinct aspects of the cross... suffered under Pontius Pilate, was "crucified, dead and was buried... Notice the narrative of events in Matthew and how this is followed very carefully by the Apostles creed! Each has very significant theological implications as to the work of Christ for our salvation.

27:1-26- The Guilt of the cross-- events leading up to the trail
27-44: Today, The Shame of the cross- events leading up to the Cruxifiction
45-56: The Accomplishment of the Cross-- The Death of Christ
57-66:The Amen of the Cross:Burial of Christ
28:1-11 The Vindication of the Cross: Christ's Ressurection
28:12ff The Present Ministry of the Cross: Ascended Power of Christ Among Us


Exposition:
I. Two scenes, oneTheme
A. Two scenes:
1. First Scene is at the General's headquarters:

27) Then after the soldiers of the governor took Jesus into the praetorium, they gathered around him all the battalion.

The praetorium: denotes the praetor's tent or dwelling place just as the Praetor head soildier or general. Therefore, Jesus is taken into the general's headquarters... The trail had taken place outside of the headquaters in front of the crowds where a judgment seat would have been placed for Pilate, now Christ is brought in and ridiculed by the soildiers... part of the fun..

2. The second scene is located at Golgotha:

34) AS they went out, they discovered a Cyrene man named Simon and they compelled him to carry his cross. 33) And after they came to to place which is called Golgotha, which is called the place of a skull

Gogotha = Heb for "skull"
Either denotes
1. Burial Ground (although skulls would not have been laying around since this was against Jewish law to have bones exposed)
2. A small hill that had a "skull shape"

a. Some claim it to be where the present "Church of the Holy Sepulchre" is located, yet this would have been inside the wall of Jerusalem-- this seems unlikely since we are told that Golgatha was "outside the gate" while close to the city (Jn.19:20, Heb.13:12)

b. More likely such a "skull shaped hill" exists on the northern side of Jerusalem, outside of nothern wall but close to the city. It is also close to a much traveled ancient Roman road which explains the "passers by" and is a region abounding in ancient tombs. etc.. (thus "skulls" in the firt sense)
Therefore, two places or "scenes"-- all the more significant when it is noticed that Matthew clearly presents one them common to them both...

B. One Common theme presented in both Scenes-- The Shame which was brought upon Christ in association with the Cross

1. Notice that Matthew uses Three "mocking" words used for a total of five times to describe the "shame" brought upon Christ:
vs. 29, 31, 41, empaizw--ridicule, make fun of, mock, deceive, trick, make a fool of
As Christ predicted, his humiliation would consist of wearing the shame of sin...

Matt. 20:19 then they will hand him over to the Gentiles to be ridiculed and flogged and crucified; and on the third day he will be raised."


vs. 39, blasfhmew-- injure the reputation of... evil speech...

Matt. 9:3 Then some of the scribes said to themselves, "This man is blaspheming."
Matt. 26:65 Then the high priest tore his clothes and said, "He has blasphemed! Why do we still need witnesses? You have now heard his blasphemy.


vs. 44 oneidizw-- reproach, revile, heap insults upon... A word that is often used by the New Testament writers in speaking of believers duty to endure the "shame" that comes in sharing in the humiliation of Christ under the present age

Matt. 5:11 "Blessed are you when people revile you and persecute you and utter all kinds of evil against you falsely on my account.
1 Pet. 4:14 If you are reviled for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the spirit of glory, which is the Spirit of God, is resting on you.
Rom. 15:3 For Christ did not please himself; but, as it is written, "The insults of those who insult you have fallen on me."

Therfore, three words speaking of the shame that was imposed upon Christ!

2. Notice also that Matthew frequently used the grammatical "imperfect" with the words so as to communicate that the ridicule was unceasing... Indicates that Matthew wanted the reader to get a sense that the redicule and insults overshadowed this holy period... -- idicates Matthews main point.

3. Notice then the particular details of the shame:
a. At the General's Headquarters: Shame of a mock coronation ceremony

28) After they stripped him, put a scarlet robe around him 29) and they twisted thorns into a crown, they placed it upon his head and a reed into his right hand and they fell on their knees before him and ridiculed him saying, "Hail, King of the Jews! 30) and then they spit on him, they took the reed and kept striking him (Pf) on his head. 31) and after they had ridiculed him, they stripped him of the robe and put on him his cloths and lead him away in order to crucify him.


Making Sport of Christ-- using the truth against him, dressed him up as a king and then prentended to worship him, calling him in a sarcastic manner "king of the Jews"
Can you Imagine this... (Illus)

2. On the road to the Cross: the shame of being two weak even to carry his own cross.

32) AS they went out, they discovered a Cyrene man named Simon and they compelled him to carry his cross.


I.e.to carry the cross-- Christ has already endured a sleepless night, supper and farewell address, the agony in Gethsemene, series of trials, repeated beatings and ridicule such that in all likely hood he was simply too weak to carry the cross...

H. Ridderbos: Simon's taking of the cross ...probably indicates that He was at the end of His strength; for there is no evidence that the soldiers ever showed any consideration for Him. Jesus apparently was unable to walk any farther.

a. Continuous Ridicule by Passers By:

38) Then two robbers were crucified with him, one on the right and one on the left. 39) And those passing by were continuously abusing him verbally (imperfect-- irreverently, villify) while shaking their heads. 40) and saying "the one who destroys the temple and in three days would build it up, save yourself (Impv), in you are the son of God, and come down from the cross.


b. Continuous Riducule by Sanhedrin

41) Likewise the chief priest made fun of (ridiculed, vs. 29) him with the scribes and elders, saying "42) others he saved, himself he is unable to save, King of Israel is he!, let him come down now ifrom the cross and we will believe upon him. 43) He trusts upon God, let God save (impv) him now if he wants, for he said, "I am God's Son."


c. Contuous Riducule by even the theives themselves while beside him on the cross:

4. The Cross-- the Shame of Truth being turned against him:

1. They put his proper proper title over his head so as to mock him with it

2. They properly acknowledge his power but again to mock him for it.

42) others he saved, himself he is unable to save, King of Israel is he!, let him come down now ifrom the cross and we will believe upon him. 43) He trusts upon God, let God save (impv) him now if he wants, for he said, "I am God's Son." 44) In like manner the robbers who were crucified with him rediculed him.


I.e. "The members of the Sanhedrin wanted to stamp out Jesus' influence once and for all. They asked him to prove his laughable claim to be the King of Israel by coming down from the cross; for in their eyes the cross on which he was hanging was the best proof that He could not be the Messiah (see 1Cor.1:23) How could God deliver Israel through such a man... "

Exegetical Conclusion: By an abundant use of shaming verbs, the use of continuous language, the circumstances themselves and how everyone participated... Matthew clearly indicates his mainpoint... that Christ endured the shame of our transgressions!
This point is not forgotten by the writters of the epistles:

Rom. 15:3 For Christ did not please himself; but, as it is written, "The insults of those who insult you have fallen on me."
Heb. 12:2 looking to Jesus the pioneer and perfecter of our faith, who for the sake of the joy that was set before him endured the cross, disregarding its shame, and has taken his seat at the right hand of the throne of God.


"Jesus suffering on the cross thus was the culmination of all that the godly in Israel had ever suffered when their enemies say their distress and mocked them with the words "Where is your God?"

Clearly God had indicated that the Messiah must suffer great shame for the sins of the world, and Matthew wants the readers to see that shame!

Implication by Inference: What are we to make of this mocking?
1. It is hellish: One of the traits of hell is the hateful, despicable mocking of one creature by another. Hell will be a place of taunting...

Isa. 14:9 Hell beneath is stirred up to meet you when you come; it rouses the shades to greet you, all who were leaders of the earth; it raises from their thrones all who were kings of the nations. 10 All of them will speak and say to you: "You too have become as weak as we! You have become like us!" 11 Your pomp is brought down to Sheol, and the sound of your harps; maggots are the bed beneath you, and worms are your covering. 12 How you are fallen from heaven, O Day Star, son of Dawn! How you are cut down to the ground, you who laid the nations low! 13 You said in your heart, "I will ascend to heaven; I will raise my throne above the stars of God; I will sit on the mount of assembly on the heights of Zaphon; 14 I will ascend to the tops of the clouds, I will make myself like the Most High." 15 But you are brought down to Sheol, to the depths of the Pit.


Therefore, Christ endured the pain of hell itself when he endured the shame of the cross.

2. It is Paradoxical: That his shame was endured for things said that were true-- albeit truth turned against him. "Truth is often spoken in jest, but it can also be spoken in cruelty-- here it is used in cruelty" T. David Gordon
Shame directed against Christ and Christian faith is often this way-- using the truth of it's claims to mock it when they appear frustrated...

3. It is Ironic: By causes us to reflect on the shame imposed upon Christ, we ourselves are shamed! We feel the shame of our sin, for we know by Christ enduring shame, that sin itself is a shameful thing... Perhaps this the single most ironic thing about Matthews intentions!
Whenever we sin against Christ, we do a shameful thing-- the very thing which sent Christ to the cross!

Phil. 3:19 Their end is destruction; their god is the belly; and their glory is in their shame; their minds are set on earthly things.
