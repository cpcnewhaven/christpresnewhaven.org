Exposition:

I. First Question: Is There an Ordinance for Ordination: A Succession of Ministry after the Apostolic Pattern

Notice a kind of pattern-- where Paul moves from making observations about his own ministry in Ephesus to his leaving, and the subsequent charge to those he is leaving behind to succeed him... a kind of passing to the future something that is in continuity with the past...

Call it what you want-- it is historically called apostolic succession through "ordination."

Language depicting "ordination" is found in both of the key verses with a command

For instance, Notice:

1. vs. 28--and the language of appoint

(Therefore,) Pay attention (imper.) to yourself and to all the sheep in which the Holy Spirit has appointed you an overseer (episcopos) to shepherd the church of God, which he purchased (derived from "appoint" "made) by his own blood.

This language is used here and elswhere to describe (among other things) the divine placement of a person to some role or office,

For instance:

1Cor. 12:28 And God has appointed these in the church: first apostles, second prophets, third teachers, after that miracles, then gifts of healings, helps, administrations, varieties of tongues.

2Cor. 5:19 that is, that God was in Christ reconciling the world to Himself, not imputing their trespasses to them, and has appointed to us the word of reconciliation.

1Tim. 1:12 ¶ And I thank Christ Jesus our Lord who has enabled me, because He counted me faithful, putting (appointing) me into the ministry,

1Tim. 2:7 for which I was appointed a preacher and an apostle--I am speaking the truth in Christ and not lying--a teacher of the Gentiles in faith and truth.

2Tim. 1:11 to which I was appointed a preacher, an apostle, and a teacher of the Gentiles.

Here-- the "appointment" is to the office of "episcopos" sometimes trans "overseer or "pastor" whose calling is--

to shepherd the church of God, which he purchased (derived from "appoint" "made) by his own blood.

Its the same person who is also called "presbuterous" (vs. 17) from which we derived the term "elder" as those who are exemplary in faith and life

2. vs. 31-32: and the language of commit

vs. 31-32, Therefore, keep alert and remember that for three years, night and day, I didnot cease with tears warning every single one. -- And now, Icommit you over to God and to the word of his grace, to that which is able to build you up and to give you an inheritance among the saints. (those being sanctified)

This language of commit - Is used here and elswhere to describe the authorized commissioning of a man to the ministry of the word... a passing down to others the office of ministry

Acts 14:23 So when they had appointed elders in every church, and prayed with fasting, they commended them to the Lord in whom they had believed.

These "elder" might very well be "pastors" (presbuteros)

In the case of the pastoral epistles, it is clearly used to describe the authorization of those for the office of a teaching ministry.

1Tim. 1:18 ¶ This charge I commit to you, son Timothy, according to the prophecies previously made concerning you, that by them you may wage the good warfare,

2Tim. 2:2 And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also.

Observation

In each of these passages, those being "committed" to the pastoral ministry of the word were not "self-committed" but rather committed by someone who had already been committed-- which began ultimately with Paul as an apostle-- a passing down from those authorized to do that which ultimatly succeeded the apostles...

J. Edwards (Of errors connected with lay-exhorting p.417):

Teaching is spoken of in Scripture as an act of authority (1Tim.2:12). In order to a man's preaching, special authority must be committed to him, (Rom.10:15) "How shall they preach except they be sent?".. No man but a minister duly appointed to that sacred calling ought to follow teaching and exhorting as a calling, or so as to neglect that which is proper calling. Having an office of a teacher in the church of God implies two things: 1. As being invested with th authority of a teacher; and 2. As being called to the business of a teacher to make it the business of his life. Therefore, that man who is not a minister, taking either of these upon him, invades the office of a minister... It will be a very dangerous thing for lay-men, iin either of these respects to invade the office of a minister. IF this be common among us, we shall be in danger of having a stop put to the work of God...

We have then in this passage an ancient ordination whereby those called to the office of "overseer-pastor" were to succeed the apostles so as to shepherd God's flock who where purchased by the blood of Christ.-

We know that this "passing down to others" a ministry in succession to the apostles was ceremonially depicted by the "laying on of hands" so as to signfy the passing down of a gift or "calling" that was likened to that of the apostles.

1Tim. 4:14 Do not neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the eldership.

2Tim. 1:6 Therefore I remind you to stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands.

The qualifications and order of ministry were established by the apostles themselves so as to lay a foundation for the church which was being built upon the cornerstone of Christ

R.L. Dabney: "by the authority of Christ through the foundational teaching of the apostles,

"He (God) provided expressly how the preachers were to be selected and appointed. the qualifications of the men he bestows by the gifts of his providence and grace.

That this was to continue is clearly established in 2 Tim. 2:2

And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also.

Therefore, before we move on-- lets make note of the significance here-- that "ordination" is in fact Biblical-- as representing the authorized Succession of Ministry after the Apostolic Pattern--

Therefore to one of the first "pastors" ordained to succeed the apostolic pattern, Paul wrote to Timothy

2Tim. 1:13 Hold fast the pattern of sound words which you have heard from me, in faith and love which are in Christ Jesus.

Which is to say that the those who would succeed the apostles were NOT to exceed them in their teachings, but to hold fast to the pattern of sound words passed down to them by the apostles.

Clearly then, whatever else can be said about those who are ordained ("committed") to the office of pastor-- they ought then to resemble the ministry of the apostles so as to maintain continuity between the past and the present--

II. The "Succession" of Ordination-- What Paul is concerned to "succeed" him in ministry-- that we now concern ourselves from this passage.

That Paul was concerned with this question himself is clear from the outset--

Notice how Paul draws attention to his ministry so as to pass it to those who will succeed him--

vs. 19: You yourselves know as from the first day in which I was sent into Asia what manner I always existed (lived, came) among you--

And vs. 20-21) in what manner I withheld nothing of importance so as not to preach to you and teach you both publically and with respect to your houses, having solemnly declared to both Jews and Gentiles as unto God the repentance and faith unto our Lord Jesus.

Such that throughout our passage, Paul weaves various observations from his own ministry with his exortations to the pastors so as to charge those whow would succeed him to "pay attention to yourselves...

By way of summary-- let me point out some aspects of Pauls ministry that ought then to be represented in the pastoral ministry even today:

1. He considered his own life expendable for the Gospel

vs. 24-- a curious phrase in response to his expected bondage and tribulation in Jerusalem-- lit

but none of these things make the soul precious to myself so that (I) complete my race and the ministry which I received from the Lord Jesus, to solemnly declare the gospel with respect to the grace of God.

In effect, Paul is saying-- nothing is more precious to him than the gospel

Whereas it would be a false spirituality and view to see this as over against other divine callings, say as your calling as a father and husband-- it is to say that your calling to ministry is to see yourself and your own ambitions as expendable!

Mel-- as observed first in Christ and then in the apostles-- those called to the ministry are called to put off all selfish ambition

2. Paul made it his primary business to preach and teach the gospel---vs. 24

Described as

the gospel with respect to the grace of God.

as unto God the repentance and faith unto our Lord Jesus.

But what I find instructional concerns the many ways that Paul avoided various reductions of the gospel.

1. Against the tendency to reduce the gospel to merely those more popular truths considered "essential" to the gospel-- Paul here says that he did not restrain himself from preaching the whole counsel of God to his flock--

It was only then, and we to succed him in ministry, that he could obsolve himelf as "innocent from the blood" of those he ministered to-- another ancient way of saying that whatever guilt our flock may need to endure for their sin-- it would not be our guilt if we did not refrain from teaching them all things concerning Christ.

But, by implication-- the opposite is true-- we will suffer the guilt of our people if we failed them in teaching the whole counsel of God's word--

(Note-- the importance of historical theology so as to help you avoid teaching only those gospel truths currently in vogue... (the history of evangelicalism is littered with in response to the controvercies of that day, whole categories of doctrine were forgetten for the sake of polemics-- fundamentalism as case in point-- )

It is true, that some will prefer not to have the whole counsel of God spoken to them-- some will be offended, others feel rejected.(as much is promised by Paul when he speaks of those wanting to have their ears tickled)

More specifically, those gospel truths that are not in conformity to the current fad philosophy or social plausibility will be used so as to indict you for being "narrow minded" perhaps the worst modern indictment of all...

But you will want to consider the advice of Luther, when he said that to fail in preaching the gospel at that point of contention is to fail the gospel altogether.

While we ought always to be humble to reconsider our views in scripture-- we would always want to remain committed to the whole counsel of God for his people-- believing that if Christ saw fit to inscripturate a truth, it is fit to teach as "the gospel."

2. Against the tendency to reduce the gospel to merely an ethnic or political interest group-- Paul observes that his ministry was to both the Jews and Gentiles (vs.21)-- those who would have been both ethnically and politically antagonistic in his day--

What this means of course is that you must resist all impulses that would confuse the gospel with political, social or economic ideology. You will suffer for this-- the modern church is almost always given to culture wars-- but for Paul-- his was a ministry that could transcend all culture wars-- and so must yours be. Whatever personal views concering economic, social and political theory that you may endorse, you will want to take every precaution to keep these seperate from the pure gospel.

For the gospel is something like an organisim-- it can become sick (unsound) as much with the invasion of cancer as with the absence of certain vital organs-- so as to make your gospel for all nations-- you will want to rid it of all nations so to speak...

3. Against the tendency to reduce the gospel to one or another "season", Paul observes that his ministry was "both publically and house to house" (vs.21)

I take this to mean that Paul did whatever was necessary to preach the gospel--

It is always easier to preach in public--

a. you feel like you are getting more for your labors

b. it is far less tedious since real people and problems can be left alone

But the fact remains, that the gospel will be advanced to the degree that both public and intimate ministry is enjoined.

When you haul off from your warm family room into the cold of night for pastoral visitation-- remember the apostolic gospel that went "house to house.

When you get that dreaded phone call or unexpected visit-- perhaps right when you you were lost in your study working on that project-- remember the apostolic gospel that went "house to house"

When you are called in to the hospital or bedside- remember again the apostolic gospel that went house to house

It is true, that such things don't tend to grow the church or make for a new program that will be spoken of in the next denominational report-- but when you meet house to house, person to person-- you will remember what Paul here writes, that these precious people were purchased with Christ's precious blood-- they deserve, by right of their being the "flock of God" a shepherd to tend them and care for them.

John Owen, The Especial Duty of Pastors of Churches

A man is a pastor unto them whom he feeds by pastoral teaching, and to no more; and he that doth not so feed is no pastor. James W. Alexander:

To declare God's truth so as to save souls, is a business which angels might covet: acquire the habit of regarding your work in this light. Such views will be a source of legitimate excitement; they will lighten the severest burdens, and dignify the humblest labour, in the narrowest valley among the mountains. John Calvin, Ephesians

They therefore are insane who, neglecting this means, hope to be perfect in Christ, as is the case with fanatics, who pretend to secret revelations of the Spirit; and the proud who content themselves with the private reading of the Scripture, and imagine they do not need the ministry of the church.

b. Secondly, as you witness the laying on of hands, you would want to do all that is in your power to consider how you would let your pastors do the work of "shepherding the flock" through the ministry of the word

On the one hand-- do not think that you hinder him in his office to seek his counsel and help as from the word of God-- for this, as you have seen, is his calling. Never, never think that you "bother" your pastors when you seek their counsel or prayers as from the word of God.

On the other hand-- do not expect of him that which he is not called-- do all that is in your power to releive him of those duties that are not specically related to the ministry of the word-- for this would be to YOUR advantage

Finally, As you see then the hands laid upon your brother so as to set him apart for the sacred office of "pastor" you will remember that God's love was such as to not leave you as without under-shepherds-- and that your chief shepherd, Christ himself, your only Lord and Savior-- has procurred a means to mediate his teaching, counsel, grace and forgiveness even visibly to you through his church until he returns-- but he will return.
