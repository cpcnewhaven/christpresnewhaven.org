Col. 2:11-13

Christian Baptism

Many questions petaining to the Sarament of Christian Baptism.

What does it signify?

What is its function?

Who should recieve it and when?;

On a more trajic note, many see baptism only as a point of contention and controversy. Some would even advocate that it ought to be under-valued in the church.

This passage enables us to understand something of the meaning of Christian baptism withing the context of redemptive history.

How does Baptism fit the whole scheme of God's redemption?

Out of this fundamental question spins the answer to the rest!

Text:

A. Historical Context: Circumcision controversy.

-Judaizers wanted to circumcize Converted Gentiles.

-Paul asserts that there is no need since baptism is the new covenant equivalent to the Old covenant sign of circumcision.

B. Literary Context: (vs. 6-19)

Main Point: You are made complete in Christ (9), not in the philosophy and empty deceit that is according to human traditions ,etc. (8, 16)

Subsequent Command: "so live in him, not..." (vs. 6)

Totally: rooted, built up, established...(6)

Ground #1: In Christ is all fullness and wholeness. (9-10)

Why those "elemental"things (vs.8) which are taught by people merely according to human tradition and superstitions are not necessary, since all fulness is in Christ. Nothing more needed!

Ground #2: It defies your new nature in Christ to live under the old ways of sin and misery. (vs. 11-15)

New Life: signfied by the sign of circumcision under the Old administration of God's redemptive economy and by the sign of baptism under the New administration of God's redemptive economy.

You were cir. with respect to a cir .made w/out hands.(11)

in baptism, you were raised up with him...(vs. 12)

Second reason why those "elemental" things don't accord with faith in Christ, it is contrary to the very teachings signified by both the old and new covenant entrance signs, namely, entrance into that life that has died to sinful and human resources and made alive in the fulness of Christ.

C. Gramatical Analysis of vs. 11-13:

vs. 11: Main verb: "you where circumcised...w/respect to Christ

vs. 12: Clarifying verb: "having been buried in baptism..."

Adds to and further explains the idea conveyed in the main verb. (Trans: In other words, having been, not "and".)

*Logic: Cir. signified in its day the same thing that baptism signifies today.

Implicit Assumption:

Both C. and B. function in the same way under their respective administrations:

Both C. and B.signifies the same realities under their respective amnistrations.

Explanation from the History of Redemption:

Gen. 3:24, He drove out the the man, and at the east of the garden of Eden he placed the cherubim, and a flaming sword turning to guard the way to the tree of life.

Idea: Must pass through judgment in order to have spiritual and eternal life.

Sign of Covenant is born: in terms of a "berith" must pass through the "cutting of the sword" in order to have blessed life with God.

God will accept a substitute: Prefigured in Sacrifices

Note: "Swords" placed on Curtains entering into the Holy of Holies.

Once a year, Day of Atonement where priest would pass through curtain with atonement sacrifice.

Gen. 9:11, I established my covenant with you, that never again shall all flesh be cut off by the waters of a flood.

Idea: Water of flood as a "cutting off the flesh", A passing through a great judgment ordeal.

Second Sign of Covenant is born: Water depicts a "berith" or passing through judgment, the judicial authority of God's flaming sword.

1 Peter 3:20-21, who in former times did not obey, when God waited patiently in the days of Noah, during the building of the ark, in which a few that is eight persons, were saved thourgh water. And baptism which this (flood) prefigured now saves you, not as a removal of dirt from body (not literally, but that which it signifies)

2 Peter 3:5-7

1 Cor. 10:1-2, In similiar fashion, waters of Exodus refered to as prefiguring baptism.

Note: Coevenant made with appointed representative bringing benefits to all that they represent.

Flood- Noah, Exodus through Red Sea- Moses

Therefore, Two signs depicting same reality:

A passing through God's judgment.

Doesn't prejudge outcome either way.

Mt. 3:11-12, will baptize with fire and water...(condemnation or salv.)

Redemptive Significance:

I.e. Signs signify a spritual reality!

1. Circumcision: Signified that by faith in God's provisions via a vicarious substitue, they could pass through God's judgment back to the Tree of life and that blessed condition of spritual and eternal life.

I.e. Second birth, dying to sin, living unto righteousness

Deut. 30, 6ff, Moreover, the Lord your God will circumcise your heart and the heart of your descendents, so that you will love the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul in order that you may live.

Col. 2:11, "a spritual circumcision, made without hands..."

2. Baptism: Signfies same thing, that through faith in God's provision via a vicarious substitute, we can pass through judgment into life eternal, and that blessed condition of new birth.

Rom.6:4, therefore, we have been buried with him by baptism into his death so that, just as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, so we too might walk in newness of life.

Col. 2:12, "when you were buried with him in baptism, you were raised with him.

POint: New life as one who has passed through the sword and waters of judgment and have eaten of the tree of life.

Redemptive Function:

Can't confuse what is signified with how it functions!

Entrance into that community that would nurture a person to the point of experiencing what the sign signified.

Example: Fraternity badge, School emplem

Emblem or badge doesn't make you the things represented on them, simply state your entrance into that institution that is supposted to foster and nurture those qualities.

A sign of Entrance by birthright or conversion:

1. Circumcision: Given to all males indicating that persons are admitted into that context where God's redemptive covenant is administered simply by right of birth into that community.

Principle of representation:

Modern Examples: Citizenship, status in life, etc.

Gen. 17:10-11, You shall circumcise the flesh of your foreskins and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and you throughout your generations every male among you shall be circumcised when he is eight days old...

Note: Didn't seem to bother God that the sign (signifying new birth) was given to those whom have not yet experienced it.

2. Baptism: No indication that it functions differently.

a. Doesn't save but admits you into that community of Faith that is used by God to save you.

b. Doesn't signfy the experience of New birth, only the promise of it.

Note: No command in scripture that says for us not to continue giving the sign to children. But plenty of evidence that we are to continue to give the sign of entrance to children.

Acts 16:14-15, 25-34, 1 Cor. 1:16

All "household" baptisms. Each case, the text isn't nervous about that fact that only the faith of the representative parent is refered to.

1 Cor.7:14, Instrucitons of the status of children in case of divorce. Even if only one parent is a believing members of God's church, the chidren are to be considerd "saints" (term used to depict those who are visibly members of God's visible church.

Conclusion: Baptism signifes the same spritual realities and functions in the same way as circumcision in the Old Testament.

Both = Function as entrance into the visible church.

Both = Point to a spiritual reality of passing through judgment into salvation by faith.

"I baptize you" means that you enter into that covenant community where the promise of passing thorugh the waters of judgment with the saving benefits provided for you by God the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

 

Application:

1. Baptism calls the unbeliever to put their faith in Christ, who alone is able to pass through the waters of judgment on your behalf and lead you into that blessed estate of life and happiness.

It proclaims the coming of a great judgment ordeal of which all people must endure. Some will have as their representative, Christ. Others must suffer it alone!

2. Baptism reminds the baptized that they have been admitted into that covenant community that God uses to save you. I contains a promise that the Christ of the Lord's supper, the Christ that is proclaimed in the preaching of God's word, and the Christ nurtures and sustains you through the church's watchful care is the same Christ that has passed through the fiery swords of God's wrath for you.

3. Baptism calls those who have experienced the promise of baptism through faith in Christ to a life of holiness. We are to live in Christ, no longer looking to human inovations, trinkets, worldly garmets, etc, for fulfillment in life. We are to put off the old self, and put on the new self of righteousness, holiness andpeace with God.
