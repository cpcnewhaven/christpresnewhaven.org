Exodus 1:1-7

God’s Sojourners

 

Introduction:

 

Children—every time you ask “when are we going to bet there” or “how long”.. you are a “sojourner”—

“How” and “when” will we get there?—these are the questions introduced by our passage today, if implicitly, such as to introduce us to the story of Exodus as a story about God’s sojourners to a promise land.

 

Exposition:

 

The authors introduction might at first glance seem arbitrary or like meaningless housecleaning… an abbreviated genealogy starting with Jacob and a comment about their prolific numbers.   And to be sure we might then be tempted to merely give it a “glance” and read on..  But lets look again and more closely…. For indeed, these first 7 verses are backed with meaning and insight into the gospel according to Moses in Exodus!

 

I. Exodus as the Sequel to Genesis

And these are the generations of the sons of Israel, Jacob…

 

And these are the generations of..

Very significantly, this is the exact phrase that divides the book of Genesis into 10  “major” chapters/histories” as related to God’s covenant and the formation of a covenant people. 

 

Just as significant is that the last of these histories is introduced in Genesis 46:8 concerned the “sons of Israel” or “Jacob” such as is repeated here in Exodus 1:1. 

Gen. 46:8

Now these are the names of the Israelites, Jacob and his offspring, who came to Egypt. Reuben, Jacob’s firstborn…

 

So then, the 1st six words of Exod. 1:1 are in the Hebrew text an exact quotation of the first six words of Gen. 46:8 concerning the “son’s of Israel” as beginning with Jacob.

 

Moreover, the verses that follow in Exodus 2-7 are following  a pattern that again is first introduced in Gen. 46..

 

First, in vs. 1-6, the canon of Exodus opens with a listing of the essential names of the detailed genealogy of Gen. 46:8-27.

 

Second, vs. 7 the canon of Exodus picks up with the account of the settlement in Egypt with a reference to the Gen. 47:6-12 making special note of their numbers!!!

 

Yet before we go on to look more closely at these two comparative aspects we should acknowledge how all of this clearly establishes the history of Exodus in continuum with the history of Genesis…Exodus is the sequel to Genesis and the whole Genesis story!

 

The summary of the genealogy of Gen. 46 points backward to what has been promised and accomplished in order then to point forward as to what is about to be accomplished.

 

 

II. The Sequal To What Exactly: The Promise

 

Notice again the two comparative aspects regarding Exodus 1:1-7 and Gen. 46… and how Exodus is introduced as a sequel to Genesis.

 

1. Just as Gen. 46 lists the names of Jacob’s covenant progeny—so too in Exodus 1:1-6—why?  Because it points the reader to the significance of these names in so far as they represent a covenant history…

E.g. Covenantally speaking, in the words of Heb. 7:10, they were in the “loins” of Jacob even as Jacob was in the loins of Isaac who was in turn in the loins of Abraham…

 

To be sure, the names themselves reference a “family history” – those especially who in the words of Heb. 7:10, were “in the loins” of Jacob, even as Jacob was in the loins of Isaac, who was in turn in the loins of Abraham….etc.

 

E.g. The author of Exodus begins with a family history even as this represented a history of covenant makings between God and these families especially…

 

What then was the Covenant Promise?

 

What is continued?  The story of Jacob’s family, begun in the history of the patriarchs…and especially the story of how God would accomplish the covenant promises that was given to Abraham and reiterated to his sons Isaac and Jacob…E.g. naming of the 12 sons is a link with both the past and the future (the patriarch with the 12 tribes of Israel) …

Joseph:

Gen. 48:3

Think about this!  God’s covenant promise as given to Abraham is reiterated over and over again to Isaac, then Jacob, and then Joseph.  Over and over again, the covenant was twofold:  1) that HE was going to make from their progeny a great and numerous people, even eventually a people consisting of many nations!  2) That HE was going to give them special promised land of their own! 

 

This then brings us to the second reference in our passage as compared to Gen. 46.

 

2. Just as in Gen. 46, there is a reference to their location and numbers… so too in Exodus 1:7

 

In Gen. 46… we are left with the “son’s of Israel” relocating in Egypt and totaling out at 70.

26 All the persons belonging to Jacob who came into Egypt, who were his own offspring, not including the wives of his sons, were sixty-six persons in all.  27 The children of Joseph, who were born to him in Egypt, were two; all the persons of the house of Jacob who came into Egypt were seventy.

 

In Gen. 47:27, we are again told that

Israel settled in the land of Egypt, in the region of Goshen; and they gained possessions in it,

And especially concerning the numbers:

and were fruitful and multiplied exceedingly.

 

Therefore, before the death of Joseph, the “sons of Israel” were as sojourners and aliens in the land of Egypt in the region of Goshen, but the fulfillment of the covenant promises was beginning to reveal itself as Israel was beginning to multiply. 

 

From the vantage point of the Israel living in Egypt, the promises as expressed in his instructions concerning the burial procedures at his body after his death – instructions that clearly looked forward the fulfillment of the covenant promises.

 

E.g. Looking forward into redemptive history Joseph made the “sons of Israel” take an oath concerning his burial…

Gen50:24 (NKJV) And Joseph said to his brethren, "I am dying; but God will surely visit you, and bring you out of this land to the land of which He swore to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob." 25 Then Joseph took an oath from the children of Israel, saying, "God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones from here."

 

Joseph’s instructions were clearly an  expression of faith and hope that what God has promised concerning the land was to be accomplished in due time…such that he gave instructions to embalm his body at his death and to bury his body in the promise land on the day that they arrived…

 

In Exodus 1:7…the history is still in Egypt, but there has been a significant, if not supernatural proliferation of Israel…

Vs. 7:  the fertility of the sons of Israel in Egypt is dramatically underscored by the use in vs 7 of the verb “sharatz” which generally refers in the OT to the “swarming multiplication of frogs and fish or other animal life.  It is used in reference to human beings only here and in Gen. 9, albeit for two different purposes.  …

 

This intended emphasis is further made by no less than five sequential statements of it in this one verse “fertile … swarm … so many … numbers… simple filled…”

 

 

What is the point?

The genealogical reference coupled with the observation that the miraculous growth of the seventy souls into a teeming swarm is meant to direct the reader to the promise of a vast descendents to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (Gen.17:1-8 or 26:1-5 or 28:13-15….

 

Very intentionally, Exodus begins where Genesis left off – how by the end of Genesis, the promise is beginning to be fulfilled and how in Exodus 1 it is still being fulfilled…and yet, the people of God are still in Egypt as sojourners, awaiting still the full fulfillment of God’s promises to Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Joseph.

[Pause]

But how?

But when?

 

This is what our passage today would have us to ask, in order then to anticipate the story of Exodus and the eventually establishment of great nation in the promised land!

The drama as derived from Genesis is in clear view…

 

III. How then should we live in continuity with this story of Exodus in its continuity with the past?

WE will see in continuity with the past that the promise will be fulfilled by God’s presence then, no less so than today! 

 

Explain:

When we read the list of names in Exodus 1, we of course read the names of family heads…we are then perhaps tempted to conclude from this that the growth of the sons of Israel was because of the faithfulness and health of the covenant heads of these families…

 

We often have idealized images of the great Christian families of Old, even as today the “Christian family” is also idealized in Christian literature!

 

As Mel Sensenig has noted:

Sometimes, in our current religious climate, the Christian family has almost taken on mythic overtones. We see the pictures of the ideal Christian household on the front covers of Christian books, and the glossy bulletins advertising the latest Christian conference on Christian families. They are often young, athletic, beautiful people, with happy smiles on their faces, and always there are two parents and a couple of young, healthy, happy children around them.

 

E.g it was not less the temptation then, especially by the time we get to Christ and the Pharisee tradition,  as it is clearly today—to put confidence for God’s blessings in the strength of the family or the sacredness of our progeny…

 

So then, the typical evangelical might be tempted to read the list of names here given as representing the descendents of Jacob and conclude that this is the story of how great families with great lineage are blessed by God—or something like that… but look again, (as again Mel has pointed out)

 

i)                    here is Reuben, the firstborn, who had committed incest with his father’s wife, and who had subsequently lost his birthright. Here are Simeon and Levi, who had engaged in unwarranted cruelty on the men of Shechem.

ii)                  Here is Judah, who in his arrogance, presumption and lust had committed incest with his own daughter-in-law.

iii)                Here are Joseph and Benjamin, both men with whom their father had played favorites, and caused untold grief and strife in the covenant family.

iv)                Here are Dan and Naphtali, Gad and Asher, sons of a concubine.

 

E.g. These are not the pretty poster children of phony religious idealism, but rather, real-life sinners who had been redeemed out of the idolatry of the land of Babylon, simply and purely by the grace of God expressed in his covenant with them.  All of them had come forth from Jacob, all of them, as the book of Hebrews tells us, figuratively were in their father’s loins when the promise was given.  [Note Heb. 7:10]

 

Clearly, the point here is not to say, “look at this great family history – oh how God blesses good families!!!! To do this would be yet another expression of works righteousness and Phariseeism such then to read back into the pages of biblical history that God’s covenant promises are for those who are in themselves righteous!!!

 

The book of Genesis, if it was about anything at all, it was about God’s presence in order to accomplish God’s promise…

Looking then forward to redemptive history, we see gain how Joseph’s faith was not in the faithfulness of the “son’s of Israel” but in God and his presence to accomplish his covenant!

Gen 50:25(NKJV)  And Joseph said to his brethren, "I am dying; but God will surely visit you, and you shall carry up my bones from here.”  26 So Joseph died, [being[ one hundred and ten years old; and they embalmed him, and he was put in a coffin in Egypt.

Summary—whatever else God may be, God is first of all a God at hand, a God with his people, A God who rescues protects, guides, provides for, forgives and disciplines the people who call him their God and who call themselves his people.

B. New Covenant: A second and more profound fulfillment of the promise is accomplished by Christ.

 

Explain:  even as the “son’s of Israel” were sojourners in a foreign land in Egypt, eventually, by the end of the Old Covenant, we will learn how Israel, the true Israel by faith, are still as sojourners in a foreign land, even if they happened to reside in the land of Israel…

 

There is in the NC the Formation of a New Covenant people as the “Israel of God” yet no longer as related to a physical land, a temple made with hands and a temporal political regime, rather, as a kingdom not of this world. 

 

The gospel-writer Luke, who had referred to Jesus as the ruler of the house Jacob, in Luke 9:31 uses the word exodus to refer to the impending death of Christ, that through the gates of death and burial he would pass to his eternal inheritance to which also he would infallibly bring his own people. 

 

Paul then refers to the Christian journey as a people  who were once as “aliens” to God and his promises but how by faith are made into the “sons of Israel” or “ the church.”

 

Eph. 2:11

So then, remember that at one time you Gentiles by birth, called “the uncircumcision” by those who are called “the circumcision”—a physical circumcision made in the flesh by human hands—  12 remember that you were at that time without Christ, being aliens from the commonwealth of Israel, and strangers to the covenants of promise, having no hope and without God in the world.  13 But now in Christ Jesus you who once were far off have been brought near by the blood of Christ.

Eph. 2:19

So then you are no longer strangers and aliens, but you are citizens with the saints and also members of the household of God,  20 built upon the foundation of the apostles and prophets, with Christ Jesus himself as the cornerstone.

 

Notice then how the church is defined by the presence of God in her midst!

 

Eph. 2:21

In him the whole structure is joined together and grows into a holy temple in the Lord;  22 in whom you also are built together spiritually into a dwelling place for God.

So then, how should we live as we are on this incredible journey?

 

a)Don’t look back:  Some of Israel  was tempted to make Egypt their home – to remain under the bondage of Pharoah in fear of the unknown … today, to make this world in its present situation “your home” would be to make the same mistake – e.g. to plant yourself too much into this worlds offerings is to become more and more in bondage to it …… we should think twice before we buy into this or that promise – for these things are not our home – our “investment” rather ought to be in the things not of this world –in the gospel, in the true recreational work of restoring the world to God, making our planet and the people on it more and more a holy temple with a holy people for God, etc.

 

We should in short, set our minds on things above, where Christ is seated…

We should walk by faith and not by sight…

We should make no provision for the flesh, but rather walk according to the spirit..

And what will this look like—to be sure, it will be to flee from the idolatries of this world, even as you remain in faith, hope and love as to the present in anticipation to the future.

 

God will succeed ..this is the whole point of the book of Revelations… as predicated upon the works of God in the past… we can say, just as God succeeded in Exodus to bring people to the promise land by Moses as succeeded by “Joshua,” so too even as Christ is the Moses of God, (last week), so he is the “Joshua” for us who has already secured for us a home.. (John 6)

 

Think even now about the worries you have for your kids, your loved ones, your friends, your friends….think even now about the worries you have about your life in this world…and keep it all in perspective – whatever these worries are, they are all part of a greater plan that is being ordained for you…

Think about it, the history we have heard about today is in many ways no different than our own….how we are living in a time when God is fulfilling his promise…

How?

By the incarnation of Christ, both bodily in the 1st century to establish a new covenant and new community people…

By the continuing presence of Christ in our midst at mediated through word, sacrament and communal participation…

But ultimately, not until he visibly comes again will the story that began thousands of years ago come to its climatic end – even as life in the promised land will finally and eternally begin.
