Exposition:

 

I. Summary of Story

 

1. Two weeks ago— Exodus as the sequel to Genesis, and therefore God’s covenant faithfulness to Israel as according to his promise to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob…

E.g.  God’s covenant promise as given to Abraham is reiterated over and over again to Isaac, then Jacob, and then Joseph.  Over and over again, the covenant was twofold:  1) that HE was going to make from their progeny a great and numerous people, even eventually a people consisting of many nations!  2) That HE was going to give them special promised land of their own!

 

Therefore, before the death of Joseph, the “sons of Israel” were as sojourners and aliens in the land of Egypt in the region of Goshen, but the fulfillment of the covenant promises was beginning to reveal itself as Israel was beginning to multiply.

 

Chapt. 1:1-7

 

1:7 But the Israelites were fruitful and prolific; they multiplied and grew exceedingly strong, so that the land was filled with them.

 

The sons of Israel increase miraculously in accordance with the divine promise given to Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob—Gen. 12, etc. (last sermon) and yet still were awaiting their promised land.

 

2. (8-10)Israel is all the same exposed as without a Covenant head under a tyrant King who no longer “knew Joseph.” 

Ex. 1:8

Now a new king arose over Egypt, who did not know Joseph.

 

who did not know—surely not that he had never heard of Joseph, etc.   Rather, he did not “choose to know” e.g. did not respect the deal made with Joseph on behalf of the people… the relationship between Israel and Egypt was no longer operating under the terms contracted with Joseph, but on opportunitistic terms regarding the benefit of Egypt only. 

 

And so, our story picks up with a new Egyptian king who did not honor the earlier agreement with Joseph on behalf of his family.)

 

Why?

9 He said to his people, “Look, the sons of Israel  people are more numerous and more powerful than we.  10 Come, let us deal shrewdly (wisely)  with them, or they will increase and, in the event of war, join our enemies and fight against us and go up from the land.” 

 

Hypothetical situations envisioned by the king- what if …

Go up… perhaps “gain ascendancy over”

 

Why fell threatened?

 

Calvin notes, the kings’ action  is a classic example of using an alleged threat as an occasion for ones own wickedness

Moses relates that the commencement of their troubles proceeded from jealousy, and from the groundless fear of the Egyptians, because they conceived that danger might arise from this strange nation, unless they hastened to oppress it.

 

Bottom line—rather than seeing God’s blessings upon Israel as in effect a blessing on Egypt in so far as Egypt and Israel were allied through the treaty negotiated by Joseph, this Pharaoh choose instead to view Israel’s prosperity as a threat to Egypt… a clear indication that human authority and power was being set against God’s.

(Something of a biblical pattern)

Num 22:3 And Moab was exceedingly afraid of the people because they [were] many, and Moab was sick with dread because of the children of Israel.

Rather than seeing the blessing of God on His people, and desiring to participate in it as well, they turned with suspicion and distrust against what appeared to be a defenseless people, but for whom God, the God who made heaven and earth was king, and he was well able to defend them and raise up a Deliverer for them.

 

So the situation is this—Israel, even as she is blessed, is found vulnerable under a political regime threatened by God’s blessing upon Israel.  

 

Jacob, Joseph and all that generation had died, leading the offspring of Jacob without a leader to mediate between them and Egyptians.  And they are now exposed to yet a powerful king that has turned against them.

 

 From a human perspective they have been left to themselves and despite their covenant blessings as indicated by their numerical growth even in a foreign land, they were politically and humanly speaking in a weakened condition.

 

So what happens?

II. Main Theme: God’s Strength Revealed in Human Weakness

 

12 But the more they were oppressed, the more they multiplied and spread…

 

E.g. the more they were blessed of God, the more they were persecuted, the more they were blessed…etc.

 

20 So God dealt well with the midwives; and the people multiplied and became very strong.

 

What’s going on here?

1) First, at one level, the focus of our passage is on the actions of the midwives especially as against the actions of Pharaoh!

 

E.g. the text tells us that the actions of the midwives flowed from their “fear of God” rather than being intimidated by Pharaoh’s power and shrewdness.

17 But the midwives feared God; they did not do as the king of Egypt commanded them, but they let the boys live. 

 

What does this mean exactly, “the fear of God?”

 

In short, it is an expression that means “faith in God.”  

Job 4:6

            Is not your fear of God your confidence,

                        and the integrity of your ways your hope?

 

I.e. Fear of God is synonymous to “confidence” in God…

 

Psa. 36:1

            Transgression speaks to the wicked

                        deep in their hearts;

            there is no fear of God

before their eyes.

 

I.e. The lack of Fear of God results in transgression…

 

Prov. 8:13

The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil.

 

Prov. 16:6

            By loyalty and faithfulness iniquity is atoned for,

            and by the fear of the LORD one avoids evil.

 

I.e. Fear of the Lord is in synonymous parallelism with “loyalty and faithfulness…

 

Prov. 19:23

            The fear of the LORD is life indeed;

            filled with it one rests secure and suffers no harm.

 

E.g. to be sure, it is a “faith”  and “hope” that is predicated upon their conviction about God and their subsequent awe, even literal “fear” of God given who God is… but the phrase then speaks of the resulting posture, conviction and behavior as well…

To fear God is to live with confidence in God… whereas to not fear God is to live with our confidence in something else such as to “transgress”.

 

in this case, it was the “fear of God” that  led them to place their ultimate loyalty upon God rather than upon the political regime of Pharaoh.

 

And so,

In one sense, our text tells us that the shrewdness of Pharaoh was matched by the shrewdness of the midwives acting by faith…

 

18 So the king of Egypt summoned the midwives and said to them, “Why have you done this, and allowed the boys to live?”  19 The midwives said to Pharaoh, “Because the Hebrew women are not like the Egyptian women; for they are vigorous and give birth before the midwife comes to them.” 

 

2) But ultimately, the text makes it clear that it was GOD who is the primary actor-- acting as by means of the faith such as to give the people of Israel success and prosperity!

 

20 So God dealt well with the midwives; and the people multiplied and became very strong.  21 And because the midwives feared God, he gave them families

There is a “wisdom motif” as related to the telling of this story that we would do well to heed.

 

E.g. Childs:

The story is told in a style that is associated with historicized wisdom tales in the ANE.

 

1) The characters in the chapter, especially Pharoah seem to represent typical figures—Pharoah thinks to act shrewdly, but is really the wicked fool who is duped by the clever midwives…

 

2) the piety/faith of the midwives/parents is the religious ideal of the wisdom of God.. ie their refusal to obey Pharoah stems from their “fear of God”—i.e. “fear of God” is a respect for God’s power, God’s dominion, God’s authority in a way that the results in faith in God such as to act for him and with confidence in him over all other confidences!

 

3. Notice especially, God’s deliverance is via providence—e.g. nowhere does God appear to rescue the child, rather everything has a Natural cause and yet, it is clear that the writer sees the mystery of God’s providence through action of the humans involved…

 

Point: By faith, (not by sight) they believed in God… why, because their fear of God was by faith greater than their fear of their circumstances, including even the circumstance of an otherwise very powerful ruler!  

This is a classic OT story of walking by faith rather than by sight…

 

Those then who have believed in Christ, ought then to walk and act like they do…

 

Therefore, we should see in the faith of the midwives their true wisdom!
