Exodus 2:23-3:22

The God Who Delivers

 

Introduction:

How big is your God?   Is he big enough for your problem?  Is he big enough to deliver you from your greatest fear?

Is he big enough to deliver you from the guilt and shame of your sin? 

 

To be sure, today we are drawn to perhaps the most theological passage in all the Bible—if by “theological” we mean a study of God himself! 

Who is God?

Does it matter that he is with us or not?

 

We go back in time, many thousands of years, to Israel and its cruel bondage under the tyrannical super power of that day—Egypt.

 

Exposition:

 

I. Post/Prescript- “And God took notice”

 

Ex. 2:23

After a long time the king of Egypt died. The Israelites groaned under their slavery, and cried out. Out of the slavery their cry for help rose up to God.  24 God heard their groaning, and God remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.  25 God looked upon the Israelites, and God took notice of them.

 

The prayers of the Israelites in their bondage touched God's heart, and He began anew to work for them (cf. 3:7-9). This is another of the many references in Scripture that indicate that prayer affects some of God's actions. Remembering His covenant with the patriarchs God acted for the Israelites by commissioning Moses. Dr. Thomas L. Constable

 

1. A long time had passed and the Pharaoh of Egypt who had condemned Moses to death had since passed away… 

The current Pharaoh was very likely Amenhotep II who succeeded Thutmose III and ruled from 1450 to 1425 B.C. He ruled during the very zenith of Egypt’s power, prestige, and glory as a world government.

 

2. God took notice of the agony of the Israel people

the continued suffering of the people of Israel… is especially depicted by the quick succession of terms used for their agony and cries of distress (forced labor 2x’s in 23b, groan 23b, cry/call out 23b, cry for help 23b, groaning 24)

Therefore, we are told that God

a) “remembered his covenant with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (remember—two fold—growth of a nation, land…)

b) “heard their groaning-- … where very for “hear” is coupled with the verb for “know” such as to imply that God heard with compassion or “empathy”

c) God “looked upon” them and “took notice”

 

We should consider here what this says about God, even before we meet him in his theophanic presence in the burning bush! 

E.g. A God who sees! A God who cares! A God who remains faithful, even when it doesn’t seem as though He is!

This then is the context wherein we are brought to one of the greatest moments of self-revelation on the part of God—this time to his chosen deliverer Moses…

 

II. A Surprise Appearance 3:1-4

 

One gets the sense that Moses was going about his business “living happily ever after” as we say—  a husband, father and herdsman of a sizable flock in business with his father in law—Moses was living the “Medianite dream.”

 Anticipating then the most momentous event in Moses’ life—as even the history of Israel—Moses is presented not as one seeking fame or power, but happily content tending to the flock of his father in law (Durham)

 

As common in that day, Moses was evidently a significant distance from the present camp of his new people and family—probably in search of water and grazing…

 

Most importantly, we are told in retrospect that he happens to be at Horeb—or “the mountain of God” referring to the place where God would later meet with Moses and give them their precious covenant and law as under the temple administration of priest…

Horeb is another name for Sinai (v. 1). It probably indicates a range of mountains rather than a particular mountain peak. The writer called it "the mountain of God" because it was the place where God later gave the Mosaic Law to Israel. The traditional site of Mt. Sinai and the Horeb range is in the southern Sinai Peninsula. However some Scripture references cast this location into question (cf. Deut. 33:2; Gal. 4:25). These references suggest that the site may have been somewhere on the east side of the Gulf of Aqabah. (Constable)

 

It was here, in this soon to be sacred space—that God appeared to Moses as by fire, even as we would again later! 

 

2 There the angel of the LORD appeared to him in a flame of fire out of a bush; he looked, and the bush was blazing, yet it was not consumed.

 

The messenger of Yahweh is not an “angle” as separate from God, but  rather a messenger that is clearly meant to be understood as God himself as manifest in the flaming bush—

…God (elohim) called to him out of the bush,

I.e. there is in this passage a fluid interchange between symbol and representative and God himself…

Note therefore, “the addition of Elohim (vs. 4) to the messenger, the fire and Yahweh of vs. 2 simply provided four designations of the same and single reality” (Durham)

 

Thus the fire that attracted Moses attention is clearly a “theophany” – e.g. an appearing of God symbolically manifest in the supernatural event of the flaming bush that failed to consume the bush itself!

Note God advent in the OT is often identified with fire:

Ex. 19: 8 Now Mount Sinai was wrapped in smoke, because the LORD had descended upon it in fire; the smoke went up like the smoke of a kiln, while the whole mountain shook violently.

 

4-5: AS Moses moved closer to get a better look—a voice sounds “from the middle of the bush” and says “come no further” – the verb used is significant—for it is used throughout the OT to describe an approach to the Presence of God in worship, or to seek an oracle…

 

And most especially, the voice declares for the place on which you are standing is holy ground.”

 

The significance of this is huge:  God was WITH Moses

A literal “place” had been consecrated and set apart for a holy use (even as the temple would be in the history of Israel, even as this place is today by God’s presence in Spirit acting through the means of grace!!!!)

E.g. And Jesus said to them, Lo, I am with you until the end of the age”  (Mt 28)

 

 

Notice also that Later in vs.7-9: The theophany having thus been described, the call to Moses is prefaced with  a review of the plight of the sons of Israel in Egypt… the urgency of the need for deliverance is then matched by the power of God’s expression that he has “come down”—e.g. as from the place of his dwelling above the heavens to this place of his appearance to Moses  where he is about to lit “snatch” his people from the Egyptian grip of tyranny. 

 

E.g. the Power of God as from the heavens is such as to see and empathize with the suffering of his people on earth—

The cry of the Israelites has now come to me; I have also seen how the Egyptians oppress them

 

The Response of Moses makes it clear that he was now aware of being the presence of the living God!

And Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at the God.

 

Notice the sacred tension—The God of great compassion, one who sees and notices the suffering of humanity, the God who draws near to humanity out of his great mercy and grace.. is all the same the Holy God, in whose presence is of such great power, authority and purity as to evoke the proper response of fear, yes, even distance….  even at the same time that the presence of God is embraced as well!

 

God is holy other—even the same God who comes to us to save us!

 

III.  God’s identity Revealed to Moses (6-22)

Having appeared by direct presence to Moses, God now is identified: More specifically,

 

Throughout Vs. 6-22, woven within the call narrative (next week and attention of Moses as the one sent) we then see who this God is that has appeared to Moses and extended a call for him to mediate his deliverance on behalf of Israel.

 

1) A covenant keeping God as seen through the history of his promises and dealings with the fathers:

 vs. 6 as “the God of his father” (singular- e.g. his direct family of origin) as then linked with the designation of being the same God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob…

6 He said further, “I am the God of your father, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob

 

Again, in vs. 15-17

15 God also said to Moses, “Thus you shall say to the Israelites, ‘The LORD, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you’: 

            This is my name forever,

            and this my title for all generations.

 

2. God is Yaweh!

 

In Vs. 10: God now comes to the point of his theophanic presence—which is to call Moses to be his representative “deliverer”

 

Therefore, The call evokes in Moses a humble, albeit misinformed confusion… such as to ask two questions:

 

First:

 Vs. 11:  He asks the rhetorical question, "Who am I?"

 

The response of God is again, perhaps the most significant self-revelation of God in all of the OT!  And with this, God introduces to Moses a name that is the most sacred of all names, one so sacred that the Hebrew people are not even allowed to speak it fully. 

Notice carefully what God says:

12 He said, “I will be with you; and this shall be the sign for you that it is I who sent you: when you have brought the people out of Egypt, you shall worship God on this

 

Vs. 12-- In response to Moses saying ““Who am I… that I… that I… ?

God says “I am with you”… it is I”  (or “I am”)

 

Moses’s emphatic “I” is echoed by God’s emphatic “I” which is immediately reinforced by the statement “that I have sent you forth” … with the most important word in the sequense being “I am” and the introduction of the so called “tetragrammaton” (four consonant name for God—Yahweh!) 

 

Therefore, this section established first of all the certainty of the presence of God in the fire of theophany such then to establish that the whole deliverance of Israel will be accomplished by God’s presence as well. 

Divine presence is everything here—as it will be throughout Israel’s journey through the wilderness to the promised land, as it will be in the tapernacle of meeting, as it will be in the temple and the holy of holies in their midst!

 

Having then resolved that Moses is of no consequence to God’s plans… Moses is then struck with another question:

Ex. 3:13

But Moses said to God, “Suppoes I come to the Israelites and  I say to them, ‘The God of your ancestors has sent me to you,’ and they ask me, ‘What is his name?’ what shall I say to them?”

 

IF Moses first question is “Who am I?… (vs. 11) and  God then responded with the more important information as to the fact that he intended to be present with Moses (vs. 12)…

 

Vs. 13-- Moses second question is —“Who are you that I should tell the people of Israel?

 

Durham: “Moses says in effect, “If I address the sons of Israel in your name, since my lack of status is well known to them, they will understandably want to know about you?  

 

 “What is his name?”

(The question is not so much for a proper name as such but more concerned with the reputation/ history/ qualifications… of the one who has sent Moses…

E.g. what Moses is asking then has to do with whether Gold can accomplish what he is promising.. indeed the plight of the Israelites in Egypt is entirely unparallel in the history of the fathers even… Egypt was a great world power—how then could Israel trust in an unseen God to counter what is seen!

 

What follows is perhaps one of the most theological texts in all of the Bible as then concern in a statement defining God for all future generations! 

 

Vs. 14—the answer Moses receives from God is therefore not a name per se… it is an assertion of authority , a confession of an essential reality and thus an entirely appropriate reponse to the question Moses poses…

 

14 God said to Moses, “I AM WHO I AM.” He said further, “Thus you shall say to the Israelites, ‘I AM has sent me to you.’” 

 

At the heart of the “name” that is given by God for himself is a series of plays on the word “to be” (hiyah”) such as to be coupled with the Hebrew first person pronoun “I” “aleph”   E.g. I am the one who is” or “Yahweh” as then repeated throughout the OT… 

 

Literally “ I am that I am “ replied God… the verbs are first person “to be”  in the imperfect such as to connote continuing unfinished action… e.g. I am being that I am being… or “I am the Ising one” or  “I am who was, who is, who will be…”  (Durham)

 

"The repetition of the same word [I am] suggests the idea of uninterrupted continuance and boundless duration." Keil and Delitzsch, 1:442-43.

 

 “Therefore,  upon the foundation laid by this declaration “I am” repeated four times in succession (vs. 12, vs 13 3x’s) the special name of God “Yahweh” is revealed…

 

E.g. the effect of this section is to make it absolutely clear that the salvation of Israel is not so much related to who Moses is as who God is!  Again, Who Moses is is not the issue—rather who is with Moses is the issue!

 

Therefore, this section established first of all the certainty of the presence of God in the fire of theophany such then to establish that the whole deliverance of Israel as through the call to Moses is God’s doing! 

I.e. God’s Power is Alone sufficient!

 

Yet the name as derived from “I am”  means more than this.

 

"To the Hebrew 'to be' does not just mean to exist as all other beings and things do as well—but to be active, to express oneself in active being, 'The God who acts.' 'I am what in creative activity and everywhere I turn out to be,' or 'I am (the God) that really acts.'"

Sigmund Mowinckel, "The Name of the God of Moses," Hebrew Union College Annual 32 (1961):127.   Quoted from Constable

 

 

"I am that I am" means "God will reveal Himself in His

actions through history."

 Charles Gianotti, "The Meaning of the Divine Name YHWH," Bibliotheca Sacra 142:565 (January-March 1985):45.  Quoted from Constable…

 

Notice then the significance of this name in light of what else God tells Moses concerning his power in the past and in the future…
