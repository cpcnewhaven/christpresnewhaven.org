Exodus 4:1-17

The Moses Type

 

 

Introduction:

 

"As long as a man holds that he is easily able to do some great deed of heroism and faith, he is probably incompetent for it, but when he protests his inability, and puts away the earliest proposals, … he gives the first unmistakable sign that he has been rightly designated." Meyer, p. 45.

 

This may well be true as applied to personal issues of calling—where self-confidence coupled with presumption is a sure indication that he/she is not called to the task, at least not yet..

 

But our text today shows this in context of a greater pattern, whereby in R/H, those called are often times those who are least, even while those greatest are those who are not called.

 

Exposition:

 

I.  Brief Review of God’s Call of Moses:

Note:

3:1-22, Theophany—where Call and Self-Disclosure  are interwoven such that last week we focused on the revelation of God as related especially to his compassion, faithfulness and authority

 

Today, in 4:1-17, the text addresses the question of Moses’ authority and how that authority is to be made credible to the Israel people.

E.g.

To the question “who am I” (thus confessing his own inadequacy for the task—God responded with a promise to be with him! (3:12)

Therefore, this section established first of all the certainty of the presence of God in the fire of theophany such then to establish that the whole deliverance of Israel will be accomplished by God’s presence as well.

To the question “what is his name” (or “who do I tell them you are exactly”)  God explained giving them the special and sacred name “Yahweh” such as to emphasis his omnipresence and authority as testified by ages past…  and especially calling to mind his previous promises and  acts of deliverance to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob…

 

Therefore, God commanded Moses to get on with his task (16-17) assuring him that the leaders of Israel would listen, and that even though Pharoah would resist, God would eventually give Israel such a resounding victory and deliverance that they would even be enabled to “plunder” Egypt. 

 

It is then this assurance as granted in vs. 18 especially that the Israel people would in fact listen to Moses that gives rise  to the next series of interactions between God and Moses in vs. 4:1-17. 

 

Thus, Moses responded to God by expressing again his doubt  that Israel would listen to him, which then results in God’s giving Moses three authorizing signs.. or “three witnesses” such that Moses can be assured of Success.

 

A. Yet another self-doubting question:

Ex. 4:1

Then Moses answered, “But suppose they do not believe me or listen to me, but say, ‘The LORD did not appear to you.’” 

 

Calvin:

Moses answered. Moses relates in this chapter how hesitatingly he obeyed God, not from stubbornness, but from timidity, for he does not shake off the yoke, as unruly beasts do, but shrinks away from it, that it may not be placed upon him.

 

The issue is one of trust—five times the same Hebrew word for “trust” is used in this section (1, 5, 8, 9)… Interestingly,  the grammatical form of this verb  that is used here in each case is used most often elsewhere in a negative context as related to skepticism. 

 

e.g. What will enable God’s people to “trust” that Moses is authorized by God.. trust as related then to Moses’ authority… this was Moses’ concern!

And from a human point of view, Moses has a strong argument—  Who is Moses at this time—a man who was raised under Egyptian protection, not the bondage of his people,  a man both banished by Egypt and rejectd by Israel, now residing in a foreign land… Surely the people will not believe him for they have no basis for trusting him…

(Circumstantially, it just didn’t make sense that God would call Moses to this task—so much for the easy door theory of determining God’s call, where the best thing to do is what seemed, on the surface at least, to make most sense)

 

The issue then is trust in the context of skepticism… How to overcome the skepticism of the people of Moses day.

 

God then shows Moses that the elders will hear him and why… For evidently, even Yahweh acknowledges the validity of Moses’ claim for he provide Moses with three signs—in keeping with this whole episode regarding Israel’s deliverance, we could even say that just as Pharoah was raised up by God in order that God can make known his saving power—so too this skepticism is an opportunity for God to make known his power in authorizing Moses… and so the point will be made as through the three signs themselves

 

And it IS significant that in  vs. 8 miracles called “signs”—e.g. the miracles themselves are not the thing we are to focus on so much as the thing signified by the signs… namely, that Moses is authorized by God himself! So too, those called of God throughout R/H to represent him to his people are given signs!

Ezek. 24:24

Deut. 34:11 (NRA) He was unequaled for all the signs and wonders that the LORD sent him to perform in the land of Egypt, against Pharaoh and all his servants and his entire land,

 

As then testified too by the signs given—we see how  the  authority of Moses is only in so far as  authorized by God himself.  So that again, “the real hero of this call and commission is not Moses  but God and the trust that will produce belief must be placed not in Moses , but in God. Moses is but the medium of the message.” (Durham 44)

 

As then for the signs themselves, we need not spend too much time on focusing on them in themselves, for the real issue is the relation of the sign to Moses and his self-disclosure as to fit a pattern in the Old and New Testament..

Summary of Signs

Each of these signs attested Yahweh's creative power. Normally at least two witnesses were necessary to establish credibility under the Mosaic Law (Deut. 19:15; et al.). A third witness further strengthened the veracity of the testimony. Here God gave Moses three witnesses to confirm His prophet's divine calling and enablement. God entrusted Moses with His powerful word and endowed him with His mighty power. He was the first prophet with the power to work miracles.  A Pattern was set into motion that would forever distinguish a true from a false prophet as related to the working of signs and wonders…

 

But this is not the only pattern in R/H that is revealed in our text today… notice what happens next!

III. Redemptive Historical Implications:

 

All of this raised a question:

 

"Is there no discrepancy between these two announcements  ["I have come down to deliver," v. 8, and "I will send you," v. 10]? If God has Himself come down to do the work of redemption, what need of Moses? Would not a word from those almighty lips be enough? Why summon a shepherd, a lonely and unbefriended man, a man who has already failed once, and from whom the passing years have stolen his manhood's prime, to work out with painful elaboration, and through a series of bewildering disappointments, the purposed emancipation?”

 

Answer:  “This is not an isolated case.  Throughout the entire scheme of Divine government, we meet with the principle of mediation.."59 Meyer, p. 43.

 

As a type—the call of Moses fits a two fold pattern of the call of the weak to be strong as God’s mediator of salvation—in order that God may in all things be glorified. This is evidenced by the giving of supernatural signs!
