Exodus 4:18-26

A Circumcision that Saves

 

Introduction

In God’s perfect providence, this is a perfect sermon to probe into the mysteries of things here accomplished through baptism and church membership…

 

Exposition:

 

This is one of those passages that you would probably never hear preached in a sermon if it weren’t for our conviction to preach through whole books of the bible… for it is perhaps one of the most perplexing and counterintuitive passages you will ever read…

 

But again, being forced to treat every portion of God’s word as sacred and trustworthy—we are forced to deal with the strangest of things..

 

For here we have it, the same God that pursued Moses to be his mediatorial deliver for his people ISRAEL, the same God who gave him signs and wonders  and promised to be with him and even his mouth in order to accomplish his task, is here shown to be after Moses in order to kill him when Moses finally is about the task that he so reluctantly assumed!

 

What to make of this?  

 

The general theme binding this somewhat wide range of material together is the return to Egypt of Moses, now the deliver equipped with God’s staff to perform God’s deliverance of his people, so designated as a corporate entity, “my son” set in stark contrast to “Pharoh’s son.” 

 

 

Vs. 18-19—Moses goes home to Jethro’s camp, there to ask permission for the journey…Moses coming to Jethro with this request is an important indication of his identification with his Midianite home… note “Moses’ reference to his “brothers who are in Egypt” as then related to his roots…

see whether they are still living--- again, evidently quite a large period of time has passed, the phrase seems to be a Hebrew idiom such as to include in its meaning to investigate as to the general welfare of a person (Gen.45:3)

 

Vs. 20-21--The staff which began as Moses tool of shepherding (4:2) has now become in vs. 20 “the staff of God”—e.g., as related then to the signs and wonders that God will perform so that Moses is told to take these “wondrous deeds” seriously, just as Yahweh clearly does… so that moses is then told in a marvelous summary of both the purpose and range of the signs and the mighty acts that God will be working through both him and the Pharoah..

Ex. 4:21

And the LORD said to Moses, “When you go back to Egypt, see that you perform before Pharaoh all the wonders that I have put in your power; but I will harden his heart, so that he will not let the people go.

 

E.g. The staff and Pharoah are God’s instruments for salvation even as this statement in vs. 21 anticipates the frequent reference to the “Hardening of the heart” motif as related to God’s sovereign action throughout the narrative in both parties in order to reveal his power and glory in Israel’s salvation:

 

Vs. 22-23--  In summation of what God is already doing and will do as related to the salvation of Israel, Notice especially a very significant pairing of themes as related to “First born” – this pairing and the point that is made by it is crucial to understanding what happens next to Moses in his journey to Egypt.

22 Then you shall say to Pharaoh, ‘Thus says the LORD: Israel is my firstborn son.  23 I said to you, “Let my son go that he may worship me.” But you refused to let him go; now I will kill your firstborn son.’”

 

E.g. Israel is declared to be Yahweh’s own firstborn (vs. 22, note “singular” son) who will be the recipients of God’s protection  as contrasted with Pharaoh’s firstborn son who will be the recipient of God’s judgment leading to the ultimate punishment even death itself should Pharoah be disobedient (even as he will because of God’s hardening his heart)

So then what distinguishes one son from the other son ULTIMATELY—e.g. why was Israel the “son” treated as special to God whereas not Pharoah’s son… was it anything intrinsic to the decisions or behaviors of Pharoah’s son vs. Israel?

Vs. 24-26:  Considered to be among the most difficult passage to interpret in all of Exodus…  

 

E.g. Why should Yahweh suddenly seek to kill his messenger—no explicit reason is given!

What is the meaning of “blood bridegroom”? and how does it relate to the circumcision of Moses’ child by his wife? 

And why does this get Moses off the hook?

 

what is clear by way of observation is this. 

1)      Yahweh is plainly named as the one who meets Moses during the journey at one of their encampments with the explicit goal of killing Moses!

 the LORD met him and tried to kill him

 

2)      The reason for this attack is vaguely inferred in vs. 26b—because someone at least had not had not previously been circumcised…

a.      Moses perhaps not at all, perhaps an Egyptian circumcision which would not have had the ceremonial significance as done by a priest, even as Egyptian circumcisions were in effect only “partial” by nature anyway.. whatever, it is clearly affirmed that in the eyes of God, Moses was not circumcised! Thus, as per some (Welhousen), Moses had not been circumcised and was therefore attacked.  Zipporah circumcised her son as a substitute and established a permanent change in the rite by its successful outcome. 

b.      The Rabbinic midrashim developed the idea that Moses had failed to circumcise the child by the eighth day

 

IN both cases, the attach upon Moses was due to the sin of Moses, not of commission but of omission, in this case the omission relative to keeping what was clearly already the law of God for his people relative to ritual entrance into the community of faith by blood as through the “cutting ceremony” of circumcision… which pointed then to the salvation of the people of God not by human might, but by God’s gracious provision as had been dramatically enacted to Abraham in the cutting ceremony that proceeded the institution of the circumcision rite—a ceremony that pointed to God’s provision of keeping the covenant in order that his people would not be held accountable in themselves for their failure to do so.  (Gen. 15, 17, 23)

 

n

a.      She circumcised their child

b.      Touched Moses with the foreskin and blood—the smearing of the blood served as a visible demonstration that circumcision had indeed been performed…

c.      She spoke the words “you are a blood bridegroom to me” seizes the mandatory flint tool (Josh.5:2-9)

What does this mean exactly?

E.g. the ancient phrase “a bridegroom of blood by circumcision” is a phrase indicating marital relationship, so that to marry a Jewish woman is to enter into the Jewish household, even as would be ceremonially indicated by circumcision of the groom. 

 

4)  The meaning of the phrase by Zipporah is most likely related to  what she did—which again was to take the bloodied foreskin from the circumcision rite of their firstborn and to touch Moses with it (most likely, though it is obscure in the text, it would be to touch Moses’s genitilia—“”His legs”) thus—to demonstrate as by the marital relationship and their son that the actions performed on their son was vicariously applied to Moses as well.. so that now, both Moses and their son were “circumcised” 

 

Childs” “Whatever Zipporah had done—she had cut off the foreskin, touched Moses pronounced the words (which) comprised the act of circumcision and this is what saved Moses…again,

 

What is clear however is the effect of her action “he let him alone”… that is to say “the attach by Yahweh on the life of Moses ceased” (Childs)

Therefore, “circumcision explains the meaning of Zipporah’s action”

 

 

5)      The final statement “so he let them alone” as regarding God’s intent to “Kill” Moses is therefore a climatic way of saying that due to the circumcision of Moses and his Son, God’s wrath was averted…

6)      One at this point cannot but help seeing in this a prophetic enactment whereby this circumcision, as was to represent God’s judgment ordeal, would come upon the Egyptian people, but not the Israel people whose doors were touched by the blood of the covenant such as to see God’s wrath pass over Israel’s firstborns! 

 

Point: As a whole, the story serves to dramatize the tremendous importance of circumcision in relation to the corporate identity of Israel as “God’s son”…   And to be “God’s Son” is to be saved, whereas not to be God’s son is to be condemned by God’s wrath.

 

To be sure, Moses is held accountable for his sin of omission relative to the circumcision rite, but even more to the point, its commission saves Moses from the wrath of God!  

 

Note also, being named as “the son of God” is dramatically linked then to circumcision as well.

 

All of this then raised the question, why?

To us, especially in a context that thinks of ones relationship with God in relatively private ways, and in “heart” vs. “ritual” ways—this whole things seems so arbitrary and strange…,

 

Here again, we are confronted with a kind of spirituality that doesn’t seem to fit our own living in the 21st century America.

 

1)       Corporate identity having with it significant implications

2)       Sacramental identity, again having with it significant implications.

 

E.g.

1)       Corporately—God knows Israeal, not only as a collection of individuals but as a corporate person—such that ones relationship to God IS measured in terms of ones relationship to the people of God’

2)       Sacramentally—God knows us as we are visibly set apart by means of sacramental entrance to the corporate community… which means that the corporate community is more than an organizational entity, but is defined by God’s sacramental presence whereby he transacts in the midst of his people his covenant terms and privileges..

 

Whatever else is going on here—what is clearly declared is that Israel is God’s first born by virtue of having been covered by the blood of the covenant—

To pass through the entrance rite of circumcision was to pass through God’s judgment and wrath unscathed.

 

This story then picks up with and perhaps further explains what we saw last week when God’s anger was kindled against Moses, and yet so as not to consume him..

 

Why didn’t God’s wrath consume him?

Answer, the blood of the covenant… e.g., as predicated upon God’s election of a people, having entered them into the covenant family by ceremonial rite of circumcision.

 

OT Application:

Clearly then, in its “First level” use of this passage in the life of Israel under the Mosaic convenant—this passage would be used to encourage covenant fidelity such as to maintain the ceremonial aspects of the law…but why

 

1)       To be sure, God’s visible covenant community was set apart by circumcision of its male firstborns… to be uncircumcised was paramount to being outside of the OT church! 

2)       What then was being established by this rite of entrance?  Basically this—that to be on God’s good side (such as then not to come under his wrath) we must have some vicarious means of passing through God’s judgment without being destroyed by it.. this was symbolized by the circumcision rite itself, as one passing thorugh the blood, this pointed one then to the blood of Passover, which then said basically this—to be saved from God’s judgment, we must put our hope in God and his provision for our deliverance—

3)       First level—as typified by the sacrifice of animals..

 

NT Application:

Second level—we of course think of the Col. Passage read earlier, even the ceremony that we have witnessed here today—as to say basically the same thing as then but as more fully recognized as being fulfilled in Christ.

Thus,

 

1)       Christ is the embodiment of corporate Israel as the “son of God”… designated the “first born” since by his work, those who are covered by his blood are adopted as his “brethren” in order to be saved…

e

Rom. 8:29 For whom He foreknew, He also predestined [to] [be] conformed to the image of His Son, that He might be the firstborn among many brethren.

 

2)       To be sure, one is not in the church until one is baptized, even as according to Col. Baptism replaced circumcision as the rite of entrance, though it meant the same thing (both symbolizing a passing thorugh God’s judgment)

 

3)       And again, the rite itself is not what saves us, but faith in God’s provision for salvation from God’s wrath as by blood sacrificed—in the NT context, as clearly accomplished by Christ.

 

So therefore,

Ever since Abraham,  all believers “with their children” are set apart in the world as belonging to God…

As circumcision directs us to Christ, so too, in fulfillment of circumcisioin Christ is named as the ultimate blood by which we are saved.. even as the cutting ceremony of circumcision was a visible reminder that the terms of God’s covenant must be kept, either by us or, as in the case when God himself without Abraham passed through the cut in half animals, by God on our behalf—

 

Christ is the “God on our behalf” means of keeping the covenant, as by his blood, he suffered the consequence of our disobedience, -- this is what circumcision meant in its day—even as Paul explicitly reviews as fulfilled in Christ in Col 2

 

Notice then also how circumcision and its symbolic and sacramental meaning is likewise attributed to baptism under the NC

 

Eg.. Whereas we do not need to be circumcised to be part of the visible community of faith, we do need to be baptized—thus the command on Christ’s part in the great commission “go and make disciples… baptizing them”

 

Likewise, no less so than of old, you are not a participant of God’s saving grace if you are not a participant in his wholly covenant community!  (at least not ordinarily)

 

It is a serious thing not to be baptized

And even more to the point, it is a serious thing not to believe in the one in whom baptism directs us—namely, Christ, his blood, his atoning work for our salvation..

What can be done—again, here are some suggestions by Robert Patterson

 

What can be done to raise the status of the church among evangelicals?.

 

1.      Evangelicals need to affirm aggressively the necessary connection between faith in Christ and commitment to his church.  One cannot exist without the other, as demonstrated in Acts, where no one was counted as a Christian until he or she was baptized and received into the community of God’s people.  So-called solitary or independent Christians need to be incorporated into the life and discipline of some congregation.  Those who are already church members need to remain committed to their church, taking seriously their accountability to the congregation and resisting the temptation to “jump ship” when problems develop.  At the same time, church leaders need to take more seriously their responsibility to discipline and nurture parishioners under their care.

 

2.      Churches, whether they are independent or denominational, need to cooperate to reduce transfers of members from one congregation to another in a given locality.  Before accepting transfers from another congregation in the same community, churches could urge them to settle grievances with the transferring church, which might encourage transfers to remain in the original church.

 

3.      Evangelical churches need to recover the Reformation consensus that the Lord’s Supper and baptism are important elements in the life of the church.  These are, or course, administered in different ways in different traditions, and sometimes given different names.  However, practiced, churches should more fully appreciate these as necessary marks that help define the corporate people of God. Baptism needs to be practiced as a necessary rite of initiation into the fellowship of the church, while the Lord’s Supper should be served as a nurturing rite for continuation in the life of the church.  Church discipline could then be restored as churches administered Communion only to those in good standing.

 

4.      Parachurch organizations need to be assimilated into the fellowship and discipline of the churches.  For example, parachurch college ministries could place staff on campus through a church or a coalition of churches, which would direct and supervise the local campus ministry.  In turn, a church or churches could adopt the parachurch entity as an official agency
