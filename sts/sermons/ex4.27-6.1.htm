Exodus 4:27-6:1

Between Promise and Fulfillment

 

Introduction:

 

Exposition:

Ex. 5:1ff is the fleshing out of 3:18-20…

Ex. 3:16

Go and assemble the elders of Israel, and say to them, ‘The LORD, the God of your ancestors, the God of Abraham, of Isaac, and of Jacob, has appeared to me, saying: I have given heed to you and to what has been done to you in Egypt.  17 I declare that I will bring you up out of the misery of Egypt, to the land of the Canaanites, the Hittites, the Amorites, the Perizzites, the Hivites, and the Jebusites, a land flowing with milk and honey.’ 18) They will listen to your voice;

 

and you and the elders of Israel shall go to the king of Egypt and say to him, ‘The LORD, the God of the Hebrews, has met with us; let us now go a three days’ journey into the wilderness, so that we may sacrifice to the LORD our God.’  19 I know, however, that the king of Egypt will not let you go unless compelled by a mighty hand.  20 So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders that I will perform in it; after that he will let you go.

 

Eg. Its all according to plan (much like we hear over and over again these days about the war…) Tension is decreed by God between Egypt and himself, in order that God might make known his power and compassion for Israel…

 

3 parts:

1) 16-18b,   4:27-31—Moses goes to the elders of Israel and they Listen

2.) 18b-19,  5:1-23— The request made to Pharaoh and the initial negative consequences that followed for Israel

3) 20, 6:1—The prologue to the proof of Presence sequence in chapter 7 especially  as promised by Yahweh in order that God might make known his power until the salvation of Israel from Egypt.

 

I. High Expectations!

16-18b,   4:27-31

Just as was decreed by God earlier—Moses went to the elders of Israel (for even then the church was governed by a government of elders, or “presbuteros” in Grk-) and acting for the people of Israel, we are told that the elders believed Moses, even those who Moses had previously declared to God would NOT believe… But their belief was based not on anything to do with Moses, but upon the Words and signs of God through Moses…

 

Note: Just as later, Jesus will say

John 8:54

 “If I glorify myself, my glory is nothing. It is my Father who glorifies me, he of whom you say, ‘He is our God,’

 

Thus, the credibility of Moses as the mediator of God’s covenant is founded upon the works accomplished by God on his behalf—A salvation from God through Moses!

 

Things were on the up and up!  Moses had survived his treacherous journey, the elders, much to Mose’s astonishment, had believed, even as God said that they would based on the signs and wonders given to Moses…

 

Now quickly, the narrative takes us to the next big event… the confrontation with Pharaoh… what to expect? 

 

II. Temporary Set-Backs…

Based on God’s Previous warning, Moses, Aaron and God’s people should have expected a temporary set back—but evidently they forgot what was to take place between promise and fulfillment! 

 

5:1-23— Just as God instructed, Moses quickly moves from the elders belief to his bold request of Pharoah

 

vs. 1: Moses’ approach to Pharoah is authoritative and direct, using the typical “messenger formula” of a prophet coupled with a command saying “Thus says Yahweh, the God of Israel, ‘Let my people go…

 

Note: the same formula will be seen again and again between Moses and Pharaoh until the Fulfillment of God’s redemptive plan… (4:22, 7:17, 8:1, 9:4)… the formula become typical throughout Israel’s history as between various other confrontations between God’s prophets and those who would be king! 

 

Vs. 2: The response by Pharaoh is equally authoritative and blunt…

“Who is the LORD, that I should heed him and let Israel go? I do not know the LORD, and I will not let Israel go.”

 

E.g. the almost arrogant confidence on the part of both parties is deliberately set into opposition as it sets up in dramatic fashion a confrontation like nothing ever seen before in Redemptive History – even between the superpower of the world and the superpower of heavens and earth! 

 

The sequence of command and defiance introduces a conflict that will then continue all the way through chapter 12… the impending drama could not be more intense, as from the very beginning! 

 

And again, notice carefully, while the human players are Moses and Pharaoh—the issue is God…

ON the one hand—Moses declares God’s existence and sovereignty over Pharaoh and His kingdom

On the other hand—Pharaoh declares “who is God”—i.e. what is your god to me? Or “I do not recognize your god as “MY” god… (keeping in mind that the Egyptian divinity was closely tied to the authority of Pharaohs)

 

Childs: “The theme of Pharaoh’s not knowing Yahweh, which is introduced here for the first time continues to be picked up and developed within the plague narrative.  And with considerable relish the biblical writer describes how Pharaoh comes to know who Yahweh is as he demonstrates his power over Egypt (8:18, 9:29, 11:8)

 

But what about the interim, before Pharaoh is sufficiently humbled into submission? 

 

Vs 3ff..  and the initial negative consequences that followed for Israel can be detected as early as in their immediate response to Pharaoh perhaps…

For it seems that the first response to Pharaoh’s decisive reply is the demoralization of Moses and Aaron—… they well could have said” Our God is the one and only true God… or something like that… but instead, they reply… “the God of the Hebrews” (not even Our God) but “their God… “ and somewhat apologetically adds that their request is only for  the sake of avoiding God’s judgment as stated “or he will fall upon us with pestilence or sword.” 

 

Vs. 4-5 the response then of Pharaoh is as if to say—you should not fear God so much as you fear me!  Again, the rival powers are in full view, even as Moses and Aaron drop out of the narrative for the moment and Pharoah begins to berate them for even thinking about such a request at the risk of bringing the economic regime of Pharaoh to a halt…

E.g. “why are you taking the people away from their work!  The language of Pharaoh admits that Israel’s duties are “heavy” and even “oppressive” but reasserts how they must “get on with” their task and refers to the people literally as “the people of the land.”  Their worth to Pharaoh is clear—as servants and slaves to his power and wealth…   even as the whole interaction reeks of sarcasm against Israel…

 

Vs. 6ff… We see then how Pharaoh planned to squelch Israel’s confidence in “their” god…  

 

The severity  of Pharaoh’s “take charge” response is set in motion by a decree to increase the burden of their work—perhaps thinking that they must have too much time on their hands if they are thinking and planning a pilgrimage trip—Pharaoh of course orders that even while the people of God must continue to maintain their production quota, they are given the added task of harvesting their own materials—in this case, the chopped straw that was used to make the bricks (a kind of mortar of that day). Evidently, this essential ingredient had been provided to them.

 

The burden of this decree is made evident as the test tells us that the people were then scattered all about in search of the needed stubble

 

Vs. 10-14—the text presents a scenario whereby the whole bureaucracy was working against the backs of the Israel people… from the Eghyptian work bosses to the Hebrew section leaders down tot he people themselves, the beatings and verbal berating increased—the whole system was now under greater strain and oppression than even before even as they were unable to sustain the added load…

 

Vs. 15ff—we are told that the supervisors, even the ones who were being beaten in vs. 14 by the Egyptian taskmasters for their lack of production appealed through the chain of command to Pharaoh inquiring as to why the greater burden…. Again, the reply of Pharaoh makes it clear, that his intent was to take all fight out of them.. and blames their wandering ambitions to their laziness…

 

Modern parallels between this event in the history of Israel and the later events that fell upon the Jews under Nazi Germany are hard to miss—when in Nazi concentration camps, impossible quotas failed to be achieved and the Hebrew foremen were beaten… and when they complained the same old charge of idleness was rehearsed… so then in our text, the biblical writer succeeds brilliantly in picturing the utter sense of helplessness before the highly organized machinery of the hierarchial system…

The Israel supervisors withdraw in frustration

 

Vs. 19-21—it is at this point then that the “chain of command” on the Hebrew side is shown to be as demoralized as the chain of command on Pharaoh’s side is defiant and oppressive… for having gone from the people to their supervisors, the demoralized Hebrew supervisors after being rebuffed by Pharaoh seek out Moses and Aaron in order to turn on them… and to give them a piece of their mind…

 

One of course wonders why Moses and Aaron had not themselves appealed to Pharaoh instead of the Hebrew superviors- except that perhaps they were defeated already as well. .

 

The picture then one gets is that Moses and Aaron were waiting to meet the supervisors in hopes of hearing about a successful mission given their own failure.. but instead  such hopes, if there was any at all, were quickly dashed as the superviors fix their blame on Moses and Aaron for their plight… they even call upon God to bring judgment upon them for the oppression that was brought upon the Israel people because of them!   In short, from their vantage point now, it would have been better if their hopes of what was promised had never been raised.

 

22-23, Thus, did Moses, the last of the human chain of command in God’s spiritual economy, now and in the words of the text “turn on” Yahweh with words that clearly reflect his profound feelings of disillusionment! 

Note: the chain of “you’s”

--Supervisors blame Moses and Aaroon “You (plural) have done harm,

21 They said to them, “The LORD look upon you and judge! You have brought us into bad odor with Pharaoh and his officials, and you have put a sword in their hand to kill us.”

 

--Mose then turns on God and say : “ you have sent, following your message , you have not even begun to rescue

Ex. 5:22

Then Moses turned again to the LORD and said, “O LORD, why have you mistreated this people? Why did you ever send me?  23 Since I first came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has mistreated this people, and you have done nothing at all to deliver your people.”

 

The effect of his words is that all of Israel, and even Moses himself, had clearly loose sight of the ultimate purpose  of their promised redemption and how even this most recent turn of events was also decreed by God as then related to God’s ultimate plan.  Moses in effect says, it would have been better if this would never have been initiate… the people would have been better off without you and your great plan! 

23 Since I first came to Pharaoh to speak in your name, he has mistreated this people, and you have done nothing at all to deliver your people.”

 

Reflection:

One can only imagine how the people were at this time profoundly demoralized and defeated… having accepted their elders judgement to trust Moses as according to the signs performed by God.. they were surely at this moment in time exasperated and disillusioned by this whole scheme.  The elders, to the supervisors, to Moses and Aaron themselves were rethinking this whole thing!

 

Caught as it where between the promise and fulfillment—their condition was indeed worse, not better!

 

Before we read the final verse, we would do well to take all this in as it clearly is a pattern that will be repeated yet again in redemptive history..

 

E.g. the period between promise and fulfillment is often a time of disillusionment, discouragement and trial resulting in the temptation to turn away from God…

 

We will encounter it later as related to entering into the promises land… not one but two times… as recounted in Num 32…

 

E.g. Moses recounts how the Israel people would later be intimidated by the apparent circumstances that were presented against them before going into the promised land for instance, which resulted in their lack of faith in God and their being disciplined in the wilderness for forty years… This is recounted in order to warn the Israel people in yet another situation to not allow the harsh circumstances to turn them away from following God.   

 

One later thinks of Ezra, and the rebuilding of the temple after the exile…

 

Eg. After the exiles of Judah and Benjimin were intimidated by those who apposed the rebuilding of the temple the project was put off…

 

This of course raises the question: Where are we in this redemptive historical pattern? 

 

What about today?

III. Ex. 6:1

Then the LORD said to Moses, “Now you shall see what I will do to Pharaoh: Indeed, by a mighty hand he will let them go; by a mighty hand he will drive them out of his land.”

 

Just as was earlier promised

3:20 So I will stretch out my hand and strike Egypt with all my wonders that I will perform in it; after that he will let you go.

 

E.g. “after that”--  it is not enough that fulfillment follows promise… it was to be fulfilled in a manner that would accomplish God’s ultimate purpose, not even the redemption of Israel, but the glorification of his name among the nations… !

 

Thus,

The whole of chapter 5 and the trails that lead to Israel’s discouragement  must be read in the light of this response by God, just as the whole of life that is between promise and fulfillment!  For in the light of Moses’s disillusionment and anger at things that seem to be worse than ever due to his faith, albeit reluctant, in God… 6:1 reminds Moses and us that what Moses thinks has not begun has indeed begun! 

 

That what seems to be a setback is in fact a setup!
