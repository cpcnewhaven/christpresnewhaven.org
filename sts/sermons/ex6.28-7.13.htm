Exodus 6:28-7:13

A Salvation Accomplished by God and for His Glory

 

Introduction:

Why do things that happen, happen?

And especially if you are a Christian, why did God save you? 

 

E.g., the Purpose Question!

And if “great minds have purposes” according to Washington Irving, then what do we know about the purpose of God in all his mighty acts throughout history? 

Notice how our passage today will frame the mighty acts of God in Israel’s salvation from Egypt in order to answer the “purpose question.”

 

Exposition:

Today’s passage can be divided into two parts:

6:28-7:7, Preview of God’s Plan of Salvation 

7:8-13, Preliminary Episode that reaffirms the point of the Preview

 

Together, they form what might be described as a theological prologue to the Salvation that is about to be accomplished. 

 

Let me explain: 

 

As noted by our last sermon in Exodus (Nate), we remain in a section that more or less functions as a reminder /reiteration to what God has already promised, even as it functions as a prelude to what God is about to do. Its purpose is both in respect to things promised in the past, and things that will be accomplished in the future and especially the 10 mighty acts of God that will lead to Israel’s salvation from Egypt.

 Therefore, the point especially of our passage today is to theologically explain the meaning of the Exodus as before it happens, lest in the midst of the story, we loose sight of God’s purpose in it. 

 

 The Point will be this-- as a Prologue to the Salvation about to be accomplished, our passage will frame it all theologically to be A Salvation Accomplished by God and for his Glory

 

I. 6:28-7:7, Preview of God’s Plan of Salvation 

 

7:1-5, is a response to Moses question in vs. 28-30

 

As noted before, it is more or less the same question that was raised first as far back as in 6:12 and repeated again in 6:20

 “through the means of God’s response to the same question  of Moses, God gives what amounts to a theological explanation of the entire sequence of the mighty acts which will bring the promise of deliverance to reality.”  (Durham)

 

The Question

 

A.  vs. 30

question driving our passage is this:

Now look, I am a clumsy speaker, just how is Pharaoh going to pay attention to me? 

(again and again, Moses is looking at things from a human perspectiveÑas shall we the reader not only concerning the events in Exodus, but the rest of the events of redemptive history, no less than the events that happen in our lives,  if we not carefulÑwhich is the point of God’s response and this passage for us today!). 

 

Yes, the question has been heard before, as in varied forms already noted in chapter 3, 4 and 5(3:11-=2, 13-14, 19-20, 4:1, 8-9, 10-12 and 5:22-6:1). 

E.g. Who am I, they won’t trust me, I am heavy of lip and thick of tongue, why have you sent me there for thisÉ etc.etc. 

 

The Question is asked by us as well? Who am I, (as then often driving out of our insecurities about ourselves, our abilities, our circumstancesÑso that we are lead as well to “what is my purpose for life, what is the purpose for things that happen to me, this or that seeming tragedy, this or that seeming successÑwhy me, why now, why at all, is there some overarching driving purpose to all of this which then would direct us in how we are to respond? And if we Christians, we should be asking this question about our salvation as wellÑwhy me?  Look tame, I am a clumsy sinner, and if God is sending me out to be his ambassadorÑhowis that person in my world going to pay any attention to me?  It was no doubt asked by the apostle when he acknowledged that he was “the chief of sinners” in his mind the most unlikely of ambassadors for GodÉ yes, in his mind according perhaps to his limited notion of things as according to human perspective!

 

It’s also a question that we could must ultimate think upon with respect to JesusÑwho is Jesus that any would look upon him and trembleÉ

Isaiah 53:2 he had no form or majesty that we should look at him, nothing in his appearance that we should desire him.

3 He was despised and rejected by others;

Answer: As we have seen already, God’s answer is basically, “what does it matter who you are, or what the circumstances areÑI am God I can and will do whatever I want in any circumstances that I choose.”  I am God!

Durham “Thus a question of Moses is once again an opening for an assertion of Yahweh that makes clear that Moses is but an instrument of God’s activity.” 

 

Notice then with me how this is stated and further amplified in so far as God is said to be the only “prime mover” of the events in Exodus for the purpose of his own glory! 

Notice for instance, that God doesn’t disagree with Moses’ self-assessmentÑhe doesn’t say as we are often prone to say in such contextsÑ”oh no Moses, you are being too hard on yourself, you really are a good speaker and an incredible leaderÉ.

 

Rather, God says,

 That in spite of who Moses is in reality, by God’s power he will be as a God to Pharaoh! 

Vs.1   “Look here , I have made you like God to Pharaoh, and your brother Aaron shall be your prophet.

 

Note: The Hebrew word “re-ah” which literally means “see here” is like the contemporary use of the verb that makes us say “Look here” in order to get one’s attention. 

 

That Moses is to be a god (elohim) to Pharaoh will be Yahweh’s doing, not his!

Note: Again, the Hebrew reads in the first person “to give” so that literally it says “I will give you, a god, to Pharaoh”

Notice especially the “BUT”-- And what is particularly interesting in Ezekiel, is that each time God is said to “act for his own name sake” it is directly after commenting on Israel’s unworthiness of God’s actingÑin other words, God’s acting had nothing at all to do with the worthiness or greatness of Israel, but for the sake of God’s glory.  

 

This phrase as introduced in Exodus 7 in vs. 5 is repeated again and again in Ezekiel to make the very simple pointÑthat God’s actions, even God’s action to save Israel, no less than his actions to curse Israel’s enemies as in the case of Pharaoh, has less to do with Israel or Pharaoh or any human person and more to do with God’s purposes among the nations to glorify his nameÉ or, said using this important “purpose formula”Ñthat they may know that I am Yahweh!

7:1-5, in response to Moses self-defacing question, introduces a phrase then that is meant to interpret the events which are about to happen, even as these events will be remembered in this way as well.

 

And in so doing, we are taught the meaning of all of redemptive history, even as this portion directs us to a greater mighty acts of God in yet a greater kind of salvation that is accomplished through one greater than Moses!

 

We will see how it is that the Exodus story and especially the manner by which God will in everyway be shown to orchestrate things, is toward the ends “so that they might know that I am the Lord their God”Ñeven the hardness of Pharaoh’s heart is so orchestrated to escalate so that at the perfect time, God’s power is perfectly manifest in the defeat of Pharaoh’s stubborn heart and might.

 

Not only do we see flawed circumstances as related to Pharaoh, Moses, the IsraelitesÑbut we see that these circumstances themselves are ordained by God in order that God’s ultimate purposes can be fulfilled! 

In summary, the circumstances that frame the Exodus event are these:

a) A reluctant and inept hero

b)an unbelieving and recalcitrant Pharaoh

c) a crushed and dispirited Israel,

d) a proud and ruling Egyptian people

e) anon-nation against the greatest of nations

Why these circumstances?  That the proof of God’s supremacy and sovereignty in salvation, which is to turn everything upside down, may be established irrevocablyÉ we will see that even as Moses will speak to Pharaoh, even as the 9 mighty acts of God leading up to the final mighty act of God will increase, rather than decrease Pharaoh’s resistance thus creating what seems to be an impregnable impasseÑso that at the end, “they might know that I am Yahweh! 

 

Durham “Thus, Yahweh is orchestrating in a combination of opposing and unlikely forces a deliverance that will above all be a proof of his active Presence.”

 

Calvin: In fact, God willed that Pharaoh should pertinaciously resist Moses, in order that the deliverance of the people might be more conspicuous.

Summary: This section introduces what some have called the divine purpose formulaÑI have wanted us to slow down enough to recognize it for ourselves as well!  It  represents a theological explanation for the sequence of mighty acts that is about to occur toward the deliverance of Israel from EgyptÉit tells us not only what is about to happen, but what it means even before it does! 

 

More specifically: Not only then does it frame the events in Exodus, it frames all salvific events throughout the whole of redemptive historyÉ even that history that is continuing to be worked out in our lives as well..

 

WhyJesusÑa humble carpenter’s son, born into humiliation, living under humiliating circumstances, treated with humiliating crueltyÑ”so that they might know that I am the Lord” as through the mighty acts that are accomplished through himÉ

 

Why meÑa miserable moral person if the truth were to be known, a person weak in every way, who more times than not suffers under weak circumstances and events and trials as related to myself, my family, my world?  -- “so that they might know that I am the Lord” as through God’s ultimate purpose accomplished working in us his glory as awaits all who will be ultimately glorified in heaven!

7:8-13, Preliminary Episode that reaffirms the point made in the preceding  Preview

 

8-13:  when Moses presents himself to Pharaoh, Pharaoh requested that Moses and Aaron perform a miracle to prove their divine authority since they claimed that God had sent them (vv. 9-10).

And notice then what happensÉ

The staff of Moses is turned into a serpent (evidently a dangerous type because earlier you will remember that Moses ran away from it the “snatched “ at it very cautiouslyÉ .

 

The illustrative story then proceeds to tell how Pharaoh went to great trouble to discredit the eventÑthus beginning is long journey of resisting and even combating the authority of God rather than to be submissive under it.   He called upon his wise scholars, magicians, learned men and arcane artist- who are then able to duplicate this act, as if to say, Aaron simply knew the trick”

 

11 Then Pharaoh summoned the wise men and the sorcerers; and they also, the magicians of Egypt, did the same by their secret arts.

 

The Jews preserved the names of the chief magicians even though the Old Testament did not record them. Paul said they were Jannes and Jambres (2 Tim. 3:9). These were not sleight-of-hand artists but wise men who were evidently members of the priestly caste (cf. Gen.41:8). The power of their demonic gods lay in their "secret arts" (v.11). They were able to do miracles in the power of Satan (1 Cor. 10:20; cf.Matt. 24:24; 2 Thess.2:9-10; Rev. 13:13-14).122

 

Even then, however, God is shown to eventually show himself to be the ultimate powerÑIn what is perhaps even meant to be delightfully funny in vs. 12 when everyone throws down his staff and Pharaoh’s palace is about to be overrun with snakes, we are told that Aaron’s staff (not the snake interestingly) gobbled up or “gulped down” everyone else’s staffÉ

 

Thus, while Pharaoh is represented as having some powerÑhis power is shown to be no match for the power that is working through Aaron and Moses.

 

The superiority of the Israelites' God is clear in the superiority of Aaron's serpent over those of the Egyptian magicians (v. 12). The rod again represented regal authority and implied that Yahweh, not Pharaoh, was sovereign (cf. 4:2-5).

 

This miracle should have convinced Pharaoh of Yahweh's sovereignty, but he chose to harden his heart in unbelief and disobedience. Consequently God sent the plagues that

followed.

 

"The point of this brief section is that Yahweh's proof of his powerful Presence to the Pharaoh and thus to the Pharaoh's Egypt will be miraculous in nature."  (Durham)

 

we should notice especially vs. 13 and the concluding statement as to what happens “just as Yahweh had spoken.” 

 

For God to “speak” is the same as for God to “act” throughout scriptureÑ

So too, he spoke salvation into its accomplishmentÑEzek. 37 and the valley of dry bones is about God speaking life back into IsraelÉ

 

“Just as Yahweh had spoken”

Point: the first miracle of the Rod and the Snake functions also as a prologue to the ten mighty acts of God. Again, in order that they might know who the true and eternal god really is!

 

Thus the illustrative scene introduces the 10 mighty acts as representing

1)      how God will prove his power by means of working great miracles

2)      how this proof will at first be matched by Pharaoh’s own considerable power, even if outdistances and overcome by God

3)      how the proof will be resisted by Pharaoh even in the face of reason

4)      How in the end, God’s will triumph over Pharaoh, in order that his name and his power will be glorified among the nations..

 

Even as it is said, just as God had spoken!

 

Main point of our text today:  as by introducing the purpose formula  (that you might know that I am the Lord your God) and by this first illustrative miracle in the presence of Pharaoh, our passage will frame all 10 might acts of God that is about to happen  theologically to be A Salvation Accomplished by God and for his Glory.

 

The point of these two sections are the sameÑone explained in response to Moses’ questions, the other illustrated by means of a miracle before Pharaoh

 

In both, the point is theological as related to a crucial message that we must be reminded of over and over again Egypt is just the stage, Pharaoh, Moses the actors, Israel the audience, but the prime mover, the director, even the ultimate actor is God.. he is performing ultimately through the players, like a playwright and director perform through the circumstance and players on the stage, and the point is simple and clear, What we are about to be told regarding the salvation of Israelis about “a salvation by god and for God’s own glory” 

 

As then this story will be played out in history, we cannot of course miss the clear fulfillment that is accomplished in yet a greater salvation by yet a greater one than MosesÉ

 

Application:

 

1.      We must never forget the purpose formula as applied then to our salvation in Christ and even the meaning of salvation working out in our own lives and history!

 

It would be a grave mistake to forget what moves history ultimatelyÑnot our circumstances, not our abilities, not our genius, but GodÉ

 

What comfort would we forgo if we, even for a moment, forget that our circumstances are ultimately ordained by God, so that in our circumstance, his salvation will be manifest for his glory.

How?  When we complain of our weakened circumstancesÑas if God owed us something more?  When we are defeated by our weakened circumstancesÑas if by our strength and the strength of our circumstances God’s glory will be manifestÉWhen we are unwilling to serve Gods’ purpose, which is to make him known because we are so preoccupied, even consumed by our own sense of our own weak circumstances..

That is to say that even our good works are works that we are enable to do by God’s strength, not our own, but this takes us back to the first pointÑgod’s strength is most revealed when it is clear by our circumstances or our person that it is and cannot be our strength.
