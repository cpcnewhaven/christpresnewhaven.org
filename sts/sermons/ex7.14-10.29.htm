The Tragedy of a Hardened Heart

 

Introduction:

“What you see depends mainly on what you look for . . . How you see things on the outside of you depends on how things are on the inside of you.”

-John L Mason

 

If ever a story illustrates this proverb, it is the one we read about today—especially as related to issues of faith and spirituality, we discover how it is that what we see is much less about the facts and much more about the condition of our heart!

 

Exposition:

 

I. Context/Introduction:

We should first notice vs. 13 and remember the concluding statement as to why what happens happens “just as Yahweh had spoken.” 

 

For God to “speak” is the same as for God to “act” throughout scripture—

E.g. He spoke creation into existence,

So too, he spoke salvation into its accomplishment—Ezek. 37 and the valley of dry bones is about God speaking life back into Israel…

 

This phrase that will show up over and over again throughout the sequence of God’s mighty acts as the concluding statement (7:23, 8:11, 8:15, 9:12, 9:35) And where it isn’t stated explicitly, it is implied as by a notation that God made what happened to happen.   (10:37, 11:10). 

 

Most especially, it will show up as related to the hardening of Pharaoh’s heart… even as it is here introduced in vs. 13:

 

Still Pharaoh’s heart was hardened, and he would not listen to them, as the LORD had said.

 

As we turn then to the first 9 mighty acts of God leading to the 10th and salvific act— we will notice that this is pretty much the point—over and over again, the focus is on God’s mighty acts that would turn any reasonable person to repent, but how in the face of reason Pharaoh is all the more hardened to the plans and purposes of God.  

 

e.g. By way of an outline and summary: 

17-1-14: Salvation Prologue

vs. 3-  I will harden Pharaoh’s heart

vs. 4-  I will lay my hand upon Egypt and bring my people the Israelites, company by company, out of the land of Egypt by great acts of judgment.

 

But why?

The answer is given immediately in vs. 5, and reiterated throughout our passage:

5 In order that the Egyptians shall know that I am the LORD, when I stretch out my hand against Egypt and bring the Israelites out from among them.”

II.                The First Nine Mighty Acts of God Against Egypt: 

(Please note: this review relys heavily upon three sources—as noted throughout—Durham Exodus (WCS), Thomas Constible (Note on Exodus—internet source)  and Mel Sensenig (unpublished sermon notes, all quotes from other sources may be found in Constible’s notes)

 

1. The water turned to blood (first plague) 7:14-25


E.g.  a plague of judgment upon Egypt’s great god, the river Nile. (7:14-24)

The Lord sends Moses to Pharaoh while he was at the river. Some have speculated that this may have been Pharaoh’s morning “devotions”. Certainly the Nile River was a god to the Egyptians and he may well have been engaging in the worship of Egyptian gods by the river.

 

The first mighty act of God serves in the narrative as a paradigm of the nine plagues that follow. Striking the Nile with the rod suggested dominion over creation and all the gods of Egyptian mythology. The Egyptians linked many of their gods with the life-giving force of the Nile.

 

"Surely the pollution of the Nile would have taken on religious implications for the average Egyptian. Those who venerated Neith, the eloquent warlike goddess who took a special interest in the lates, the largest fish to be found in the Nile, would have had second thoughts about the power of that goddess. Nathor was supposed to have protected the chromis, a slightly smaller fish. Those Egyptians who depended heavily on fish and on the Nile would indeed have found great frustration in a plague of this nature."137Davis, pp. 94-95.

 

All of it happened right before the very eyes of Pharaoh and all his court entourage. He was a witness to the way in which Yahweh worked. There was no mistaking – it was announced, it was performed exactly as it was announced, and the results happened exactly is predicted. Everyone saw it.

 

Verse 22 tells us that Pharaoh’s magicians did the same thing. We have to wonder where they would have gotten the water to do it, but we do know, or at least it seems apparent from the previous passage where they got the power – from God himself who had allowed them to perform miracles as well, but miracles that would confirm a hard heart in unbelief, despite all the efforts to bring him to repentance.

 

2. Frogs (second plague) 8:1-15

"The god Hapi controlled the alluvial deposits and the waters that made the land fertile and guaranteed the harvest of the coming season. These associations caused the Egyptians to deify the frog and make the theophany of the goddess Heqt a frog. Heqt was the wife of the great god Khnum. She was the symbol of resurrection and the emblem of fertility. It was also believed that Heqt assisted women in childbirth. . . . The frog was one of a number of sacred animals that might not be intentionally killed, and even their involuntary slaughter was often punished with death."140 Davis p. 100

 

"Since the frog or toad was deified as the Egyptian goddess Heqt, who was believed to assist women in childbirth, there may be a touch of irony in the statement that large numbers of frogs would invade the Pharaoh's bedroom and even jump on his bed (v. 3)."143 Youngblood p. 54

 

Pharaoh’s hardness of heart brought forth his public embarrassment at the hands of Moses and frogs! (7:25-8:15)

Once again Yahweh says to Moses took him and Aaron to stretch for this rod and speak the words and bring forth frogs, and he does. And, just as twice before, the magicians, by their enchantments bring about frogs as well.

 

Now The Egyptian magicians were able to bring up frogs, too (v. 7), but they seem to have lacked the ability to make them go away since Pharaoh asked Moses to get rid of them (v. 8).

 

, this is beginning to get a bit embarrassing for Pharaoh. What he needs from his magicians is for them to get rid of the frogs, not to create more of them!

 

And so another irony happens – that Pharaoh, who several chapters previously had sent Moses and Aaron from his presence with a scornful “Get back to work, you lazy good for nothings!” is now forced to ask them to pray for him.

 

Pharaoh’s hypocrisy becomes evident, as it will three more times in this plague narrative, so that as soon as he was given a respite (the Hebrew gives the impression of “a breath”, or “breathing room”), as soon as he was given some room to maneuver, he goes back to his old tricks.

 

“Pharaoh is nothing more than the figure that every single human being can recognize, the religious hypocrite. His worship of God is designed only to get things that he wants and a soon as he has them he gives up the worship of God and defies him once again.’ Mel S.

 

 

3. Gnats (third plague) 8:16-19

The Hebrew word translated "gnats" (kinnim) probably refers not to lice or fleas but to gnats.144 144Kaiser, "Exodus," p. 353, suggested that mosquitoes may be in view.

 

The magicians failed to reproduce this miracle (v. 18). They had to confess that it was of divine origin and not the result of Moses and Aaron's human ability. The "finger of God" (v. 19) is a phrase denoting creative omnipotence in Scripture (31:18; Ps. 8:3; Luke 11:20)

 

"The new element introduced in the account of the third of the mighty acts is the realization by Pharaoh's learned men that God or a god is in the midst of what is happening in Egypt."  Durham p. 109

 

Again,

One of the striking things about this passage is how it is used later on in the Old Testament. Have religious hypocrisy was not a problem merely with the Egyptians, but had found its way even into the heart hearts of the sons of Israel:

Isai 29:13 Therefore the Lord said: "Inasmuch as these people draw near with their mouths And honor Me with their lips, But have removed their hearts far from Me, And their fear toward Me is taught by the commandment of men,

 

E.g. The problem was a problem of the heart, that no mere genealogy or outward sign can change. The miracle needed to happen internally, lest not only faro but even God’s covenant people, while being rational and in control of their wills, act so irrationally is not to see the meaning of the signs before their eyes.

 

Note again: Jesus would quote this passage to the sons of Israel themselves as they were living in Palestine, the land of promise, but once again under the tyranny of another came, Herod in the Roman Empire. Yet the problem this time was not so much that God’s people needed deliverance from the tyranny of an external came. They needed deliverance from the same disease that infected the hearts of those kings, hardness of heart. So Jesus quoted this to his own people and said:

Matt 15:7 (NKJV) "Hypocrites! Well did Isaiah prophesy about you, saying: 8 `These people draw near to Me with their mouth, And honor Me with [their] lips, But their heart is far from Me. 9 And in vain they worship Me, Teaching [as] doctrines the commandments of men.' " 10 When He had called the multitude to [Himself], He said to them, "Hear and understand: 11 "Not what goes into the mouth defiles a man; but what comes out of the mouth, this defiles a man."

 

4. Flies (fourth plague) 8:20-32

These insects were very annoying, even more bothersome than the gnats.

For the first time Pharaoh gave permission for the Israelites to sacrifice to Yahweh (v. 25). He would not allow them to leave Egypt, however. Pharaoh admitted that Yahweh was specifically the God of Israel ("your God"), but he did not admit that he had an obligation to obey Him.156

 

 

5. Murrain (fifth plague) 9:1-7

This plague, apparently some kind of disease like anthrax, was more severe than the preceding ones in that it affected the personal property of the Egyptians for the first time.

 

 

All the other plagues had caused the Egyptians irritation or pain to their bodies, but now God began to reduce their wealth as it impacted their cattle and livestock! 

 

"The religious implications of this plague are most interesting and instructive. A large number of bulls and cows were considered sacred in Egypt. In the central area of the Delta, four provinces chose as their emblems various types of bulls and cows. A necropolis of sacred bulls was discovered near Memphis which place was known for its worship of both Ptah and a sacred Apis bull. The Apis bull was considered the sacred animal of the God Ptah; therefore, the associated worship at the site of Memphis is readily understood. There was at any one time only one sacred Apis bull. As soon as it died another was chosen to take its place, an event that attracted a great deal of attention in the area of Memphis.162   Montet p. 172

 

The only new element in this fifth report is the notice that Pharaoh sent to Goshen tocheck on the predicted exclusion of the Israelites' livestock from the epidemic (v. 7).

 

6. Boils (sixth plague) 9:8-12

The "soot from a kiln" (v. 8) was significant in two respects. First, the soot was black and symbolized the blackness of skin in the disease linking the cause with the effect. Second, the kiln was probably one of the furnaces in which the Israelites baked bricks for Pharaoh as his slaves. These furnaces became a symbol of Israel's slavery (1:14; 5:7-19). God turned the suffering of the Israelites in the furnace of Egypt so that they and what they produced became a source of suffering to the Egyptians.

 

This is the first time we read that God hardened Pharaoh's heart (v. 12). If a person continues to harden his own heart, God will then harden it further in judgment (cf. Rom.1). It is also the first indication that the Egyptian learned men could no longer resist Moses and his God.

"The lesson here is that when one ignores the prompting of the Lord timeand time again (see 7:13, 22; 8:15, 19, 32; 9:7), the Lord will confirm that resistance and make belief impossible."169

 

7. Hail (seventh plague) 9:13-35

God sent the worst hail storm Egypt had ever experienced (vv. 18, 24) and accompanied it with thunder, fire (lightning?), and rain (vv. 23, 34).170

"The recurring thunderclaps . . ., the lightning darting back and forth . . .,  and the severity of the storm . . . all suggest the advent of Yahweh in theophany . . . and thus the Presence of Yahweh in a more dramatic and intense coming than anywhere in the mighty-act sequence to this point."171  Durham p. 128

 

8. Locusts (eighth plague) 10:1-20

 

Locusts were and still are a menace in Egypt as well as in many other countries of the world. The wind drove them from the wetter areas to the whole land of Egypt, excluding Goshen, where they multiplied. They consumed the remaining half of the crops and trees left by the hail.174

 

Among their other gods, the Egyptians prayed to one manifested as a locust that they believed would preserve them from attacks by this devastating insect.175

 

Pharaoh's confession of sin and his request for forgiveness were also most unusual.

". . . the picture of a halting, confused Pharaoh plays well here at the conclusion of the plague narratives. It shows that Moses and Aaron were beginning to get on his nerves."178 Sailhamer, the Petntateuch p. 256ff

 

9. Darkness (ninth plague) 10:21-29

Since the other plagues to this point seem to have been natural phenomena many

commentators interpret this one as such, too. The most common explanation for the darkness that lasted three days (v. 22) and affected the Egyptians but not the Israelites (v. 23) is that it resulted from a dust storm.

 

"In the light of Egyptian theology and practice, this [ninth] plague was very significant. To a large degree it struck at the very heart of Egyptian worship and humbled one of Egypt's greatest gods. The sun god Re was considered one of the great blessings in the land of Egypt. His faithfulness in providing the warmth and light of sun day after day without fail caused them to express great joy over the faithfulness of this deity.

 

Pharaoh still did not submit completely to Yahweh's sovereign demands (v. 24).

Consequently a tenth plague followed.

"For the first time, Yahweh moves to make Pharaoh obstinate during the negotiations. Heretofore he has made Pharaoh stubborn after he has agreed to Moses' demands, after Yahweh's mighty action has ceased and before Moses can leave with the sons of Israel."184  Durham p. 143

 

"It is a sad farewell when God, in the persons of his servants, refuses anymore to see the face of the wicked."

 

The world had begun in total darkness (Gen. 1:2), and now Egypt had returned to that chaotic state.186

 

 

Summary:  In each successive mighty act—the focus is upon Pharaoh’s hardened heart! the motif of Pharoah’s disbelief is intensified by his apparent capitulation under pressure at times, but a capitulation quickly reversed when the pressure is removed.. thus exposing once again the hardened heart!

 

III.             Characteristics of a Hardened Heart

 

  1. Hardened heart is part of God’s plan to glorify himself in human salvation.  The passage starts out by saying that God will harden Pharaoh’s heart.

Ex. 10:1 Then the LORD said to Moses, “Go to Pharaoh; for I have hardened his heart and the heart of his officials, in order that I may show these signs of mine among them,

 

  1. The Hardened heart is a human condition that is most natural to itself!  E.g. it is not that God put upon humanity what humanity did not want—rather that God delivered humanity over to what it did want—

Rom 1.. “delivered them over… “

E.g. whereas we can rightly say that God hardened P. hearts, we can also say that God delivered Pharaoh over to his hardened heart according to Romans…

E.g.

Rom 22 Claiming to be wise, they became fools;  23 and they exchanged the glory of the immortal God for images resembling a mortal human being or birds or four-footed animals or reptiles. :24Therefore God gave them up in the lusts of their hearts

Hard hearts in relation to God are the fundamental human problem. There is no problem that is greater, and Pharaoh is an example of that, as Paul tells us in Rom. 9

Roma 9:16 (NKJV) So then [it] [is] not of him who wills, nor of him who runs, but of God who shows mercy. 17 For the Scripture says to Pharaoh, "For this very purpose I have raised you up, that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth." 18 Therefore He has mercy on whom He wills, and whom He wills He hardens.

 

 

  1. The hardened heart is a human condition that is more about things within than things without—e.g. less about reality/facts outside and more about reality and facts inside…

“What you see depends mainly on what you look for . . . How you see things on the outside of you depends on how things are on the inside of you.”

 

This is the thing that is so alarming… the power of self-deceit is huge—with huge ramifications when related to the things of God.  

 

1Cor.2: 11 For what human being knows what is truly human except the human spirit that is within? So also no one comprehends what is truly God’s except the Spirit of God. 2:14

 Those who are unspiritual do not receive the gifts of God’s Spirit, for they are foolishness to them, and they are unable to understand them because they are spiritually discerned.

 

  1.  At the “heart” of the Hardened heart is pride leading to unbelief!   E.g. assuming ones self as the center of things—assuming ones judgment as the final judgment, assuming ones point of view as the final point of view, assuming that the starting point of ourselves for making judgments is the proper starting point… Thus, it is the things inside of us—most especially, a fundamental disposition of self-autonomy and pride, that results in our having a defiant heart, that is at the heart of a hardened heart…

V.                 Remedy for a Hardened Heart:

 

1Sam. 6:6 Why should you harden your hearts as the Egyptians and Pharaoh hardened their hearts? After he had made fools of them, did they not let the people go, and they departed?

 

First, we most recognize our condition by God’s grace! 

 

E.g. This passage, and its subsequent applications throughout the Old and New Testaments ought to unnerve us—ought even to evoke some degree of fear in all of us..

 

E.g. It is entirely possible that thinking yourself to be wise, you are a fool!     Hear then this exhortation! 

 

Heb. 3:13But exhort one another every day, as long as it is called “today,” so that none of you may be hardened by the deceitfulness of sin.

 

Second, we most then rely more heavily upon God to discern what is true and good! 

2Cor. 2:10 these things God has revealed to us through the Spirit; for the Spirit searches everything, even the depths of God.  11 For what human being knows what is truly human except the human spirit that is within? So also no one comprehends what is truly God’s except the Spirit of God.

 

Therefore, we must put ourselves at God’s mercy and pray!  Pray especially that we would be filled by God’s spirit so as to believe what the Spirit has revealed in his holy word… lest

 

2Cor. 3:14But their minds were hardened. Indeed, to this very day, when they hear the reading of the old covenant, that same veil is still there, since only in Christ is it set aside.

 

Hearts are softened by the work of the Holy spirit acting through the means of grace (word, sacraments, fellowship/pastoral care)—we do well then to not forsake these things week in and week out!  

 

 

Third, ultimately, the only true and lasting remedy against a hardened heart is Christ… notice again.

 

2Cor. 3:14But their minds were hardened. Indeed, to this very day, when they hear the reading of the old covenant, that same veil is still there, since only in Christ is it set aside.

 

We know for instance that Israel was also judged throughout her sordid journey to be afflicted with hardness of heart!  And yet God’s ultimate decision to save them was in spite of their disposition… it was only due to his “good pleasure” due to God’s fundamental disposition of mercy and grace that he did this! 

 

It is no less so today… even as Israel was ultimately passive in their salvation, so are we… it was God then and now that saves his people, our response then is to believe as enabled by the Holy spirit that works within us.

 

Ultimately, we are saved not by getting rid of a hardened heart, at least not completely, for the vestiges of a hardened heart still remain in a Christian as with original sin… rather, we are saved by grace through faith alone, lest anyone could boast of not having a hardened heart! Our salvation is by God and for God’s own Glory!

 

Remedy for a Hardened Heart—Jesus Christ

 

“Through the outstretched arm of God in Jesus Christ – an arm mighty to salvation and to judgment. (Matt. 14:27-33)

 

Luke 1:66 (NKJV) And all those who heard [them] kept [them] in their hearts, saying, "What kind of child will this be?" And the hand of the Lord was with him.

 

 

We enter into salvation, not by pretending we are a soft-harted people, but by confessing that we are in fact a hard hearted people in need of God’s grace through Christ…
