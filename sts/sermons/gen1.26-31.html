Exposition:

Note: how the creation narrative wisps through the creation of all the other creatures and spheres-- but then drastically slows down with the creation of humanity-- this to emphasize human significance as the apex creation over all other creatures...

And in contrast to all other things-- this humanity is significant for its being made in the image of God...

What does it mean that humanity is created in the image of God? To answer this question is nothing short of solving the human quest for identity!

First...

I. Preliminary Observations:

A. The Inclusive "plural" of Hunanity

So God created man in his image, in the image of God's image he created them male and female he created them.

Whatever else is happening here-- it is viewed as by the complimentariness of men and women together.

Whatever the image of God is-- it demands

1. That both man and women enjoy an intrinsic equality of worth and significance as both equally patterned after the image of God...

2. Must involve the real and active reality of gender complimentariness! I.e. the "image of God" is incomplete without a duality of gender...

Any movement that reduces the significance of either sex-- whether male chauvinism or some forms of feminism that would view maleness as inferior-- must therefore be view here as a perversion of our true idenity...

This in its own right is significant-- if you are a women, this will mean finding your identity as a women and not a man, vice versa...

(This ought not to necessarily endorse one or another concept of women-- again, women as defined in the bible... )

3. More over-- there is a social aspect to our identity in that it requires the complimentariness of two persons-- male and female...

I find this particularly interesting in so far as the image is rooted in the plurality of God!

B. The Inclusive "plural: of God

And God said, Let us (elohim is plural thus verb agreement) make man in our image in our likeness

Who is the "Let us"... or what is the significance of the plural in vs. 26

If the "pattern" is an "us"-- then to know who/what the "us" refers will give a big clue as to the image... although not the only clue by any stretch...

Options:

1. the hebrew idiomatic way of expressing deliberation, as in 11:7;

"come, let us go down and there confuse their language, that they may not understand one another's speech."

2. it is the plural of Majesty, royal commands being conveyed in the first person plural, as in Ezra 4:18.

But neither of these options seem to make sense of the fact that the plural is used only in the creation of humanity. Why not consistent throughout?

3. It refers to the plurality within the Godhead so as to include the "spirit" who was present and active at the beginning of creation (vs. 2) and the "creative word" that was so closely identified with God in Hebrew that it was almost synonomous to God-- a kind of elastic language depicting one God yet in his various expressions...

Problem-- the Trinity, while true, was at this time not developed so as to be intended by the author...

(keep in mind that the Bible is a "progressive" revelation of God-- not reaching its maturity until the ultimate revelation of Christ...

Therefore, while nothing prevents this verse from being an illusion to the Trinity as viewed from the vantage point of progressive revelation-- it is hardly intentional by the author in that the Trinity was more or less undeveloped...

And so, "It is now universally admitted that the plural did not mean Trinity... "

4. From Philo onward, Jewish commentators have generally held that the plural is used because god is addressing his heavenly court, i.e. angels (Isa.6:8)

"Let us create man" should therefore be regarded as a divine announcement to the heavenly court, drawing the angelic host's attention to the master stroke of creation, man. As Job 38:4, 7 puts it: "when I laid the foundation of the earth... all the sons of God (angels) shouted for joy."

Luke 2:13 And suddenly there was with the angel a multitude of the heavenly host praising God and saying: 14 "Glory to God in the highest, And on earth peace, goodwill toward men''

Now what is the significance of this-- In "our" image then would include the angels in some since.

This does not lesson the fact that humanity was created in God's image-- but that here-- the point is to somehow include the angels in this "image."

Why?

It seems so as to identify human existance with that of the angels in relation to God-- which would be as servant-kings in extending God's rule over creation.

To invoke the witness of the heavenly court is to place the creation of humanity in the context of a kingdom ceremony! It is cause for great celebration in that in the creation of humanity is the establishment of a king over a sphere-- in this case, over the sphere of the whole earth (which includes heaven) itself!

Think back to Gen.1:1-- God created the "heavens" (which we difined as the spiritual realm) and the earth (defined both as the land and water below and the land/water obove--sky)

Here then, at the apex of the creation of "earth" is God appointing a king over the earth-- whose role is to mediate, together with the angels-- the rule of God over all things!

C. Notice the language associated with image of God in the text-- Kingdom langauge of rule

The image language seems to be in synonomous parrelelism with and let them rule...

The point of human rule was to bring all creation under subjection to God so as to be fruitful and multiply...

And God blessed them and said to them, be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it and govern over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the heavens and over every living thing that moves on earth

E. The Ancient Near Eastern Context which gives some indication of the use of language in that Day...

Both Egyptian and Assyrian texts describe the king as the image of God...

Mesopotamian and Egyptian terminology, the "IMAGE OF GOD" REFERED TO THE KINGS FUNCTION AND BEING.

In other words-- The "Image of god" makes man the mediatorial/representative on earth-- This was a common oriental view of the king.. here the point is that humanity is the highest "king" over all other created kings...

G. Notice the poetic symmatry (last week) -- kingdom spheres with corresponding kingdom rulers which climax with humanity as the greatest of all servant kings but subservient to the great king of kings!

"In Gen. 1, humanity is th apex of a cosmological pyramid... the plants have a very indirect relationship to God, for they spring from the ground, which God commissioned to play a part in creating them. The animals also have their immediate relationsihp to the ground, but they are the recipeints of a special word of blessing assigning frutfulness to them, in order that they may multiply. ON the topmost step of this pyramid stands man, and there is nothing between him and God; indeed the world, which was in fact made for him, has in him alone its most absolute immediacy to God. Also, unlike the rest of creation, he was not created by the word; but in creating him God was actuated by a unique, solemn resolve in the depths of his hearts. And in particular, God took the pattern for this, his last work of Creation, from the heavenly world above. In no other work of Creation is everything referred so very immediately to God himself as in this.

H. Perhaps most significant of all, our interpretation of the image of God as refering toour "vocatonal" role of mediating the rule of God over creation is the point clearly made by an analogy of scripture such as in Psalms 8--

3 When I consider Your heavens, the work of Your fingers, The moon and the stars, which You have ordained, 4 What is man that You are mindful of him, And the son of man that You visit him? 5 For You have made him a little lower than the angels, And You have crowned him with glory and honor. 6 You have made him to have dominion over the works of Your hands; You have put all things under his feet, 7 All sheep and oxen--Even the beasts of the field, 8 The birds of the air, And the fish of the sea That pass through the paths of the seas. 9 O Lord, our Lord, How excellent is Your name in all the earth

"thou has made him a little lower than the angels" (5) -- clearly defining the creation of man as being made in the image of God so as to be related to the angels as well..

and then expounds this status as a royal crowning with glory and dominion over all the earth (5b-8)

Mainpoint: Human identity, as the apex of created order, is rooted in the mandate to mediate the rule of God over every aspect of life so as to make creation as the kingdom of God be "fruitful and to mulitply."

By virtue of his creation in the image of god, man under the Covenant of Creation had the status of ruler of the earth under god, a glory that reflected the dominioin exercised in the heavenly court by god and the angelic hosts. As image of God, man also possessed the ethical glory of a state of simply rightousness with the prospect of moving on to the greater glory of confirmed rightousness.

As subordinate to this "servant-king" role-- humanity is provided with everything necessary to accomplish this...

Rationality

Sexuality

Spirituality

All of which are unique to humanity!

Discussion/Application: What does all this mean?

I. The "first" image of god...

1. It seems now to make sense, that even as modern humanity is preoccupied with discovering its identity-- it is the most insecure and uncertain about its identity in so far as it has lost its connection to God!

In contrast to the modern --

We may say that we never encounter in the Bible an independently existing, abstract, ontological, structureal interest in man. In the Bible,man is indeed analyzed, but in a very special sort of analysis... This man, now in the impossiblity of his being isolated and independant, is the whole man. G.C. Berkouwer, Man: The Image of God

It is certain that man never achieves a clear knowledge of himself unless he has first looked upon God's face and then descends from contemplating him to scrutinize himself." John Calvin

2. When we do construct our identity as from the image of God-- we discover a vocation that makes all other vocations --involving all three spheres of public service, church and family --take on new and glorious significance. To the degree to which we will bring all things under subjection to God is the degree to which we will find our "idenity."

While the "imago dei" should include our rationality, sexuality, and spirituality-- it is ultimately gained or lost to the degree that we assume the vocatoin of being a servant king-- bringing all things under the rule of God!

Often Proposed options for how to localize the "Image of God": The Imago Dei

1. Humanness in the role of "dominion over" (Work) Gen.1:26-27; Ps. 8:6-7)

a. Human person is vise-regent for God

b. Authority derivative from God, using it to re-create God's ex-nilio creation.

2. Humanness in corporeal form (Gen.1:26)

3. Humanness in Sexuality as male and female. (Gen. 1:27)

a. Man and women in relationship is the essence of who we are.

b. Sexuality is the expression of the image of God.

4. Humanness in Rationality (Eph.4:24; Col. 3:10)

a. Human person is essential his rationality as distinct from animal nature.

5. Humaness in Morality. Often spoken of in terms being in the image of God as in Sonship, revealed in Christ and therefore in the Imitation of Christ. (Luke 3:38, Rom. 8:14, 16)

a. The moral faculty of humanness is essential

b. Sin destroys image

c. Redemtion restores image

6. Humaness in Spiritual Soul (Eccl.12:7, Zech. 12:1; Heb.12:9; Num.16:22)

a. Human person's essensce in their spiritual nature

b. Defined as moral and rational in nature

7. Humanness in Community: (Dt. 23:1ff)

a. Essense of humanness in corporate personality (sin of one become guilt of all, Josh. 7:24-26; 2 Sam.14:7; 21:1-14; 2 Kgs. 9:26)

Yet all these things are but aspects of the more central concept of the image dei-- which is to participate in the lordship of God over creation...

All of these capacities are interrelated in the most complicated way, and that it is therefore a mistake to identify the imago Dei with one of them. Stephen Evans

How much greater intrinsic worth can you have but that derived from being made in the image of God-- placed as it where as the apex of all creation to mediate the rule of God--

3. Our significance is gained or lost to the degree that we do our work as unto the Lord-- i.e. to the degree that we bring all things under submission to God so as to be fruitful and multiply for his glory!

Yet- We resist this ruling. We may love to manipulate, but we do not like to rule.

Perhaps then this explains why humanity has in some since both "lost" the image of God while in another sense kept it.

Rom. 1-- lost

21 because, although they knew God, they did not glorify Him as God, nor were thankful, but became futile in their thoughts, and their foolish hearts were darkened. 22 Professing to be wise, they became fools, 23 and changed the glory of the incorruptible God into an image made like corruptible man--and birds and four-footed animals and creeping things.

Yet in the line of Seth-- the covenant line whereby God is redeeming a people for himself-- it is still in tact

That whatever the "image" is-- it was not completely "lost" after the fall--

Gen. 5:1 This is the book of the genealogy of Adam. In the day that God created man, He made him in the likeness of God. 2 He created them male and female, and blessed them and called them Mankind in the day they were created. 3 And Adam lived one hundred and thirty years, and begot a son in his own likeness, after his image, and named him Seth.

I.e. Whereas humanity didn't lose its capacity to exercise dominion over creation in representing God on earth-- such things as rationality, volition etc-- humanity did lose by the fall the moral will that would submit its rule to the ultimate rule of God-- thus, as Rom. 1 suggest-- WE BY NOT SUBMITING OUR DOMINIONS TO THE ULTIMATE DOMINION OF GOD BECAME NOTHING MORE THAN A MERE ANIMAL KING!

An irony in the max-- that as we rule over the earth in all our vocations as though we where the end of that rule-- we have detached ourselves from God making what we do insignificant as compared to what we could have done!

Closer to home-- What this means is that all attempt to find significance will fail apart from the imago dei-- which is to bring all things under subjection to God...

II. The Second Image of God of Redemption

Heb. 2:5 ¶ For He has not put the world to come, of which we speak, in subjection to angels. 6 But one testified in a certain place, saying: "What is man that You are mindful of him, Or the son of man that You take care of him? 7 You have made him a little lower than the angels; You have crowned him with glory and honor, And set him over the works of Your hands. 8 You have put all things in subjection under his feet.'' For in that He put all in subjection under him, He left nothing that is not put under him. But now we do not yet see all things put under him. 9 But we see Jesus, who was made a little lower than the angels, for the suffering of death crowned with glory and honor, that He, by the grace of God, might taste death for everyone.

Therefore,

1. Our status with God is restored together with our being made in his image not by our own ability to satisfy the imago dei, but by Christ's as the second "image of God."

2. Even as we are restored to the status by grace-- we more and more live our significance to the degree that we follow Christ's pattern of bringing all things into subjection under God!
