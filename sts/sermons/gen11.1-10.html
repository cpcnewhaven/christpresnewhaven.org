Genesis 11:1-10

The Futility of Human Enterprise For Reaching God

 

Introduction:
We live in an age of unprecedented technology, communications and entrepreneurial skill. We are surrounded by buildings that seem to defy the power of earth and sky. We access information from the other side of the world faster than we can walk to our neighbors house next door. We develop lasers to surgically remove things that can be seen only through a microscope. We create enough energy to light up Manhattan from particles as small as an atom.

We are tempted to cry out-- my God, is there anything we can't do?

And at this moment we are tempted, compelled by our human perspective, to build our utopia-- only to find it frustrated by miscommunication, computers that still take too long, and of course the weather--

Think of it, with all our technology, we are still bound to the weather-- We are frustrated, we are confused, we are "BABAL"

This is the story of humanism in its most futile and arrogance sense... its the story of the city, all city as representing human enterprise-- all religions without the true God! Let us listen carefully for the theology is in the story...

I. Literary Context:

Before I review the story, look for a few things...

A. Relationship to Chapter 10

Ch. 10 surveys the development of humanity in terms of Noah's three sons. The "parade of nations" then deals with the lineage of

Shem (blessed) as to Abraham,

Ham (cursed) as through the Canaanites and eventually leading to the valley of Shinar and the Babyonians.

Jepheth as leading to the Gentile/greeks who will be invited into the blessed tent of shem...

The Tower of B incident provideds us with a local, representative incident. Probably occurs somewhere in the context of 10:6-10. Ch. 10 gives us the general, outward description; 11:1-9 gives us the inward, religious motivation, along with God's response to what happened epecially to the prophetic curse as through Ham...

B. Sentence Flow--

vs. 1: Introduction

vs. 2-8: 5 Brief scenes

Scenes 1-2: Human Activity-- Humanism

Scene 3: Divine inspection

Scene 4-8: Divine Activity-- Frustration of Humanism

vs. 9Conclusion

3. A Theology of Babylon--

1. Found a plain in Shinar and settled there. May not mean much to us, but would have been very sig. to original readers.

Look at 10:6-12. Nimrod. Babylon Shinar-- in the "parade of nations" is where the descendents of HAM...CUSH...CANAAN.. AND EVENTUALLY BABYLON LIVED!

2. Throughout the vs. 1-11-- there is a play on sounds such as to ryme with Babal... the three consonants showing up almost everywhere...

I.e. While in narrative form, the whole story IS a thelogy of Babal-- which is then a theology of Babylon as this was derived from the word Babal...

 

II. A Story of Babel: a story about about Human pride frustrated

Once upon a time, there was a people, descendents of Ham and Canaan, they were the forefathers of Babylon-- and they shared in a common language--

It has been said that information is power-- and communications technology is the key to unlocking that power-- so here were a people with unlimited ability to communicate and with that perhaps a seemingly unlimited power to accomplish what it wanted. confidence was high, visions of utopia danced about in their minds as they contemplated what they could do with all the learning and power that was to be had by thier one language-- lit-- one lip.... One technology that transcended all barriers to working together... (we could say that the power of the internet, TV, telecommunications all wrapped up in a common culture and language in comparison )

 

Scene 1 &2-- The Power of Human ingenuity...

Scene One: They are traveling east looking for a paradise-- we already know something is amiss here-- since "east" is the ancient way of saying "away from God and his presence-- away from eden, away from the temple, away from paradice as God would make it..

-- east was a sign of their direction and east directed them to grand and prideful schemes-- perhaps they could make a heaven of their own and build their utopia.

And so the story tells of their first locatinga fertile and beautiful valley called Shinar-- a region that would eventually be home to the Babyolonian empire...

Scene Two-- We hear the voices of a great and confident people-- planning their utopian project-- most especially that of building a great tower that will respresent their aassent into heaven. It is as recorded in ancient myth stories common in that day-- the stories of ancient babyolonian culture building the great "esagals" which means "tower with a raised head"--here the point is clear-- the great pride of man as represented by the esagals or "towars with a raised head-- these towers amidst their cities were said to be "as a likeness on earth what was wrought in the heavens." Indeed, the esagals were built by the "lesser gods" (Annunkai), or humanity itsself and as told by one ancient such myth-story:

The annunaki wielded the hoe; for one whole year they moulded its bricks. When the second year arrived they raised the head of Esagil, a replica of the Apsu (God). They built the lofty ziggurat of the Apsu and established it as a dwelling for Anu, Enlil and Ea (the great Gods of Babylon culture)

And so we hear and see the building of the great ziggurats-- characteristic of later Babylonian culture, these towers where supplied with steps so as to enable humanity to accend into the heavens-- representing their ascent to the gods themselves.

Seen as glorious achievement, built by the gods for Marduk, the greatest of them. These structures existed in every Babylonian city. Such a tower was later built by the great Nebachadnezzer of Babyolon and was characteristic of their great human pride...

And so, these people with one lip, believing the power of their communications, engenuity and pride, are found busy about the task of making bricks and motar for the building of a great utopia city...

And why-- that they might make a name for themself, and most signficantly, that they might remain powerful as unto themselves as through their humanistic ingenuity and through their superior communications...

In their own language--

let us make a name for ourselves, lest we be scattered abroad over the face of the whole earth.''

their communications was their unity, and their unity was their power-- which is why to be scattered was paramount to losing their great power and with it their utopian village!

Scene Three: But how high did their towers go-- did they reach unto the heavens, whwere they successful in building a great utopian city after the ascent to the gods... ?

The language of our story makes it clear.

5 But the Lord came down to see the city and the tower which the sons of men had built.

Wonderful touch of humor in v. 5. "The Lord CAME DOWN TO SEE..." Of course God fills heaven and earth; he knows and sees all. But this language is used to make a point: Human effort is so impressive when looked at by itself; but it's nothing compared to the greatness and the majesty of God. Is. 40:22a.

 

God "came down" if but only to look... So beneath him were these towers that God is pictured as having to come down if but only to see them. The language is again clear, for these towers were but the product of the "sons of men."

Brief application on the need to regain our perspective.

It is as scripture describes elswhere about God in comparison to the humanity--

Is. 40:22

It is He who sits above the circle of the earth, And its inhabitants are like grasshoppers, Who stretches out the heavens like a curtain, And spreads them out like a tent to dwell in.

And this proves to be the turning point.

Having been sommons to "come" so as to build a great name for them selves so as not to be scattered according to the power of their one language... God determines to show them just how pitiful, how down right embarrasing and shameful was their attempts at human autonomy so as to build their utopia city...

Scene 4 and 5: Divine Activity

6 And the Lord said, "Indeed the people are one and they all have one language, and this is what they begin to do; now nothing that they propose to do will be withheld from them.

Less a statement about reality and more about their grand illusion.. I.e. Living under the illusion that nothing they propose to do is too great-- (Perhaps also a statement about the abilities of humanity if without the curse)

And so God acts in judgment of their self-centered humanism... And in language that directly parrelels the language describing human activity earlier, God says to his heavenly hosts,

7 "Come, let Us go down and there confuse their language, that they may not understand one another's speech.''

God disrupts their communication-- He takes them "off line" as it where...

The result-- they lose thier communications, they lose their unity, they loose thier power...

8 So the Lord scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth, and they ceased building the city.

There utopian illusions are frustrated-- they are scattered in judgment as grasshoppers accross the face of the earth...

Conclusion: Therefore, its name (this humanist utopian city) shall be called Babel

Thus their utopian city was called Babel. To the Babylonians this meant "gate of the the gods." But from God's perspective the meaning is confusion.

 

Point: Self-Autonomous Humanism Fails! e.g. All human efforts to secure a utopian life through human innovation and pride will be frustrated. By implication, we should look for a better city made by God!

This story is reminiscent of and earlier city in 4.17, the murderer Cain built that city, and named it after his son (let us make a name for ourselves?).

What do we mean by City- it is an attempt to secure temporal security in this fallen, cursed environment. Throughout scripture, God's people are urged to seek final, lasting security in a God- made city.

Heb. 12.8-10 Hebr. 11:8 By faith Abraham f sojourned in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise. 10 For he looked forward to the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

 

Just as the descendents of Ham through Canaan will lead us to Sodom and Gommorah, so it will lead us to Babyolon as derived from the word "BABAL" and "Babal" as it represented human self-autonomy was "confused" and condemned to judgment...

We have in essence a theology of "Babylon" or the humanistic city in narrative form-- which as followed through redemptive history is representative of human self-atonomy and pride as raised against God...

WE SHOULD LOOK MORE CLOSELY THEN AT BABYLON AS THIS THEME IS PROJECTED THROUGH REDEMPTIVE HISTORY

III. The Trajectory from Babel to Babylon in Redemptive History and Signficance

1. So for instance throughout scripture Babylon is seen as the embodiement of human pride and godlessness that evokes the judgment of God...

This is what we have today. Humanism as represented by all human pride and self-autonomy that would forget God-- it comes in all shapes and sizes.

I.e. Isaiah seems to remember the Genesis story here as he desribes the king of babylon in the following manner:

Is. 14:13 For you have said in your heart: "I will ascend into heaven, I will exalt my throne above the stars of God; I will also sit on the mount of the congregation On the farthest sides of the north; 14 I will ascend above the heights of the clouds, I will be like the Most High.' 15 Yet you shall be brought down to Sheol, To the lowest depths of the Pit.

Note how this human pride expressed itself-- through directing our culture mandate not to God's glory but to support human autonomy. I.e. doing the very things God would have us do-- being fruitful, subduing creation, multiplying-- etc-- but doing it so as to boast and secure our humanism less our creaturely worship...

Unity, communication, building things, craft, trade, research, development-- these are very good things after the command to "be fruitful and multiply and subdue the earth..." .except when it's a unified effort to exalt humanity less the God of humanity...

However favorably the B's may have viewed their exploits, it is plain that this is a prideful, humanistic enterprise. A defiant effort to achieve SECURITY, SIGNIFICANCE, and SOCIETY without submission to God and worship for his glory!

They are seeking things which a fine in and of themselves, but they are seeking them by their own efforts, without any reference to God.

As such, this is a profound commentary on the people of this world. May not live in ancient Babylon; may not build towers.

3. The fate of Babylon/humanism is linked to the fate of Sodom and Gommorah as by

b. by saying in both instances that God came down to inspect

c. Comparison made directly in Is.13:19

Is. 13:19 And Babylon, the glory of kingdoms, The beauty of the Chaldeans' pride, Will be as when God overthrew Sodom and Gomorrah.

"Even if Babylong reaches the sky and fortifies her lofty stronghold, I will send destroyers against her," says the Lord. (Jer. 51:53)

And thus we see the judgment of God upon the rebellious enterprises of sinful men. It's not a question of IF, but simply a question of HOW and WHEN.

ULTIMATELY, THE JUDGMENT THOUGH IS YET TO COME!

We remember that heaven is a place being prepared not by us but by God and that our entrance is not by our own power and technology but in the power of God revealed in the death and resurrection of his own son, JC.
