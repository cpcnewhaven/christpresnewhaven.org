Genesis 11:27-12-9 (Read only 12:1-9)

The Gospel Declared Beforehand To Abraham

Introduction:

In reference to our passage in the NT, Paul could say about our passage that it was "the gospel preached beforehand to Abraham"

This is an amazing statement–for it establishes two of the distinctives of the Christian religion in comparison to others.
First, Paul declares that the gospel is preached to Abraham…

I.e. one difference is that in other religion man seeks after God and builds systems so as to reach up to God so to speak (Babel) Whereas in the Judeo/Christian religion, God seeks after man and brings himself down to us.. Ours is a system of grace and theirs is a system of works, ours is a system whereby we receive mercy, theirs is a system whereby they make themselves acceptable to God or some cases make themselves gods.

Paul’s NT gospel is characterized by divine initiative and grace… The story of Abraham will testify to this and serve as the very basis of NT theology.

Second, Paul’s states that this "gospel" was preached beforehand…

Yet another important difference is that the Christian religion is established upon Divine and supernatural prophecy. In reading the sources of other religions, one is immediately struck with the absence of a redemptive history– a story written about God’s intervention and a story that is often fore told even by the supernatural revelation of God through special prophets–

Even as the story of Abraham is about divine initiative and grace, it is prophecy so that Paul could say that the gospel was preached beforehand to Abraham… Our text should be read as prophecy looking for fulfillment–we will want to be careful to recognize its fulfillment in the manner that Paul did under the NT…

Exposition:

I. Turning Point-- Introduction

We have come to a major turning point in the history of redemption–a point from which all the rest of history will turn…

1.Two Histories-- Have left the "Primeval History" of chapters 1-11 (the world that "then was" and into the world that now is according to Peter)

Have entered the "Patriarchal History" of chapters 12-50. So called because of the focus on our "fathers" Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob.

2. 11:27-32, Geneology taking us From Shem to Terah, Abraham’s Father… to Abraham and another level of fulfillment in God’s redemptive plan as initiated back in Gen. 3:15

11:27-32 sets the stage for us.

"This is the generation of Terah." Introduces everything from 11:27 to 25:18. Not about Terah; primarily about Abraham. But it all begins with Terah. Descended from Shem (11:10-26), the line of blessing (9:26).

11-26–Shem to Terah (The father of Abraham); 27-32–Terah’s lineage

Point: opens the Abraham cycle which is not completed until 25:11.

27-32–deals primarily with the familiy background,

vs. 30–with stark brevity, the bitterness of the childless wife is summed up in the "family history" about which much of the story is going to revolve… Digressions of this type in a geneology are significant to notice–the whole of the Abraham cycle is witness to the deperate desire for children in primitive society (Pss 127, 1289) Without children the man had no one to perpetuate his name and th wife enjoyed little prestige and much frustration, for she had not alternative career to motherhood. Furthermore, in old age, childless couples had not children to care for them and after death to carry out the funerary rites regaqrded as vital to the soul’s well being in the afterlife.

This is all the more accentuated in that God’s redemptive promises to Abraham are contingent upon their having a child…

Therefore, the redemptive plot is firmly established even here by this geneologically Nuance…

12:1-9 focuses on the divine promises.

3. Specifically, focus we'll be upon seed of A, which is to become a great nation.

Genesis the book of beginnings. One of the major beginnings is the beginning of Israel.

All begins with God's initial promise and call to Abram. Called by many the most important section in the Book of Genesis. Why? Contains, in a nutshell, God's plan for all of history.

The fourfold promise of land, descendants, covenant and blessing to the nations is gradually fulfilled in Genesis and succeeding books of the Pentateuch.

Fulfillment:

Did Abram receive the things promised to him? Yes and no

Typologically, the promises were fulfilled during his lifetime and through the Sinai period in that God established a political nation Israel that existed in the geographical region of Canaan.

But many NT texts expressly say that Abram did not receive what was promised:

Hebr. 11:11 By faith Sarah herself received power to conceive, even when she was past the age, since she considered him faithful who had promised. 12 Therefore from one man, and him as good as dead, were born descendants as many as the stars of heaven and as the innumerable grains of sand by the seashore. 13 These all died in faith, not having received what was promised, but having seen it and greeted it from afar, and having acknowledged that they were strangers and exiles on the earth.

Hebr. 11:39 And all these, though well attested by their faith, did not receive what was promised, 40 since God had foreseen something better for us, that apart from us they should not be made perfect.

So where do we look for the real fulfillment of God’s promise to Abraham?

Where do we expect to be blessed if we bless it and cursed if we curse it?

Where is "nation" that will carry us to the promised land?

Some still look for a political nation and geographical place.

Two basic forms:

a. Some will reconstruct this passage to imply that any nation today who are faithful to God receive the blessings…

I.e. they look to a political nation in a geographical place to be the new Israel of God so to speak…

b. Others still look modern Israel and predict its restoration during the literal milliniem reign of God on earth before the return of Christ which will eventually culminate in a great war in the valley of Armeggedon.

If you do hold to this view, that you should always remain loyal to the nation of Israel and remember that God has a special plan for her.. etc.

But we reject these views–believing they don’t do justice to God’s word…

We notice the following in the NT…

  1. That when Paul applies this passage, he applies it to the church–that institution that is neither political or geographcial, but rather follows after Abraham by faith.

Gal. 3:7 so, you see, those who believe are the descendants of Abraham.

Gal. 3:8 And the scripture, foreseeing that God would justify the Gentiles by faith, declared the gospel beforehand to Abraham, saying, "All the Gentiles shall be blessed in you."

Gal. 3:9 For this reason, those who believe are blessed with Abraham who believed.

2. Peter also applies this passage so as to apply to those of all nations who put their trust in Christ as being the descendents of Abraham…

Acts 3:25 You are the descendants of the prophets and of the covenant that God gave to your ancestors, saying to Abraham, ‘And in your descendants all the families of the earth shall be blessed.’

    1. Moreover, Peter specially refers to the church as the fulfillment of the "nation" established by God through Abraham.

1Pet. 2:9 But you are a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, His own special people, that you may proclaim the praises of Him who called you out of darkness into His marvelous light;

What kind of home and land will we be called to leave?

It is different for everyone…

Perhaps you are comfortable in that lifestyle that gives you the perception of safety. God teaches that the desire to be rich is the root of all sorts of evil…

Perhaps it is the comfort of trusting in your own decisions based upon selfish desires. God says that those who seek to gain their own lives will lose it..

Perhaps it is the home of legalistic religion of trusting in your own human rules for gaining acceptance with God–Chist refered to these as whitewashed tombs…
