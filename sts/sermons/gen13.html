Genesis 13

By Faith and Not By Sight

 

In this episode, Moses sets up a contrast between two believers, Lot and Abram. While both are counted within the covenant line-- one walks by sight and the other by faith-- the ominous details then that are inserted throughout are carefully woven so as to make a point about which way of walking is the way of blessing.

 

A. Setting up the Relevant Details vs. 1-4

How a person tells a story says more than what happened, it tells you what the person telling the story hopes for his audience to take from the story.

As we have seen over and over. Moses's interest in telling us about an episode in redemptive history is not merely to give us some details in history-- but to instruct his readers in theology within the context of the covenant.

It is no less the case here--

Moses in Chapt 11 mentioned that Lot was with Abram counted among the geneology of Shem and in Chapt 12 it is mentioned that Lot was Abram's nephew.

Yet the first half of Chapt 12 focus on God's promise to Abram who would be the father of a great people after the seed of promise that was first given to Adam and Eve after the fall.

The second half of Chapt. 12 presented the first major threat to that problem, how Abram tried to resolve the problem, how it backfired and how God intervened so as to protect his promise of a people as through the seed of Abram and Sarah. The story ends with the exodus of Abram and Sarah- told in terms very reminiscent to the way a future Exodus would be told under some very similar circumstance-- Surely God was good to his promise, then and later-- to protect for himself a promised, covenant people-- whose salvation rested not on themselves but upon the divine interventions and power of God!

Yet very significantly for us today-- we will note that there was no mention of Lot in the Egypt episode. Now all of a sudden the story picks up and it immediately draws our attention to both Abram and Lot-- Sarah now is in the shadows.

Chapt 13, we have the exodus of Abram and Sarah from Egypt

¶ So Abram went up from Egypt, he and his wife, and all that he had, and Lot with him, into the Negeb.

Evidently, Lot was with him in Egypt as part of his household, but only now does Moses want us to make note of this-- and for what reason? This will prove to be the key toward unlocking Mose's intent in this passage for us even today as it was for the people in his day.

2. Notice carefully how the Moses describes Abram's condition now!

a. Whatever else happened in Egypt, however questionable were some of Abram's decisions, God had made good on his promise to curse those who cursed Abram and bless those who bless Abram- our text then describes Abram as coming from Egypt a wealthy and prosperous person-- which in the OC context would have been indicative of God's favor.

2 And Abram was very great with livestock, silver and gold)

"rich"-- this is the same word that can also mean "severe" and was used also to describe the famine that led Abram to Egypt-- perhaps to make the contrast between Abram's situation before and now-- I.e. that God even as by the means of his precarious stay in Egypt, had provided Abram with the kind of resourse that would make him the father of a great nation.

Moreover--

a. While in Egypt, even in the decision to go to Egypt-- there had been no special revelation from God. While in Egypt, there was no mention of building and worshipping at an alter, no special word from God, only God's intervention in spite of some very questionable decisions of the part of Abram

Yet in contrast, our text very carefully tells of Abram's return home-- perhaps more ways than one-- for we see the author going out of his way to eccentuate the emphasis upon Abram's "returning" not only back to a covenant geography using langauge identical to that used earlier in Chapter 12.

3. And he walked (went) to journey from Negeb even to Bethel unitl (he reached) the place his tent was from the beginning between Bethel and Ai--

But the author wants us also wants us to see Abram returning to a spiritual context and again said so as to emphasis the idea of return.

4 to the place where he had made an altar at the first and there Abram called upon the name of the Lord.

So that whatever else we might gain from this piece of history-- Abram's journey from Egypt represented a journey under God's power back into a context of spiritual blessing.

WE are now set up for another problem arises, and this time, Abram is seen to be walking by faith as accentuated by the contrast with Lot-- again, told in a way that wants carefully to draw a spiritual conclusion that would be relevant to Moses's day as it is to our day.

D. Reaffirmation of God's Promises through Abram vs. 14-17ff

And notice then how God reassures Abram again-- and even in more clearly language than before-- the promises that had been initially made in Chapt. 12.

1. A survey-- a kind of "walk" the perimeter-- and see as through the eyes of faith what I will do!

¶ The LORD said to Abram, after Lot had separated from him, "Raise your eyes now, and look from the place where you are, northward and southward and eastward and westward; 15 for all the land that you see I will give to you and to your offspring forever.

In contrast to Lot's land being the future sight of God's wrath and destruction-- Abram's land will be the location of God's covenant people well into the future.

Note: the term "forever" -- the english is fine, but it abscures the various ways tha the same language can be qualifed. As noted in every lexicon of the Hebrew language..

So for instance.

1. At least 30 times the construction refers to a long period in the past, not the future (see TWOT)

2. Many times, the contruction can mean "into the future" as contrasted with the present

3. It seems that others times, it can mean "into the perfect future" or even "age" (as this Hebrew word is trans into the Grk as "eion" as this two has a wide range of meaning including "age" which then can cease with the beginning of a new age.

This observation is important in that there are many times then that this same exact construction is applied to things that clearly come to an end--

Places where "forever" were terminated by death of person…

Deut. 15:17

then you shall take an awl and thrust it through his earlobe into the door, and he shall be your slave forever

There are other times when it is applied to things that will change significantly in its form even as a more deep and spiritual meaning associated with it will continue.

So for instance.

-- applied to the Davidic dynasty that will continue forever-- note how this is both true and false in the NT. (Pss 89:29)

--Zion (Jersulam) is God's dwelling place forever(Ps48:14) but again notice how literal zion sees the Lord leaving in a chariot according to Ezekiel as then followed by the literal destructino of the temple, whereas heavenly zion is the real and continued presence of God which will ultimetly be found in heaven.

-- the "law" is eternal-- yet in Rom. 7 Paul tells us that it has ceased-- again, it continues as accomplished by Christ and used to judge the living and the dead-- but it no longer is associated with land and at least according to Paul, it no longer is our covenant rule if in fact, as Paul illustrates, we have died to our old self and husband (law) and now live as a new person as married to a new husband (Christ)

-- Again, according to Lev 24:3 and Num. 18:19-- the priesthood is forever. Yet again, the NT in Heb.7:12ff says that the priesthood has ceased- at least in its previous form since now the priesthood is continued only in the perfect and sufficient work of Christ!

-- One more illustration: it is said in the OT that the sabbath is forever-- this according to Lev. 16:29, Ex.31:17, included laws not only regarding the 7th day but also the 7th month and the various laws of jubilee. Yet i

Lev. 16:29

¶ This shall be a statute to you forever: In the seventh month, on the tenth day of the month, you shall deny yourselves, and shall do no work, neither the citizen nor the alien who resides among you.

NT-- while there remains an appointed time to worship God under the NC terms of worship-- it is not longer on the 7th but rather on the 1st day of the week.

Therefore, as with Calvin-- we should understand here that what God is promising is in fact covenantally conditioned!
