3.vs. 7ff The Ceremony: Covenant Ritual-- so as to ratify/confirm God's promise

I.e. How would Abram be credited as being righteous so as to benefit from God's promises? The answer is ceremoniously proclaimed!

 

vs. 7-21 describes an ancient covenant making ritual-- it was relatively typical of covenant makings of any type-- that once the parties agreed to the terms of the contract-- a ceremonious ritual would seal the contract with an oath! (usually, an oath that would invoke a curse upon themselves if they failed the oath)

Illus. Today-- if a man and women express their dispositions toward one another so as to committ themselves to marriage-- yet the thing is not settled until that time that oaths are made in the context of the marriage ritual/ceremony! (even with the statement-- and what god has joined together, let no man "break" apart)

So what's going on here--historical surveys, slaughtered animals? What does the ceremony itself mean?

Help from other treaties from ANE. Turns out that this was a somewhat standard ritual for ratifying a covenant. And the significance was this: those who are making the covenant were subjecting themselves to the curses of the covenant if they didn't do their part..

The curse ordeal

Keil-Delitzsch, "The proceeding corresponded rather to the custom, prevalent in many ancient nations, of slaughtering animals when concluding a covenant, and after dividing them into pieces, of laying the pieces opposite to one another, that the persons making the covenant might pass between them." p. Æ214.

Kline, NBC, 95, "The oath ritual for which Abram prepared was customary in treaty ratifications.The curse conditionally invoked in the oath was symbolized by this slaying and sundering of animals, signifying 'so may it be done to him who breaks this covenant.'

Illustration:

1. Bar-ga'ayah with Mati'el, 8th century B.C.

"And just as this calf is cut to pieces, so may Mati'el be cut to pieces and his nobles be cut to pieces."

2. Treat of Esarhaddon, 7th Century

"May Ninurta, chief of the gods, feed your flesh to the eagle and jackal."

3. Syrian text from 18th/17th century

"Abba-AN is bound to Yarimlim by oath and he has cut the throat of a sheep."

4. Babylonian covenant

"This head is not the head of the goat...it is the head of Mati'ilu...If Mati'ilu breaks this oath, as the head of this goat is cut off...so shall the head of Mati'ilu be cut off."

5. Jer. 34:18-20 confirms that this was standard practice.

Ie. An Oath by Ritual: "You want to know my level of commitment? You want to know how trustworty I am? I swear to you, may the fate of these animals befall ME, may I be put to death, may the curse and humiliation of being food for the birds be my lot, if I fail to deliver. It was a self-maldictory oath. "Cross my heart and hope to die

This explains then vs. 10-- a a desicription of "cutting" the slaughtered animals and then

Vs. 18-- the ceremony being described as literally "cutting a covenant"

Where's Abram?

The "payments" for this mortgage of like are assumed by me such that you Abram-- have no payment to make and consequently you, Abram, have not curse to invoke upon yourself for not making the payments!

You need absolutely perfect righteousness to be made acceptable to God. And you don't have it. And you can't get it on your own.

b.The nature of the transaction :

"credited" (imputed) is different than say "infused" rightousness…

But not with making us righteous by actually changing how we live our lives. But by accounting us as righteous, by forgiving our sins, by accepting us not as pretty good, but as absolutely perfect in his sight.

Rom. 4:6-8, 11.

This doesn't mean that God isn't also "changing us" as by regeneration, etc-- but that the doctrine of "Justrification by faith" is that God credites us with rightousness as by the gurantee of Christ so as to have the benefits of being shielded by God.
