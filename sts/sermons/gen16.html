Introduction:

One of the more dangerous patterns of sin is to seek noble ends by ignoble means?

Seeking to make friends, we will say and do things that are not right.

Seeking to do whatever work you do in excellence, we rob the appointed time from our family and church.

Seeking to promote a righteous cause, we treat people harshly or disrespectfully as our enemies, or perhaps usurp God appointed authorities by taking things into own hands by gossiping, slandering and circumventing the "process" God has given to us…

We call it "taking things into our own hands"…

Well this sort of thing is established as a pattern in the Bible-- where perhaps noble ends are sought for by ignoble means-- it began in the garden of eden-- wanting to find "life" but determining to do it our way vs. God's way-- it is the pattern of self-reliance rather than God-reliance-- and its this very pattern that is again portrayed by our passage today.

A. A perfectly normal solution-- her plan was unobjectionable from the custom and law of the ancient near east… (law number 146 in Hammurabi's code)

ANE

It was a serious matter for a man to be without child since the whole political economy was "family states"-- therefore-- throughout the ANE polygamy was resorted to as a means of obviating childlessness-- even as wealthier wives resorted to surrogate motherhood whereby they allowed their husbands to "go in" to their maids but the child would be his even as she was under her authority.. I.e. a solution that kept the husband in check so to speak even as the maid remained under her control… rather than her husband taking a second wife.

Evidently here-- this ancient and social custom resulted in Hagar resenting the relationship. Her conception with Abram resulted in her resenting Sarah's authority and perhaps wishing herself to be a "wife" to Abram less a "surrogate mother"

Note then who "wife" is used several times, but not from Sarah

Therefore, given the social mores of the ANE, Sarai's suggestion was a perfectly proper and respectable course of action -- although it was evidently not God's course of action (a great example of when social mores may in fact clash with divine mores)

I.e. . What Abram and Sarai did and did not do!

1. They committed no sin against known marital laws according to the custom of that day; the laws of the ANE clearly permitted the wife supplying a slave to her husband as a concubine.

2. They did committed a sin against the covenant. God promised to give them numerous descendents, and when he was not quick in fulfilling the promise, they attempted to have descendents by natural methods.

3. They pursued a noble goal by ignoble means.

Whatever responsibilities Abram and Sarai have, they have no responsibility to produce by human means a single promised heir. That is God's business

I.e. In 15, a covenant is formally ratified, in such a way as to make clear that God alone is obliged to fulfil the covenant, and to bear its curse. This, naturally, raises the question: Are there any duties of any sort which fall to Abram? 16 and 17 answer this question, negatively and positively. 16 Tells us what is not his duty; 17 tells us what is his duty

 

Application-- Therefore, we should be careful not to slip into the sin pattern-- of seeking noble ends by ignoble means-- and especially as this applies to the promised salvation

First, consider how the pattern may play itself out in everyday life…

1. Personal life:

A great number of times-- the sin pattern of taking things into our own hands reveals itself when we deny a Biblical process for doing a Biblical thing.

Some examples if but to help us see the way the pattern looks in our lives…

a. So for instance--

God says especially to fathers "raise your children in the fear and admonition of the Lord!

Oh but we want to provide for our family-- but we fail to trust God and his provision-- we want more success-- and so we take time away from other moral oblilgations say in raising our children by getting involved in their lives so as to apply spiritual disciplines to it, etc-- why? because God has not provided for us with what we need? NO,, rather because he has not provided the amount of success that we want!

Like in Abram's day-- it is customary for fathers to not be involved in their childrens discipleship

Like in Abram's day-- we want our success quicker-- and perhaps even more of it…

But are we doing really! we are taking things-- our time--into our own hands rather than letting God provide when and what he wanted with a principled and right allotment of time given to our jobs…

 

b. God says-- submit to those in the church who rule over you…

Oh but We care for the church-- we think we see something that could be done better. And so we take things into our own hands-- we go off on our own and start something, or we begin to talk around the leadership--

Why? Is it perhaps that God's means of leading could pose a threat to your plan? Underneath this of course is the social broth that we all swim in in modernity-- that context which first second guesses and is sceptical about authority and institutions in general…

Like in Abrams day-- it is customary to circumvent and distrust authority

c. God says-- Be in the world but not of the world…

Oh but we want to have friends at school or in the neighborhood-- perhaps even to share Christ with them-- so we laugh at jokes that are nasty or disrespectful, we put others down to make ourselves look better, we participate in things that we know is wrong-- but we do this because we want friends

Again, we trust in our own ignoble ways to bring about a noble end

The pattern is everywhere-- it leads to almost every sin of the bible-- gossip, going into unwise debt, abusive speech, circumvention of authority, etc. etc.

It all comes down to a way of life, a pattern-- of taking things into our own hands rather than being content with God's provision, God's timing and God's means!

We should learn to think before we plan and act and speak-- is this God's means to God's ends! Not just-- is it for God's ends!

2. What about in the church-- the same thing is very often done…

We want more people to believe in Christ--

What does the Bible teach is the means to this end-- ministry of word, sacraments and prayer….

Paul says it this way-- I am not ashamed of the gospel for it is the power of God for salvation to everyone who believes…

And who will believes-- those whom God has choosen so as to enlighten their minds, etc.

But what will we do?

The church will rely on all sorts of things other than the means provided for us in scripture--

Perhaps most subtle-- we deny basic Biblical values and principles in order to "appeal" to the world…

So for instance-- taking each of the 3 means of grace,

a. Corporate kiononia worship can be replaced with entertainment-- where instead of being led to Christ table-- we are being led to clap and exhalt human ingenuity!

Why-- for the noble ends of bringing more people into the church---

b. Faithful preaching of a text driven, redemptive-historicalliy revealed gospel is replaced with efficient, topically driven devotionals… Or we take things into our own hands if we believe God has not raised up enough competent preachers of the word by the proliferatoin of self-appointed preachers!

c. Programs replace prayer-- we get busier when we should pray more-- it isn't that we should have no methods for discipleship and evangelism-- its when those methods become more important than prayer itself…

So for instance-- we are concern to reach our youth-- and think that one or another "program" or "youth ministry" will do it…

I wonder how the covenant children in Paul's day were led to Christ-- was it some exciting youth group with cool music-- probably not--

It was through parents raising their children in the context of word, sacraments and prayer! Instructing them in the doctrines of grace..

If you think about it-- given what we often think about what makes Christians today, it’s a wonder the church even survived historically--

Whre was the efficient sermon ditti when the church grew under Paul's "well into the night" sermons.

How did the gospel get passed down throughout the generation without a youth group

In other words:

From Medieval Romanism-Early 19th century American Revivals-t Pragmatism in the church today-- it all comes down to not trusting in the noble means of the faithful preaching of the gospel with sacraments and prayer in order that people might be saved from their sins and transferred from the kingdom of darkness into the kingdom of light….
