Introduction

What must we "do" to be saved? The million dollar question…

What is faith and how does it relate to obedience?

Is there faith apart from obedience?

What, if any, responsibility do we have in the covenant of salvation?

The question makes us uneasy for sure-- and it begs an analysis regarding God's divine sovereignty and our salvation-- but there is no doubt that the Bible at times will indicate something of a human part.

James 2:18

¶ But someone will say, "You have faith and I have works." Show me your faith apart from your works, and I by my works will show you my faith. 19 You believe that God is one; you do well. What both of these passage share in common is

a. an acknowledgement that authentic Christianity will show itself by obedience

I. The Covenant Name of God-- El- Shaddai

According to Exod.6:3-- this was the name by which God was known to the patriarchs…

LXX-- translates it "almighty"

Early Jewish etymologizing translated it "he who is sufficient" (i.e. there is no other power that is needed but God-- he is more powerful even than nature)

However it is translated-- it describes the God who "so constrains nature that it does His will and so subdues it that it bows to and subserves grace" Keil Delitzsh 2:32)

I.e. "God the miracle worker"

In this context-- this clearly anticipates God's renewed promise to so constrain nature in its reproductive capacities that miraculously-- a woman and man well over the age of childbearing will have a child-- a covenant child through whom God's redemptive plan will be accomplished.

I.e. what does this tell us-- that whatever else we are about to discover-- it will not diminish the fact that God and God alone is the source and power unto salvation!

El-Shaddai-- then means salvation that is accomplished by God's miraculous power and this power alone!

If we have seen nothing else so far-- it has been just this-- that apart from divine intervention in every successive generation-- the promised salvation would have been extinct if not for God's intervention!

And this was God's point exactly when the covenant was guaranteed by God alone in Chapter 15 as indicated by only the flaming torch passing through the slain animals…

II. The Condition-- An Uneasy phrase

Walk in my presence and be blameless, such that I may give my covenant between me and you and multiply you exceedingly.

Here are the facts:

A. The Grammar-- So that

-- waw + 1 sg. Cohortative of the word "nathan" (to give) followed by the impv. = means that there is an intended consequence being expressed…

I.e. do this… and as a consequence this will happen

The relationship between the command to "walk before me and be perfect" and vs. 2, "and I will make my covenant between me and you" is that the latter is the consequence/result of the former-- and all this makes us uneasy… as protestants for sure!

I.e. If we will treat honestly with this passage, we...

1. Can't overturn Chapt 15-- God as the sole guarantor of the covenant

2. Can't negate the clear language here-- result clause…

I.e. be blameless "so that"( result) I might give to you…

Wenham-- "In structure and content this opening sentence seems to echo 12:1-2… "Go… be a blessing… that I may bless…"

So how do we understand this?

B. The verbs: walk before me and be blameless

Both are imperatives,

Walk before me

Walk-- often used to describe a persons disposition in living… i.e. not merely "stepping with feet forward" but how we mentally and spiritually step through life-- here

Before me-- a common way to describe "presence"

I.e. live in my presence!

We have seen this language before as indicating a life under the care and protection of God

So that Cain was banished from the "presence of God" and this meant that he was excommunicated from the special and saving activity of God!

But how is Abraham to walk?

Blameless -- is this a good translation?

blameless (adj) ( be perfect) to complete or perfect something-- but what exactly?

It is to perfect the law, or perfect/complete faith, complete law or complete faith…. Or does it refer to moral innocence i.e. blamelessness morally.

Notice again that here the word is an adj.-- which then begs an object complete with respect to ? –

Clue #1: How used to describe others.

I.e. Therefore, what does it mean that Abram is to walk completely/perfectly with respect to God---

a. probably what it meant with respect to Enoch and Noah!

That these men lived wholly dependant upon God even as this resulted in a life characterized by obedience.

So Abraham, while not perfect, was a man characterized by faith even as he obeyed God when he left Ur for the promised land…

Key-- walking with dependence and trust upon God which then shows itself through significant acts of obedience.

Kline:

Abraham's call, the initial revelation of the covenant, confronted him as a demand, an imperative to which the promises were attached. It was only as Abraham responded in obedience to the command of God's call that he could enter into the hope offered in the promises. This is not to say that the promised heavenly inheritance was to be secured by the works principle, that is, on the ground of obedience. It does mean that the divine promises of the covenant never existed apart from human obligations.

God's call to Abraham may be likened to Jesus' call to the discipleship, the familiar summons to "follow me" (Mt.8:22, 9:9, 19:21, etc)

I.e. Like In Chapter 12, faith (dependence upon God trusting in his promises and trustworthiness) resulted in Abraham leaving Ur for an unknown place.. God secured the place, but Abraham wouldn't have received the promise without his response of obedience.. (now we can say that God gave Abraham his faith---) but this doesn't deny that faith will show itself by steps of obedience such that if one lacks steps of obedience then one lacks faith itself!

Kline:

A two-level structure of the meaning and realization of the promises of the Abrahamic Covenant… First, there was the level of the typological kingdom of Israel under the Mosaic covenant. Temporal level… of a temporal nation and their occupation of a temporal land in Canaan. But the promises also possessed a second, perpetual level of meaning which are the spiritual aspects realized even in the patriarchal period as well as in the old covenant era (here as a substructure to the simultaneous typological level fulfillment) and which under the new covenant comes to fulfillment in every respect in the antitypical, eternal kingdom of righteousness and peace in the Spirit.

Viewing the promises at this permanent and true (not merely symbolic) level of meaning, Paul argues that not all the natural descendants of Abraham contistuting the Israelite people were the intended seed of promise (Rom.9:6-8)
