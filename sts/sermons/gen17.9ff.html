(Explain)

I.e. This passage will not make sense through the lens of modern individualism-- where religion is mostly a private thing and salvation only about a personal conversion-- rather this is about God building a trans-generational community-- which is his church-- here under the OC institution of circumcision as the rite of entrance!

So what is the meaning and significance of circumcision and how does this effect us under the NC?

5 important aspects revealed in this passage:

I. A Faith Rite:

An expression of dependence upon God for the execution of his promise throughout the generations…

God said to Abraham, "As for you, you shall keep my covenant, you and your offspring after you throughout their generations.

1. Observe, keep, watch-- "guard"

I.e. last week-- total dependence upon God will result in a life characterized by obedience-- here that obedience is expressed in "keeping" the covenant as through the command to circumcise all males…

Divine Commentary from Romans:

Rom. 4:9

We say, "Faith was reckoned to Abraham as righteousness." 10 How then was it reckoned to him? Was it before or after he had been circumcised? It was not after, but before he was circumcised. 11 He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith while he was still uncircumcised. The purpose was to make him the ancestor of all who believe without being circumcised and who thus have righteousness reckoned to them, 12 and likewise the ancestor of the circumcised who are not only circumcised but who also follow the example of the faith that our ancestor Abraham had before he was circumcised.

This was NOT a work for salvation, it was an expression of faith on the part of the covenant community with respect to God's promise for the generations after them…

Calvin: For the bare promise of God was effectual to their salvation. He did not so attest this salvation by external signs as to restrict his own effectual working to those signs… circumcision was added as a confirmation it neverthe less did not deprive the word of its force and efficacy…

2. What about Abraham's "laugh": Does this confirm with Paul's commentary?

Fell on his face: … laughed

Skepticism or astonishment?

Calvin: This was in token not only of his reverence but also of his faith. For Abraham not only adores God, but in giving him thanks, testifies that he receives and embraces what was promised concerning a son. But as is commonly wont to happen in things which are least expected partly exulting with joy and partly being carried beyond himself in admiration, he breaks forth into laughter. For I do not assent to the opinion of those who suppose that his laughter flowed solely from joy; but I rather think that Abraham was as one astonished; which his next interrogation also confirms, "Shall a child be born to him that is an hundred years old." … I answer that this admiration which did not obstruct the course of God's power was not contrary to faith nay the strength of faith shone the more brightly in having surmounted an obstacle so arduous. And therefore he is not reprehended for laughing as Sarah is in the next chapter.

II. A Sacramental Rite:

10 This is my covenant, which you shall keep, between me and you and your offspring after you: Every male among you shall be circumcised.

Notice-- that "This" refers to circumcision such God equates circumcision in some sense to the promise of God itself to Abraham.

Now remember what was that promise-- to build a great community out from Abraham… a community that would ultimately attract the nations to God for salvation from sin!

This gets at 1 of the aspects mentioned by Paul which make circumcision a "sacrament" of the OT church!

11 He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith while he was still uncircumcised.

Seal-- So that in a visible sense-- the covenant was being accomplished when a child or person was brought into the covenant community of Abraham by circumcision. God's oath as signified through the rite was here being transacted as well through the rite-- it served as a means of grace in so far as:

1: It visually testified to God's saving promise (see below)

2: it visually transacted and accomplished God's saving grace in so far as a community was formed when so ever a person was circumcised into that community where the parents and children would grow up under the influence of God's covenant word, etc.

The rite was itself effecting the covenant in a very profound way such that the rite here is equated with the covenant-- the rite transacted the promise resulting in the formation of a real and visible community of faith!

Calvin:

For circumcision was as a solemn memorial of that adoption by which the family of Abraham had been elected to be the peculiar people… since circumcision is called by Moses the covenant of God, we thence infer that the promise of grace was included in it… For had it been only a mark or token of external profession among men, the name of covenant would be by no means suitable for a covenant is not otherwise confirmed than as faith answers to it. And it is common to all sacraments to have the word of God annexed to them, by which he testifies that he is propitious to us and calls us to the hope of salvation, yea a sacrament is nothing else than a visible word (promise) or sculpture and image of that grace of God which the word more fully illustrates. If, then, there is a mutual relation between the word and faith; it follows that the proposed end and use of sacraments is to help promote and confirm faith. But they who deny that sacraments are supports to faith, or that they aid the word in strengthening faith, must of necessity expunge the name of covenant…

Therefore-- Between me and you-- the name of covenant is transferred to circumcision which is so conjoined with the word, that it could not be separated from it.

III. And Entrance (initiation) Rite:

How would God build this community of faith?

Clearly two ways:

1. Conversions-- people from the nations who believed upon God's promises given to Abraham-- who would then be circumcised.

2. Births of children into the families already in the covenant community…

So that even though the rite was a "faith rite" as shown already as it directed the community to God's saving promise-- it was given to all who where under the jurisdiction of the community!

So, notice the instructions:

. 12 Throughout your generations every male among you shall be circumcised when he is eight days old, including the slave born in your house and the one bought with your money from any foreigner who is not of your offspring

1. those born-- "and your descendants"--God gives to Abraham a covenant which includes yet unborn descendants…

2. those bought and/or under the jurisdiction of the covenant families in the covenant community

Clearly, circumcision did not necessarily save a person from their sin-- this Paul states emphatically.

11 The purpose was to make him the ancestor of all who believe without being circumcised and who thus have righteousness reckoned to them, 12 and likewise the ancestor of the circumcised who are not only circumcised but who also follow the example of the faith that our ancestor Abraham had before he was circumcised.

Nor was circumcision-- even while it represented justification by faith-- say anything necessarily about the individual faith of the person being circumcised-- What it DID not is mark those who where members of a saving community in so far as it functioned to enter a person into that community.

(note-- as for the women, they were included, not excluded, in so far as the representative heads of homes where all circumcised)

And an entrance rite-- this explains then vs. 14 of Gen 17-

14 Any uncircumcised male who is not circumcised in the flesh of his foreskin shall be cut off from his people; he has broken my covenant."

I.e. For a parent to deny their children circumcision or for a person converting to Faith denied circumcision was to deny themselves membership in the covenant community-- they were in effect "cut off"

IV. A "cutting" Rite: A judgment rite

Notice carefully the word choice--

Even as circumcision was a "cutting ceremony" so then a person who denied circumcision to themselves or their children were then "cut off" from the community…

This is important since this is not only descriptive of the function of circumcision, but also describes the sign of circumcision itself..

I.e. What exactly was signified by Baptism?

It is described in Gen. As a sign:

Vs. 11: and it shall be a sign of the covenant between me and you…

So too in Rom 4:

. 11 He received the sign of circumcision as a seal of the righteousness that he had by faith while he was still uncircumcised.

So what does this image conjure up?

As a "sign" then circumcision signified a great ordeal of judgment.

You may remember earlier how a "covenant" was said to be cut (kind of like the way we now say "cut a check" to depict the transaction being made through the check..

Why-- because of the ancient "cutting rite" of the slain animals that signified the church of the covenant

Gen. 15 and the cutting ceremony whereby God walked through the covenant curse himself so as to guarantee its success!

V. A Perpetual Rite:

12 A covenant that would last " throughout their generations."

13 Both the slave born in your house and the one bought with your money must be circumcised. So shall my covenant be in your flesh an everlasting covenant.

This is true in at least two levels…

Explain: See a OT in relation to NT Pattern.

In Christ-- we see old promised being fulfilled such that new sacraments are instituted to replace old

I.e. New Covenant means "new sacraments" depicting the same realities being promised in old covenant contexts..

So for instance-- there is no doubt that the sacrament of sacrifice as especially practiced at Passover was replaced by the sacrament of the Lord's Supper.

I.e.. Christ's fulfillment of the things signified at Passover resulted in a new Passover, albeit under very different elements.

The same pattern exists concerning circumcision with respect to baptism…

Calvin on "perpetual":

… that since Christ is the end of the law, the perpetuity which is ascribed to the ceremonies of the law, was terminated as soon as Christ appeared. (temple, Sabbath) … for at length by the coming of Christ, circumcision was substantially confirmed so that it should endure for ever and that the covenant which God had before made should be ratified.

And therefore, although the use of circumcision has ceased, yet it does not cease to be an everlasting or perpetual covenant if only Christ be regarded as the Mediator; who, though the sign be changed, has confirmed the truth. And that by the coming of Christ external circumcision ceased, is plain from the words of Paul who not only teaches that we are circumcised by the death of Christ, spiritually, and not through the carnal sign; but who expressly substitutes baptism for circumcision (Col. 2:11) and truly baptism could not succeed circumcision without taking it away…. For it would be absurd to be initiated into the Church by two different signs; of which the one should testify and affirm that Christ was come and the other should shadow him forth as absent.

Notice then how "baptism" has all the same 5 characteristics as circumcision:

1. Faith rite-- an expression of faith

2. Entrance rite--

Matthew 28: 16-20

"all authority in heaven and on earth has been given to me. As you go, therefore, make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit..."

I.e. As a sacrament, it forms is used to build a covenant community. A person joins the covenant community through baptism. As a community entrance rite-- it is given on the same terms as was circumcision-- i.e. all believers and those under their jurisdiction as children.

I.e. In so far as children are under the jurisdiction of parents… and parents under the jurisdiction of the church we see children then as within the jurisdiction of the church!

And so-- in the NC context-- we have the several household baptism that say nothing explicitly about the faint of the dependants but does explicitly refer to the faith of the head of household…

And so we have this very compelling passage:

Acts 2:38 Peter said to them, "Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ so that your sins may be forgiven; and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit. 39 For the promise is for you, for your children, and for all who are far away, everyone whom the Lord our God calls to him."

a. "this promise"-- refers to the covenant promise even as "baptism" (like circumcision) is states in a way that makes it synonymous-- i.e. to baptize people is to transact the covenant promise here states

b. "this promise is for your and your children"-- almost identical language as applied to circumcision!

3. Sacramental Rite--

. Romans 6:3-4

This promise of union with the Father through Christ by the Holy Spirit is depicted in Romans 6:4 where Paul states that "We were buried with Him by baptism into death so that as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father, we too might walk in newness of life."

 

1. Matthew 28:19

This passage connects baptism with the "remission of sins" obtained through Christ.

2. Titus 3:5

This passage connects baptism with the "washing of regeneration and of renewal in the Holy Spirit."

It is important to note that these expressions go beyond merely symbolical language concerning these blessings such that there is an intimate connection between the right reception of Baptism and the privilege of passing through judgment by the blood of Christ and the sanctification of our nature by the Spirit. Just as preaching produces faith, so to the sacrament of baptism as a means of grace functions in the church to affect faith to those elect by the Father.

4. Judgment rite: In so far as baptism signifies the same exact realities of passing through judgment

Cutting- judgment

Water-- judgment

. 2 Peter 3:5-7 and 1 Peter 3:18-22

Both relate the idea of judgment by water as observed in Genesis flood to the day of judgment. In 2 Peter, the same waters which destroyed the wicked in Noah's day are shown to typify a future destruction by fire reserved for "ungodly men.

Yet in 1 Peter 3:21, Peter states that "this water (referring to the flood) symbolizes baptism that now saves you ...by the resurrection of Jesus Christ."

From one point of view, the water depicts judgment unto destruction. Yet from the vantage point of the elect, the same water depicts judgment unto salvation. And this is referred to as a "baptism" by Peter.

3. 1 Cor 10:1-2

In a similar fashion, the exodus through the Red Sea is spoken of by Paul in 1 Cor. 10:1-2 as a baptism in water. Paul writes, "our fathers...all passed through the sea, and ... were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea." For those who were represented by Moses in covenant with God, the waters represented salvation. Yet for those who were not represented by Moses but the Pharaoh, the waters of baptism represented curse and destruction.

5. Pepetual-- in that contrary to our Baptist brothers and sisters-- we see that Baptism is still about God building a covenant community… not individual faith per se…

We are in continuity with Abraham even as baptism functions to do exactly what circumcision did-- namely-- to build a community after the covenant promise of God into the next generation-- which is why the mark of circumcision and baptism are placed upon the next generation even as before they understand its meaning--

It was done then as now anticipating that the promises transacted in it are promises they will grow up hearing about such that eventually they will come to self-consciously recognize their place within it..

 

Conclusion: God is building a covenant community as entered into by the sacrament of circumcision under the OC and baptism under the NC…

This is then a perpetual thing that will last until the consummation of history… i.e. into future generations…

Christ fulfills the promise as he is the guarantor of the covenant:
