What is impossible for man is possible for God

 

Introduction:

By faith or sight-- we have seen over and over again in Genesis how this has been a constant theme.

The one-- fixates on God, his character, his word, his power and lives accordingly.

The other-- fixates on ourselves, our circumstances, the appearances of things, the human odds, etc.

The one, is content to rest in God's Word- content to float upon a sea of the infinite

The other, must reason every detail of life so as to squeeze the infinite into our limited minds, only to split them and with it our capacity for peace and hope!

Perhaps nothing is more detrimental to faith than to look upon things as from the vantage point of "our sight" vs. faith…

We fixate on the details of our circumstances such as to see faith drowned out by a sea of circumstances…

What are the circumstances of your life that tend drown out faith and hope?

What are the details that clutter your vision of faith?

For Sarah and Abraham, it was clearly their age?

You can imagine-- All of their dreams, their hopes, their place in history was wrapped upon in their progeny in child bearing, and yet they are just too old.

Sure, they have hear over and over again by Chapter 18 God's promise-- but nothing seemed to have happened.

B. Not just an ordinary meal-- Covenant Meal

Details

Why are we given all these details? Is it a lesson in hospitality? What to do when unexpected guests arrive?

--While one may be justified in seeing lessons in the first part about hospitality, it can scarcly have been for the sake of inculcating hospitality to strangers that the text reports the events in the first section…

Vs. 6-8-- suddenly Abraham changes gear for despite the midday heat, he hurries to Sarah and tolls her to hurry--

Thee seahs of fine flour-- lacks a verb -- again suggesting the hurry of the moment… - would make a great quantity of bread…

As we shall see, the point of the visit is God's Word of promise to Sarah. Somehow all of this must be tied in to that. OT scholar Allen Ross maintains that all this was meant to convey intimate fellowship, and on the basis of such a close relationship, the Lord would guarantee the imminent birth of the child of promise.

eating of a meal-- Covenants in the ancient world were often arranged with meals

What's significant here is the role of meals in the making of covenants. Eating together cemented and certified the terms of the covenant. We still do it today.

In the Levitical code, the peace offering was intended to signify that the worshiper enjoyed a peaceful relationship with the Lord-- this sacrifice was a meal to be eaten by the congregation in the sanctuary as a celebration of the Lord's provisions for them in his blessings… it was a holy meal, a communal meal with non other than God…

II. A Confirmation of God's Power To Fulfill His Promise

After sharing a meal prepared by the household of Abraham, the Lord announced the fulfillment of the promise, declaring in rebuke of Sarah's laughter, that nothing is too hard for the Lord.

Where is Sarah-- rhetorical (knew her name, surely knew her whereabouts)

The specifics of the promise--"vs. 10, I shall surely return to you this time next year and Sarah shall surely have a son--

(notice the emphatic certainty in which this is said)

Sarah laughed-- again, like Abraham

-- the narrative exposes the impossibility of God's word (if viewed by sight rather than by faith) as expressed this time by Sarah. Like Abraham, she "laughed"-- an indication of how it looked as from a human perspective!

I.e. vs. 11 commentary: Now Abraham and Sarah were old, well advanced in age and Sarah had passed the age of childbearing…

quietly in her heart lit "inside herself"-- (this describes an inner state

-- here outward appearance had evidently been unchanged-- wow-- isn't this true , how we can put on a face of faith, even as we rely inwardly on the logistics of reason)

What did the details of reason tell her-- her own mental map of reality said-- "After I have grown old, shall I have pleasure, my lord being old also")

Pleasure in child bearing… ?

Why did Sarah laugh? "shall I surely bear a child" (again exposes the divine presence

-- God knew Sarah's inner disposition-- on resting on sight and not on faith-- one filled with the details of her circumstances and less the simplicity of God's word)

Sarah denied it saying "i did not laugh--

Word play here-- for in her denial--

Whatever the thinking, her denial allowed her to pronounce albeit in a jumbled form, the name of her future son (sahaqti "I laughed" yshaq "Isaac") In this way, Sarah unwittingly confirms the divine promise and provides a simple reiteration, "not so, you did laugh" which clinches the discussion…

Here we come to the climax of the narrative-- the point of the meal itself and all these details-- for in the face of doubt and despair-- in the face of a sceptics map--filled with all the details of reason filling the mind in the attempt to understand the infinite-- God reiterates what has been the message of Genesis throughout!

Confirmation:

I shall certainly come back to you-- sounds like another visit, but may simply refer to God's intervention as elsewhere (Zech 1:3, Ps 80:15)

 

There is no room for doubt-- what God's was promising was impossible to man and impossible even to imagine, when reliant upon reason so as to map out the possibilities-- it could only lead to a cynical laugh-- one all the same denied by Sarah as she maintained her outward "face of faith"

But the message was clear-- faith isn't the same a reasoned map-- this is the point of this narrative-- this is the point of this covenant meal-- that God was confirming that what is impossible to man, is possible with God-- that nothing, and he meant "Nothing" is too hard for God -- that his word and his power are inseparable!

And it was upon this reality that God wanted the covenant to rest, not on any reality that could be mapped out in the schemes and mind of man-- lest the covenant be anything less than God's doing!

The "laughter" scene-- intended to draw out the "unbelievablity" of what God was confirming--

Point-- not to condemn Sarah as much as to condemn the point of view-- that what is impossible with man is possible with God!

Point: God's Word and God's Power are Inseparable -- What is impossible for man is possible for God

But herein lies the problem-- we have reduced God to our own mind, to our own mapping out of the details of our lives so as to want to get the infinite will, intentions, knowledge and power of God into our own minds such that our own minds split--

Conclusion:

Calvin:

It is not surprising that in arduous affairs we fail, or that we succumb to difficulties, but God's way is far otherwise or he looks down with contempt from above upon those things which alarm us by their lofty elevation… we now see what was the sin of Sarah; namely that she did wrong to God by not acknowledging the greatness of his power. And truly we also attempt to rob God of his power whenever we distrust his word…

… for as soon as any extraordinary difficulty occurs, then, whatever God has promised, seems to us fabulous; yea, the movement he speaks, the perverse thought insinuates itself, How will he fulfil what he promises? Being bound down, and pre-occupied by such narrow thoughts, we exclude his power, the knowledge of which is better to us than a thousand worlds.

What for Calvin was a condition of our sin-- is all the more accentuated by modernity!

We depend too much on reason and not enough on faith!

This is the problem sin and especially it is the problem of modernity!

 

G.K. Chesterton: The Maniac (insane)

Poetry is sane because it floats easily in an infinite sea; reason seeks to cross the infinite sea and make it finite, the result is mental exhaustion… the poet only asks to get his dead into the heavens. It is the logician who seeks to get the heavens into his head. And it is hes head that splits.

Everyone who has had the misfortune to talk with people on the edge of mental disorder knows that their most sinister quality is a horrible clarity of detail; a connecting of one thing with another in a map more elaborate than a maze… If you argue with a madman, it is extremely probable that you will get the worse of it; for in many ways his maid moves all the quicker for not being delayed by the things that go with good judgment. ..

Indeed, the common phrase for insanity is in this respect a misleading one. The madman is not the man who has lost his reason. The madman is the man who has lost everything except his reason!

We need to confess that we are prone to this, perhaps especially as made all the more plausible given our modern confidence in the mind--

I have said it before-- the modern illusion is that unless we understand it, we can't believe it! We construct with great clarity of detail a map which cannot sustain the infinite power and mind of God-- we begin to trust our map rather than God's word and this destroys all true religion!

But God in his graciousness-- as to Abraham and Sarah-- presents himself to us, even in our senses--

Here, we have God with us-- eating a meal as it where-- and in this meal-- there is the simple message-- "Nothing is too hard with God!

It is a confirmation Communion Table!

What does he say in this meal--

What are some of those promises that in the light of our reasoned map seem impossible but in the power of God's word will come true…

1. The seeming impossibility of being reconciled to God:

Sure, if reliant upon our sight, we would look within ourselves and begin to laugh-- there is no way I am reconciled to God… look at how I still struggle with sin-- look at my failures-- failures to be selfless, to think good thoughts about others, failure to put my interest behind the interest of others, failure to trust God, failure to give us the things of this world for the good of others…
