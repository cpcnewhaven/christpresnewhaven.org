At this his relations were sore amazed not for that they believed that what he had said to them was true, but because they thought that some frenzy distemper had got into his head therefore it drawing towards night and they hoping that sleep might settle his brains with all haste they got him to bed,,, so when the morning was come, they would know how he did; he told them worse and worse, he also set to talking to them again, but they began to be hardened; they also thought to drive away his distemper by harsh and surly carriages to him: Sometimes they would deride sometimes they would chide, and sometimes they would quite neglect him…

And so begins one of the most renown journeys ever taken, the journey of Pilgrim away from the city of destruction to the Cross of Christ and the celestial city as envisioned by John Bunyan.

It is a story reminiscent of every age-- when warnings as to the certain judgment of God are met with chiding, neglect and down right derision. Even in the church, the idea of God's wrath and judgment has been chided and disbelieved. Many recommend that if you wish to grow and increase your church budget, you speak of love inclusion but avoid sermons on wrath and hell.

But, we are bound to preach the whole counsel of God-- expository preaching is bound to preach whole books of the Bible-- we cannot but deal with this topic of fire and brimstone-- since our text demands we give such as this its due attention…

And for sure, no single judgment event in redemptive history is more referenced in scritpure so as to warn us from the wrath of God than the judgment of Sodom and Gommorah. It was an event that became a symbol of God's justice against sin, second only to an even greater judgment which is to come…

Exposition:

I. The Justice of God Probed (18:16-33

A. God's Revelation to Abraham 16-21

The story begins where last weeks story left us-- the Lord and his angles, have just eaten a special feast with Abraham, a feast that resulted in God's confirmation to Abraham concerning his plans to give them a special covenant child in spite of their age… In the larger sense, it was a confirmation that Abraham would be the father of a covenant nation and people that would eventually result in reconciling the elect to God by grace through faith!

It is important here to remember God's covenant with Abraham that he and his spiritual descendants after him would be reconciled to God since they will be judged rightous by faith rather than by meritorious works-- and yet the evidence of such faith would be in their walking by faith and not by sight… Abraham's sttatus with God was rightous as by faith and not by works!

But what of those outside the covenant promises-- The divine mission now turns from salvation to judgment against those not reckoned as rightous…

Lit. vs. 16 and they looked toward Sodom

And yet we are invited in to an internal conversation within the divine counsel-- whould Abraham be privy to what God is about to do? We are allowed to listen so as to introduce why the narrative that follows is given to the church in the first place.

For in so far as Abraham is the father of the church, we are in fact told why the story of Sodom and Gommorah is included in the canon.

1. vs. 17Since Abraham was to be a blessing to the nations (and so too then the church), and explanation is given as to why all the nations are not included. I.e. that the promises salvation does exclude some-- lest we were to expect otherwise.

2. vs. 19-- It is told Abraham what God is about to do so that Abraham and those after him might be instructed by the events so as to be warned away from the wrath of God!

vs. 19 No, for I have chosen him, that he may charge his children and his household after him to keep the way of the LORD by doing righteousness and justice; so that the LORD may bring about for Abraham what he has promised him."

I.e. God intends that what is going to happen to Sodom and G. be recorded and passed down through Abraham and his descendants lest they not turn by faith to God's grace offered to Abraham through faith! The whole thing is to become a great warning to those being saved!

And so, Abraham, and all of us after him-- are informed as to God's plan to destroy the wicked-- here those wicked that are in the cities of Sodom and Gomorrah

20 Then the LORD said, "How great is the outcry against Sodom and Gomorrah and how very grave their sin!

The narrative next turns to a dialogue between Abraham and God-- in effect, We see Abraham probing the justice of God…

B. Abraham's Inquire as to God's Justice 22-33

Over and over again, we hear Abraham ask-- "What if"

(doesn't this sound familiar)

in effect--God, according to my understanding, your ways seem unfair, God, don't you know that I have kinsmen in that city of destruction-- what about….

All of us with loved ones can identify with Abraham's "what if" in the shadow of God's judgment against sin-- for we live in the amidst of a city of destruction-- our human relationships obscure to us justice and fairness…

The Lord in his judgment is probed-- and throughout the negotiations we find the contrast between the righoteous (saddiq) and the wicked (rasa)--

Abraham's point-- the whole city God-- what if there is any righteous

God's point-- not even one righteous person shall be destroyed…

(two levels:

1. Reminds us that we all deserve God's wrath… there is none that are righteous such that God would be justified to destroy the whole city…

2. But , if any may be reckoned as rightous, they will be spared the wrath that is to come-- given the previous chapter we might here include those who by faith are righteous-- then God promises that they would be spared. I.e. the concept of righteous must be related to the covenant that has already been given to Abraham as confirmed by the covenant feast!

I.e. if any have believed upon God, even though they are sinful, they will be reckoned as righteous by god-- as we will see-- Lot will show this truth very well-- who inspite of his proclivity to sin-- has saving faith-- and it’s the kind of faith, the the quantity of it, that saves…

Point to this section-- God is justified in his judgments-- His wrath is nothing less than absolutely fair! And God can, even if only 10 amidst thousands, discern the rightous among the wicked, even if rightous by faith alone!

The righteous may be confident that the rightious judge of the earth will not destroy the righteous with the wicked…

Even as the angels of the Lord appear at Lot's home in order to give warning to what is about to come-- God's judgment against Sodom for their sins are more than justified…

For while Lot will show himself a man of faith-- (albeit sorely entangled in the worldliness of Sodom)-- Lot is but a very small exception…

For what happens:

One the one hand, Sodom is exposed in their sin in their desire to have sexual relations with Lots angelic visitors.
