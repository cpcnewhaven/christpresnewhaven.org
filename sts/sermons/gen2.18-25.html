II. A Covenant Vow-- bone of my bones...

21-25: the success of God in forming a helper suitable is met with the rapturous acclaim in poetic outburst... (as indicated in your english bible by its indention)

bone of my bone and flesh of my flesh--

-- kinship formula, as in Laban to his nephew Jacob (29:14, see aslo Judg. 9:2, 2 Sam.5:1; 19:13-14, etc) I.e. "relative"-- says in effect-- she is family!

-- but why this formula-- is it merely a statement depicting the unity of marriage relationships...

When Adam made his solemn affirmation that "this is now bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh" it was not some inane observation as if God needed to be informed concerning Eve's origins. Rather these words constituted a solemn oath of covenant faithfulness-- an abbreviated way of saying "may this woman be to me as my own body-- and because of this, should I ever fail to love and care for her as my own body, thereby breaking the covenant bond between us, I hereby solemnly invite God to make it hurt me like bones and flesh being wrenched out of my body." That this statement was a covenantal oath is further demonstrated by the use of the phrase "bone of my bones" in other similar oath taking ceremonies in the Ancient Near East as depicted in Genesis 29:14. (See also the use of this formula in Judges 9:2, 2 Samuel 19:12-13) This formula then informs the NT teaching that husband are to love their wives as their own bodies. (Eph.5:21-33)

The oath itself confirms that marriage is a covenant whereby two people, a man and women, are joined together as one person-

the interest of one becomes the interest of the other as if there is now but one interest.. again, the complementariness of marriage rather than competition that would extend most especially to vocational calling as creature kings over creation...

Again, we have a NT commentary by Paul in Eph. 5, where the husband is commanded to "love his wife as himself-- the point being that their interest and callings are now so intimately connected, that to not care and love a wife is within a marriage covenant the same as self-destruction itself--

IT is to take the help that God has provided and destroy it...

 

III. A Covenant Committment--

Therefore, (and for this reason)-- a statement of inference given the covenant nature of marriage

a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined (cleave) to his wife

"leaves"-- the traditional trans. "leaves" suggests that the man moves from his parents and sets up home elsewhere...

whereas in fact Israelite marriage was usually patriolocal, that is the man continued to live in or near his parents house... it was the wife who left home to join her husband.

Therefore, the preferable trans. is "forsake"-- As Israel is forbidden to forsake the poor, levite or even covenant (dt.12:9, 14:27, 29:24) so then the husband is told to forsake his parents-- i.e.discontinue his primary loyalty and support for one family in order to be loyal and supportive to his new wife and the formation of his new family... I.e. a man's priorties change!

and cleaves to his wife-- or "sticks"so for instance Shechems love for Dinah is decribed as "his soul stuck to Dinah" (Gen.34:3) so too Israel was to "stick" to their inheritance so as to render a permant loyalty to...

Again, the addition of "cleave is to give double emphasis to the loyalty that is due to marriage...

Again, it is important to see the covenantal language in this word! This is not just descriptive language, but also language that places this relationship into a covenant context of binding vows! For instance, when Scripture says that a man shall "cleave" to his wife (Gen.2:24), it is using a covenant term used elsewhere to express the way the Israelites were to cleave with affection and loyalty to the Lord. (Dt.10:20, 11:22, 13:4, Josh.22:5, etc.)

Clearly th point is that the OT marriage was as a covenant-- one of permance and undivided loyalty!

the language of leave and cleave-- affirms that a marriage relationship trumps all other relationships... the formation of a new home

Marriage is Recognized as Sacred by Christ

In Matthew 19:1-9, both the original design of marriage and its covenantal nature is recounted by Christ in response to the then popular laxity with respect to divorce. Christ's point, in vs. 4 is to reiterate that what God has joined together, let no one separate.

This of course raised the question-- are there any grounds for divorce?

Clearly divorce is hated in all circumstances, but while hated, it might all the same be necessary in a fallen world.

We discover that God grants it only as a common grace provision in a fallen world so as to protect an innocent party from abuse!

B. God allows for divorce by way of a reluctant provision so as to restrain evil against innocent parties. (Matthew 19:3-9, Mark 10:2-11)

Matt. 19:3 Some Pharisees came to him, and to test him they asked, "Is it lawful for a man to divorce his wife for any cause?" 4 He answered, "Have you not read that the one who made them at the beginning ‘made them male and female,’ 5 and said, ‘For this reason a man shall leave his father and mother and be joined to his wife, and the two shall become one flesh’? 6 So they are no longer two, but one flesh. Therefore what God has joined together, let no one separate." 7 They said to him, "Why then did Moses command us to give a certificate of dismissal and to divorce her?" 8 He said to them, "It was because you were so hard-hearted that Moses allowed you to divorce your wives, but from the beginning it was not so. 9 And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for fornication, and marries another commits adultery."

Grounds for Divorce-- when the covenant is broken by the other party

Observations:

a. Divorce is NOT instituted by God, only sadly recognized in some limited cases.

In Dt.22:19-20, 24:1-4,, Lev.21:7, 14; 22:13 and Nu.30:9, the Mosaic law takes the custom of divorce for granted, it does not institute divorce, but regulates it to limit and preclude its abuse. In cases where divorce is regulated in the OT, it is only applied specifically to cases involving sexual immorality. God allows divorce only as a common grace provision to restrain the evil of fornication (see explanation below) against the righteous/innocent party.

b. As Christ clearly stated, it is only provisional in response to the hardness of hearts. (Mt. 19:8, Mark 10:5)

See also Matthew 5:32, Luke 16:18, 1 Cor.7: 10-11.

*c. The limited condition for divorce allowed by God is for instances of "fornication" only.

Mt. 19:9 And I say to you, whoever divorces his wife, except for fornication...

Fornication is used in scripture to describe acts that violate the sexual union between a man and a woman in marriage.

But keep in mind how sex is understood so as to be a kind of sacramental ritual that reflects the relality of a coveant commitment to one another...

d. For this reason, "fornication" is defined by Paul to included abandonment or dissertion!

1 Cor.7:2-15: In an impressive way, Paul insists on sexual mutuality in marriage in this passage.

a. 1 Cor.7:5-6, Do not deprive one another except perhaps by agreement for a set time to devote yourselves to prayer, and then come together again so that Satan may not tempt you.

There is no "separation" recognized in Scripture apart from the legal, valid divorce. The Greek term "separate" is used synonomously to mean "divorce." The modern practice of "separation" is in direct violation of the scriptural provision for divorce. The "concession" Paul gives in 1 Cor.7:5-6 is the permission given for this extremely limited kind of separation for the brief period of concentrated prayer. Note then, how Paul phrases it in terms of a limited refraining from sexual union.

b. 1 Cor.7:15, Yet if the unbelieving partner separates, let it be so, in such a case the brother or the sister is not bound.

Paul is not adding here to the conditions of divorce specified by Christ's clarification of the Mosaic law regarding the limited conditions of fornication. Rather, Paul is merely demonstrating that desertion does in fact violate the "one flesh" union that is insisted upon in 1 Cor. 7:5-6. (see above) Though often overlooked, it is crucial to see that "fornication" as a comprehensive term for sexual immorality does have for an attested meaning a specific reference to willful desertion-- an explicitly sexual sin in that it entails the defrauding of a spouse conjugal rights.

(See for examples, Judges 19:2 [note that the second clause is in apposition to the first], Exodus 21:10-11, Hosea 2:2 and Jeremiah 2:2-32 (the later two passages specifically single out the sin of Israel's desertion as an additional ground justifying God's contemplated "divorce" of His wife-people.)

c. By good and necessary inference, it can be demonstrated that perhaps several other instances could likewise fall under Paul's understanding of "desertion" as a sub-category of fornication. For instance, since life precedes marriage, the marriage is therefore conditioned upon the viability of those living within it. Therefore, it can be argued that in such cases as spousal abuse, the innocent party has by necessity of life the need for separation. This separation would be a "desertion" category of fornication since the effect is that sexual union is prevented due to threat of life. Likewise, one could argue that perhaps a person who has violated the civil law so as to be put in jail, (justly) has likewise violated the "one flesh" contract in marriage so as to fulfill the conditions of fornication as in the case of desertion. (However I am less confident of this inference and the various implications.)

3. It is utlimately a typological expressing of humanity's relation to God himself!

The covenantal aspect of marriage is often used in Scripture as an analogy of God's covenant relationship with Israel and in the New Testament of Christ's relationship with the Church. Marriage served to provide people with a living paradigm depicting the redemptive relationship between God and his bride-people. (Hosea 2:19-20, Ezekiel 16:8, Ephesians 5:21-33, Matthew 19:4-6) Like the divine-human covenantal relationship, the marriage covenant between man and woman was intended by God to be loving, loyal and permanent.
