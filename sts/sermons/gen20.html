Human foolishness Is No Threat to God's Plan

Introduction:

What if our salvation depended upon us?

What if when our good intentions fall through our security in heaven fell through.

What if, Gods purpose for good to us was contingent upon our activities in this world…

As bazarre as all this seems perhaps-- this is exactly what most people seem to believe, not only in those many other religions of this world-- all of which have some meritorious principle at work in them, but all to often this is how the Christian seems to think as well.

Talk to a typical "Christian" on the street and they are worried that their action will effect God's ultimate will and purposes for them…

Whether it be

Well again, as contained within yet another great story of redemption history, it is shown that the purpose of the story is to direct us to God and his divine intervention at a dangerous moment-- our attention then is upon God and what He does when Abraham acts so as to put into jeapordy the covenant of God.

We will see the truth as taught by our confession, WCF-- "nothing is to him contingent or uncertain" God's plans for salvation rest wholly upon Him and what is in his own good pleasure to do!

No doubt, some of you will fell uncomfortible with such a message-- it will seem to suggest that our actions are meaningless, or that our salvation would encourage even lawlessness. Yet we know that this is not the case-- that our actions are meaningful and what we do does matter to God--that in fact we are held accountable by God for our actions-- all this is also taught in scripture…

So we have a common delimna when we preach expositionally rather than doctrinally or moralistically….

God's story of redemption is quite simply more messy than all our simplifying categories, most of which were driven by the polemics of its day…

I.e.s cripture seldom conforms perfectly to our little theological categories and boxes-- we are confessional which means that we live and think within the context of the church, that church which has deliberated upon matters of controvesy in the attempt to discern what the scritpures teach and has codified into what we call a creed. This of course is a good thing for we humbly admit that no single person living in a single age in a single cutlure could hope to have the same grasp of truth as say aliving community of faith that has theologized in contexts that transends any and all particularliteis.

Yet no creed could ever exhaust the majesty of God, nor could it properly express the nuance that flows out of that mystery-- expositional preaching within a confessional context should therefore never transgress the church's teaching, but it will surely never be exhausted by the church's teaching as well.

So here we have again a great story of redemption-- one where the teaching is in an anomilie, an event that doesn't fit our usual expectation--

For here-- we have a Caininite King acting more the part of the noble covenant believer

And abraham, the most noble of all covenant believers, one ordinarily a type of exemplary faith-- acting more the part of a Cainanite that one elect of God to be the father of a Godly nation.
