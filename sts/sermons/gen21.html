Genesis 21:1-21

The laugh of faith

Introduction:

Birth of a child a special even whenever it occurs. Particularly so if there’s been difficulty in having a child. But we all must concede that, next to the birth of Jesus, this is the most significant birth ever, because it confirms the fact that, indeed, the promises of God will be fulfilled.

Not simply the promise of a child by Sarah (which God promised in ch. 17 & 18), but all that that implies in terms of God’s covenant.

Exposition:

I. God Keeps His Word At The Appointed Time

1) And the Lord visited (see promise to return in Chapt18) Sarah according to(his) word and the Lord did to Sarah according to his promise.

1. visited--

-- elsewhere shows that it can describe appointment to new positions, mustering of troops, intervening for blessing or for judgment

-- i.e. used here to announce God's intervention so as to bring about the promised seed-- later in Genesis it will be used at yet another great moment of redemptive history to announce God's intervention to deliver Israel from Egypt (50:24)

It is a visit of salvation!

3. God's timing: Appointed time…

How often our calender doesn't match up to God's calender. The point -- Again the narrative draws attention to God, his powerful intervention, His faithful word, His perfect timing… This very significant event-- while seemingly on the brink of being thwarted so many times before-- was never really in jaopordy since God was behind it!

Let's see…

1. The word "play" is the piel ptc of the same root as the word earlier for "laughter" (this in itself draws our attention to the word, so that it could read "And sarah saw the son of Hagar laughing" qEjAxVm with/at her son Isaac…

2. Traditionally-- either

a. "laughing" as in mocking -- i.e. Sarah witness Ishmeal asserting his dominance over Isaac such as to represent a threat to his sole heirship and put in jeapordy the covenant promised exclusively through Sarah and Abraham's miraculous child

b. "laughing" as in "playing" i.e. what was indicated to Sarah was an equal footing with Isaac such as to jeapordize the covenant…

Usage of th word elswhere in Genesis:

19:14-- used to describe Lot as he attempted to warn his family and they though him to be "jesting" or "mocking" with them

26:8-- describes Isaacs conjugal playing with Rebekah

39:14-7-- Potiphars wife's accusation of Joseph as one who "mocked" or "trifiled" with them

Point-- in each of these incidence, the word seems always to be in th context of mischief

Exodus 32:6-- used to describe the Mischief of the people "playing/laughing" in the camp while Moses was on the mountain

Judges 16:25-- when the Philistines brought out the blinded Samson to "laugh/mock" at thim

Therefore, the word it seems is not describing an innocent and harmless "play" but rather indicated the nature of a relationship whereby Ishmeal was inserting his dominance over Isaac such as to represent a significant threat to Isaacs destiny to the eyes of Sarah.

I.e. On the surface, vs. 9 describes something very harmless, but in reality it indicates that Ishmeal wasn't taking the child "isaac" nor the promise related to the child seriously

Therefore better "and the son of Hagar "laughed at/mocked" Isaac

Gal.4:29-- Paul used a stronger word in relating to the event-- the word "persecute" such that Ishmeal is said to have "persecututed" Isaac…

Again, then, we see the play on words so as here to indicate that kind of laughter that was the response of unbelief-- here that Isaac should be the heir.

 

Therefore, Sarah plan was to expel Hagar and Ishmeal

2. She distinguished Ishmeal as "Hagar's son" from Isaac as "my son".

I.e. the language of Sarah is to cast Ishmeal as a slave, not a son per se…

Note ANE:

There was a legal tradition inANE whereby the slave son was entitled to inheritance; he could forfeit this inheritance in exchange for freedom. Here, Ishmael's being cast out frees him, but takes away inheritance from him

 

Conclusion: God's Word is Sure so as to accomplish his redemptive prupose then… and since it is recorded for us to read now-- we are to be encouraged to believe this now as well.

What's important about this passage then is less the "Human" elements and more the redemptive purpose of God--

Eg. We are tempted to get emotionally involved and sympathize with Hagar and Ishmeal.. When what the text would have us see is

1. God interveening in order to fulfill his covenant promise as according to his word

2. The response of hope and faith on the part of Abraham and Sarah

3. God interveening to preserve his promise as through Isaac, yet not so as to neglect the justice due Hagar and Ishmeal.

It proves again that the purpose of the "stories" of redmeptive must be viewed as within the greater "story" of redemption itself.. This portion of the narrative IS NOT about Abraham, Sarah, Isaac, Hagar and Ishmeal per se-- rather it is about the Word of God-- his promise to redeem a people as through miraculous intervention, even the miraculous seed and heirship of one born, not in the ordinary sence but one born of God!

God's word endures and can be depended upon-- the stuff that makes for good worship, less good moralisms!

New Covenant Significance:

According to Gal.4:

Ishmeal was born according to the flesh through the slave wife'

Isaac was born according to the promise and was the heir--

By inspired analogy--

Ishmeal will come to respresent our bondage to Sin as under the law-- that law that would be expressed even in the Sinai code… Ishmeal, by analogy, is life according to the flesh-- seeking to assume blessing by human ability and merit… (remember that Ishmeal was the son of Abraham's human solution to the problem of bringing salvation through a covenant child! )

Isaac-- will come to represent the freedom of sin as under the law--- that freedom that would be given by the supernatural working of the spirit to regenerate us and give us faith in God's word-- that freedom that will assume blessing not by human ability but God's ability!

I.e. Salvation is not by human origin or human works, but by God and according to the work of the Spirit!

 

According to ROMANS 9:6-9

Paul wrestling with the agonizing problem that so many of his kinsman according to the flesh do not believe in Christ. How come so many Jews don’t believe in the JEWISH Messiah?

Answer: Not all who are descended from Israel are Israel. Not all of Abraham’s descendants are his children. Double-talk? No--simply making the point that pedigree is not enough. In order to be a true child of God one must be, like Isaac, a child of promise, having a supernatural birth and a heavenly Father.

Noteworthy that it occurs in context of election. The only way one can receive the new birth is if God chooses to give it to him.

Then Paul applies it to present day believers. Like Isaac, children of promise. Now as then, those born according to flesh persecute those born according to Spirit.

But what does Scripture say? "Cast out the slave and her son; for the son of the slave shall not inherit with the son of the free woman." God rejects those who are born into slavery; those who are free inherit the promises.

We are the true children of Abraham.

SUM: The true children of Abraham are not those who trace their physical descent to Abraham, but those who have the faith of Abraham. They and they alone inherit the promises. And they become children by faith not of their own accord, by a birth that is given by God alone.

This has some fairly obvious implications for the significance of Israel and being a Jew in our day.

But what about today-- what are we directed to by all this…

APPLICATION: How does this affect our lives?

1. It sets before us the fact that we can't secure God's favor as from anthing that is human in origin…

Even many Christians are accustomed to speaking of Christianity in terms of what we do.

Even while we say that we are saved by grace-- we live each day thinking God is either pleased or displeased with us according to what we do--

Consider that your status with respect to God's affections is not of human origin but divine origen through Christ, the miraculous son…

2. It affects our lives by setting before us the doctrine of the new birth.

Sherwood--

The child born by ordinary means could never inherit the promises of God. He may become a great nation. He may be a skillful archer. He may acquire great skill and learning. He may be held in high esteem by his peers.

But he can never, never, never inherit the blessings of God, never be a recipient of the promises. This is so clearly portrayed in Ishmael. For how was he conceived? Remember. According to human planning, strength, and ingenuity. A product of human power. Not born according to special bestowal of God’s power.

Such was the case of all born under the Sinai Covenant. No matter their parentage, their law keeping, their commitment to their religious scruples. They could not inherit the promises in an ultimate sense. No forgiveness of sins; no hope of everlasting life; no knowledge of the true and living God. Because they did not share the faith of their father according to the flesh. And why did they not have that faith? Because they had never been born according to the Spirit.

"That which is born of the flesh is flesh, and that which is born of the spirit is spirit." This is one of the great and foundational principles of true Christianity. That nothing of any eternal and spiritual value can come from the natural man. We can only produce according to our nature. An apple tree produces apples, an orange tree produces oranges. And a sinner produces sin. And no amount of religion, good intentions, moral living can produce the life of God in the soul of man.

Jews counted themselves Abraham’s children because of their pedigree. So do many today. They suppose that because.....they’re Christians. None of this brings new life. None of this changes the heart. None of this calls Lazarus out of the tomb.

Can it be said of you that you have received the new birth? That you’ve been regenerated from on high? That a principle of life resides on your soul that was placed there by the Holy Spirit? Can it? Can it?

John the Baptist was not impressed by those who found themselves in Abraham’s family tree. "God is able from these stones to raise up children for Abraham." Neither is God impressed by what you have to offer (exp).

We all stand in desperate need of the new birth
