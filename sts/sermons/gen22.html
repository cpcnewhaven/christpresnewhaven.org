Exposition:

The Divine call is reminiscent of the call that started this whole Abrahamic story-- Gen.12:1-3-- go you out to the land that I will show you…

And so the story began

2 I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you, and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. 3 I will bless those who bless you, and the one who curses you I will curse; and in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed."

How many times was the promise put in jeapordy by Abraham's lack of faith-- how mean threats from within and threats from without to the promise of God

-- yet God had demonstrated even by the ceremonious passing throught he slain animials that He, the Great Creator God, had taken it upon himself to secure the promises given to Abraham-- that the covenant depended on God and not on man.. Over and over again, we have seen through the narrative of Abraham the divine intervention at just the right time to save his covenant from being twarted..

-- yet at those very significant moments, Abraham believed in God and to count his life on God's promise…

He believed God when he left from Ur to a place yet know as the promised land

He believed God when he left it to God's providence in letting Lot choose the land to settle in

He believed in God when he received the blessilng from Melchezideck and not the neighboring kinds

He believed in God when he obeyed God in circumcising all of his household,

Etc.

Finally, as all the promises hinged on the birth of a child to Abraham and Sarah-- we have seen God intervene miraculously to give Abraham a covenant child at the birth of Isaac!

Now, at the end of the story, we hear yet again a call with the same language, "get you out to the land of Moriah…

The repitition forms an inclusion in the narrative structure of the Abrahamic portion of Genesis-- it began with a call and now concluded with a call-- a call that will test Abraham's confidence ina radical way.

I. Abraham's Final Test of Faith -- vs. 1-2

The issue according to the text-- "test" (the reader is clued into God's intention-- But Abraham wouldn't have known or it wouldn't be a test at all!) The significance of this first vs is very large with respect to our method of interpretation…

I.e. we are tempted given our place in the covenant to immediately clue in on the substitutionary ram and how all this typologizes Christ-- and no doubt we will get there…

-- but we first must let the text speak in its own way-- and here the focus is on Abraham and more exactly Abraham's "confession" as through this great test of faith!

What does God require? sacrificial death, v. 2: "and offer him there as a burnt offering.

Not out of the ordinary for Abraham-- sacrificial ordanices had long existed-- probably even as early as Adam and the sacrificial animal from which God clothed Adam and Eve--

We have seen throughout Abraham's life that the alter of sacrifice was a central element of how Abraham worshipped God, always representing a place of meeting with God and a confirmation of God's promises to Abraham…

What God is here requiring was for Abraham to worship in the way proscribed-- as within a a system that revealed God's substitutionary sacrifice as an atonement for sin resulting in covenant renewal…

Where does God require it? Vs. 2-- land of Moriah… on one of the mountains

Somewhat abnormal-- first time such a requirement was made of Abraham…

Same description is again found in 2 Chronicles 3:1

2Chr. 3:1 ¶ Solomon began to build the house of the LORD in Jerusalem on Mount Moriah, where the LORD had appeared to his father David, at the place that David had designated, on the threshing floor of Ornan the Jebusite.

-- the mountain on which the temple stood in Jerusalem.

I.e. the future sight of Israel sacrifices

Who does God require sacrifice? Vs. 2: Abraham's son He said, "Take your son, your only son Isaac, whom you love,

--this heightens the test, because we instinctively wish more for our children than for ourselves. We would rather die ourselves than see our children die; and this is especially true in Abraham's case. Abraham's hopes revolve around Isaac, not himself. He would surely rather offer his own life than Isaac's.

The gravity of all is exposed by the languge which described the intimate and precious aspects of Abraham in relation to his son..your son, your only son, your beloved son

So why the test?

Highly abnormal within the Abrahamic religion, but not so abnormal for the surrounding pagan religions.

Note: Human sacrifice was fairly commonplace in that ANE such that it might have seemed appropriate…

So perhaps it was as Delitzsh suggests…

Delitzsh:

In fact, the God who requires Abraham to sacrifice his only son after the manner of the Canaanites (2Kings 3:27; Jer.19:5) is only apparently the true God. The demand was indeed only made to prove that Abraham was not behind the heathen in the self-denying surrender of his dearest to his God, and that when th demand had been complied with in spirit, the external fulfillment might be rejected.
