Exposition:

We come to the end of the Abrahamic era with the death of Abraham and Sarah–how will the author of Genesis conclude his "history of God concerning Abraham"?

If we are looking for a hero’s eulogy–we will be disappointed. For not a whole lot is said about either Abraham or Sarah. What the author does do however is give a detailed account of two things

1. the burial of Sarah

2. the securing of a Hebrew wife for Isaac

Why? I.e. we are given less an account of persons and more an account or history of a uniquepeople in a unique land in transition to the next era…

I. Lets first Remember:

The grand them ever since the fall of Adam has been God’s saving activity to preserve a people for eternal blessing…

Over and over again–there has been the contrast between those who would trust in God for these blessing vs. those who who trust in human wisdom and power! So prominent is this theme that it is reflected at various levels in the story.

1. The distinction is presented in two distinct legacies coming out from Adam:

Described by God that there would be enmeity between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed was that of rival kingdoms for the possession of the world... (Mt.4:8ff, 1 Peter 1:4; 2 Peter.3:13, Rev. 11:15; 12:10) Genesis has given us two histories...

1. The history of Adam through Cain... Lamech and the eschalation of sin-- so much so that all apperances are such as to suggest the extinction of the women's seed.

2. The history Adam through Seth-- the women's seed which though small is divninely preserved as a people after God's own special covenant...

E.g. God’s promise to Adam was that he would preserve a people to receive the promise of eternal life once given to Adam before the fall–and yet these people would be in constant strife with a people that were not to receive this promise. E.g. there would be two kingdoms out from the heritage of Adam–one that would be the recipients of a special grace promise of eternal life and the other who would not, although for a time be given the common graces of life and temporal prosperity even. One would be recognized by their trusting in God’s provision, the other would represent human trust for blessing (thus the legacy of Cain, tower of babel, even Ishmeal in so far as he was a human solution to the promise of God vs. Seth, Noah, and Abraham/Sarah in their better days)

2. At another level, we continue to see these two legacies continuing to be divided out as from within the covenant family–thuse

a. Noah–we see the dividing out of Shem vs. Ham

b. Abraham–we see the division of Isaac vs. Ismeal–one resprenting God’s power to preserve a special people for a special blessing, the other respresenting human power and wisdom to find blessing!

So that by the end of the Abrahamic era–we are told about Ismeal–that he resides outside of the promised land and in a state where by it is said that "he settled in defiance of all his relatives (speaking of course of Isaac, vs. 25:18)

So get the greater historical picture–all of Genesis is divided into two camps, two kinds of people, one representing trusting in God’s power to bless, the other representing trust in human power to bless!

This brings us to these passages that describe a transition into a new historical era of the patriarch’s after the legacy of Isaac such as will eventually get us to Joseph.

And what do read…

Yes, the death of Sarah and Abraham are recorded… but much more the events surrounding their death in the securing of a burial ground and Hebrew wife for Isaac…

Lets look more closely and we will discover that the grand theme of Genesis is in full view:

–E.g. from the very beginning–God’s covenant with Abraham in succession to his covenants with Adam, Seth, Noah… was a promise that God would himself secure the covenant blessings as represented by a unique land (Canaan) and a unique descandants as by the womb of Sarah especially (ethnic Hebrew) (as we have seen, both will be realized in Moses day literally but in the NC are shown to be symbolic or "typologically" related to a heavenly land as for a people of faith (vs. ethnicity)

-- Gen.12:1-3-- go you out to the land that I will show you… 2 I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you, and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. 3 I will bless those who bless you, and the one who curses you I will curse; and in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed."

After these promises were put in jeapordy over and over again by human wisdom and power–God’s promise has been secured by his own power as this is given to Abraham through faith–a faith that was climatically tested in Chapt 22 where we have God ordering Abraham to a unique land (moriah–which we know to be in the Jerusalem area) to offer a unique child Isaac a miraculously provided through Sarah–again demonstrating that God and god alone is to be trusted in for the covenant blessings once offered even to Adam!

So we are now back full circle where God re-affirms his promise to Abraham about the eternal blessing as symbolized by both a special land and a special descendants…

Gen. 22:15

¶ The angel of the LORD called to Abraham a second time from heaven, 16 and said, "By myself I have sworn, says the LORD: Because you have done this, and have not withheld your son, your only son, 17 I will indeed bless you, and I will make your offspring as numerous as the stars of heaven and as the sand that is on the seashore. And your offspring shall possess the gate of their enemies, 18 and by your offspring shall all the nations of the earth gain blessing for themselves, because you have obeyed my voice."

What’s next but an account of both land and descendants leading to the next era!

Therefore, we see the following two concerns, closely interweaved in the narrative:

A. Geneology of Rebekah–22:20-24

B. Purchase of Burial Ground for Sarah–23:1-20

C. Betrothal of Rebekah–24:1-67

Take a closer look:

II. Chapter 23–the Burial of Sarah

Sarah’ death is told–Here, in this text, Moses records her death and burial, but there is no eulogy, no speech about Sarah as a faithful woman. Rather, there are almost 20 verses of haggling about real estate. Indeed, from a literary level, the high point of the action is the section in which Abraham and Ephron come to terms over the field and cave at Machpelah.

E.g., the text driven by Abraham’s statement: "I am a stranger and a sojourner among you; give me property, v. 4

So for instance

I. 1-3, Introduction: Sarah dies; Abraham mourns.

II. 4-9, A request to Hittites for opportunity to negotiate with Ephron for the field and

cave at Machpelah.

III. 10-18, Abraham and Ephron negotiate for the field.

IV. 19-20, Abraham buries Sarah's remains. And the emphasis throughoutis that Abraham takes legal possession of the land.

A. The language of legal possession-.- let him give it to me in your presence as a possession, v. 9- was made over 18 to Abraham as a possession, v. 18.- The field and the cave that is in it were made over to Abraham as a possession, v. 20

B. The land is twice offered to Abraham as a gift (vv. 6, 11), but he refuses the gift and pays for the land.

C. The Hittite witnesses to the purchase are mentioned 5 times-10, and Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the hearing of the Hittites.-11, in the presence of the sons of my people I give it to you; ".-13 And he said to Ephron in the hearing of the people of the land.-16, Abraham weighed out for Ephron the silver which he had named in the hearing of the Hittites.-18, to Abraham as a possession in the presence of the Hittites.

D. The purchase price is recorded (15, 16), four hundred shekels of silver.

E. The location is recorded. This location is within the land God promised to Abraham. 19 After this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Mach-pe'lah east of Mamre (that is, Hebron) in the land of Canaan.

Conclusion: God had promised to Abraham that he would give him land–land that was to be a temporal and prophetic sign of the greater land offered once to Adam and promised to a covenant people by God’s own doing… Here, the author wants us to see that God made good on his promise–

For just as God called Abraham to sacrifice his son in the land of Canaan, where God then provided a substitute… here Sarah’s death is "in the land of Canaan" and this leading to Abraham’s acquisition of land!

Therefore, Abraham’s search for burial "land" was itself an expression of faith in God’s promises regarding the land and his ultimate inheritance given to him and his descendants by faith–we have here recorded the first acquision of land making Abraham and Israel after him a "land-owner"

I.e. a prophetic sign… of things to come…

A mighty prince–or "a prince of God"–by now sizable and very wealthy…

III. Chapter 24–The securing of a wife for Isaac

Again–with the death of Sarah and the legacy of Isaac–would Isaac be married to

T. David Gordon: "The challenge of this text is not to find out what it says; what it says is perfectly clear: That Abraham insists upon Isaac not marrying a Canaanite woman; and that no effort is spared to attain a non-Canaanite wife. The challenge is to discover why this is so important that Moses would devote 67 verses to it. Why is this so important to the establishment of the kingdom promised to Abraham?"

To answer this, we need to examine two things, before we are able to grasp the significance of Isaac not inter-marrying with the Canaanites.

I. The role of intermarriage in the ancient NearEast. Intermarriage was a common way of allying two nations.

Biblical evidence

1. 1 Kg. 3:1 Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt; he took Pharaoh's daughter, and brought her into the city of David 2 Chron. 18.1 Now Jehoshaphat had great riches and honor; and he made a marriage alliance with Ahab.

.II. The persistent rejection of intermarriage by God

Gen. 28.1, Then Isaac called Jacob and blessed him, and charged him, "You shall not marry one of the Canaanite women."

Josh. 23.11-13 Take good heed to yourselves, therefore, to love the Lord your God. For if you turn back, and join the remnant of thes nations left here among you, and make marriages with them, so that you marry their women and they yours, know assuredly that the Lord your God will not continue to drive out these nations before you; but they shall be a snare and a trap for you, a scourge on your sides, and thorns in your eyes, till you perish from off this good land which the Lord your God has given you.

Dt. 7:1-3 When the Lord your God brings you into the land which you are entering to take possession of it, and clears away many nations before you d when the Lord your God gives them over to you, and you defeat them, you must utterly destroy them; you shall make no covenant with them, and show no mercy to them. You shall not make marriages with them, giving your daughters to their sons or taking their daughters for your sons.

III. The reasons for God rejecting intermarriage

A. Intermarriage will corrupt the people of God (will deal with this later, at chapter 29)

B. Intermarriage is the world's way of establishing kingdoms; and God refuses to allow his kingdom to be established by worldly methods.

Daniel 2:32-33,41-44 The head of this image was of fine gold, its breast and arms of silver, its belly and thighs of bronze, its legs of iron, its feet partly of iron and partly of clay d as you saw the feet and toes partly of potter's clay and partly of iron, it shall be a divided kingdom; but some of the firmness of iron shall be left in it, just as you saw iron mixed with the miry clay.yAs you saw the iron mixed with miry clay, so they will mix with one another in marriage, but they will not hold together, just as iron does not mix with clay. And in the days of those kings the God of heaven will set up a kingdom which shall never be destroyed, nor shall its sovereignty be left to another people. It shall break in pieces all these kingdoms and bring them to an end, and it shall stand for ever"

Conclusion: In the ancient Near East, inter-marrying was a way of becoming more powerful, by combining the resources of two nations. God warns that his kingdom will not and cannot be established in this way. To try to blend worldly power and divine power actually dilutes the power.

Mainpoint:

So what do we see here–what is the bigger picture in conclusion to the Abrahamic Era?

Namely–that the author wants the reader to be assured that God remains faithful to preserve for himself a people of promise–here sympolized by a literal land and a literal (ethnic) people…

Again we see two levels of application:

Level One of Application–In that Genesis was a prologue to the Mosaic covenant–a covenant that maintained the outward, symbolic and typological aspects of the eternal promise… this passage was first applied to the people of Moses day…

To Moses' generation;on the verge of Canaan, about to enter the promised land. It seemed impossible to them, too good to be true. Why do they care that Abraham took title to part of Canaan? Because it is the down-payment, the provisional fulfilment of the promise that they will inherit Canaan. Why do they care that God provided a Hebrew wife for Isaac–even when there seemed none to be found–because it again reaffimed that God would preserve a special people by divine means and not human means–a "remnant" kingdom who were destined for eternal life…

More specifically:

As in Abraham’s day–real and living faith required that God be trusted for the eternal things even as this would impact some very practical things…

Yes–for Abraham–trusting in God’s promise involved who to marry and where to live kinds of things–

And while it would be a mistake to make from this that we should live in modern Canaan or that we should marry ethnic Jews over against ethnic Gentiles–the scipture does teach that we should be driven in life out of a concern to exhibit trust in God and after a sense of calling about his purpose for us in life–trusting that God in will secure for us the eternal blessing–even when it seems to the contrary.

So for instance–

We all want blessing–but will be tempted perhaps to find it by human means rather than by trusting in God provision as through Christ!

Examples that illustrate the New-covenant application of our text–

1. With respect to inter-marriage--- then, to trust God meant that they married within the ethnic descendants of the promise–today–we marry into the spiritual desdendants of the promise–such that under the new covenant, we are forbidden to marry an unbeliever… why are we tempted–because we seek blessing and this or that person seems the human way to get it–

Those who get the message of Genesis will understand that God will bless us by his power less human contriving…

 

2. With respect to land– then, it meant trusting God that real blessing is in the typologically significant Canaan… Today–trusting God to know that our covenant land of prosperity is heaven… so that the scripture is full of warnings against making earth our heaven, telling us even that those who desire to get rich in this world are in grave danger..

Why–again because it looks to a worldly/human solution to what only God can really give..

To those who get the message of Genesis–they will understand that God will bless us by his power manifest in Christ less in getting more land on earth as it where…

1) As a church, we must employ our resources in ways ordained by God.

2) As individuals, we must develop our faith in the ways that God wants it developed. The issue is not: what do I like?, but what has God ordained as the means by which he develops faith?

 

There are in short two kinds of people

1. Those born of God, those who have blessing that are secured by God, those then who live on the principle of faith so as to be dependant upon God vs. self-sufficient… this then translates not only in the ultimate decision to embrace Christ, but also in everyday decisions as to who we plan for the future–trusting in God for blessings so as to be calling driven vs. taking our blessing into our own hands

2. Those born of the flesh who seek after those temporal blessing made with human hands after human wisdom… those who depend on self vs. God–again, the ultimate decision being to reject God’s provision for blessing as through Christ, but more everyday rejecting God’s counsel and the Christian world view in preference for a secular and human point of view to order our lives by!
