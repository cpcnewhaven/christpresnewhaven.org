Genesis 23

Home!

Introduction:

Where will you be buried? Where will you bury your spouse?

At first glance, a kind of morbid question?

But think about what your answer means–how it may in fact reflect what you would call home.

It’s for this reason that it is been our custom always to go to great lengths to bring our soildiers back to "home soil" as it where when they died.

Exposition:

It could be said that after Eve, Sarah is the most important woman of the Old Testament and perhaps even the whole Bible aside from Mary. Ordinarily in primeval histories, women are rarely mentioned–but here, Sarah has been central to God’s plan to make good his promise of redemption as through a promised seed of which Sarah has been the central figure–here we have met a women of historic proportions–the grandmother of Israel herself, yet even more significant, the mother of the covenant promise–a women whose life was far from easy, who suffered the shame (in those days) of childlessness till she was ninety, who was twice trapped in a foreign kings harem by her husbands unbelieving schemes but who is all the same noted for her own faith in believing in God’s power to protect her and her destiny even in spite of her husband such that she remained submisive to him, who as often provoked by her slave-servant no less when she was granted a son by Abraham–who would have seen her own son leave home to be sacrificed on an alter… etc.

So what will the author say about Sarah at her death–what will be recorded in eulogy over her?

PRACTICALLY NOTHING!

Rather Sarah’ death is told-- but there is no eulogy, no speech about Sarah as a faithful woman. Rather, there are almost 20 verses of haggling about real estate. Indeed, from a literary level, the high point of the action is the section in which Abraham and Ephron come to terms over the field and cave at Machpelah.

So for instance

I. 1-3, Introduction: Sarah dies; Abraham mourns.

--when? We are told that Sarah is 120 years old

-- where? In Kiryatarba (Hebron), which lies about 20 miles south of Jerusalem on the way to Beersheba… it is close to Mamre where of course Isaac had been offered up…

The point here of course is that they have arrived into the promises land–and here our text tells us they have died there.

-- Abraham morns-traditional mourning customs would have included the rending of his garments, disheveling his hair, cutting his beard, scattering dust on the his dead and fasting–all carried out in front of the corpse hince the phrase in our text–"He came int" speaking of his entrance into the tent where Sarah would have been placed--

BUT THIS IS ALL WE ARE TOLD–AND AGAIN NOT EULOGY…

IT ALL TURNS ON THE NEGOTIATIONS FOR PROPERTY

II. 4-9, A request to Hittites for opportunity to negotiate with Ephron for the field and cave at Machpelah.

III. 10-18, Abraham and Ephron negotiate for the field.

IV. 19-20, Abraham buries Sarah's remains.

 

What is driving all this?

Clue–vs. 4 and Abraham’s introduction to the Hittites.

"I am just a resident immigrant with you?

What is significant about this phrase is the double emphasis using two different terms for sojourner and resident…

Ie..

"sojourner"–often used to denote foreigners living in an alien land–(Israel in Eypt 12:10)

"resident"–often used alongside of "sojourner" so that they appear virtually synonymous — yet the terms added suggests they they were even more on the fringe of socieity than just sojourners…

The important thing then is that Abraham, in his own mind and according to the text–had not yet arrived home–he was not yet in possession of a promised land… would he travel back "home" to Ur in order to bury Sarah, or would he seek land in the new land? The answer speaks volumes about where "Home" is to Abraham…

And the emphasis throughout is that Abraham takes legal possession of the land.

A. The language of legal possession-.- let him give it to me in your presence as a possession, v. 9- was made over 18 to Abraham as a possession, v. 18.- The field and the cave that is in it were made over to Abraham as a possession, v. 20

B. The land is twice offered to Abraham as a gift (vv. 6, 11), but he refuses the gift and pays for the land.

C. The Hittite witnesses to the purchase are mentioned 5 times-10, and Ephron the Hittite answered Abraham in the hearing of the Hittites.-11, in the presence of the sons of my people I give it to you; ".-13 And he said to Ephron in the hearing of the people of the land.-16, Abraham weighed out for Ephron the silver which he had named in the hearing of the Hittites.-18, to Abraham as a possession in the presence of the Hittites.

D. The purchase price is recorded (15, 16), four hundred shekels of silver.

E. The location is recorded. This location is within the land God promised to Abraham. 19 After this, Abraham buried Sarah his wife in the cave of the field of Mach-pe'lah east of Mamre (that is, Hebron) in the land of Canaan.

Clearly–the author wanted the reader to know that the occasion of Sarah’s death led to the purchase of property–why, because in short it meant that Abraham was home, in possession of a very significant piece of property!–in the symbolic place where God had promised to bless Abraham and his people into eternity!

Get the picture!

We have come to a transition moment in the history of redemption as recorded for us in Genesis–there is the concluding of one era and the beginning of another era…

Before we move on then to Isaac then Jacob and the amazing history of Joseph which will then bring young Israel into Egypt awaiting Exodus and the Mosaic period… we are told about the death of Sarah and Abraham…

–how will the author of Genesis conclude his "history of God concerning Abraham"?

If we are looking for a hero’s eulogy–we will be disappointed. For not a whole lot is said about either Abraham or Sarah. What the author does do however is give a detailed account of two things

1. the burial of Sarah

2. the securing of a Hebrew wife for Isaac

Why? The answer is way back in Gen. 12-- God covenant with Abraham in succession to his covenants with Adam, Seth, Noah… was a promise that God would himself secure the covenant blessings as represented by a unique land (Canaan) and a unique descandants as by the womb of Sarah especially (ethnic Hebrew)

-- Gen.12:1-3-- go you out to the land that I will show you… 2 I will make of you a great nation, and I will bless you, and make your name great, so that you will be a blessing. 3 I will bless those who bless you, and the one who curses you I will curse; and in you all the families of the earth shall be blessed."

There was in fact two very important aspects to God’s promise to Abraham

1. promises land

2. promised descendants

After these promises were put in jeapordy over and over again by human wisdom and power–we have come full circle where At the end of the Abrahamic era–the author will tell us two very important things.

1. The acquisition of property for Sarah’s burial–in Canaan where the temple will eventual be built and of course ultimately where Christ will be crucified, buried and will ascend unto heaven

2. The asquisition of a wife–the descendant of Sarah as it where as for Isaac (next week)

Conclusion: God had promised to Abraham that he would give him land–land that was to be a temporal and prophetic sign of the greater land offered once to Adam and promised to a covenant people by God’s own doing… Here, the author wants us to see that God made good on his promise!

--Therefore, Abraham’s search for burial "land" was itself an expression of faith in God’s promises regarding the land and his ultimate inheritance given to him and his descendants by faith

-- the circumstances that led to the purchase of a burial property is in itself an expression of God’s trustworthiness to make good on his promise–by his own power and wisdom, in a way which often confounded the p;ower and wisdom of Abraham, God has made Abraham a "land-owner" of a very special piece of property!

I.e. a prophetic sign… of things to come…

A mighty prince–or "a prince of God"gaining access to a special place by God’s own power!

Significance in Redemptive history:

This morning, we come to a moment in redemptive history where of all the things the author could tell us about the death of Sarah and Abraham, we are told about the purchase of property for a grave

Level One–In that Genesis was a prologue to the Mosaic covenant–a covenant that maintained the outward, symbolic and typological aspects of the eternal promise… this passage was first applied to the people of Moses day…

To Moses' generation; sojourners from Egypt on the verge of Canaan, about to enter the promised land.It seemed impossible to them, too good to be true. Why do they care that Abraham took title to part of Canaan? Because it is the down-payment, the provisional fulfilment of the promise that they will inherit Canaan.

Their application–to rely upon the promises of God–to plan their lives in such a way as to rely on those promises, trusting that even as God has made good on his promise to Abraham and Sarah to bring them to the promised land–so he will do in his way and in his timing after his laws–bring Israel to their promised land.

 

Level two–

Land-- NT writer can say that Abraham did and did not receive the promise–

Hebr. 6:15 And thus Abraham, having patiently endured, obtained the promise. (level one)

Hebr. 11:13 These all died in faith, not having received what was promised, but having seen it and greeted it from afar, (level two)

I.e. that what the land really represented was yet to be purchased–not until Christ purchased it with his won blood!

Accounts for the statements in NT which say that Abraham was looking beyond Canaan to something else.

Heb.11:16 But as it is, they desire a better country, that is, a heavenly one. Therefore God is not ashamed to be called their God, for he has prepared for them a city.Abraham's faith is described in terms of his seeking a heavenly city.

Hebr. 11:8 By faith Abraham obeyed when he was called to go out to a place which he was to receive as an inheritance; and he went out, not knowing where he was to go. 9 By faith he sojourned in the land of promise, as in a foreign land, living in tents with Isaac and Jacob, heirs with him of the same promise. 10 For he looked forward to the city which has foundations, whose builder and maker is God.

 

Significance for you and me today:

Illustration: Let me ask you again the same question I introduced the sermon with this morning…

Where will you be buried?

With temporal eyes–you may say "new haven" or ????

But with the eyes of faith, the author wants us to reflect on a much deeper meaning to this question–

Will you be buried in the promised land–ascended unto heaven that is?

And how ought this to be reflected in the way you live your life now!

What will you invest in

There is all this energy to make a home here, in this temporal setting… but might we be like Abraham who sought for a better country, that is a heavenly one…

How do we purchase the heavenly property?

Answer–we don’t–it is purchased only by the blood of Christ–yes worth only the cost of a paupers field to some in his day–but alone able to purchase heaven for you and me.

How do we then live our lives now?

Well, what would it look like if we really did make heaven our home?

How might we cultimate a greater love of heaven and less a love for the "things of this world."

Is this an unspiritual way of thinking? Not according to Paul… who admonished us to

1. not seek the riches of this world

2. Rather to seek the riches of heaven–even as now we life in obedience to God trusting that God’s ways are wiser and more able to possess heaven, even that beginning gheaven on earth now–than all the wisdom that I might use to do things my own way…

1 Tim.6

"but those who desrie to be rich fall into temptation and snare and into many foolish and harmful lusts which drown men in destruction and perdition… but you o man of God flee these things and pursue rigthtousness, godliness, faith, love , patience endurance… that you keep his commandments… commond those who are rich in this present age not to be haughty nor to trust in uncertain riches but in the living God who gives us richly all things to enjoy. Let them do good that they may be rich in good works ready to give and willing to share storing up for themselves a good foundation for the time to come, that they may lay hold of eternal life!

We are in short to rest our hope on nothing less than Jesus and his rightousness- as this will reveal itself by faith and a life of faith–living according to God values, God’s calling, God’s moral laws… trusting that these are the ways that lead to heaven… even if the whole world begs to differ!

 

Like the Old Covenant church (Israel) we should make plans such as to trust in God’s provision for a promised land–except we know that the land that we seek is not property in this world, but rather possession of a world to come–a heavenly and eternal world!

We are told by the writer of Hebrews exactly how we should apply the history of Abraham.

1. That We, too, should seek that city, that land, and think of ourselves as inheritors of that land. "For here we have no lasting city, but we seek the city which is to come" (Heb. 13.14)

2. But in seeking that promised land, that eternal home–we will not do this by our own strength and wisdom, but rather by God strenth and wisdom.

So what are the "signs" of a person whose grave is ascended into heaven?

It is a person who has first of all embraced Christ as God’s demonstrates his/her reliance upon God by trusting in his commands, by ordering his her life after God’s wisdom…

Let us embrace Christ and then be imitators of Christ which then evidences our embracing of Christ for a heavenly home!
