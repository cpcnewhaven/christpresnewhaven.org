Gen 25
The Contempt of Esau

(Read vs. 19ff)

Exposition:

I. The symbolism is everything.

Twins are born (in a most unusual way) the one (Jacob) is born clutching the hill of his older (by seconds) brother (Esau)—

Esau—means "reddish" or "ruddy" same word used to describe David—perhaps a reference to robustness… he is also hairy—why noted?—as it is a word play with the word that refrences the region of Edom of whom Esau will be father to the Edomites…

older by seconds, Esau would all the same have the rights of a first born under the ancient near eastern custom—the successor to the patriarch father, the guardian and head of his household such that he would be granted double his share of his fathers wealth, he would have been dedicated to God…

It’s hard to know exactly what the name "Jacob" means— perhaps derived from the word combination, "may God protect/reward-- but the author is clear that whatever it means, it is related to this most unusual of circumstances a younger twin brother clutching the heal of his older twin….circumstances that very much set the pattern for things to come…

For as the boys grow up, the robust one, Esau—becomes a great hunter and quickly gains his fathers favor for bringing fresh meat to the table…

Jacob is described as "quite" which can also mean "perfect" as under the law(Job 1:1, Gen.6:9) , which would mean "right with God" and is the apple of his mothers eye…

(Curious that again we see the mothers favorite being the "perfect one"---

Interesting even more that like Sarah before her, Rebekah was barren and after the intercession of Isaac, God grants Rebekah not one child but two—but that it is Rebecah’s "favorite son" that is the "perfect one… )

And so the story goes, the perfect one who is the younger gains the birthright of the older and in a way that is humanly speaking suspiciously cunning over agains this brothers evident carefree attitude about his great privilege as a first born—taking advantage of his brothers feriousous appetite Jacob makes him a bowl of "reddish soup" (vs. 30) having him swear by oath the benefits of the first born… the passage makes it clear later that the redness of the soup was not even the reddness of a rich meaty stew—but of a watery lentil soup…!

And so it happens, that Jacob—soon to be named Israel—is heir to the covenant of the seed born of a women… even that women who gave birth to Seth… by succesive generations begot Abraham Isaac and now Jacob!

A profound ending to an set of events—events that began In conflict albeit within the womb where the two brothers before their birth is shown to be smashing themselves within Jacob—has now ended by Jacob cunningly outwitting his brother who seemed to care less about his most cherished of possession—the rights of the first born—rights of the heirship to the seed of a women no less!!!

II. How are We to Think of All this?

A. Level One set of questions…

Do we despise Jacob who with cunning cheats his brother out of his birth right?

Or do we pity Esau—whose appetite for life made him grow dull and apathetic to his eternal rights?

Our text makes it clear…

"So Esau treated with contempt the rights of a first born"

In Moses—day this would have been significant— a reminder to those who carried the name of Jacob"s destiny—Israel—never to despise their birthright of a first born corporate child in exhange for the bounty of this world!

And this is no less the point derived from this text in our covenant context as well as specially revealed in Hebrews 12

Heb. 12:16 lest there be any fornicator or profane person like Esau, who for one morsel of food sold his birthright.

Heb. 12:17 For you know that afterward, when he wanted to inherit the blessing, he was rejected, for he found no place for repentance, though he sought it diligently with tears.

How stupid was Esau to prefer that which was fading away, that which was soon to perish over that which can never fade away

And before we judge Esau—we should consider ourselves and the countless analogies of the same spirit within us in everyday life…

For how many will give away precious things for not so precious things…

I.e.

We give up family happiness in pursuit of prestige

We loose our children in pursuit of grade up in lifestyle.

We loose our self-respect and personal integrite when we cheat, lie or scheme on a test or deal or income tax—merely for things that over the course of life will usually amount to nothing…

It is this same spirit then, that all of us possess—that will apart from God work within us… grow increasingly apathetic to the glorious tresures that are gained by Jacob’s ultimate sucessor—Jesus Christ—where the eternal birth rights of the eternal first born are squandere for such things as are in comparison a bowl of lentil soup… enslaved to our worldly appitites that we forfeit the world… wanting to gain life, we lose it!

1 Tim.6:7 for we brought nothing into the world, so that we can take nothing out of it; 8 but if we have food and clothing, we will be content with these. 9 But those who want to be rich fall into temptation and are trapped by many senseless and harmful desires that plunge people into ruin and destruction. 10 For the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil, and in their eagerness to be rich some have wandered away from the faith and pierced themselves with many pains.

And so, at level one, Jacob, "right with God" loved what Esau despised—and to this day, we say the "God of Abraham, Isaac and Jacob (not Esau)

The first level point as according to Hebrews—do not show contempt for that which is eternal and more precious than even life itself! Pray God to put in you a new spirit such a Jacob’s and not Esau…

B. A second level of questions:

Is this the way God preserves for himself a people destined for mercy and not wrath? Through scheming and cunning of men—is the point only that Jacob loved what Esau despised…

Take a closer look!

The greater theme is God differentiating for himself a nation of peoples…

2x’s the familiar phrase that divides Genesis into 10 parts shows up in this single chapter

A. The Close of Abraham’s Life (1-11)

Marriage to Keturah and bearing of sons only serves to highlight the emphasis on Isaac. (Note: all this not necessarily prior to Sarah’s death).

Abraham’s death finds him being buried in the promised land, a portion of which he owns.

Now our principal concern will be with Isaac.

B. The Descendants of Ishmael (12-18) "these are the generations of

Fulfillment of God’s promise that Ishmael would become a great nation—but not the covenant nation through whome would come redemption…

You will remember that Ishmeal also looked with contempt upon the miricle child—Isaac, given to Abraham and Sarah by divine intervention!

Later—Esau would marry into the nation of Ishmeal!

C. 19) ¶ These are the descendants of Isaac,

God is dividing the peoples of the world yet another time…

Again we see the election of God that is in view—which directs us not to the human circumstances of cheated birthrights, but of a God who is orchestrating the events of the world to make good his promise to Adam and Eve good…

See how the prophet Malachi interprets these events.

Mal. 1:1 ¶ The burden of the word of the Lord to Israel by Malachi. "I have loved you,'' says the Lord. "Yet you say, "In what way have You loved us?' Was not Esau Jacob's brother?'' Says the Lord. "Yet Jacob I have loved; But Esau I have hated, And laid waste his mountains and his heritage For the jackals of the wilderness.''

I.e. Used to guarentee God’s love for a people that have now over and over again proved themselves to be a stiff necked people!

So what about now, under the new covenant?

Rom. 9:10 And not only this, but when Rebecca also had conceived by one man, even by our father Isaac

Paul’s problem: his kinsmen according to the flesh are lost. What about the faithfulness of God to his promises?

Answer: God’s word has not failed. Not all Israel is Israel.

--And just because you’re part of the seed of Abraham doesn’t mean you’re a child of Abraham.

CASE IN POINT—ISAAC vs ISHMEAL

Children named through Isaac, the child of promise.

JEWS RESPOND: But there’s a loophole. Isaac and Ishmael had the same father, Abraham, but a different mother. In fact, Ishmael was the son of a slave girl. Perhaps that explains the difference. BUT WE ARE SECURE IN BEING ISAAC’S DESCENDANTS…

So Paul produces an example that closes the loophole: Jacob and Esau. One father, one mother, one conception. Come into existence under identical circumstances.

Yet

Before they were born,

Before they had done anything good or bad,

In order that God’s purpose of election might continue,

Not because of works but because of his call…

We have God’s word to Rebekah in Gen. 25: "The older will serve the younger."

And then that startling phrase from the prophet Malachi: "Jacob I loved, but Esau I hated."

POINT:

Against false security in ethnic lineage—Paul shows that our only security is in the election of God—not ethnic idenity!

Good news—those loved of God are loved on no condition of their own, not their skin color, not their works, nothing—

Application for Paul:

Take no security except in the divine work of God!

I,e 1. Make sure your election—do you see the hand of God in your life as evidenced by being born again—i.e. a work of the spirit that is changing you inside out… i.e. nominal membership won’t do

2. On the other hand, for those who are born again, be sure of your election—it is God who preservers you, it is God who loves you with a love that will never let you go!

FOR EVEN though this principle applied originally to nations and their histories, Paul is applying it to individuals and their eternities. How do we know this? The context of vv. 1-5 (exp).

So what’s Paul saying. That the determination of who receives the eternal mercies of God is based on precisely the same principle as the determination of which nations received God’s favor: A sovereign choice, not based on anything the creature could or would do, but only on God’s perfect plan and will.

 

Summary (exp). Thus we know it’s not merely a prediction that was made to Rebecah when God said the younger will be served but the older-- . It’s a predermination on the part of God, not based on anything in Jacob or Esau, but simply on God’s sovereign purpose. Could anything be plainer? And this is GOOD NEWS!

Unconditional election.

Oh, but it’s not unconditional election for the sake of unconditional election as a dogmatic assertion alone—

Paul’s point even as Moses point in recording the story is that our security in the covenant mercies of God are NOT according to anything we must do or complete—but on what God does, even from all eternity!

Rom. 8:29

For those whom he foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son, in order that he might be the firstborn within a large family.

Unconditional grace! (as goes unconditional election, so goes unconditional grace and vice versa)

YOU ARE SAVED BY GRACE—AND THAT NOT OF YOURSELF, IT IS THE FREE GIFT OF GOD…
