–and we are now in the great room of antiquity called Genesis, filled with magnificent tapestries telling the single story of God’s activity in order to bless a peculiar and set apart people who are descendants of a promised seed of a women… Of course, the story begins with tragedy–the tabestry is filled with the brush strokes of crimson red–with the blood of death over the green of life… and yet, in the midst of the crimson red is the hint of gold–a faint glitter hardly noticable to the eye but clearly there… it is the seed of promise–a promise to bless with such things as was only imaginable before the tragedy, but preserved for a people all the same that God has chosen..

As we tour the tabestries, the faint gold of blessing becomes more clear, more bold even as the context of crimson red together with its spider web of activity is weaved in and out around and over the gold–and in the gold are the scenes of ancient descendants–pictures of Seth, of Noah, of Abraham and of Isaac and God’s activity in saving them and their posterity…

And today we find ourselves staring at another great tapestry–it is the golden epiphany which surrounds Jacob– bruch strokes now of gold which are contained in a surrounding brush strokes of intrigue, suspence even deceit–and if it weren’t for the emerging gold, we would be tempted to stare at the dullness and earthy tones of human enterprize–of humanity rather than at divinity! But herein lies the secret to understanding these tapestries–we must keep our focal point on the gold, the seed itself and the redemptive activity of God which points it…

So as we stare then at this tapestry–you must not take your eyes off the gold– the epiphany of blessing that is at the heart of the story…

Exposition:

I. A Tapestry Framed in Marriages

Notice how the tabestry is carefully framed with all sorts of marriages…

26:34-35–Esau marries Hittite wives (outside of Abrahamic-Isaac lineage)

27-28:5–All the details related to Jacob receiving the blessing of the first born rather than Esau and the thwarting of Esau’s revenge against Jacob resulting in Jacob being sent to Paddan-Aram to find a wife who is of the family of Abraham…

28:6-9-- Esau marries an Ishmaelite (again, outside the blessed line of Abraham to Isaac)

For Jacob–marriage into the covenant line, Esau into the non-covenant line…

i.e The account of Esau’s loss of the first-born blessing is carefully bracketed with a record of his marriages outside the line of promise–this in itself speaks volumes as to what the author is wanting us to understand from this passage…

For the golden epiphany of blessing is by the seed of a women–and already, we have seen the maticulous detail that has been given to the marriages of Abraham and Isaac as to Hebrew women.

When it is remembered how God supernaturally interveened to preserve the seed as through Abraham’s Hebrew wife Sarah rather than by Abraham’s maid-servant Hagar

When Abraham’s intense concern that Isaac should on no account marry a Canaanite is recalled, it is somewhat disturbing that our passages opens with a description of Esau, Isaac’s firstborn of twins, who marries not one but two Canaanite women! And with commentary no less that these women caused great disturbances (bitterness) to Isaac’s family.

What do we see in this–we see already how Esau seems not to care much about Abraham’s principle of not marrying Canaanites–an observation that seems to implicate both Isaac for not going to the extreemes of his father in securing a wife from the covenant family line, or of Esau himself.

Such indifference to the demands of Abraham’s principle and the "seed of a woman" context for the posterity of blessing is exactly where we must start with this story–recognizing already that Esau, like Ishmeal before him–shows contempt for the promise and the gold which has emerged in this tapestry of covenant.

Indeed–the story will end in 28:9 with Esau joining Ishmeal’s geneology as through marriage to Mahalat–the daughter of Ishmael Even as Rebeccah, arranges for Jacob to marry out of the covenant line by going to Param-

II. A Tapestry filled with the Earthy tones of human deceit and suspense:

As noted earlier, as we look upon this grand tapestry, we are tempted to make the earthy tones of human deceit and suspense our main focal point–indeed, the tapestry is busy with such earthy colors!

As framed in the women of Esau–the focus of the earthy colors throughout is clearly upon Rebecah’s naked appeal to maternal authority as excercised with deceit and cunning.

Scene one: 27:1-5–has Rebekah secretly listening in the wings as Isaac speaks with his oldest son (by seconds) about his plans to bless him with the blessing of the first born.

This is of course not merely a symbolic expression of favoritism, but rather an oath taken in the presence of God–even as Rebekah understands this in vs. 7 as she describes to Jacob the seriousness of what Isaac is about to do to Esau when she notes about the oath that it would be made "in the Lord’s presence." This was the covenant blessing in the form of an oath which then was binding upon all parties lest the wrath of God would be invited upon the parties for their not keeping the oath.

-- oath taking then, as it ought to be today–was a very serious thing!

Eccl. 5:4 When you make a vow to God, do not delay fulfilling it; for he has no pleasure in fools. Fulfill what you vow. 5 It is better that you should not vow than that you should vow and not fulfill it.

-- in fact, legal evidence from ancient sources show that the oral wills delievered by a dying father and cited in court were of decisive validity–thay had in short an oath character to them. — this was serious business and the whole story is focused on this decisive moment.

(the reader of course already knows the metanarrative which has Jacob, not Esau, as receiving th blessing of the first born according to God’s prophetic omen as recorded at their birth–the whole of this tapestry is colored with this expectations, such as God is here shown to be working even in a way that will transcend the most vile and ugly of deceptions…such is the mystery and power of God–that can make all things work out for good to those called according to his purpose… )

Given the way Esau had flouted family custon by marrying two Canaanite wives and the distress this action had caused upon the parents–it should come as a surprize to find Isaac on his deathbed preparing to bless Esau–but again, the text alludes to the earthly appitites of Isaac as seeming to play a role in his decision making–even as in vs. 3-4 Rebecah secretly listens to Isaac as he instructs Esau to "go into the field and hunt me some game… so as to "make me a tasty stew that I love."

And so, scene one is represented by the Brush stroke of some earthy color–perhaps olive green with hints of black…

Scene two: 27:6-17– It is now a pea green strokes– has Rebekah taking command of the situation as she schemes a plan which will result in Jacob receiving the blessing.

From secretly listening to scheming, Rebekah orchestrates and nearly does everthing which eventually results in Isaac being deceived into blessing Jacob rather than Esau–she makes the plan, commands what seems to be a rather passive albeit compliant Jacob as to each step he is to take, she makes the clothing, the artificial hair and even the stew itself.

Only three hebrew words describe Jacob’s actions–he went, took and brought–everhyting else decribes Rebekah’s actions. Rebecah, the women from whom the seed in secuccsion to the great seed of promise, is the focual point of our passage.. Something greater even than she is happening here which accounts for why the author has Rebecah as the central figure of all this…

Scene three: 27:18-29-- now the sole actors are Jacob and Isaac–the supense is great as at several points it seems as though Isaac is suspicious even as Rebecah’s plan seems about to be exposed.

For one, it seems as though Jacob nearly gives it away by talking too much.

Isaac then wants to feel him and asks him to come close

He finally asks him–"are you really my son Esau? To which the now sweating Jacob response tersely "I am"!

Of course, all the while the reader is tense as it is understood that any time now Esau could come back for his blessing.

But finally–Isaac blessed Jacob–the blessing itself given poetically such as to be reminiscent of both the Abrahamic covenant looking forward to the promised land and descendants as then promised to Jacob, not Esau, at their birth. (see later)

Scene three: 27:30-40--

III. A Tapestry That Accents the Golden Epiphany of Divine Blessing

CONCLUSION: As with most tapestries of art, we must Take a step back in order to look at the real picture contained among all the rich colors and lines.. What’s going on here? Tapestry of Divine Blessing…

The point of the whole passage might rightly be summarized in vs. 23–so he blessed him

I.e. the golden epiphany of this tapestry is God’s activity to bless and not curse a chosen people and in such a way as is clearly not dependant upon man but upon God!

This explains the authors detailed account of God’s prophetic decrees at the birth narrative of Esau and Jacob–even as this narrative clearly wants to refer back to this…

2. A second inference from this story could be this-- T David-- GOD’S BLESSING DOES NOT MEAN GOD’S APPROVAL.

A fairly simple truth, but easily confused because of what happened next in the history of redemption;the Sinai covenant

In the covenant made at Sinai, God made a conditional arrangement with the Israelites, that they would inherit and prosper in the land of Canaan if they were obedient, and that they would be cursed if they were disobedient. Attainment of the covenant blessings of Sinai was dependent both on God’s faithfulness and the faithfulness of the Israelites. In the Sinai covenant, God’s blessing did mean God’s approval.

In the new covenant, as with the covenant God made with Abraham, the attainment of the covenant blessings is dependent on God, and on God alone. GOD’S BLESSING DOES NOT MEAN GOD’S APPROVAL.

1. Church considerations: God’s blessings are not a sign of God’s approval. "They must be doing omething right."

2. Individual considerations: God’s blessings are not a sign of God’s approval. If things are going well for you, don’t be fooled into thinking you must be doing something right. Such complacency could destroy you.

If things are not going well for you, count it all joy to share the sufferings of Christ. You may be doing very well, and experiencing the rich experience of a loving God, who disciplines whom He loves.

 

If you ever get confused about this, simply remember the cross. The only perfectly obedient human who ever lived suffered, and died on a cross. And yet, in His self-offering He was loved and approved by the Father; and the fatherly approval expressed at his baptism followed Him to his tomb: "THIS IS MY SON, IN WHOM I AM WELL PLEASED."

COMMUNION MEDITATION: The Gospel message is not about how God’s mercy depends upon our faithfulness. Rather, it is a message about how God’s mercy prevails in spite of human unfaithfulness.

God brings salvation through death of his son; All things work for good; God fulfills the promises of his covenant in spite of and sometimes even through our sin.

Let us come to the Lord’s Table rejoicing that we have such a mighty Savior who saves to the uttermost!!!
