Expositional Introduction:

Redemptive Context

The enmeity between the serpent's seed and the woman's seed was that of rival kingdoms for the possession of the world... (Mt.4:8ff, 1 Peter 1:4; 2 Peter.3:13, Rev. 11:15; 12:10) By the time of Noah, Genesis has given us two histories...

1. The history of Adam through Cain... Lamech and the eschalation of sin-- so much so that all apperances are such as to suggest the extinction of the women's seed.

2. The history Adam through Seth-- the women's seed which though small is divnines preserved as a people after God's own special covenant...

When therefore, the remnant people of Noah's household were on the verge of being driven from the face of the earth, their covenant protector could delay no longer to repsond to their appeal-- God's redemptive name-- Yahweh-- was identified with those 8 souls, such that all stops would be pulled in order to protect them from extinction!

That the serpent's seed even survived was due to the forebearance of God, "not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentence... " It was only because of God's merciful common grace for the sake of his self-revelation that the non-elect line shared common status with the elect line in the temporal blessings of the world... I.e. as long as the line of Adam through Seth was incomplete according to God's foreknowledge an plan, there would be a common grace to preserve civilization from total analiation... i.e. the revelatory plan of God was not complete such as to warrant his total judgment against sin... even the history of redemption itself was part of God's plan for self-relvelation.. this account of Noah being one of the most notable of all revelatory events... !

I. The Ark of Salvation

Amazing attention and detail is given to the construction and inhabitants of an ark-- somehow, whatever this episode of redemptive history is about-- it is focused on an ark!

This Heb. word for "ark" occurs only one other time in the OT-- would you want to guess there that is?

Exodus 2:3, 5-- the basket in which baby Moses was hidden

What did that basket represent then but God's divine intervention for safe passage into the salvation planned for Isreal.

This ark-- whereas it is not responsible for saving Noah and Moses, was the means of grace given by God in order to affect his decree to save his special elect people from destruction.

This ark for Moses later-- was safe passage as against the political and military might of Eqypt against Israel-- it was safe passage as against Pharoh's decree that all first born should be destroyed-- it was the "safe-house" that protected nothing less than God's representative who would deliver his people from destruction into a promised land!

Here-- it is the safe-house for Noah and those under his jurisdiction... -- it was safe passage as thorugh the jugment waters of God's wrather-- haveing the dual effect of both destruction and salavation-- destroying those who after the line of Cain would bring destruction upon the line of Seth-- saving the elect from extinction at the hands of the "giants"-- those who through poltical might would bring destruction to the line of Seth-- who after the line of Cain were apostate and antagonistic to the religion of Yahweh!

What are we to think of this ark if according to the author's intention?

A safe-house--

Nothing is said of the other seven. Plainly, they board the ark ON THE BASIS OF NOAH'S RIGHTEOUSNESS. Confirmed by 7:23, "Only Noah was left, and those that were with him in the ark."

Nothing said about them other than their relation to Noah. Does not say that they obeyed God (though twice says that Noah obeyed God completely),

6.22, 7.5.

Does not say they were righteous, they were faithful, they were obedient, or that they walked with God.

Why are they spared, then?

Federal representation...

Noah's qualifications:

Noah was a righteous man, blameless in his generation; Noah walked with God. Three things said about him: righteous, walked with God, blameless "in his generation," about which generation Moses goes on to say twice that it is "filled with violence" and three times that it is "corrupt" or "corrupted" in the space of two verses, 6.11,12.

Note "works" principle that is here clearly in view-- Noah's typological works (note his later sin) as fulfilled in Christ..

Key-- Noah achieved creation mandate and his rewards are extended to all those represented by him...
