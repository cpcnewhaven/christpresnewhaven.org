Introduction:

One of the more remarkable sights in God's created order is the rainbow. We behold its breathdaking beauty. Young and old alike are fascinated by its brilliant colors, its spanning the sky.

But do we appreciate its significance? Some wonder if there's a pot of gold at the end. Others may think of Jesse Jackson's "Rainbow Coalition." Still others think of the rainbow as a symbol of the New Age Movement.

But God has already laid claim to the rainbow. Long before fairy tales, long before Jesse Jackson, long before the new age movement, God assigned a meaning to the rainbow. But what does it mean?

IV God's Common Grace Sign

a. Covenant is restated in connection to sign given

b. Sign of bow

In general, it means what the covenant means: a withholding of God's judgment. But what are the nuances of the symbolism?

-- described as a "bow"

The words for "bow" and "rainbow" are the same in Hebrew. Bow is seen throughout Scripture as part of God's deadly arsenal, by which he shoots his arrows of judgment to pierce the wicked.

Ps. 7:12-13; 18:14; 64:7; 144:6; Lam. 2:4; 3:12;

Hab. 3:11.

In case of rainbow, the bow is not raised vertically and drawn taut, but is in the relaxed horizontal position. Ancient Near Eastern pictures of kings. Do you see the symbolism here? God, for a time, has laid aside his weapons of war, and is committed to terms of peace with his creation.

-- seen as coming out of a dark cloud

"Shining upon a dark ground, it represents the victory of the light of love over the fiery darkness of wrath. Originating from the effect of the sun upon a dark cloud, it typifies the willingness of the heavenly to penetrate the earthly. Stretched between heaven and earth, it is as a bond of peace between both, and, spanning the horizon, it points to the all-embracing universality of the Divine mercy." (Delitzsch)

2. A second issue facing us relative to all this is on how to define life-- Abortion

What is clear by this passage is that persons, unlike any other creature, are unique in their being made in the image of God such as to protect life is to sanctify God in a certain sense. All Christians must therefore agree that taking life outside of just war is wrong.

Question: How do we define human life, for once we do, it deserves the protection of common grace against murder.

Hermeneutic observation: The command is stated such as to establish the burden of proof on those who would take life. I.e.

Explain "burden of proof"-- it buts a bias in favor of one side or another so for instance, the burden of proof rest with the occuser not the accused to determine a persons guilt in our system such that a person is "innocent until proven guilty."

Regardless of how you feel about this system of justice in general, our passage makes it clear that the concern is the sanctify of life so as to preserve the sanctify of God himself.

Therefore, the burden of proof must be "life until proven not to be life"

This is crucial it seems in the debate..

Why

1. The scripture assumes that "human life" is recognized and readily apparant. The burden of proof is again to prove that something we might suspect is human life is not human life. IT is not defined in such manner as to enter into modern debate.

2. The reason this is so important is that the Bible doesn't in some proof text manner define life as within the modern controversies. There is not scripture that I am aware of that defines life at conception for instance. And I am aware of such places like Exodus 21:22ff, Pss. 139, Rom 9 and the recognition of say John the Baptist in the womb of Martha, etc. Yet the case can me made, rightly or wrongly, that these texts do not speak to the issue of when life begins, but rather the foreknowledge of God. etc.

3. However, the burden of proof that IS clearly prescribed is that sanctity is assumes such that one must prove that something that has any relation to human life is in fact not human life. So for instance, while a single skin cell is related to human life and I am told have all the DNA it needs to be human, but all the samedoesn't have the capacity to be fully human without some genetic engeneering. For this reason, the single skin cell can be destroyed withou this being labled as taking innocent life...

4. So what has become a key issue is defining human life after the principle of potential viability. Now hear what I mean by this under the burden of proof. What is NOT needed is independent viability-- if so, then in one sense no one, including those who are born and even adult-- could survive the text since all people are in some sense dependant upon others for their viability. In the case of an infant, this dependance is clearly the case yet no one would advocate legally killing babies after they are born. So the burden of proof must be to assume human life that is therefore sacred until proven not to be human life. Given this burden of proof, while there are successive stages of "dependency" once could arge that any stage following conception, while dependent on many things perhaps, is human life all the same and therefore sacred.

By virtue of the burden of proof, we do believe then that life is sacred as from conception such that "just war" ought to be used to protect life after conception.

Again, Just war is not the perogative of any person, but of government as appointed by God in its governing capacity. Rom. 13 as with Gen. 9 makes it clear that the power of the sword is given to governments, not individuals. We must work within the limits of government, not around it-- anarchy is wrong and two wrongs don't make a right!

One more observation-- While we may therefore conclude that killing an embryo is killing a person, we don't then accuse those who do this as "murders." To do this would be to beg the very question being debated. To murder means to kill a person that you know to be a person. While we do believe that it is taking innocent life to destroy a conceived human cell pattern, the intention of the person is not to take innocent life. We must get beyond the political rhetoric and get with the project of civil discourse and conversatoin for th sake of persuasion. In so far as our government is "by the people" we need to change the minds of the people before we will be governed in this area as we believe that we should.

B. Issues relative to Science in Relation to Common Grace...

Science itself must not be viewed as negative, it is one aspect of the providence of God.

Yet on the other hand, science can't be viewed as morally neutral either-- it can either serve or destroy the common grace covenant...

J.A. Robertson in an article entitled: The Scientist's Right to Research"

Science is not an unmitigated blessing. It is expensive and its discoveries like the tree of knowledge in Eden, expand man's capacity for evil as well as good. More knowledge is not a good in itself, nor is it necessarily productive of net good. Socieity, as the provideer of the resources, the bearer of the costs, and the reaper of the benefits, has an overriding interest in th econsequences of science, hence an interest in the direction and the routes that research takes.

I.e. it is the general publics right to scrutinize science, even as it is the church responsibility to bring its own ethical criteria to bear upon the issues that are raised by science.

Take for example the whole issue of Genetic research and Cloning--- "A dilemma for the 21st century...

Based upon new recombinant DNA technology--and the discovery of enzymes that break DNA apart at unique places, enzymes that can seal together fragments of DNA that were broken apart, elements that replicate within cells such as viruses or plasmids, procedures to transfer this DN A from one cell to another-- all have opened up a new field of biotechnology leading to enzymes and medicinals that can be cloned in bacterial and mammalian cells including the ability to deliver genes into devloping embryos, and as recently reported in sheep, the successful cloning of a mammal through somatic cell neculear transplantation. This unleashed a moral outcry that was amplified by the statement of Dr. Richard Seed of Chicago who proposed to open a comercial human cloning clinic.

Make it simple-- we are now capable of "messing with life" at a fundamental level...

How does "comman grace" as in the sanctify of life relate to all this?

At the very most basic level-- the question must be asked

Does this procedure or outcome protect life or destroy life, not an easy question, and the burdon of proof must be applied...

Dr. Frank Young...

3. The Sacrifice of Noah anticipating the Sacrifice of Christ

Common grace is subservient to the purposes of special grace? That common grace is intended to preserve a context for God's redemptive plan to unfold culminating in the last revelation in Christ?

For one greater than Noah has come, a man whose righteousness was perfect. And he came to present an offering of blood. Not the blood of bulls and goats, not the blood that had to be offered time after time under Moses, but his own blood, a blood offered once for all to take away sin. Jesus Christ himself is that perfect offering. "Christ loved us, and gave himself up for us, a fragrant offering and sacrifice to God." (Eph. 5:2) It was that offering and that offering alone that could perfectly satisfy the justice of God, completely turn away his wrath from his people.

And upon the basis of that perfect sacrifice, God was please to make a covenant, a new covenant, by which he would not count our sins against us, but forgive us, and accept us as righteous in his sight. And for those under this covenant, they know of God's favor not only for a lifetime, not only for the duration of this world, but for ever and ever.

Ultimately then the sacrifice of Noah as it forshadows the ultimate sacrifice in Christ is all the more appreciated in light of common grace stipulation.
