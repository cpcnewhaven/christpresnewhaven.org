Exposition:

I. Interpretative question:

Moses has selectively narrated the story of redemption leading up to the Sinah covenant-- at special places redemptive history, the story "slows down" so as to illustrate some special moment and theology relative to God's salvation plan.

Why this episode? Of all the things that happened in the span of thousands of years, why tell about this particular episode in the life of Noah?

As to the story itself-- it is a one of most bizarre and perplexing stories in all of Bible.

• What has happened to the righteous man, the blameless man, the man who walked with God?

• What has Ham done that is so wrong? Surely many of us have seen things we wish we hadn't seen. But does this make us guilty of some sin.

• Why is a curse pronounced on Canaan, Ham's son, instead of Ham?

• What is meant by the blessing upon Shem --and Japheth dwelling in the tents of Shem?

These are some of the knots we must unravel if we are to understand the mind of God in this portion of Scripture and make application to our lives.

II. First Clue: Genesis 3

Already, we have seen that the Flood is told by Moses so as to describe the destruction of the old world and the remaking of a new world-- According to Moses, the flood was nothing less than a new creation! Peter reinforces this idea when he refers to the world before the flood as the "world that then was" and then the world after the flood unto the present as a "world that now is."

After the first Creation, there was a fall which led to promises and curses.

Is it coincidental that after the second creation there is yet another "fall" leading to promises and curses? We think not...

Notice:

• Both events occur at the beginning of a new world.

• One mentions a garden, the other a vineyard.

20, And Noah began to be a farmer, and he planted a vineyard.

• Both Adam and Noah are initially "blameless."

• One falls by partaking of the fruit of the tree, the other falls by partaking of the fruit of the vine.

21 Then he drank of the wine and was drunk, and became uncovered in his tent.

Even as the ground was cursed in Gen 3, so from out of the ground came a second fall in Gen. 9

• Both lie uncovered in shameful nakedness.

• Both have their nakedness covered.

I.e In this story, like in Gen 3, nakedness and what happens in relation to nakedness is the the focus of attention.

• In both there is the pronouncement of curse and division.

• In both there is the pronouncement of blessing.

• Both contain far-reaching prophecies.

Is this mere coincidence? No--it is a deliberated re-telling of the Fall. Thus we should be looking for the same principles, the same spiritual realties, the same Gospel promises, simply in a different form. And as I hope to show you, that's exactly what we find.

II. A Second Clue: The Table of Nations

From the very beginning of this passage, we are told that the story is less about Noah, and more about his three sons!

More specifically: Shem, Ham and Japheth.

But notice two very important details that Moses wanted to hightly in vs. 1-2.

1. That Ham as the father of Canaan

I.e. that whatever happens here-- it is happening to a nation-- the Canaanites

2. That Moses interest transcends the immediate episode is again demonstrated by vs. 19

And these were the sons of Noah and from these the whole earth was populated...

• The word trans. here "populated" is better translated "dispersed" or "scattered"

I.e. this episode anticipates a strategic dispersal of humanity into three very distinct and important groups.

3. This is especially significant in light of Chapt 10 and the "Table of Nations" in Chapter 10:

And Notice how chapter 10 will conclude in vs. 32.

These were the families (geneologies of the sons of Noah according to their generations... in their nations and from these the nations were divided on the earth after the flood...

Point: Ham, Shem, Jepheth are here representing three distinct "nations" -- Ham, who is cursed, Shem, who is blessed and Jephet who will be invited into Shem's tent-- i.e. the blessings of Shem.

While there is a real historical event here, we disover that these event are typological in signficance, for if you were to trace out these three brothers into their repective nations, you will discover this..

1. Ham-- as the father of Canaan-- represents the seed of the serpent in conflict with the promised seed that is deposited in Israel

2. Shem-- is the father Abraham to Moses and of Israel which means "son of God" and represents the "seed of promise" that will be in conflict with the seed of the serpent

3. Jepheth-- Gentiles, especially the Greeks-- who will be allowed to enter into Shem's tent-- i.e. to become equal inheritors of the deliverance accomplished by the promised seed

This is more than spectacular literature! This is spectacular history! And this is spectacular prophecy as well.

Typology-- not an allegory, not an illustration, but real events in history so construed by an omnipotent God that they foreshadow and depict patterns and spiritual realities that will be accomplished in latter history.

I.e. in this case, as with Sinah itself-- the historical events serve as types BOTH to the redemptive history of the past and the redemptive history of the future..

Therefore,

1. We have here nothing less than a new creation with a new fall and a new promise and curse-- the events are typological with respect to the Gen 3.

2. We have here nothing less than a prophecy in the form of types (typology) of what is to come.

• Ham...Canaan-- who would be defeated by Israel in the conquest of th promised land and will ultimatly be defeated by the true Israel in Christ in conquest of heaven itself! Ham is acting as in the seed of the serpent.

• Shem-- Israel-- who will be the guardian of the promised seed as through its priesthood will protect and guard the holy sanctuary of God under the Law of Moses-- this Israel under the OC will prove however to be insufficient in the end so as to direct us to Christ who by his own perfect sacrifice will sanctify a sanctuary made without hands and will deliver the elect back into the blessed presence of God in the garden of delight. Shem is acting as in the seed of the promise.

• Jepheth-- Greeks Gentiles(Ephesus, etc) Perhaps a forgotten scheme in OC history, but that God's purpoes for Israel was always to include the Gentiles being brought into the "tent" of meeting-- Thus the court of Gentiles that was abused in Christ's day leading to his condemnation of the Temple! Jepheth, in association with Shem-- is acting as those who will be grafted into the promised seed!

Therfore, in light of these two very important clues as derived both from the text and the context, lets look at the story its itself-- a kind of...

III. Story Within the Story

A. The Remaining Problem of Nakedness

Noah impeccable up to this point. But just as Adam fell from the height of perfect righteousness, so Noah was to fall from godly integrity into grievous sin.

Man of soil, proceeds to plant a vineyard. Drinks wine, becomes drunk, lays uncovered inside tent--

Noah'w sin:

--Nothing wrong with having a vineyard; nothing wrong with drinking wine--it's a blessing from God.

wine was seen as one of God's gifts to man (Ps.104:15). Every burnt or peace offering had to be accompaned by a libation of wine (Num.15:5-10 , Dt.14:26) Indeed, the vine was one of Israel's national symbols indicative of divine blessing (Isa 5:1-7, Mk.12:1-11)

--But drunkenness is a sin. And it's the sin that brings righteous, blameless, walking with God Noah into a sinful and miserable condition. He's a new Adam, and he has fallen from a great height. Symbolized by his shameful nakedness as he lies in his tent in a drunken stupor.

However! Might think that this is the focal sin. It's not. In fact, it's almost incidental compared to the sin of Ham (22).

Notice that the whole event focuses upon Noah's nakedness and what happened relative to it

Nakedness in public was considered not only demeaning (2 Sam.6:16) but was viewed as incompatible with living in the presence of God (Ex.20:26, Dt.23:13ff)

-- in fact the real focus of the story is less upon Noah and more upon Ham vs. Shem and Jepthah And something happened that was so horrible as to curse upon Ham and all the Canaanites after him...

-- What was Ham's sin: We know that this is a great sin because of the curse that comes in v. 25. But what is it? How can simply looking at someone be a great sin?

There's looking...and then there's looking. There's the look of a moment of noticing. And there's the look that lingers, in lust, in contempt, in rejoicing over the fall of another. That's what's going on here. Also has to do with a lack of honor and respect for his father.

Ham tells brothers, not out of sorrow or concern, but out of ridicule. Even brings out Noah's clothes as a trophy of his discovery. (It is THE garment that Shem and Japheth take in).

Noah awakes from his drunknness and discovers what Ham has done. Delivers a prophecy in response to the events that had transpired.

B. Nakedness Exposed leading to Curse

1. Ham is depicted as behaving in a manner that exposes him as one of the serpents seed. For in the fall of humanity, Satan had contrived to bring Adam and his wife into a conditio nof shameful nakedness... so too this narrative portrays Ham when he maliciously aggravates the shame of his drunken father. Ham is a Satan figure, rejoicing in the fall and shameful nakedness of the man of God who has fallen into sin.

2. Curse:

9:25, a servant of servants will he be to his brothers-- expresses the rejection of Canaan over against the election of his brothers (note Esau and Jacob as representing the elect and non-elect lines) such that the enmity instituted between the seed of the woman and the seed of the serpent in the Gen. 3:14-15 curse would come to a strikingly real historical expressing in the antithesis between the Canaanites and Israel.

Canaan is mentioned in the curse rather than Ham, his father-- where by a word pun is introduced-- the curse of servitude was suggested by the very sound of the name Canaan-- for the work "kana" means "subdue" and is used to describe later God's jubjugation of the Canaanites under the Israelits at the time of Israels entry into the promised land (Dt. 9:3, Jdg.4:23, Neh 9:24)

[Deut. 9:3 "Therefore understand today that the Lord your God is He who goes over before you as a consuming fire. He will destroy them and bring them down before you; so you shall drive them out and destroy them quickly, as the Lord has said to you.

Judg. 4:23 ¶ So on that day God subdued Jabin king of Canaan in the presence of the children of Israel.

Neh. 9:24 So the people went in and possessed the land; you subdued before them the inhabitants of the land, the Canaanites, and gave them into their hands, with their kings and the people of the land, that they might do with them as they wished. ]

C. Nakedness Covered leading to Blessing

1. Shem-- responds very differntly to his fathers nakedness-- rather than rejoicing, Shem takes th lead in covering his fathers nakedness so as to cover his shame!

Blessing: Typoligical meaning of the blessing of Shem-- "Shem" means literally "name"-- such that the nenediction on Shem assumes the form of a doxology" Blessed by Yahweh the God of Shem." Shem then is noted as the father of those who would be named with God and would call upon God's name.. the hallmark of the covenant community and the promised seed in the line of seth and the line of Shem is in uniting them with the name of God... pin-ultimately, Israel would be the Name bearer of God... but ultimately, Christ would be the Israel of God himself who would receive doxology as by the name of God!

 

2. Jepheth-- In assisting his brother Shem in covering the shame of his father Noah, Jepheth also receives a blessing:

May God enlarge to Japheth: lit. "may god open (entice, invite in) up to Japheth" and "dwell" (neighbor") in the tent of Shem...

Ie.. Blessing with huge eschatological significance...

Japheth-- moves beyond the stage of the Mosaic era and its ethnic Jewish particularism into the NC era and the inclusion of the Gentiles... These "two brothers" represent later the Jews and Greeks that will together share in the ultimate redemptive work of the seed promised... (Acts 14:27, 1 Cor.16:8)

Acts 14:27 Now when they had come and gathered the church together, they reported all that God had done with them, and that He had opened the door of faith to the Gentiles.

Mainpoint: After the flood, the plan of redemption remains the same as through the seed of promise in conflict with the serpents seed.

We are perhaps discouraged that even after the great and miraculous events of the flood that saved the promised seed from extinction-- there still remains the reality of sin that needs redemption...

However, we are concouraged to discover that God's plan of redemption remains in tact after the flood.. and that here we have in the historical narrative a typology of greater and more profound events to follow...

We see here the classic idea that the promised seed will be in conflict with the serpents seed and this will result in many being cursed and many being saved!

Our passages leads to a table of nations-- nations that ultimately foreshadow three types of people
