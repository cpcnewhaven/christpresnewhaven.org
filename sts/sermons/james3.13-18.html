James 3:13-18
Who is Wise?

Introduction:
Who is wise? Is it the same as being crafty? Is it someone who makes alot of money? Is it someone who has alot of degrees? Is it someone who smokes a pipe, or perhaps has gray hair? Is a wise person someone who is "successful" in worldly terms? Are these things the characteristics of a wise person? We have in James 3:13-18 a discription of wisdom that above all else informs us as to the practical nature of wisdom and how we would know if we have it.

Exposition:
Rather simple passage
Structure:
vs. 13: Question and answer
vs. 14-18: Contrast between that wisdom that is from below with that which is from above
vs. 14-16: Wisdom from Below
vs. 17-18: Wisdom from above

A. vs. 13: Question and Answer
Both are stated in the conventional "parallelism" of a proverb-- in the case, synonomous parallelism since the second part restates the first part...
1. 13a (Question): Who is wise?
I.e. the one with understanding among you?
2. 13b. (Answer): IT Shows by the "good (noble) way of life!
I.E. by works in the humilty (meekness, unpretentiousness ) of wisdom

Point:
1. The wise person, or the person who really has understanding (about life and living) is the person whose conduct is noble (good)
Note, then, that wisdom is not some speculative ability, or merely intellectual capacity. It is the ability to live well, to make practical choices about life.
2. Note the word "meekness" --
Better: "humility" or even better in this context" unpretentous" -- and so, Notice especially how James describes wisdom so as to disqualify pretentiousness in the second phrase.

James 1:21 Therefore rid yourselves of all sordidness and rank growth of wickedness, and welcome with meekness the implanted word that has the power to save your souls.
Gal. 5:23 gentleness, and self-control. There is no law against such things.

"Humility" is that ability to know one's limits, and to recognize that we can learn from others. Here, humility is to live a good and noble life without cheap talk about it. Wisdom is unpretentious-- without a "front"


B. Wisdom from below contrasted with wisdom from above
1. Wisdom that is vs. 15 (not from above, but earthly(vs. heavenly) , natural (vs. spiritual), devilish (vs. godly).
Characterized vs. 14 by

  • holding to bitter opinions (critical, finder of faults, jealous)
    (Illus-- building a brick wall-- any monkey can tear down a brick wall, but only a skilled masonary can build it... so to life. Any fool can criticize people's lives, only a wise person can build a noble life)
  • party spiritedness
    James says to have these things and to call yourself wise is to boast and lie according to the real truth

Characterized in vs. 16:

  • opinionatedness (cheap talk)
  • coupled with selfish ambition(lit-- used to describe a person who is in pursuit of a political office by means that are less than honest)
    (i.e. pretentiousness so as to make oneself look better than you really are)

vs. 16: where (these things are) there will be disorder and every evil practice

Again, you see James emphasis upon conduct--
These are things that produce Disorder and EVIL PRACTICE
lit- this word is practice. (Grk- pragma) or our "pragmatic"
Point: Wisdom is not the ability to find fault-- to tear down and analyse-- rather wisdom is the ability to build a good and noble life
I.e. Where then should we look to discover wisdom--
It is NOT in who can be most critical about what everyone else is doing. (Today, talk shows and political pundants make a living on being critical-- but what have they actually built and tried in public or social or moral policies? They are NOT wise, one fault-finding... So were should we look for wisdom?
Try a persons way of life during the week!
Is the persons private life well ordered?
Is the persons family life will ordered?
Is the persons public life will ordered?
And by well ordered, we mean the things which James speaks of concerning wisdom from above. (see below)

Wisdom ,then, from below is fault finding, envious and self-seeking. It measures everything in terms of self and ones own ambitions. It cannot delight in the gifts, providence, or blessings of others, but envies the happiness of others. Wisdom from below may parade itself under the pretense of being wise, it may even be such as to be exalted in the world, but by ones practice-- it is revealed to be from below. It is not well ordered after the pattern of vs. 17ff.


2. vs. 17) On the other hand the wisdom from above
Characterized by:

  • Wisdom is pure, 17 But the wisdom that is from above is first pure.

2Cor. 11:2 For I am jealous for you with godly jealousy. For I have betrothed you to one husband, that I may present you as a chaste virgin to Christ.
1Tim. 5:22 Do not lay hands on anyone hastily, nor share in other people's sins; keep yourself pure.

I.e. Undefiled from the values and conduct that is not according to God's values and conduct!
A wise person orders his/her life so as to reflect purity (holiness) -- who values and conduct make the same signal of single hearted devotion to God.

  • Wisdom is peaceful, then peaceable.

Again in vs. 18 Now the fruit of righteousness is sown in peace by those who make peace..
I.e. A wise person orders his/her life so as to have peace.
Here-- a life that is calm and without trauma due to folly--inward, relational, etc.
Example: Job-- wisdom was discovered when in the midst of tragedy, there was not "trauma"
Example: Was it wise for the New Haven Register to report on the Olympics "Terror hits the Olympics", or was it "reporting" but without wisdom so as to produce "calm." We must be careful to avoid the kind of paranoia that might sell papers, but does not make a person wise.

  • Wisdom is willing to change one's mind,

RSV Trans: gentle, open to reason
gentle: "humility" , or even "willing to yield"
open to reason: willing to be persuaded by truth
I take these two to be saying the same thing: wisdom is willing to change one's mind when pursuaded by principle!

We often think of Luther as an inflexible, strong-opinioned man, standing there saying, "Here I stand, I can do no other." But before he said that, he said, "If I am not convinced by scripture or evident reason, Here I stand, I can do no other." Luther was willing to be persuaded, and, indeed, was persuaded, that the religion he had been trained by was wrong.

  • Wisdom is able to consider the needs of others!

Lit: full of mercy, good fruits, without impartiality
The world exalts relentless and shrewdness as wisdom-- those who can manipulate so as to get ahead-- wisdom from above is merciful.
Again, look at how a person orders his/her life-- does it put others interest before his/her own-- or is the person consumed with his/her own schedule, plans... is the person preoccuppied by his own life and not the life of others...

  • Again, Wisdom is said to be without insincerity

I.e. without pretense so as to talk and look wise but in truth to be unwise. Again, James points to conduct that makes a person wise!

Mainpoint: Who is the wise person? To be wise is above all else a practical matter. To be wise is to know how to live, and to make good choices in the practical affairs in life so as to be revealed by a "good or noble way of life." A person whose private and public life is will ordered!


Stated negatively: Who is not the wise person?
The pretentous person who may give an outward appearance of wisdom but whose conduct reveals otherwise.

Illus-- A kid wants to be a baseball player
He watches the majors on TV and the next day, he buys black paint to put under his eyes, he gets a batting glove and tucks it neatly in his back pocket (flapping out ever so little), he finds a bat and glove, cap and nice uniform, he goes around talking about being a baseball team, he reads books about it, he learns to keep the baseball charts, he can tell you the batting averages of all the great major league players, he reads books about baseball, he can get his bat and hold it just like the majors do.. perhaps bouncing it and practice swings just like that famous player... he can tell you what a slide is, a sacrifice, a bunt, a squeeze, etc etc-- BUT IS HE A BASEBALL PLAYER?
No-- he is a basseball player when (with or without a batting glove, with or without black paint, for that matter with or without a baseball uniform) he hits the ball, catches the ball when hit to him, and throws the ball to the right place!

Who is Wise?
Not just the person who can sight catechisms, sing Christian songs, know famous Christian people, put on Christian t shirts, do the popular things that Christian do, not do the popular things that Christians they know don't do, go to the places where Christians go, etc, etc.
Who is Wise?
Not the person who can buy big houses, drive nice cars, go to nice places, hold degrees, talk on talk shows- all these things James call "pretentious"...

A wise person is the person who has a "good, noble" way of life, one that is not characterized by selfishness, or disorder in his home or life, -- one that is full of mercy to others, showing good fruits, giving evidence of reasonableness such as he/she is able to change ones mind and life when pursuaded by principle!--
In a word, a wise person is a person who by his/her conduct on Monday through Saturday, follows the values and commands of God!

Application:
1. Warning: There is much "false wisdom" or "pretentous Christians" out there in "Christian land."
1Cor. 3:18 Do not deceive yourselves. If you think that you are wise in this age, you should become fools so that you may become wise. 19 For the wisdom of this world is foolishness with God. For it is written,

2. Promise: God knows the difference--
you may fool your friends, your collegues, the participants of those within your Christian group, etc, but we do not fool God...
Gal. 6:7 Do not be deceived; God is not mocked, for you reap whatever you sow.

3. How can you become wise?
a. It begins with a disposition to trust and obey God!
As we will see from the proverbs, "Fear" is the beginning of wisdom-- why, because it is that disposition which orders ones life after God's!
Clearly, knowing God and his ways are important--perhaps one of the prerequisites-- for there is no other way to obey God than to understand him and his ways
But!, before even knowledge is a disposition that "fears" God..., so as to trust and obey Him.

Note: Only God can give you this disposition-- you will want to pray for it
Humility-- are you humble?

b. You will need to always be willing to forsake the "sacred cows"
Whereas you do not want to quickly change your mind every time you hear something new-- you will want to always ask yourself if you are willing to be pursuaded by principle as to a new value, or new doctrine...
I.e. why do we automatically give preference to previous values and beliefs?
From the time you were born, your mind has been washed with certain values and attitudes-- only the unwise person will then automatically assume these values and consider anything to the contrary "brainwashing" so to speak-- rather a wise person can reason with principle so as to always be "reordering" his/her life after principle!
etc.

c. You will need to concentrate on the real stuff of everyday life-- wisdom is not a "special event"--
No matter how good you are at talking the Christian life, or fitting into the Christian subculture-- real wisdom is revealed by our conduct-- especially when noone is watching. Having a religious experience does not make one wise-- singing and talking about personal surrender does not make one wise-- a life ordered after godliness, peace, mercy, truth and love does!

Husbands-- are you wise? It will be revealed by our obedience to God in loving your wives sacrificially-- not when you are at the church picnic, but Monday morning before work or Monday evening when you come home from work...

Wives-- are you wise? It will be revealed by your obedience to respect and submit to your husband-- not just be calling him honey and giving him a sweet kiss in the morning, but in the context of that discussion in the car or kitchen when no one is looking.

Children, are you wise?

Business women or man, student, baseball player-- are you wise? It will be revealed by your speech and behavior in the middle of the day or game... when you put aside selfish ambition so as to treat people with mercy, honesty...

Families, are you wise-- it will not be revealed by reading Dobson books and subscribing to that latest tape series on parenting, etc-- it will be revealed by parents who discipline their children, by a lifestyle that will not be ordered by the whims of a world out of control but rather ordered by the values of scripture...

Who is wise-- look to Christ-- who in the stuff of everyday life showed himself to produce the fruits of righteousness in peace sown to those who make peace...

"Christ came not to possess our brains with some cold opinions, that send down a freezing and benumbing influence into our hearts. Christ was a master of the life, not of the school; and he is the best Christian whose heart beats with the purest pulse towards heaven, not he whose head spins the finest cobweb." Cudworth, Ralph

Behold this table-- what do you see--
I see wisdom-- wisdom that is revealed in putting others interests first, wisdom that produced a godly life... wisdom that ultimately satisfied God's demands for wisdom so as to be declared righteous-- wisdom that in God's rich mercy, was applied then to us when by faith we receive Christ as our substitute for us in our judgment before the heavenly father. It is this wisdom-- counted as foolishness to the world, that is the salvation to all who believe...
