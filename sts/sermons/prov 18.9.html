Text: Pro. 18:9
Rethinking the Value of a "Good Days Work"-- Avoiding the Time Clock Mentality

Prov. 18:9 Also the one who is slack in his work he is brother to the master (baal) of destruction.

Introduction:
What do you think of work?

Would you characterize your thinking so as to most resemble the seven dwarfs in Snow White-- "Hi Ho, Hi Ho, its off to work we go..." Something we love to do...but
Is work something you come home from? Is it something you don't do on weekends? Are you working now?
Or does your thinking most resemble the cliché" another day, another dollar, another 10 cents income tax" or "thank God it's Friday?
Work is at best utilitarian in value-- done because we have to--

All this gets at our view of work--
Now I'm not sure this Proverb will enable us to get at all these questions-- but it will take us one step in better understanding a Christian view of work and our relation to it...
For as Dorothy Sayers reminds us

In nothing has the Church so lost her hold on reality as in her failure to understand and respect the secular vocation. She has allowed work and religion to become separate departments, and is astonished to find that, as a result, the secular work of the world is turned to purely selfish and destructive ends, and that the greater part of the world's intelligent workers have become irreligious or at least uninterested in religion." Dorothy Sayers


Exposition:
Straightforward: A statement of consequence
Key words:

1. sloth-- slacken, or to let go, leave
I.e. here applied to our work-- those who are "slack" in their work-- whatever vocations or responsibilities that we have been given by God
I.e. Wherever we have been given a responsibility-- and we slacken in it-- we are akin to the master of destruction

2. Play on words perhaps-- Hebrew, the word "master" is also the word "baal"--
Here the "baal or lord of destruction"
Maybe a reference to the devil himself,

John 10:10, The devil comes to steal, kill, and destroy...

Or more general, a reference to any who causes destruction in the world... however,
A person who does not work diligently, is akin to destruction.
How can this be?
That the one who is slack in his work is brother to the master (baal) of destruction.

3. The answer lies in a Biblical understanding of" work" and The role of work in God's created order.

As the word itself can be applied to a mission or "calling" in scripture as well as a specific task...


A. The Intrinsic value of work as derived from God Himself:
The positive role of work within the created order is made explicit in Genesis 2:2, when work is viewed as being derived from God himself as the first cause of all creation.

Gen. 2:2 And on the seventh day God ended His work which He had done, and He rested on the seventh day from all His work which He had done. 3 Then God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because in it He rested from all His work which God had created and made...Gen. 2:9 And out of the ground the Lord God made every tree grow that is pleasant to the sight and good for food..

Notice that the above passage affirms God at work, and what God does is itself intrinsically good.

For instance, the "rest" which God did-- surely it was not because God was tired-- but because creation was worth celebrating, and consecrating to God even as God himself was crooned as the king over all creation-- work therefore, was worth celebrating, worth doing so as to proclaim Him Lord over all things...


Moreover, God's work is in fact described as 'pleasant to the sight" and "good for food"-- both valuable in its aesthetics qualities and its utilitarian quality...

The "arts" and the "sciences"-- work is poetry/painting in amusement and "pleasantness", work is sowing and reaping in its productivity... The Creational work of God is valuable in its aesthetics and utility!


It is no surprise then that when God made humanity in His own image, he made him to work, to in effect "re-create", not so much in making a new creation, but to follow after God in his creation-- and this as a calling which preceded the fall.


B. The intrinsic value of work as given form people to do in the image of God

Gen. 1:26 Then God said, "Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness; let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over the cattle, over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth. 27 So God created man in His own image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them. 28 Then God blessed them, and God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply; fill the earth and subdue it; have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over every living thing that moves on the earth.".


Here, it is clear that people are made to follow after God in his work-- it is a moral and spiritual thing to imitate God! It is closely linked by this passage to being made in God's image...
Notice also the extent to which work is related-- it has to do with managing God's creation, ordering it, making it to be more beautiful and more productive-- it extends to all the common graces of life, over "every living thing that moves on earth---
Its NOT something we do on Monday and not on Saturday
Its not some thing we do at 3:00 but not 9:00 when the children go-- we simply can't reduce "work" to things pertaining to outside the home but not inside the home, or vice versa...
Remembering also that the created order which we are to cultivate was created by God with both beauty ("pleasant to the sight") and the capacity to nurture life ("good for food"). Therefore, we have the unique privilege and responsibility of cultivating the inherent blessing in the created order both in applying "re-creational" energy to the art and utility of our world.

Notice then what we have seen thus far about work:

1. Work is "spiritual" or "sacred" Why?

a. God did it!-- Creation is work
b. We are commanded to do it in following after God! Re-creation is work.

2. That work itself is essentially moral-- it is what we were made to do so as to be in favor with God.

3. Work extends to everything in life! We can't compartmentalize work so as to just include one sphere of it-- "re-creation" then extends to what we do in the family room, on the fields, in the public sphere, even in church in so far as it is a service to God!

4. Work is by nature, both utilitarian and aesthetic as both of these are valuable to creation itself and is therefore valuable to the re-creation of human work.


Clearly then, work is not the result of the fall; it is our very creaturely mandate; it is what we were created to do.

II. The reality of the fall simply heightened the necessity of diligent work after the Fall.
Perhaps here we can distinguish between labor (toil and frustration that accompanies work after the fall) and work-- what we do to imitate God.
Labor (toil) was written into the created order so as to humble us back to God.

Gen. 3:17 Then to Adam He said, "Because you have heeded the voice of your wife, and have eaten from the tree of which I commanded you, saying, 'You shall not eat of it': Cursed is the ground for your sake; In toil you shall eat of it All the days of your life. 18 Both thorns and thistles it shall bring forth for you, And you shall eat the herb of the field. 19 In the sweat of your face you shall eat bread Till you return to the ground, For out of it you were taken; For dust you are, And to dust you shall return.".


After the fall-- a destructive quality was introduced into creation-- i.e. if left alone, creation would de-construct rather than construct.

a. God graciously introduces his grace in that he preserves creation from total destruction....
b. Work is even more necessary for production and aesthetics in the world.

Said in the reverse-- to slacken in our work is paramount to destruction given the reality of the fall. This is the point of this proverb!

To be unproductive in a world that cries out for productivity, is to be destructive--
Therefore, whereas we should expect in every aspect of our work or calling to have many pains and needs, to slacken in our work is all the more to be destructive. To not work is to not eat, and to not enjoy in a fallen world.
This is why the apostle Paul said:

2Th. 3:10 For even when we were with you, we commanded you this: If anyone will not work, neither shall he eat. .


Conclusion: It is our duty to do a "good days work-- to work, and to work diligently since to do otherwise is to acquiesce in destruction given a fallen world.

A. The Danger of a Time Clock Mentality to Life

1. its "off to work we go"... --
2. Another day... --
Illus.: Frozen foods dept. at Grocery Store


This scenario illustrates what our clichés suggest-- that in a post industrial world, most of us have compartmentalized our lives into at least two broad categories-- work and play. This picture about how we think of life-- as if life can be divided into "work" and "play"-- I will call , "the time clock" approach to life. Under the "time clock" schema, play is considered "off time" whereas work is considered "on time."

Work is ordinarily "utilitarian" in nature-- about "making a living" and perhaps in our better moments about doing something worthwhile for the world. It is considered to be that activity which is obligatory and for the purpose of sustaining our lives-- housework, income related jobs, task-related studies, etc. Perhaps for some, work is even thought of as a necessary compensation for the fall as somehow related to God's curse upon Adam. In yet another sense, work is often experienced as a means to self-gratification and fulfillment-- the way to psychological satisfaction. Which ever one or more of these descriptions fits your understanding of work-- they are all "utilitarian" by nature.
Play, on the other hand, is ordinarily considered to be "re-creational" in nature-- about amusement, enjoyment, leisure and perhaps even associated with the frivolous. Play is usually considered to be that activity which is discretionary. At the very least, play is often thought of as "non-work"-- whatever we do when we are not "working." A more ambitious description of play might be that activity which stimulates cultural appreciation, personal restoration, creativity through hobbies, social engagement and entertainment. Often, if work is thought of as "sustaining a life," then play is about "a quality of life."

PROBLEM: Having Compartmentalized life into "on time" and "off time" -- we may artificially exclude some areas of work from "on time" to "off time" so as to be slack in it...

Depending on what you place into the category of work, then everything else is "free time" or "off time " so as to maybe exclude the idea of moral "duty" and perhaps even moral evaluation. What it looks like in everyday life.

Family-- one or both marriage partner(s) goes "off to work" so that when he or she comes home-- he/she is "off from work so as to begin his/her "off" time. -- it's the ole "when I'm home, its time to wind down" kind of attitude. Within this schema, home is merely a "safe haven" so as to enable the spouse to go back into the world of work-- where the meaningful stuff really happens.

Raises some questions:
What for instances does this mentality do to a child who both wants and needs a parent-- how involved would this spouse be in child discipline? How responsible would this spouse be in caring for and loving his spouse? In other words, is there "work" to be done by this marriage partner in the home-- work in relation to chores, but more significantly, relationships.

Example-- if this is the mentality of the husband-- that the home is there merely to help me go back to work-- what does that do the home-- it trivialized it as merely the prop for work-- ironically, it trivializes fatherhood to merely be about putting money in a family pot-- how awfully tragic!

Church-- In that Sunday is "off time" in the time clock week, so too church related activities are perhaps inadvertently put into the "play box." Tragically then, the very concept of sacred is redefined so as to correspond with "off" or "leisure" time.

Christian discipleship and training is something we merely play at-- as if it would be a sin to actually break a sweat learning about God. When applied to worship-- we look for entertainment so as to remain passive. For instance, worship itself may be viewed as valuable if it has a positive impact on my production at work-- either by providing me with helpful insights into being more successful, or perhaps being sufficiently therapeutic so as to heal the wounds gained from work so that I may go back to work. Again, all this out of the mentality that play has intrinsic value only in so far as it is "non-work"-- either for amusement or getting ready for work.
On the other hand, church attendance may be viewed as one of many options for what to do in ones leisure time-- the key element is that it is discretionary time without obligations and moral duties. In this sense, the church fits into my schedule under the same conditions as say the option of playing tennis with some friends. Which ever option will be most satisfying under our expectations for leisure and play is the logical choice given the time clock mentality.

Career-- what most people think of when they think of "work" -- given the time clock mentality-- this is "on time"-- Yet "work" is both tragically demeaned of its sacred value and relevance while at the same time exalted in its secularness.

In other words, the work/play-secular/sacred dichotomy associates the sacred (religious) with the private worlds of family and church, but considers the sacred to be irrelevant to the realities of public life outside of the home and church. As noted above, such a schema reduces religion to merely an enabler of the secular values of public life...
The secularization of work often produces a worker who is unable to organize and manage their careers in relation to the whole of life. Moreover, personal careers that are void of a "sacred" meaning inevitably leads to careerism-- "where ones work is viewed so as to establish one's self-worth and becomes the controlling center of one's life and is the last in a series of priorities to go." It is the so called "workaholic" wherein personal identity is reduced merely to a job one does, bereft of those other spheres of identity which gives balance and meaning to the whole of life and persons.

But just as tragic-- the sacred/secular dichotomy demeans your career to something apart form your service to God-- it robs it of the moral and spiritual duties that God calls you to while in your career-- you practice on Monday is religious-- the more you understand scripture, the more you understand God's commands and world views there, them more you will be able to fulfill God purpose for you in work...


Problem with all this:

1. Even as we may be expending alot of energy if life, we may be slackening in our work having compartmentalized out of the category of work some very important parts of what God has called us to do...

2. What we do work at, may be dis-associated from "re-creation" and "spirituality"

2. Work is Valuable as Re-creation

Work in a Fallen world will be difficult
a. Labor- (toil) is the realistic expectation that our work will at time be frustrated and difficult--
b. Regarding Work-- the toil in it must not be allowed to undermine the dignity of it as under the creation of God
c. Dignity in Work extends both to its aesthetic and utilitarian value-- it is "re-creation" in the most lofty sense of the word recreation-- it is spiritual to work

I.e. When you further order the cosmos so as to make it more beautiful and/or more useful-- you are doing recreation-- you are working-- you are in the image of God!

Therefore, Work for God.
Col. 3:22 Bondservants, obey in all things your masters according to the flesh, not with eyeservice, as men-pleasers, but in sincerity of heart, fearing God. 23 And whatever you do, do it heartily, as to the Lord and not to men.


3. Work is related equally to all three spheres of our "calling" in life-- we must avoid the time clock mentality

We can't compartmentalize our lives so as to be "on" and "off"-- work is what we do Monday through Sunday, work is what we do 8 to 5, 5 to 11, work is what we do in family time, in public life, and in church


4. We need to re-order our lives to work -- the issue of personal calling as derived from this study

1. From our conception of time and history-- we know all things are intended for God's glory and the enjoyment of him-- this is our first and primary calling of which all other "callings" will seek to accomplish

2. From the general teaching of God's word, we have the mandate of God as applied to each of the three spheres of life--duties of mothers, fathers, husbands, wives, church members, church officers, social workers, artists, rulers, subjects etc. -- God has given the ethical instructions in so far as we need so as to glorify God in these areas.

3. From a unique providence in your life, you determine which roles "callings" you have been led to and therefore which ethical demands you should fulfill...
I.e.- If in God's providence you are a father-- you simply do not have the ethical freedom to be a career-aholic! You are commanded to instruct and raise your children with loving but present discipline, to love and serve your wife, etc.-- this must be balanced with your duties to society and church as well...
If you are a mother in goods providence, you simply don't have the ethical freedom to "slacken" in your home responsibilities so as to be more esteemed in a world that has ceased to dignify the home...
If you are student...

Therefore, work for your family, for your church, for your society--
Industrial society robs of us of our joy in that work is about individual production
Therapeutic society robs us of our joy in that work is about personal self-actualization
