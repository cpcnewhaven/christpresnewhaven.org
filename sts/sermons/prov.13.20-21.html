I. A Preliminary Concept both assumed and stated:

A. vs. 20: Is the amazing assumption hidden within the symmetry of the proverb

"The one who walks with the wise become wise"

Which stated negatively means:

"But the companion of fools will suffer harm"

A unexpected parallel: become wise... vs. suffer harm?

I.e the result of the association-- expect "become fools" instead of "suffer harm" in contrast with "become wise"

But instead-- "become wise" is contrast with "suffer harm"

Why?

Nature of Proverbs-- to say things using the "wisdom model of ethics" vs. the legal model

I.e. rather than saying-- do this because it is wrong, it says, do this because it is foolish not to... Here: It is understood that the

Essense of Wisdom-- to fear God since God alone is the one who has both the power and authority to ultimately bless or curse

Therefore, to be on God's good side is to be under God's blessing

To be on God's bad side is to be under God's curse! (and therefore to suffer harm)

Therefore, to be in companionship with those who are on God's bad side is to suffer the harm of God's curse with them...

B. vs. 21: The Assumption Stated Plainly

Sinners are pursued by evil (same work as companions), and rightous are paided by good.

Notice the words

"pursued" -- chased, hunted... to be sought out by someone

"paid"-- to receive as payment by someone

Here: The point is that God will not be mocked

Sin will be hunted out and will recieve a just punishment

Rightousness will be receive its rewards...

"sinner"-- same three consenant word as "companion" with some minor differences in construction...

Here-- the "companion" who is a "sinner" is implied in the symmetry of the proverb... so as to press the point even harder-- that sinner companions will bring harm to those who walk wit them...

II. vs. 20: What exactly does this proverb mean by "walk" or take as companions"

"The one who walks with the wise become wise, But the companion of fools will suffer harm"

Does this mean literally that if you "walk" down the sidewalk next to a wise person that you will become wise? Of course not--

dlh

1. Literally-- walk-- move feet so as to go somewhere...

2. Metephorically-- To be joined with someone... to be in association with, or to participate with, even to follow someone as in a teacher...

a. walk = behavior

Is. 33:15 He who walks (behaves) righteously and speaks uprightly, He who despises the gain of oppressions, Who gestures with his hands, refusing bribes, Who stops his ears from hearing of bloodshed, And shuts his eyes from seeing evil:

Prov. 30:29 ¶ There are three things which are majestic in pace, Yes, four which are stately in walk: (i.e. behavior)

b. walk - following a leader

1Kings 18:21 And Elijah came to all the people, and said, "How long will you falter between two opinions? If the Lord is God, follow Him; but if Baal, follow him.''

Deut. 4:3 "Your eyes have seen what the Lord did at Baal Peor; for the Lord your God has destroyed from among you all the men who followed Baal of Peor.

c. walk = To live with with a social agreement or contract,

Jer. 3:18 In those days the house of Judah shall walk with the house of Israel, and they shall come together out of the land of the north to the land that I have given as an inheritance to your fathers.

Rev. 3:4 You have a few names even in Sardis who have not defiled their garments; and they shall walk with Me in white, for they are worthy.

Prov. 10:9 He who walks with integrity walks securely, But he who perverts his ways will become known.

Hos. 11:12 Ephraim has encircled Me with lies, And the house of Israel with deceit; But Judah still walks with God, Even with the Holy One who is faithful.

So, in a certain sense, to "walk" with someone is to be in companionship with them--

Langenscheidt: "to be fond of, to delight in, to associate with".

Thus, to be a "companion of fools" does not mean that you bump into an occasional fool; it means that you have selected such to be your companions-- you are in some relation to the person

Therefore, this Proverb states a certain Reality -- That our destiny is very much related to the destiny of our companions :

Two possible scenerios"

1. We are unavoidably transformed by who we hang around with--

2. The curse or blessings of a persons life carry over to those lives who are in relationship to the person

The sins of one generation flow over to the next generation...

Both ways-- the point of the proverb is to say-- be very careful who you engage yourself to in life-- for you WILL be affected by it-- for the good or for the bad!

I.e. The choice of companions profoundly influences our living. Those who choose to walk with the wise profit from that choice, and become wise themselves. By contrast, those who choose fools as their companions, will suffer great harm.

Lesson: You are to who you associate with, so be deliberate and principles about who you "walk" with in life!

Application:

1. Avoid "foolish" companions

The Bible says much in defining the fool and the wise man.

At the core-- a wise person is the one who walks with God in holy fear of him-- the fool is the person who walks without God as if it doesn't matter...

At its most basic level, a fool does not know how to live well; and a wise man does. Wisdom and folly are not primarily intellectual matters, biblically; they are practical matters.

How does this warning about being a companion of fools relate to the biblical requirement that we be the light of the world?

Key: Walking with is not the same as walking beside. Difference between delighting in someone as your preferred company, and serving someone who needs your influence.

Some tests:

- Does your association with an unbeliever cause you to any less Christianly

-Do they know you live by different standards?

-Do they respect that difference?

-Do you pray for their conversion?

-All things being equal, do you enjoy being with those who don’t attempt to follow Christ more than you enjoy being with those do attempt to follow Christ?

-Are the majority of your social acquaintances followers of Christ? Jesus did indeed eat with tax collectors and sinners; but he did not eat with them exclusively. He spent an extraordinary amount of time with his disciples and followers.

2. Cultivate "wise" companions.

Don’t merely select as companions people with common interests; select as companions people who help you learn to live well.

Example: A single Christian might learn a great deal about life from a married Christian, even though their responsibilities in life are somewhat different.

Don’t merely select as companions people with whom you agree. Often we learn a great deal about life from people with whom we disagree on many points.

Don’t merely select as companions people who make you feel good about yourself, or who make you feel comfortable. As sinners, we often need the companionship of people who make us feel bad about ourselves.

Do select as companions people who bring out the best in you; people who rekindle your desire to live for Christ and commune with God. Select as companions people who are stronger in areas where you are weaker.

CAVEAT: A wise companion is not necessarily a perfect companion.
