Text: Pro. 12:15

The Wisdom of Counsel

The path of the fool is right (straight) in his own eyes, but the one who listens to (heeds) counsel is wise.

Intro:

 

Exposition: A typical contrast proverb, contrasting the fool with the wise.

I. The fool is "right in his own eyes." He does not need advice or counsel; in his opinion, he is right. Why listen to anyone else?

Picture: "the path of a fool is straight in his own eyes"

Cutting a path to a destination-- some cut a straight path, others a crooked path, others a path that will never make it!

II. The wise, by contrast, "heeds (m´vo) counsel."

"heeds" (hears)

1. subj. a. hear (perceive by ear), … hear one speaking; acc. pers. only.

2. hear/understand-- Gen 11:7, 1 K3:6

3. (God) subj.: hear and forgive, help, etc.; hear and be angry.

4. be regarded, obeyed; be obedient

Ex. 24:7 Then he took the Book of the Covenant and read in the hearing of the people. And they said, "All that the Lord has said we will do, and be obedient.''

Gen. 17:20 "And as for Ishmael, I have heard you. Behold, I have blessed him, and will make him fruitful, and will multiply him exceedingly. He shall beget twelve princes, and I will make him a great nation.

Gen. 22:18 "In your seed all the nations of the earth shall be blessed, because you have obeyed My voice.''

2Kings 14:11 But Amaziah would not heed. Therefore Jehoash king of Israel went out; so he and Amaziah king of Judah faced one another at Beth Shemesh, which belongs to Judah.

5. (favourable) hearing was granted to their voice.

Counsel = counsel, advice

1. Counsel = Prevenient-- instruction, advice,etc.

Prov. 8:33 Hear instruction and be wise, and do not neglect it.

2. Counsel= Corrective-- is used in reference to rebuke

Prov. 1:25 Because you disdained all my counsel, And would have none of my rebuke,

Prov. 1:30 They would have none of my counsel And despised my every rebuke.

Prov. 1:25 Because you disdained all my counsel, And would have none of my rebuke,

Both cases, the wise is one who not only hears it, but also listens to it and takes heed to it... the fool is the person who resists it and doesn't seek it out....

Mainpoint: The fool listens to no one, and injures himself and others, but the wise listens to advice.

Discussion:

1. Proverbs has alot to say about the wisdom of Counsel: (advice)

"counsel"

1. Good counsel is always grounded in God's counsel

Prov. 8:14 Counsel is mine, and sound wisdom; I am understanding, I have strength.

Prov. 19:20 Listen to counsel and receive instruction, That you may be wise in your latter days. 21 There are many plans in a man's heart, Nevertheless the Lord's counsel--that will stand.

2. Counsel is something we should seek after, not just wait for...

Prov. 20:5 Counsel in the heart of man is like deep water, But a man of understanding will draw it out.

3. Counsel is essential to making plans (family, church, business, etc-- )

Prov. 15:22 Without counsel, plans go wrong, but with many advisers they succeed.

Prov. 20:18 Plans are established by taking advice; wage war by following wise guidance.

4. To give good counsel is an act of friendship

Prov. 27:9 Ointment and perfume delight the heart, And the sweetness of a man's friend gives delight by hearty counsel.

5. To despise advice is to despise yourself

Prov. 15:32 Those who ignore instruction despise themselves, but those who heed admonition gain understanding. 33 The fear of the LORD is instruction in wisdom, and humility goes before honor.

Raises the question: Why don’t fools seek counsel?

1. Pride:

Prov. 13:10 By pride comes nothing but strife, But with the well-advised is wisdom.

I.e. Unwilling to know ones limits in life-- being a good carpenter doesn't make a good theologian, nor a good theologian a good carpenter...

2. Willfulness. We fear that the counsel will prevent us from doing what we intend to do.

3. Insecurity: We think that to get councel means that we are "incomplete"-- assumed view of rugged individualism-- as if God meant for people to be self-sufficient... Not so... God created you to live in a social system... you are therefore mor "complete" when you live in that system...

4. Takes Time-- we live in a culture that esteems "speed."--

Prov. 21:5 The plans of the diligent lead surely to abundance, but everyone who is hasty comes only to want.

Qualification-- can't be frozen by decision either...

Illus: Open field tackle

5. Afraid of rebuke--

A Wise person is willing to receive rebuke:

Prov. 17:10 Rebuke is more effective for a wise man Than a hundred blows on a fool.

Prov. 10:17 Whoever heeds instruction is on the path to life, but one who rejects a rebuke goes astray.

Prov. 13:18 Poverty and disgrace are for the one who ignores instruction, but one who heeds reproof is honored.

 

Contrast this with Christ, who, though God incarnate, listened to and obeyed His father’s will.

I.e.

He lived within the trinitarian society...

He lived with his will trained on the fathers standard of right

He lived humbly

John 8:28 Then Jesus said to them, "When you lift up the Son of Man, then you will know that I am He, and that I do nothing of Myself; but as My Father taught Me, I speak these things."

Conclusion:

The fool is unlike Christ; the fool listens to no one, and injures himself and others because of his pride and his willfulness.

Application

1. Know when you need counsel.

A. When you feel anxious. Whenever you find yourself saying, "I’ve got to do SOMETHING," don’t do ANYTHING.

B. When dealing with important matters.

C. To the degree that your actions affect others, more and better counsel is needed.

2. Seek several counselors.

Prov. 15:22 Without counsel, plans go awry, But in the multitude of counselors they are established.

3. Seek "good" counselors:

Who to expect good counsel from:

1. Those appointed by God to give it--- God would be inclined to bless what he establishes-- parents, church leaders, etc.

Children:

Prov. 1:8 Hear, my child, your father's instruction, and do not reject your mother's teaching;

Prov. 15:5 A fool despises a parent's instruction, but the one who heeds admonition is prudent.

Prov. 4:1 Listen, children, to a father's instruction, and be attentive, that you may gain insight;

Children of God-- Christ offers you counsel through his appointment of officers in the church.

Heb. 13:7 Remember your leaders, those who spoke the word of God to you; consider the outcome of their way of life, and imitate their faith. 8 Jesus Christ is the same yesterday and today and forever....Heb. 13:17 Obey your leaders and submit to them, for they are keeping watch over your souls and will give an account. Let them do this with joy and not with sighing--for that would be harmful to you. 18 Pray for us; we are sure that we have a clear conscience, desiring to act honorably in all things. 19 I urge you all the more to do this, so that I may be restored to you very soon.

2. Those who genuinely share with you a biblical worldview and values-- who are themselves mature in Christ

I.e. more experience with less godliness is not good counsel... (Psm 119:105?)

Pss. 119:97 Oh, how I love your law! It is my meditation all day long. 98 Your commandment makes me wiser than my enemies, for it is always with me. 99 I have more understanding than all my teachers, for your decrees are my meditation. 100 I understand more than the aged, for I keep your precepts.

3. Those who are knowledgeable--Seek wise and knowledgeable counselors (not merely sympathetic counselors

4. Those whose own affairs are in good order...

5. . Seek opposing counsel. Prov. 18:17 The first one to plead his cause seems right, Until his neighbor comes and examines him.
