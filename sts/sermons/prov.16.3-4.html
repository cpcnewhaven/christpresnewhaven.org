A. First clause (vs. 3): Our duty unto wisdom
Perhaps you have heard the expression, let it roll of your back...
Here the English trans. (commit) obscures the Hebrew word for roll...

1. "galal"-- lit. roll as in stone (Gn.29:3)

2. What exactly are we to "roll onto God"?
"hcom"-- whatever happens...since in a Biblical cosmology whatever happens is always under God's sovereign control-- the word is often translated "works" implying that God is the worker

Prov. 16:11 Honest balances and scales are the LORD's; all the weights in the bag are his work.

Theological terms: word refers to "providence" or "whatever happens under the sovereign control of God!
What is "providence" here:
Not-- a description as to whatever ought to happen in moralistic or "valuative sense-- (as known by God's commands given for us to live by within our created order.
But--whatever in fact does happen in a descriptive sense-- but is all the same under God's control such that we can be assured that while it may or may not be morally good in the temporal sense, it is all the same within the control of God for an ultimate good in the eternal sense...
Vital Distinction: (many less than helpful ways that this distinction has been made... (permissive will vs. desired will..., almost implies that some things are somehow outside God's influence and control)
1. God's moral will within the system of Creation (temporal)-- commands that we are bound to keep such that within the sphere of creation, God's desire is such as to keep to his commands-- Christian ethics, or faithfulness...
This sense, it was not God's will that Judas should betray Christ...
2. God's ultimate will-- whatever happens that happens
This sense, it was God's will that Judas should betray Christ...
The Providence of God reaches to all the creatures of God and all their actions.

Acts 2:23, this Jesus, delivered up according to the definite plan and foreknowledge of God, you crucified and killed by the hands of lawless men. But God raised him up...
Acts 4:27, ... Herod and Pontius Pilate, with the Gentiles and the peoples of Israel, to do whatever thy hand and thy plan had predestined to take place...

Therefore, whatever happens, in the descriptive sense should be "rolled over unto" God---
What exactly does this mean? Notice the promise.
3. Promise: and your plans will be established, (secure.)

This phrase helps clarify exactly what is meant by "roll over, commit" everything to God
Key: "your plans"--- I.e. whatever "roll over means" it will result in plans that God will establish, or make secure... I.e. the kind of plans made in the aftermath of rolling over your burdens to God!

Reflection: Squirrel in the middle of the road...
I.e. When we make plans motivated out of anxiety or discontent-- they are bound to be plans that are unfaithful to God-- anxiety and discontent cloud our ability to make wise decisions...

But when we put our faith in God so as to trust that whatever happens has happened under his control-- when we are roll over to him our burden, we are then enabled to make plans that are submitted to his providence



II. Clause 2: The Doctrine supporting our duty
Further clarification by extension of first principle
vs. 4: Everything the Lord makes for (to, with) a purpose, even the wicked ("rasha")for the day of wrath. ("raya")

1. lk-- Lit- "everything" totality-- explains what we are to roll onto God
Key, leaves nothing else to be added
· totality as in everything (), everyone (Gen16:12)

vs. 4: "all things" = all the works of the creation-- all the events of nations-- all the dispensations of Providence, everything!

Why? We are told by this proverb to put all our worry and cares unto God?

a. Everything the Lord makes is God's doing for an ultimate good prupose -- language is clear so as to attribute everything to God in an ultimate sense" makes" or "creates" everything-- he is the author of whatever happens!
Whereas God uses second causes to accomplish his will, even the second cause of man's free will within creation-- he is the ultimate first cause of everything such that in this sense we can say that everything God makes for His purpose.

An important observation: Whereas Christians recongnize the presence of evil in the world, and that all misery is the consequence of the fall, Christians can also acknowledge that there no "ultimate" misery, and that even evil has a purpose for the glory of God in the end.

Heb: here "purpose" is stated in the strongest way possible... not merely with a grammatical construction (as is possible) but with a distinct Hebrew word so as to emphasis "all things have a divine purpose"

2. The wise person understands that he/she should make plans so as to honor BOTH God's ultimate will as reflected in his acts of providence and God's moral will as reflected in his commands.

Inferences:

1. Consider just for a moment if even one thing was somehow beyond the pale of God's control-- what would you would call it
a. It would be a dualism-- the reality of something beside and totally other than God that we in fact know nothing about and would have no hope of trusting is directing things for our good
b. It would be chaos-- fickle, chance-- random acts which are directed by nothing except nothing...

Consider for a moment the consequences of that view held by some that perhaps evil and sin are somehow beyond God's control-- perhaps that he "permits it" as if to mean outside of his sovereign government--
Then everything that happens to you, you would have genuine cause to fear

Is it sin run amuck, or is it God

Now if you are listening, I'm sore afraid that this is exactly how we act at times--

-- as if there is this war between good and evil and we are caught in the middle and without real knowledge of which force is moving the circumstances of our lives (dualism)
-- as if there is some event that God somehow voluntarily "tolerates" but is unable really to control (chaos)

Yet this passage emphatically denies the possibility of either a dualism, or chaos!
Consider:
Eph1:7 In him we have redemption... 11 In Christ we have also obtained an inheritance, having been destined according to the purpose of him who accomplishes all things according to his counsel and will, 12 so that we, who were the first to set our hope on Christ, might live for the praise of his glory.

We loose a loved one-- It first passed through the counsel of God's will and was under his control
We loose a job-- It first passed through the counsel of God's will and was under his control
We get in a car wreck and loose a limb-- It first passed through the counsel of God's will and was under his control
We get rejected for that scholarship, or job-- It first passed through the counsel of God's will and was under his control

Under chaos, we have no guarantee that it will work out for God,
Under God's providence, we are assured that it will play out eventually to bring God glory and if you are God's child, to benefit you for all eternity...

Just imagine how fragile is our sanity if there was even the remote possibility of either a dualism or even one moment of chaos

Yet our passage here tells us that everything-- yes even sin-- exist under the control of God for his purpose...

Is God the author of Sin-- no, in that he is not the immediate cause of sin-- corrupt free will is.
But God does create the environment for sin and could in a certain sense be called the first cause of that which causes sin-- but more than this, even sin itself is under God's permissive control

AND THIS IS GREAT NEWS!
FOR THIS MEANS THAT NOTHING HAPPENS TO YOU THAT IS TO YOUR HARM AFTER GODS PURPOSE WHICH IS BOTH FOR HIS GLORY AND TO YOUR ULTIMATE BENEFIT

a. God and his plans are not contingent upon anything since God is infinite and immutable in his being... i.e. if infinite, nothing else could be added to him or his plan... therefore his plan is complete and whatever happens to you is a part of it!
b. God and his plans are most wise, holy, loving, etc-- such that there is no more wisdom to be added to what he has planned...
c. God is infinite in his power such that nothing is outside of his power so as to make him only partial in his power...


Bottom line: everything, no matter what or how we understand it, is not an accident-- not that God allowed it, but in a real since he ultimately caused it!
Mystery, but comfort-- why?
For those whom God has promised never to forsake, never to do anything that is anything less than to be loving toward, i.e. those who are the chosen one of God... we can count that all fire, hail, storms, or sickness are somehow related to the way God effects his goodness to you...
This means there is no need to fear the wicked, for even they are under God's control...

2. Is "Evil" or "Suffering" an ultimate Problem?
Perhaps no other issue more reveals our perspective in life than the issue of suffering and evil in the world.
When you suffer, what do you try to look for in suffering? Why? What is affirmed as true?

A. The "Problem" of Suffering Stated:
Three "other" options applied to Suffering: It is ultimate "no good"
1. Its ugly- so bad
2. Its a war between co-equal forces, good and evil with no winner-- so bad
3. Its random and purposeless but inconvenient at best, devastating at worse-- so bad

As applied toward the belief in God:
Short Version:
1. A Perfectly good and all-powerful God would not allow evil.
2. There is evil
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Therefore God does not exist

Unpacked Version:
1. God is by definition all-good, all-powerful and all knowing.
2. An all-good God would want to eliminate all evil
3. An all-powerful God would be able to do anything He wanted.
4. An all knowing God would now how.
5. If evil does exist, then God is either not all-good, all-powerful or all-knowing.
6. If God loses one of these attributes, He stops being God.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. Therefore, God does not exist.

The Fundamental Assumption: Ultimate evil. (3 options)
Ultimate Evil: That evil that is utterly pointless. Evil with a good purpose ceases to be ultimate evil.

B. The Fundamental Assumption challenged:
Problem Restated:
1. If God exists, he does not allow any utterly pointless evil.
2. There is pointless evil from our perspective.
-------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. God does not exist.
Theocentric Response:
1. If God exist, he does not allow any utterly pointless evil.
2. God does exist.
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. Therefore there is no utterly pointless evil.

Fundamental Perspective:
Theology interprets suffering rather than suffering interpreting Theology.
God-centered Perspective vs. Human centered perspective.
