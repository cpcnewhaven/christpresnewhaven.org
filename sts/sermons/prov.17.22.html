Text: Pro. 17:22
A Cheerful Heart is Good Medicine

Introduction:

1. Exposition of Proverbs: "topical" vs "expositional"
Original intent: collection of wisdom saying without running theme or plot-- therefore, to interpret as if one chapter was intended to follow another, etc, would be to impose an artificial organization-- thus "topical" is "expositional" in the case of the proverbs.
2. Last Week-- Sovereignty of God in Providence so as to be exhorted to "roll off unto God" whatever happens...
3. This week-- take providence the next step to a "cheerful heart"

Question: What if I were to tell you this morning that you were inadvertently drinking poison every time you drank out of the water fountain downstairs? How many of you children would drink it?

Exposition: A typical contrast-proverb, where a single truth is told by looking at both the positive and negative sides of a given point.

I. Positive: A cheerful (joyful) heart does good, like medicine. (NKJV-- better trans)
A. Cheerful Heart

-- Here, "heart" is "inner person", including emotions, passions, mind, attitude, even our countenance as proceeding from our attitudes!
-- "Cheerful" --rejoice, glad, joyful, merriment
And we ought not to over spiritualize the word here in that its true spirituality extends to just about everything God has created or done!
This word in the OT is used :

1. To refer to the merriment enjoyed at festivals and celebrations!

Lev. 23:40 "And you shall take for yourselves on the first day the fruit of beautiful trees, branches of palm trees, the boughs of leafy trees, and willows of the brook; and you shall rejoice before the Lord your God for seven days.
Deut. 12:7 "And there you shall eat before the Lord your God, and you shall rejoice in all to which you have put your hand, you and your households, in which the Lord your God has blessed you.
1Kings 1:40 And all the people went up after him; and the people played the flutes and rejoiced with great joy, so that the earth seemed to split with their sound.

2. Used to describe taking pleasure in the all that God has created for enjoyment

Deut. 26:11 "So you shall rejoice in every good thing which the Lord your God has given to you and your house, you and the Levite and the stranger who is among you.

Josh. 1:15 "until the Lord has given your brethren rest, as He gave you, and they also have taken possession of the land which the Lord your God is giving them. Then you shall return to the land of your possession and enjoy it, which Moses the Lord's servant gave you on this side of the Jordan toward the sunrise.''
Note-- this enjoyment of God's creation included a responsible enjoyment of wine...
Eccl. 9:7 Go, eat your bread with joy, And drink your wine with a merry heart; For God has already accepted your works.
Eccl. 10:19 A feast is made for laughter, And wine makes merry;

4. Describes the joy one has in their salvation from God

1Sam. 2:1 And Hannah prayed and said: "My heart rejoices in the Lord; My horn is exalted in the Lord. I smile at my enemies, Because I rejoice in Your salvation.

The "cheerful heart" is the heart that takes pleasure and finds joy in everything God does -- delighting in God's gifts of creation and salvation!

This is a reminder again that for the Hebrew cosmology-- it was as spiritual to enjoy creation as it was to enjoy salvation-- both were gifts of God and both then were said to be "rejoicing in the Lord!"

Summary:

Eccl. 3:12 I know that nothing is better for them than to rejoice, and to do good in their lives,


B. This cheerful heart then... does good, like medicine

Lit: Two words in the Heb... "do good" and the word "cure" or "medicine" is supplied so as to describe the sense that it is "good"-- good for your health!

I.e. A merry, cheerful heart, is good in the specific way that "medicine" is good. It promotes health.

So also
Prov. 15:15 All the days of the afflicted are evil, But he who is of a merry heart has a continual feast.


For this reason, it is the better part of wisdom to cultivate a "cheerful heart" says the wise man

II. Negative: But a broken spirit dries the bones.

Note here also that the attitude (broken spirit) affects physical health (dries the bones).
This says in the most vivid of language that just as dehydrated bones strewn in a grave represents death, so the power of a contrite spirit


Meaning: Let us rejoice in the Lord-- all things pertaining to his creation and salvation-- for even our health can depend upon it.

Phil. 4:4 -18Rejoice in the Lord always. Again I will say, rejoice Let your gentleness be known to all men. The Lord is at hand. Be anxious for nothing, but in everything by prayer and supplication, with thanksgiving, let your requests be made known to God; and the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and minds through Christ Jesus. Finally, brethren, whatever things are true, whatever things are noble, whatever things are just, whatever things are pure, whatever things are lovely, whatever things are of good report, if there is any virtue and if there is anything praiseworthy--meditate on these things.

WLC Question 135
Q What are the duties required in the sixth commandment?
A The duties required in the sixth commandment are,
all careful studies, and lawful endeavors, to preserve the life of ourselves and others by ...patient bearing of the hand of God, quietness of mind, cheerfulness of spirit;

Amazing-- that the "cheerfulness of spirit" was understood by the framers of the WCF to be one way to "preserve life"

That such a cheerful attitude promotes health and life; a gloomy attitude promotes illness and death... has been acknowledged by the social sciences as well...

The medicinal effects of cheerfulness were explored by Norman Cousins in his famous experiment with comedy films and the therapeutic impact of laughter... His research anticipated the discovery of endorphins and other hormonal secretions which are pain blockers and are released by laughter...

Therefore, this proverbs played on the contrast between a "merry heart" and a "broken spirit" -- one brought good health like medicine, the other dehydrated the very bones of the sufferer.

Conclusion:
It is easier to prevent illness than to cure it-- plan to laugh!
A grave temperament must be resisted lest it should sink into morbid depression. Gloom is not the portion , and ought not to stamp the character, of the children of God. (Bridges)

Is a "cheerful heart" compatible with being a Christian?

Charles Bridges
The gospel encourages humiliation, not despondency. It deals in the realities not of woe and despair, but of hope, peace and joy. Its life and glory is he that bindeth up the broken hearted.

If then-- Christians-- you believe the gospel to be "glad tidings" shew that you believe it, by lighting up your face with a smile, not by bowing down the head as a bulrush, and as it were spreading sackcloth and ashes under you. Shew that it is the day light of your soul, that you really find its ways to be "pleasantness and peace

I.e. just as a dark and gloomy day tends to make us gloomy-- so the scripture reminds us that our gospel is "the good news" which lightens up the day of our souls---Christ is the "light of the world"
we should ponder the gospel more so as to fill our souls with the light of Christ's countenance upon us)

Why can a Christian have the best and most enduring "cheerful heart"-- Consider what the Christian believes:

a. God works all things, even our present sorrows, for good:

Rom. 8:28 And we know that all things work together for good to those who love God, to those who are the called according to His purpose..


b. God's grace is sufficient for us, in the midst of our afflictions:

2Cor. 12:9 And He said to me, "My grace is sufficient for you, for My strength is made perfect in weakness." Therefore most gladly I will rather boast in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me.


c. For the redeemed, there is a life to come, free from pain and sorrow.

1Th. 4:13 But I do not want you to be ignorant, brethren, concerning those who have fallen asleep, lest you sorrow as others who have no hope.


d. We do not carry the world's burden on our shoulders; Christ carries the world's burden on His.

1Th. 5:16 Rejoice always,
1Pet. 1:6 In this you greatly rejoice, though now for a little while, if need be, you have been grieved by various trials,
1Pet. 1:8 whom having not seen you love. Though now you do not see Him, yet believing, you rejoice with joy inexpressible and full of glory,
Rev. 19:7 "Let us be glad and rejoice and give Him glory, for the marriage of the Lamb has come, and His wife has made herself ready.''


Application:
What then can we do to foster a "cheerful heart"
Before turning to applications...

Two Neccesary CAVEATS

1. There are sometimes physiological reasons for a person being depressed or gloomy. When this is so, your duty is to see a physician, and to take the prescribed medication.

2. There are moments in life when it is appropriate to be sorrowful:

Eccl. 3:1 To everything there is a season, A time for every purpose under heaven: 4 A time to weep, And a time to laugh; A time to mourn, And a time to dance;. But note that the "season" does not last forever.

And even here, we called upon as Christians who believe in the sovereignty of God to have HOPE!

Rom. 8:18-25 For I consider that the sufferings of this present time are not worthy to be compared with the glory which shall be revealed in us. For the earnest expectation of the creation eagerly waits for the revealing of the sons of God. For the creation was subjected to futility, not willingly, but because of Him who subjected it in hope; because the creation itself also will be delivered from the bondage of corruption into the glorious liberty of the children of God. For we know that the whole creation groans and labors with birth pangs together until now. Not only that, but we also who have the firstfruits of the Spirit, even we ourselves groan within ourselves, For we were saved in this hope, but hope that is seen is not hope; for why does one still hope for what he sees? But if we hope for what we do not see, we eagerly wait for it with perseverance.


But given these caveats: we ought to be wise so as to cultivate a cheerful heart

1. Repent quickly-- knowing the God of mercy because of Christ his Son.

Lawson: Everything that tends to spread gloom over the mind is to be avoided-- it is sin that brings the greatest danger of sorrows--

Who has a happy heart but he that is walking in the joy of Divine acceptance.
Christ made a "merry heart" by his message of Divine forgiveness -- Mt.9:2-7
Matt. 9:2 Then behold, they brought to Him a paralytic lying on a bed. When Jesus saw their faith, He said to the paralytic, "Son, be of good cheer; your sins are forgiven you.''

2. Give thanks specifically.
("Count your many blessings, name them one by one")-- Knowing the God who is the author and maker of everything
All earthly enjoyments are doubly blessed with God

Eccl. 9:7 Go, eat your bread with joy, And drink your wine with a merry heart; For God has already accepted your works.

3. Pray quickly-- knowing that God accepts us for Christ's sake

1Pet. 5:6 Therefore humble yourselves under the mighty hand of God, that He may exalt you in due time, 7 casting all your care upon Him, for He cares for you..

4. Look for evidence of God's goodness and grace-- knowing the God who is sovereign-- who for those called by him to be his children-- he causes all things to work together for good... Employ the words "never" and "always" cautiously.

5. Responsibly Enjoy Creation!-- Morality and Fun are companions not enemies...

a. Speak out against a sort of evangelical platonism
b. Speak out against licentious and irresponsible enjoyments that turn against us and God's intention for creation

1Tim. 4:1 Now the Spirit expressly says that in latter times some will depart from the faith, giving heed to deceiving spirits and doctrines of demons, 2 speaking lies in hypocrisy,... commanding to abstain from foods which God created to be received with thanksgiving by those who believe and know the truth. 4 For every creature of God is good, and nothing is to be refused if it is received with thanksgiving; 5 for it is sanctified by the word of God and prayer.


Let these great truths cheer your heart.

Do not take poison. Do not harbor a negative, gloomy attitude; but cultivate a cheerful heart.
