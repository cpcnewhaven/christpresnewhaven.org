Proverbs 18:10-11

True and False Security

Introduction:

"Security" is a theme that sells-- insurance, financial "securities", even cellular phones-- all use security as a way to tempt you to buy...

Exposition:

Standard Contrast Proverb (Antithetical parrelelism)-- such that two kinds of "securities" are contrasted

I. Part 1: True Security

A strong tower is the name Yaweh, the rightous person runs at it and are set securely on high.

A. Why "the name" Yaweh

1. Is it mysticism-- attribuing power to the use of a name such as "Yaweh"

Note the grave danger of such mysticism among our RC friends... who use names as "charms" so as to have blessing...

By name, it is not meant lit. "Yaweh"

2. By "name" = it is not meant some meaning that could be derived from a study of its derivitive construction, or by etymology...

3. Rather this phrase, "by the name of Yaweh" is a Hebrew way of talking about someones reputation, his character as revealed over time, his power, even his promises-- all wrapped up in the concept of "the name of x"

as to the meaning of the name, Yaweh we are safer if we find the character of God from his works and from th descriptions of HIm in the Scripture rather than to depend on a questionable etymology of his name... "(Theological Wordbook of the OT, Harris, Archer, Waltke)

And so the "name Yaweh" includes those things revealed as attributed to and revealed by "Yaweh"-- it represents the self-disclosure of God as through what he has done and promised to do.

For instance, included in the concept of the "name Yaweh" was

a. the redemptive acts of God in history so as to reveal God's intentions and Character-- which is why God's "name" was often attached to historical people and events...

Ex. 3:15 Moreover God said to Moses, "Thus you shall say to the children of Israel: "The Lord God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac, and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is My name forever, and this is My memorial to all generations.'

Ex. 6:3 "I appeared to Abraham, to Isaac, and to Jacob, as God Almighty, but by My name Lord I was not known to them.

b. "name" represented God's power to save as demonstrated in redemptive history---

Ex. 9:16 "But indeed for this purpose I have raised you up, that I may show My power in you, and that My name may be declared in all the earth.

c. "name" represented God's blessing

Num. 6:27 "So they shall put My name on the children of Israel, and I will bless them.''

(This of course refered to circumcision whereby persons were put under the blessing of God's protection and care as his people...)

d. "name" represented God's reigning in his kingdom...

2Sam. 7:13 "He shall build a house for My name, and I will establish the throne of his kingdom forever.

This then begs the question-- what does this proverb have in mind by the "name Yaweh", i.e. what relevant attribute would this oracle want the hearer to remember so as to know that to have the "name of Yaweh" is to have security?

God's faithfulness to Noah

God's faithfulness to Abraham

God's faithfulness to Isaac, Jacob

God's faithfulness to Job

God's faithfulness to David

All were against the normal "odds" of worldly securities! Each redemptive story highlights that God was more to be depended upon than all the armies, wealth, friends ,etc, in all the world. Each of these men representing God's people, were asked to trust in God against all worldly expectations and means-- this is perhaps the single most important theme that anyone could get from all the OC stories...

Children: Each of the stories you learn are not ultimately about the characters, but about God who can be trusted more than all the things of this world!

Do You remember the story of the destruction of Jericho?--

How the enemies of God trusted in a very strong and well built fort/city-- and especially in a great wall with towers on it...

But who did Joshua trust in? And how does the story of Jericho make this clear to us?

God asked Joshua (whose name itself means, "God saves" from the name "Yaweh" and verb "to save") not to take up arms and trust in their fighting skill, but how did Jericho get taken by God-- throught he blowing of musical instruments (trumpets)

"so the people shouted and the priests blew trumpets... and it happened that the wall fell down" (Joshua 6:20)

Well now you see the point of this proverb--

that God is more dependable, more safe, more trustworthy, than all the strong cities or "securities" that man can make for themselves in all the world.

2. Yaweh is likened to a Strong tower... set securely on High

In ancient near eastern times the "fortified city" all had "high/strong" towers-- this came to represent "security"

But redemptive history had proven what this proverb acknowledges-- that God is a much better security than any fortified city!

and so the rightous (wise) man runs ot God instead of a man made fortified city, and he is set securely on high.

II. Part 2: False Security

The wealth of the rich is a strong city and like a wall that is set securely high in his own imagination.

Brief observations:

1. Part 2 of this proverb uses many of the same images and words so as to make the contrast stand out even clearer...

like a wall set securely high

2. Part 2 equates "strong city" to "strong tower"-- both speak of a fortified city which was synonous to "security" for the ancient near eastern person.

Part 1: the "fortified city" or "security" is Yaweh

Part 2: the "fortified city" or "security" is wealth

Therefore, the contrast is between "wealth" and "Yaweh". The construction of this Hebrew sentence is somewhat convoluted-- two words are used-- "rich" and "wealth" and "rich"seems to be an adjective of "wealth" -- but this accentuates that the "false security" here being spoken of is "wealth"

(Reminescent of Christ's saying when he also contrasted "wealth" or "mammon" to "God" saying, can't serve both-- they are somehow mutually exclusive masters!)

3. What does the proverb mean by "Wealth"?

Anything that empowers a person to have choices so as to control his/her own destiny.

For example:

Earlier period-- "wealth" would have been measured in terms of land and livestock...

Davidic Monarchy: "wealth" would also include access to certain treasures such as "silver", or access to political positions of power in the Monarchy... Wealth would by now be evident in the location of the city one lived in, and the kind of house a person lived in--

Put I hope you see the importance of measuring wealth in terms of "empowerment"-- its having what it takes to have choices and control ones own destiny-- this is the "lure" of wealth that this proverb wants to challenge.

4. Becuase the point of part 2 is to say that "wealth" (whether in terms of ownership or privileged positions in society) is an illusion for security--

The proverb teaches that it is a like a "wall" that is securely high in his own imagination.

An amazing choice of words here:

imagination:

1. Can mean "image" as in what an "idol" would be called..

Lev. 26:1 "You shall not make idols for yourselves; neither a carved image nor a sacred pillar shall you rear up for yourselves; nor shall you set up an engraved stone in your land, to bow down to it; for I am the Lord your God.

Num. 33:52 "then you shall drive out all the inhabitants of the land from before you, destroy all their engraved stones, destroy all their molded images, and demolish all their high places;

Ezek. 8:12 Then He said to me, "Son of man, have you seen what the elders of the house of Israel do in the dark, every man in the room of his idols? For they say, "The Lord does not see us, the Lord has forsaken the land.' ''

What was especially deceptive about idols where that they looked like something, when they really were not-- they had hands, mouths, etc, but they couldn't speak...

So too-- "wealth" could be described as an "idol" or "image" in that it gives the appearance of gaining one secuirty, but it really doesn't!

2. This word is also used to mean "imaganation" or "image in ones own mind"-- just as an idol is powerful in a persons own "image"or "imagination", so wealth is powerful in a persons own imagination...

It is in fact like the ancient fortified city that could never keep God out!

Mainpoint: In order to be wise, one must understand where true security is found-- not in wealth but in God.

So true is this, that the proverbs teaches in yet another proverb, 23:4, --

Prov. 23:4 Do not overwork to be rich; Because of your own understanding, cease 5 Will you set your eyes on that which is not? For riches certainly make themselves wings; They fly away like an eagle toward heaven.

The Proverbs teaches that there is in fact nothing "secure" or "permanant" about wealth such that to work hard for wealth is in fact a foolish thing to do!

Discussion:

I find thereforer, a certain Irony in this proverb: Often Christian's are thought of as the impractical dreamer-- those who perhaps have a freudian need for God so as to fabricate Him in their own imagination-- Amazingly, this proverb turns such thoughts on their head-- shows who really are the "dreamers"-- who are the ones who really fabricate "surities" in their own imagination...

What does "wealth" look like today-- what is our "fortified city"

Today-- our "fortified cities" will look like stocks, bonds, alarm systems, car phones, educations, big houses, insurance plans, retirement packages, or perhaps even poltical power and influence, etc, etc..

Its whatever we trust in to protect us...

Its whatever we trust in to feed us...

Its whatever we think will bring us the "good life"... life without harm and that is blessed of happiness... theses things then become for us our "fortified city" with high walls and strong towers...

Yet one does not have to look very hard to see just how true this proverb is-- that the real "dreamers" are those who think that such things can in fact keep tragedy away, who think that security and happiness can really be "bought"-- the irony then is that those in our society who think themselves most "practical" are perhaps most impractical.
