Text: Pro. 18:22

Marriage is a GOOD Thing

Intro

Exposition

The One who finds a wife, finds good, and (that is) he gains favor from the Lord.

I. The proverbs were addressed originally to young men who were being educated for service to the King. Proverbs is a collection…of materials designed initially for use by the young men of Israel’s society who were being groomed for positions of leadership (Hubbard, 23).

Note: In modern polemics-- patriarchialism has come to mean "chauvinism"--but we shouldn't assume this... not only would this be bad biblical hermeneutics, but its bad theology...

Here, the point is made as if speaking to a group of young me--

He who finds a wife finds a good thing, And obtains favor from the Lord .

Cf. Pro. 19:14: Houses and riches are an inheritance from fathers, But a prudent wife is from the Lord

It could have just as well been said to a group of young women-- when you find a husband, you find a good thing

I.e. Marrieage is a good institution...

II. synonomous parrelelism...

A. First part makes a general observation

B. Second part gives yet another observation that is in parrellel to the first...

Notice the parrellel observations so as to say the same thing with added emphasis

A. A spouse is a "good thing".

1. The text does not say a prudent or wise or diligent wife is a good thing, even though other Proverbs celebrate such traits. Here, for the purposes of our passage, a wife is called "good" even apart from any consideration of her character.

2. The word translated "good" here is the most comprehensive Hebrew word for that which is pleasing, suitable, lovely, appropriate, and helpful-- but expecially, it is God's doing!

At each of the creative days in Genesis 1, the text uses this word to indicate the divine approval upon that which He had made: Six times the text says "God saw that it was good," and at the end of it all, the text says:

31 Then God saw everything that He had made, and indeed it was very good.

And, prior to the creation of woman, Gen. 2.18 says, "

And the Lord God said, "It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him".

3. It's perhaps the intention of the Proverb to reflect upon the Genesis institution of marriage

Notice that in addition to using the word "good" it used the word "find" --

Gen. 2:20 So Adam gave names to all cattle, to the birds of the air, and to every beast of the field. But for Adam there was not found a helper comparable to him.

So here in Prov 18:22, the word "find" is used 2 times so as to say that those who "find" a spouse have found the one thing lacking in creation until god instituted marriage by the making of Eve for Adam...

Thus, having a spouse itself is a good thing,

B. It is an expression of favor from God Himself.

1. Here it is affirmed that a spouse is from the Lord

2. It is an expression of blessing

Religious-- favor granted by God to men in blessings -- acceptable...

II. CAVEAT: The passage does not say "He who finds a wife finds a perfect thing."

The point of the passage is not to say anything specifically about the kind of spouse you have, nor that your evuation of the marriage institution ought fundamentally to be conditioned upon how your spouse is acting on this or that day-- the proverb simply affirms that marriage, as an institution created by God, is a good thing, the one who is married ought therefore to see the good in it-- and with few exceptions, this is irregardless of the kind of spouse you have!

Conclusion. One of God’s greatest expressions of kindness and favor to the human race is marriage, an institution by which we find partnership and companionship as we walk through our earthly life.

Discussion:

What do we know about marriage that makes it so "good."?

1. It is the foundational socieity from which all other socieities are formed...

Samuel Davies:

The great Author of our nature, who has made us sociable creatures, has instituted various societies among mankind, both civil and religious, and joined them together by the various bonds of relation. The first and radical society is that of a family, which is the nursery of the church and state. This was th socieity instituted in Paradise in the state of innocence, when the indulgent Creator, finding that it was not good for a man, a sociable creature, to be alone, formed a helpmeet for him and united them in th eendearing bonds of the conjugal relatoin. From thence, the hyuman race was propagated; and when multiplied, it was formed into civil governments and ecclesiastical assemblies...

Whereas we might not want to say it in 17th century language, we are in great need to reaffirm the sacredness of the marriage institution, for its fundamental goodness and blessing as a common grace from God--. Even with all the flaws introduced into marriage by the fall-- the fact remains that their is a unique and fundamental kind of social oneness that exists between a husband and wife...

2. Marriage is the unique union between a man and a women so as to satisfy the imago dei-- in so far as social companionship between compatable and complementary pairs are ultimately an expression of God's holy socieity of the Trinity---

Gen. 1:26 Then God said, "Let Us make man in Our image, according to Our likeness; let them have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over the cattle, over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.

3. Marriage between a man and a woman is uniquely suitable for the vocational callings of God for his people.

Gen. 1:28 Then God blessed them, and God said to them, "Be fruitful and multiply; fill the earth and subdue it; have dominion over the fish of the sea, over the birds of the air, and over every living thing that moves on the earth.''

The obvious expression of this is the calling to pro-create-- to fill the earth-- which can only be accomplished between a man and a women!

But it is important to see what actually satisfies the vocation requirement of God-- not only to "fill the earth, (I.e. have babies) but also to "subdue the earth"--- I.e. but it under such care and government so as to be productive for the glory of God-- that is to say that children born outside of marriage are born outside of a suitable socieity for government and religion-- the family is and always will be the only truely ideal socieity from which civil and ecclesiastical disciplines are formed...

It was in the home that government was to be extended yet with the unique love and grace that are implanted in parents for their young...

In a family- with the dual efforts of man and women (given especially their unique temperments and roles)-- we have the "harshness of authority tempered by parental affection, interposing an effectual check tot he abuse of power... "

Moreover-- it is in the home that religion is best introduced and formed in the young... our coorporate religion is but the expression of our family religion-- without family religion-- coorporate religion become both sterile and hypocritical-- it is in the home that the real temperments of true religion are are genuinely nurtured!

Therfore, the joint jurisdiction of father and mother over children is perfectly suited so as to accomplish the vocational mandate to fill the earth and subdue it...

More than the vocational calling to raise children-- the husband-wife team is uniquely suited to accomplish the vocational aspects of both public service and ecclesiastical service--

The fact is-- even in most cases of being married to non-Christian spouses-- you are better able to accomplishe the creation mandate within marriage than without--- (not the two expections are in cases of adultery and desertion...)

Otherwise, Two people seeking to manage a home and have a civil and ecclesiastical serive are quite simply better than one accomplishing all this...

Through differentiation of roles, complementariness, etc-- two are better than one!

This is one reason why a spouse who is married to an non-christian spouse-- while perhaps missing some spiritual intimacy-- is all the same much better off to remain married-- for marriage is a common grace institution before it is a special grace institution-- it is a better "state" with two parents than with one, etc.

Application

1. To singles

A. Those who will probably remain single. You are not a second-class citizen; God calls some people to a single life. And if he does, you ought to consider what unique gift you have to offer the church especially due to your singleness...

This was Paul's point

1Cor. 7:8 But I say to the unmarried and to the widows: It is good for them if they remain even as I am;

His advice then was

1Cor. 7:27 Are you bound to a wife? Do not seek to be loosed. Are you loosed from a wife? Do not seek a wife.

Be content in your calling as it presently is-- yet if in fact God does lead you into a relationship so as to contemplate marriage--it is a good thing.

IF not, you have a special service to give to his church as in the case of Paul--

One thing for sure, andperhaps the RC at least recognized this-- although going to far... cellibacy is a service to the church.

2. To those who are married.

a. Give thanks to God for your "good" spouse. Don’t let the devil deceive you into thinking that God’s kindest gift to you is less than a gift. Even if your spouse is not a Christian-- you have the benefits of common grace in marriage and for this you should be thankful!

b. Don’t compare your spouse to an ideal spouse; compare your spouse to being single.

c. Prove God right, by being a "good thing" to your spouse. Don’t try harder; try smarter. Pick specific areas in which you could be a better spouse; whether negative or positive, and resolve, prayerfully, to change them one by one.

3. To all-- whereas I can't stand up here and tell you what political or social theory to endorse so as to protect families-- and whereas I will concede that much that comes in the way of "family politics" is just plain "politics"--

We as Christians ought to strive in our culture to protect, support and promote the sacredness of marriage and family... the family as God difines it in scripture and the freedom of parents.

Among other things, this could be something to talk about in the sermon discussion.

 

4. The Ultimate family--

Whatever good, whatever acceptable we can say about the marriage institution and family-- we must alway remember that it is only but the forshadowing of heaven as considerd a family of God...

John14:2

In my Father's house are many mansions, If it were not so, I would have told you: I go to preapre a place (home) for you."

Among the many figures used in Scripture to represent the blessedness of heaven, none is more instructive and pleasing than that of the family...

For in heaven, we will discover the perfect "father-son" relationship

In heaven, we will be as Christ's brothers calling upon the first person of the godhead as our father

IN heaven, our relation to Christ-- is the relation of a bride to the Groom-- with perfect love and devotions lasting forever in our heavenly home...

In this heavenly family-- there will totol happiness, pleasures and respect--The state of blessedness will make even the best moments of your family room look pale in comparision!

Question: Is there really such a family as this?

Christ says, if it were not I would have told you
