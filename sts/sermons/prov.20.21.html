Exposition:

A possession (inheritance) which is acquired at an early time (first phase, in the beginning) will not be a blessing in the end.

I. Assumes the Parental Ambition/Drive to "Bless" Their Children

Blessing-- the ultimate way to speak of God's goodness applied to people-- covenantal benevolence, care, well being... which included both the common and special graces.

Gen. 1:22 And God blessed them, saying, "Be fruitful and multiply, and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.''

I.e. to be "blessed" is to enjoy the "good life" in the fullest and most Biblical way of defining it! And what parent doesn't want this for their children-- this is assumed!

The Proverb doesn't refute the fact that parants want not the "cursed life" for their children but rather would do practically anything to give their children a life that is fully blessed!

 

But is there something that parents can do thinking they bless their children with the effect ofreally cursing them?

I.e. this proverb is concerned with what "Not a blessing"-- the opposite of blessing is implied here-- a curse--

I.e. to be denied God's goodness, care, well-being-- to be denied the "good life" in the fullest sense of the phrase.

But herein lies the catch of this proverb... it speaks to the issues of...

II. How the Ambition/Drive to Bless Our Children Can actually Curse Them

A. Their is a relation between how we raise our children in the beginning and what experiences our children have in the end.

1. early period- i.e. "first" years, or "beginning time"

I.e. While your child is still a "child"

Intentially "relative"-- so as to place the burden on the reader to discern when it is "too early" to give children certain things.

But generally-- talking about childhood

2. end-- "later years"-- when your child become an adult--

Point: There is something you can do to your child while he/she is young thinking that you "bless" them-- that is actually going to curse them when they become an adult!

The assumption is that if you knew what that was, you would never do it!

More is not better!

Raise your children with a constant fear of over-indulgence.

This is the one point of all on which you have most need to be on your guard. It is natural to be tender and affectionate towards your own flesh and blood and it is the excess of this very tenderness and affection whichyou have to fear.

2. Misplaced values--

Opulance proves a corrupter of their hearts, it panders to their lusts, fixing in them habits of vanity, extravagance, and luxury.

3. Misplaced endurance--(self-denial)

We deny them endulgence and we give them endurance

We give them endulgence and we deny them the character that will produce greater survice to God...

Heb. 10:34 for you had compassion on me in my chains, and joyfully accepted the plundering of your goods, knowing that you have a better and an enduring possession for yourselves in heaven.

It is extremely hard-- but we must carefully allow our children to learn self-denial--

To learn hardship so as to train them for the life of following after Christ...

Christian Life has been compared to a race and a fight by Paul-- to an athletic context... how then would you prepare the athlete for the race-- or a boxer for his fight-

Would you spare him all pains and sufferings, or would you desire for him the streneous practice necessary for winning the race, etc.

So too with our children, we ought

1. To Care for them, consider their frames at various periods of development so as to give them health and emotional happiness

2. We must be careful that this doesn't result in not giving them opportunity to learn self-denial!

4. Misplaced humaness

RC Ryle: Train with this thought continually before your eyes-- that the soul of your child is the first thing to be considered

No part of them should be so dear to you as that part which will never die. The world, with all its glory, shall pass away; the hills shall melt; the heavens shall be wrapped up together like a scroll; the sun shall cease to shine. But the spirit which dwells in those little creatures, whom your love so well, shall outlive them all, whether in happiness or misery (humanly speaking) will depend on you.

In every thing you do and say to them, you should think, "how will this affect their souls.

To pet and pamper and indulge your cyhild, as if this world was all he had to look to, and this life the only season for happiness-- to do this is not true love, but cruelty. It is treating him like some beast of the earth, which has but one world to look to , and nothing after death.

B. Practical Tips:

Parents, I beseech you, for your children's sake, beware of over indulgence. I call on you to remember it is your first duty to consult their real interests, and not their fancies and likings-- to train them, not to humour them; to profit , not merely to please. ... Do not, I pray you, make your children idols, lest God should take them away, and break your idol, just to convince you of your folly.

Some Practical Tips:

1. Be careful that your children do not have more worldly things and advantages than their peers, but who have great godly advantages

-- teach them to trust in that which can't be taken away-- their ability to think, their ingenuity and ultmately their faith

-- beware that your own worldliness is not playing out through your children...

I.e. wanting to impress your neighbors more than really doing whats best for your children in the "end."

2. Be carefuly about "bailing" your children out...

I.e. teach them responsibility and consequences while the consequences are still small.

I.e.

child leaves glove out in the rain-- at the very least, find a way for the child to purchase the next one

3. Touchy-- Grandparents...

Inheritance "too early" from them is equally within the perimeters of this proverb.

a) You will want to at an early stage, perhaps even before you have children, try to discuss your views of childraising, and as best you can "prevent" future misunderstanding

b) Choose your the battles carefully.

I.e. Grandparents see it as their "right" to "spoil" a grandchild--

And perhaps some kinds of "spoiling" is all right-- try though to distinguish those that are materialistic and can have lasting effects--i.e. gifts, "lifestyle" kinds of things

vs. those that are more relational-- i.e. eating out, spending time with doing fun things, vacations, etc.

c) Depending on the grandparents, you may have to endure some very diffucult moments-- remember that for some-- to love is to lavish

this Proverb speaks against this

So to thwart "giving" is in essense to twart love-- therefore, do all you can to help direct the love to more relational and less material things...

Spend 1/2 on a phone budget and call and/or visit

Day trips,

Things relative to possitive training (books, educational, camps.. etc)

4. As children get older-- be very careful that you let them assume more and more fiscal responsibility for their interests--

Car-- let them have some part in it... etc.

Conclusion:

He that has trained his children for heaven, rather than for earth,-- for God, rather than for man,-- he is the parent that will be called wise at last. Ryle

Pss. 37:18 The Lord knows the days of the upright, And their inheritance shall be forever.

The point of this proverb is that to spoil our children with worldly possession will make it more difficult to see their inheritance in God!

This proverb points us to the inhertiance that is eternal and forever a blessing

Heb. 9:15 And for this reason He is the Mediator of the new covenant, by means of death, for the redemption of the transgressions under the first covenant, that those who are called may receive the promise of the eternal inheritance.

In Christ, according to 1Pet. 1:4, we have an inheritance incorruptible and undefiled and that does not fade away, reserved in heaven for you,

Eph. 1:11 In Him also we have obtained an inheritance, being predestined according to the purpose of Him who works all things according to the counsel of His will,

Eph. 1:14 (Christ) is the guarantee of our inheritance until the redemption of the purchased possession, to the praise of His glory.

So that we are told to pray that...

Eph. 1:18 the eyes of your understanding being enlightened; that you may know what is the hope of His calling, what are the riches of the glory of His inheritance in the saints,

Col. 1:12 giving thanks to the Father who has qualified us to be partakers of the inheritance of the saints in the light.

Col. 3:24 knowing that from the Lord you will receive the reward of the inheritance; for you serve the Lord Christ.
