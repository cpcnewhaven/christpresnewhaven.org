Text: Pro. 21:19

Avoiding Contention in the Home

Intro

I Love Lucy-- with all its humor-- it endorsed a kind of marrige that has forever plagued our culture...

A kind of romantic contentiousness... but in real life-- the two don't mix very well...

Exposition: A "better than" proverb.

Better to dwell in the dessert-land than with a (contentious/nagging, quarreling) and angry wife.

A. "To dwell in the wilderness"

Not as we understand wilderness in our culture, as a heavily-forested area with mountain streams. The "wilderness" in the ANE was a desert; an arid region, hot in the day, cold at night, incapable of sustaining life. Recall that Israel survived her wanderings in the wilderness only because God miraculously provided water and food.

Yet this proverb teaches that Dwelling in the mid-easter dessert is actually "better than" something? What could it be better than, because it seems worse than virtually anything?

B. It is better than dwelling with a contentious and angry woman.

Two Key Words:

Contentions: fairly wide range of meaning-- to include "quarrelling, disputes, nagging, complaining, anger

Prov. 23:29 ¶ Who has woe? Who has sorrow? Who has contentions? Who has complaints? Who has wounds without cause? Who has redness of eyes?

Here, it is these things as they infect marriages that is th object of this proverb...

anger-- the "cousin of contentions" means to become vexed, angry, indignant, complaints

1Sam. 1:16 "Do not consider your maidservant a wicked woman, for out of the abundance of my complaint and grief I have spoken until now.''

Prov. 12:16 A fool's wrath is known at once, But a prudent man covers shame.

Prov. 17:25 A foolish son is a grief to his father, And bitterness to her who bore him.

I.e. The two words are put together for the sake of added emphasis, not added meaning.

The point is that there is hardly anything worse than a marriage characterized by contentions, quarreling, complaining ,nagging anger...

C. Does the proverb teach that women are more contentious than men?

i. The proverbs were written for the training and preparation of young men for positions of leadership. Thus, instead of saying "spouse," they say "wife," or "woman."

ii. Pro. 26:21 teaches that men also can be contentious: As charcoal is to burning coals, and wood to fire, So is a contentious man to kindle strife.

Prov. 29:22 An angry man stirs up strife, And a furious man abounds in transgression.

Prov. 26:20 Where there is no wood, the fire goes out; And where there is no talebearer, strife ceases. 21 As charcoal is to burning coals, and wood to fire, So is a contentious man to kindle strife.

Therefore, in spite of locker room antics and male bonding jokes--- this proverb does NOT teach that women are more prone to nagging/contentions than men are!

 

iii. Nonetheless, to do justice to the Bible and the Proverbs, we must acknowledge that the wife and husband do have particular roles which then are uniquely suseptible to sins respecting those roles--

For example-- it is the unique role of the child to obey his/her parents-- such as would warrent the particular command in scripture to avoid rebellion and rebellious attitudes-- contention for children would look like rebellion...

So too, husbands are especially commanded to sacrificially love his wife so as to protect and provide for her-- both spiritually and phyically-- therefore, -- such would warrent the particular command to not be embittered against his wife since this would be a sin against his unique role... Contention applied to husbands would look like embitterness or resentment...

So too-- the Scripture does teach the special duty of a wife to submit to her husband (Eph. 5), and it is therefore particularly wrong for a wife to be contentious so as to aggressively quarrel and nag him...

So here-- whereas the command would be generally applicable to "spouse", the mere volume of this teaching in proverbs suggests the unique temptation facing wives in this area so as to warrent this particular command...

Prov. 21:9 Better to dwell in a corner of a housetop, Than in a house shared with a contentious woman.

Prov. 19:13 A foolish son is the ruin of his father, And the contentious/nagging of a wife are a continual dripping.

Prov. 25:24 It is better to dwell in a corner of a housetop, Than in a house shared with a contentious woman.

Prov. 27:15 A continual dripping on a very rainy day And a complaining/contentious woman are alike;

All the same, the proverb is applicable to all contention in the marriage especially== nothing is worse than a contentious, quarreling marriage...

iv. To appreciate the force of this proverb, we must recall the original creative purpose for man and woman. The woman was created by God to be man’s "helper" or "ally"

Gen. 2:18 And the Lord God said, "It is not good that man should be alone; I will make him a helper comparable to him."

The purpose of God’s making us male and female was to work together as allies in cultivating the earth to God’s glory. Allies are to work together, not against one another.

Mainpoint-- The point then of this proverb is to say that a contentious spouse is in fact counter-productive and harmful to the original intention of the marriage institution... Thus, it is our special duty not to make dwelling in our homes worse than dwelling in the wilderness.

If it is bad when there are contentions in the world, it is doubly bad when those contentions are found within a home. A home should be a place of cooperation in a world of competition and contention. A home should be a place of peace in a world of strife.

Applications: Becomea Peacemaker in your home and world...

Blessed are the Peacemakers...

1) Peace is one aspect of God's character for he is called the "God of peace"

Rom. 15:33 Now the God of peace be with you all. Amen.

2) Peace is one of the great blessings of God to his people "shalom" (Num6:24ff, Prov. 16:7

Prov. 16:7 When a man's ways please the Lord, He makes even his enemies to be at peace with him.

3) God repeatedly commands his people to seek and pursue peace

Prov. 12:20 Deceit is in the heart of those who devise evil, But counselors of peace have joy.

Therefore,

Roms. 12:18, If it is possible, as far as it depends on you, live at peace with everyone.

Read The Peacemaker, Ken Sande--

How to be a peace maker in your home?

1. Change in Orientation with respect to life so as to make it your ambition to Glorify God-- when we feel mistreated or apposed, our instinctive reaction is to devote all my resourses and energies to defending and promoting myself-- impulsive decision are made and we usually make matters worse!

Therefore, our first stage ought to be a reaffirmation and determination to "glorify God"

So whether you eat or drink or whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God. 1 Cor. 10:31

Observations:

1. No one chooses, consciously, to be contentious. No one makes a New Year’s Resolution: "This year I’m going to be contrary." Rather, contentiousness is merely the natural result of our self-centeredness. We do not submit to God for his glory, so we surely aren’t going to submit to anyone else.

By contrast, as we grow in our submission to God, we will find it easier to submit to, and cooperate with, others.

2. Think about how foolish so many disagreements are. They aren’t about significant issues; they are about when and how and how much. Yet we fight about them as though they were matters of grave importance. Again We need a greater sense of God's glory and epsecially his eternity so as to make most things in life far more trivial!

If we had a greater sense of God's glory and eternity, we would not think it worth our time to quarrel over most things that we quarrel over...

3. Those who are aware of God's glory will in the inevitable times where there are conflicts,

a. Look for the opportunity provided by conflict for growth in godliness and the promotion of the common good.

Perhaps it is an opportunity to show mercy

Perhaps it is an opportunity to be humble an consider only your own imperfections and "logs" that are in your eye..

b. Look beyond the immediate issue for the good in the person so as to serve them!

Prov. 11:27, He who seeks good finds good will, but evil comes to him sho searches for it."

I.e. don't let the devil deceive you into seeing only the "bad"-- there is something "worthy of praise" in the other person and even in the situation-- or else we wouldn't be commanded to "let you mind dwell on these things" according to Philip 4.

2. Peacemaking tactics in the home

1) Avoiding--

Prov. 17:14 The beginning of strife is like releasing water; Therefore stop contention before a quarrel starts.

appropriate when trivial issues and minor offenses are involved. I.e. We must ALWAYS ask the question "Is it really worth fighting over"

Prov. 19:11, A person's wisdom gives him patience; it is to his glory to overlook an offense.

You will want to define the issues, consider how you may grow and leave the other person to God-- hoping perhaps that he/she is also doing the same...

This style trusts in God as He is at work in the other person without your "help."

You will want to count the cost of pursueing it further-- many times it will not be after the glory of God to do so-- you can simply make your peace with the situation and find a way to even use it to become a better person!

You do this when at all possible and avoid further conflict!

Don’t forget Paul’s "putting off/putting on." If you find it hard to avoid being contentious, put on cooperation. Learn to say:

"That’s a good idea; I’ll try to help you with it."

"How could I help you with that?"

"That’s an interesting point of view; I hadn’t thought about it that way."

Or even "Whatever you say, Dear."

But what about my "rights" ?

Rights can = "legal" rights-- those freedoms that are protected by civil laws.

Right can= "social rights"-- those freedoms that are protected by a soveriegn audience of peers-- th social standards or expectations...

Keep in mind that much of what is a legal or social "right" today is not necessarily "right" when viewed from a biblical perspective.

Example-- Supreme Court Justice Antonin Scalia:

What is lawful is not always right. Confusing the two concepts isparticularly easy for the English speaking because we use the word "right" to refer both to legality and to moral appropriateness.. (For example) we say we have a "right" to plead the fifth and refuse to answer questions about possible criminal activity-- even when th consequences of exercising that "right" may cause an innocent person to be convicted... Exercising such a "right" is certainly "wrong."

Be careful about "rights" -- as persons you are still very much influenced by your culture-- both legal and social culture-- but as CHristians, you ought to be concerned about only one culture-- that is the culture of God and his view of right and wrong!

So next time you are tempted to "excercise your rights" in that marraige arguement ask yourself...

1. Will exercising my rights pelase and honor God?

2. Will it advance God's kingdom in my home, church, and city?

3. Will it benefit others?

4. Is exercising my rights really essential to my own well-being as defined by God?

2. Accommodating-- This style is appropriatre when the issues are unimportant compard to the value of the relationship or when we may be wrong or have an opportunity to "consider the other as more important than myself"

3. Compromising-- second perhaps to avoiding-- this ought to be the most common style... This style is appropriate when no sin or evil intent is involved, but it is about those matters of preference such that to "split the difference" is a way to for both parties to be respected...

Be careful, however, that compromise doesn't lead to manipulation... or that both parties can make their peace with the "half" solution... so as not to end up in yet another conflict...

Example: Dog in house...

4. Mediation- - when all the above 3 fails-- you are most likely in a "competing" tactic, so get help so as to have the benefits of mediation, team work, greater objectivity, etc.

I.e. at times conflict can push you beyond your limits. You may have a difficult time understanding how to respond, what God' s word teaches concerning an issue, or being objective enough to genuinely seek after the glory of God and the common good-- this is when you will want to seek help from the church...

Notice then that the first three styles or the ones that we should most concern ourselves... and only in a last ditch effort to we go to mediation...
