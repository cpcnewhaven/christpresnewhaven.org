Prov. 21:5

"Haste Makes Waste"

Introduction:

A time of year when many are looking forward to the new year--

"new year resolutions..." And who has kept them?

Exposition:

Classic contrast proverb-- where part one is set in opposition to part 2-- part one being the right way to live...

The plans (thoughts) of the industrious (diligent) always lead to an advantage, but everything (Heb-cal) done in a hurry (haste) always leads to need.

I. What exactly is being contrasted?

Two grammaical options:

1. Perseverance-- being diligent to carry out ones "thoughts/plans"... ... I.e. "don't give up"-- whatever you are doing-- the point then is to do it with diligence...

I.e emphasis not upon what you do, but how you do it!

2. Carefulness-- being diligent in making plans-- do not make plans quickly... I.e. "think before you act" or "haste makes waste"...,

I.e. emphasis is upon what you do, being dilegent to plan carefully

Solution:

In the original, part 2 assumes that the reader will supply the subject being modified by the word "all or "everything"

1. NRSV & NKSV -- "everyone" such that the subject is a person

Result-- diligent persons (workers in general) being contrasted with "hasty workers

Problem:

a. assumes a subject not before mentioned

b., would expect "diligence" to be contrasted with "laziness" instead of "hasty or hurried"

2. NIV-- chooses not to insert a subject, but leaves it open

Provf.21:5 the plans of the diligent lead to profit as surely as haste leads to poverty. (NIV)

I.e. haste with respect to what?

Solution:

Part 2 borrows the subject "thoughts/plans" such that what is being contrasted is plans made thoughtfully (diligently) vs. plans made hastily!

1. The contrast between "hasty" and "diligence" makes more sence

2. Fits the focus of the proverb as evident by the word order (see below)

II. Focus of this proverb is evident by the "syntax" (word order)-- I.e. In the Original-- the word translated "thoughts, plans, intentions" is first in order.

Broadly used in Heb-- to refer to basically "the thoughts or intention" of a person-

Gen. 6:5 ¶ Then the Lord saw that the wickedness of man was great in the earth, and that every intent of the thoughts of his heart was only evil continually.

Is. 55:8 ¶ "For My thoughts are not your thoughts, Nor are your ways My ways,'' says the Lord. 9 "For as the heavens are higher than the earth, So are My ways higher than your ways, And My thoughts than your thoughts.

Pss. 40:5 Many, O Lord my God, are Your wonderful works Which You have done; And Your thoughts toward us Cannot be recounted to You in order; If I would declare and speak of them, They are more than can be numbered.

Pss. 56:5 All day they twist my words; All their thoughts are against me for evil.

Pss. 92:5 O Lord, how great are Your works Your thoughts are very deep.

Pss. 94:11 The Lord knows the thoughts of man, That they are futile.

In a more specific sense, one could tranlate the word as "plans:"

Job 5:12 He frustrates the devices of the crafty, So that their hands cannot carry out their plans.

Prov. 6:18 A heart that devises wicked plans, Feet that are swift in running to evil,

Prov. 15:22 Without counsel, plans go awry, But in the multitude of counselors they are established.

Prov. 15:26 The thoughts of the wicked are an abomination to the Lord, But the words of the pure are pleasant.

Prov. 16:3 Commit your works to the Lord, And your thoughts will be established.

Prov. 19:21 There are many plans in a man's heart, Nevertheless the Lord's counsel--that will stand.

Prov. 20:18 Plans are established by counsel; By wise counsel wage war.

The point here then is to focus not so much on how hard we work, but on planning itself-

III. There is Value in Diligent Planning:

Contrast-- between those who act "hastily " (hurriedly) vs. those who with diligence act thoughtfully

1.Diligent planning- leads to an advantage

Diligent: Heb-- word could also be "sharpness" used figuratively-- those who "sharpen their plans"

Advantage-- prosper, succeed, have abundance

I.e. Those who apply diligence to planning will be successful in life

Contrasted with:

2. Hasty planning-- which leads to want (need)

I.e. Those who don't apply diligence to planning, but in fact act impulsively-- will be unseccessful in life

Note: the proverb here dosn't define success in valuative terms (leaves that to the sanctified person given other scriptures)-- rather the proverb makes the general observation from history-- that those who make careful plans tend to succeed more so than those who don't!

I.e. This proverb affirms that we ought to be diligent vs. hasty in our thoughts, intentions- plans...

It affirms that our thoughts are in fact important to God-- since what we in fact do first originates with what we think!

Mainpoint: We have a Biblical duty to take planning seriously-- realizing that "haste makes waste."

One could say then that planning is a Biblical duty! The very existance of this proverb suggests that the way we spend our time is in fact a moral issue--

Prov. 12:5 The thoughts of the righteous are right, But the counsels of the wicked are deceitful.

Eph. 5:15 See then that you walk circumspectly, not as fools but as wise, 16 redeeming the time, because the days are evil. 17 Therefore do not be unwise, but understand what the will of the Lord is.


Therefore, we are commanded to "commit our work to the Lord so as to establish our plans"...

Prov. 16:3 Commit your work to the LORD, and your plans will be established. 4 The LORD has made everything for its purpose, even the wicked for the day of trouble.

What seems to be in view is the simply reality that there is a time for everything God has called us to do-- therefore, be careful that we discern what god has called us to do and do it-- the discerning process itself is what we mean by "planning."

A. Two ways this proverb could be violated--

1. That we not apply diligence to planning so as to be lazy with our time

I.e. takes one day at a time-- and with nothing to do gets nothing done

Prov. 6:6 Go to the ant, you lazybones; consider its ways, and be wise. 7 Without having any chief or officer or ruler, 8 it prepares its food in summer, and gathers its sustenance in harvest. 9 How long will you lie there, O lazybones? When will you rise from your sleep? 10 A little sleep, a little slumber, a little folding of the hands to rest, 11 and poverty will come upon you like a robber, and want, like an armed warrior.

2. That we not apply diligence to planning so as to be frantic with our time--

I.e takes one day at a time-- and with nothing planned lives under the tyranny of the urgent-- especially urgent as seems urgent at that particular moment...

Prov. 19:2 Also it is not good for a soul to be without knowledge, And he sins who hastens with his feet.

Application:

Before we then go on to apply this passage to making plans--

A. Three Common Misunderstandings about Time Management:

1. "Time management means we prioritize our schedules."

Rather, means we schedule our priorities.

2. "Time management means learning to get more things done in less time."

Actually, this can made things worse, we merely do more but not directed at ultimate values, etc. This is exactly what the Prov. 21:5 is against.

Rather, means we learn to do those things that best represent our values.

3. "Time management means keeping a schedule."

"Anything less than a conscious commitment to the important is an unconscious commitment to the unimportant."

Busyness might very will lead to a barren life wasted on the least important values.

Rather: What is needed is a new way of thinking. Learning how to manage our life after God's priorities and not so much learning how to manage our time. It means we learn how to make our calendars represent our values. It means learning how to differentiate between what is urgent and the things that are truly important.

B. Warning signs of an "unplanned life"

1. Lack of quality in Personal relationships with significant others

2. Lack of Contentment at the end of the day/week/year.

3. Busyness without accomplishment.

4. Go from deadline to deadline or the "tyranny of the urgent."

5. Inability to control "time robbers."

procrastination

interruptions by people without apt.

poor delegation

poor use of telephone

wasted time on junk mail

ruled by deadlines

6. Time spent on praise related accomplishment rather than value related accomplishments.

7. Paper shuffling/dish shuffling/cloths shuffling, etc. (which can give a false sense of order in midst of disorder measured in terms of our most important values)

8. Either miss or often late to appointment.

9. Emotional instability

10. You find yourself talking about "time" a lot.

C. How to Manage Your Time: Putting First Things First

1. Create a personal life mission statement.

a. General Purpose statement

b. General Values statement

c. Identify your Life roles (callings)

Husband

Father

Pastor

Presbyter

Citizen

d. Prioritize your life roles (no more than 7) in lieu of ultimate importance

Note: Give first priority to roles related to those institutions that your are morally obligated to. (Family, Church, State)

Example: husband, father, church member, vocation, service group member, etc.

e. Create a vision for how your purpose and values would generally be reflected in your life roles.

Write a vision statement for each role-- what would it look like if you were successful

2. Establish and Plan your Priority Goals as follows:

In order to accomplish that vision-- I need to do X

Warning-- make sure your vision pertains to things you can in fact control-- Have a biblical view of "success:

Biblical Definition of Success:

Faithfully pleasing God with the resources and responsibilities that He has give you in conformity to the teachings and practices of Christ, who alone is the author and perfector of our faith.

Conclusion: Here again, who here can say that he/she has lived perfectly in this way-- who has not squandered time by having always Planned diligently according to Godly values--
