Prov. 23:17-18

Introduction:
Sacrates, when asked, "what is most troublesome to good men? answered "The prosperity of the wicked."
Note also David, Psm.37:3, 17 and Psm 73-- who was for awhile shaken in his faith by observing that the wicked prospered...

Today-- It is still one of the most troublesome realities to the detriment of faith... just one episode of the "The Lifestyles of the Rich and Famous", or a show like "Entertainment Tonight"-- will do it.

Exposition:
(17) Do not be jealous with your heart toward sinners, but all the more (be zealous) for the fear of the Lord all the days.
(18) For surely there is an end (life after death), and your cord (hope) will not be cut off.

Note:
1. vs. 17: Antithetical parallelism: A teaching in Wisdom is stated first in the negative as what not to do, then in the positive as what to do--
2. vs. 18: The wisdom itself is grounded by an observation-- tells us why it is the better part of wisdom to do what vs. 17 tells us to do

I. What not to do and what to do in order to be wise:

A. What not to do: Do not let your heart be envious of sinners...
What exactly does this mean?

1. Is the Point-- not to envy their sin? No!
If so, why not say: "Do not sin"?
It means not to envy those who, because they are sinners, pursue (and often attain) this world's pleasures and riches.
An amazing recognition-- that there ARE immediate and apparent payoffs to sin-- and who has not observed this? It is when sin appears to work that this proverb takes seriously.
WE are not to envy the lifestyle that can result from sin!

a. That lifestyle (worldly advantage) that result in any lack of conformity unto God's commands-- where a person simply doesn't fulfill all that God commands-- stopping short in order to protect some worldly advantage
b. That lifestyle that can result from a transgression of God's command-- when a person simply does something that God has commanded us not to do-- in order to get ahead

Note-- I find the first type of sin most tempting to Christians-- its to simply not go the extra mile that God requires so as to protect worldly pleasures or lifestyles!

The point is not that we are to not envy sin, but that we are not to envy the lifestyle that seems to be "working" when people do sin...

2. The word "be not envious"

a. Same word used to denote jealousy
I.e.. Be not jealous of... close to covet...
Likewise used in:
Prov. 24:19 Do not fret because of evildoers, Nor be envious of the wicked; 20 For there will be no future life for the evil man; The lamp of the wicked will be put out.

B. What to do: But all the more be zealous for the fear of the Lord all the day.

1. English obscures the radical contrast that is being presented by this wisdom saying...
I.e. Heb.--second clause is without a verb-- which means that it borrows the verb "to be envious" from the first clause-- and yet so as to make a play on words..
Just as the verb can mean "to be envious, or jealous"
So it can also be used to mean "be not zealous for, "eager to have"
Therefore,
Rather than being zealous for the world and its fading pleasures, we should be zealous to fear God in all things.
Or, could say that rather than living with envy for the fleeting pleasures of this world, a wise person lives with envy for God's approval and blessing

2. We have already seen what the fear of the Lord is-- it is the essence, or beginning of wisdom whereby we live life fearing the one who alone has the power and authority to bless or curse...
I.e. living with the reality that...
God's curse is worse than any other curse
God's blessings are more gratifying than any other blessing
Here-- the proverb reminds us that we are to envy God's approval, since God alone is worth fearing-- rather than worldly pleasures!

3. So the Heb text contains the Curious phrase...
co yom... co yom-- hard to translate but is used so as to add exclamation to a point!
Here, it could be translated "all the more" truly, indeed...
I.e. do not be jealous for the lifestyle of a sinner, but indeed, all the more, surely-- live zealous for the approval of God... this is what it means to be wise!


II. Why is it wise to be zealous for the fear of God rather than jealous for the lifestyle of sinners?

A. Again, the Heb uses the phrase "co yom" so as to connect the above exclamation with what is here presented as a certain reality-- I.e. "For surely, or "you can bet on the fact that"...

B. there is an end
-- as in the completion of something in the future
-- as in a particular time
-- as in life after death-- the hereafter

C Their hope will not be cut off. and your cord (hope) will not be cut off.
A Possible intended image--
-- word for "hope" is also the word for "cord"
So that the picture is of someone hanging on a cliff--
For some-- their cord is cut and they fall to destruction
For those who fear God-- their cord is not cut off... and they make it to the top

Prov. 24:14 Know that wisdom is such to your soul; if you find it, you will find a future, and your hope will not be cut off.


To envy the lifestyles of sinners is foolish, not wise,-- and this is because what we know about the future, that the righteous will be blessed, and the sinner will be cursed...

Pss. 37:37 Mark the blameless man, and observe the upright; For the future of that man is peace. 38 But the transgressors shall be destroyed together; The future of the wicked shall be cut off.
Pss. 73:17 Until I went into the sanctuary of God; Then I understood their end.
Prov. 5:4 But in the end she is bitter as wormwood, Sharp as a two-edged sword.
Prov. 14:12 There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death. 13 Even in laughter the heart may sorrow, And the end of mirth may be grief.
Prov. 16:25 There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death.


Point: This life is not all there is, and the life to come is far more glorious and superior to the one now-- so that the wise person is zealous for the life that is to come more so than the life which is now!
Therefore, the wise person understands that the hope of the ungodly, the hypocrite and the worldly will only perish--

Meaning: Because of what we know about the future, it is not wise to be jealous for the lifestyles of sinners, but it is wise to be zealous for the approval of God.


Children: Have you seen a spider web recently-- not very strong, can easily be broken...

Job 8:13 So are the paths of all who forget God; And the hope of the hypocrite shall perish, 14 Whose confidence shall be cut off, And whose trust is a spider's web.

This is taught elsewhere:

Prov. 11:7 When a wicked man dies, his expectation will perish, And the hope of the unjust perishes.
Luke 12:19 "And I will say to my soul, "Soul, you have many goods laid up for many years; take your ease; eat, drink, and be merry.'' ' 20 "But God said to him, "Fool This night your soul will be required of you; then whose will those things be which you have provided?'


But the hope of those who fear the Lord will not perish but will be satisfied and then some!

Pss. 62:5 My soul, wait silently for God alone, For my expectation is from Him.
Pss. 71:5 For You are my hope, O Lord God; You are my trust from my youth.


Stated in the New Covenant:

Col. 3:1 If then you were raised with Christ, seek those things which are above, where Christ is, sitting at the right hand of God. 2 Set your mind on things above, not on things on the earth. 3 For you died, and your life is hidden with Christ in God. 4 When Christ who is our life appears, then you also will appear with Him in glory.

Application:

1. Let's not avoid the truth -- that there is an immediate and apparent "payoff" to living unaccountable to God.

I.e. we must be careful to present the gospel only in terms of a payoff-- and especially when the payoff is said to be now!

Caveat:
It is true that many things in life are made better by Christian faith--
1. Knowing the Creator-- we can enjoy more the things of creation (last week)
2. Having God's blueprint for living in the home-- our homes will be happier than without God
3. Being in concert with the ultimate purpose in life which is to glorify God-- our vocations and lifestyles will be more meaningful and "full" than without God
4. Trusting in God's sovereignty over life-- we have reason to cast all our cares upon him so as to have greater psychological peace perhaps...
5. Living for God will in fact produce deeper joys and pleasures in the experience of what we have even in the long run in life
Think, for example-- of the deep joy that is felt by a couple who lived through the tumultuous 30-40's so as to have share together the memories of life together in their later years...

But, having said this, the Bible does not avoid the simple reality that there are in fact immediate payoffs to sin--- any lack of conformity unto or transgression of the law of God will have its worldly advantages (or we wouldn't be tempted in the first place)
This is the point of this proverb-- and the point of such Pss. as 37 and 73...
Perhaps we can think of some:
Temptation-- more to fall short of God's commands in order to get ahead rather than to explicitly transgress a command

1. It is possible that if I fall short of my duties as a father or husband, and give more time to my work-- that I will "get ahead" of my colleagues who don't so as to become more powerful, privileged or prestigious in the world...

2. It is possible that if I fall short of my duties as a wife or mother, and give myself to personal career and ambition-- that I will find greater "personal fulfillment" in a worldly sense.
The ole: but I've got to do something for me" temptation-- it might work for a time...

3. It is possible that to fall short in my service to God so as to be less sacrificial in my time and spending as would be called upon for the love of Christ and Christ's people-- that I will have less extravagant lifestyle than those who are less sacrificial-- resulting in less of a house, less of a car, less of clothing, less of freedom to play sports, etc., etc.
4. Surely, for those who live for God--their time is under more restraints-- simply for reason that they are willing to be in commitments...

5. Irresponsibility does "cop-out" so as to be less encumbered by expectations of others, etc.


And who among us has not at times looked at the apparent ease of those less committed to Christ only to wish for a moment that you were them-- as the proverbs states, "envied sinners"

And lets not reduce this merely to being jealous of blatant sinners-- for it can happen at every level.

1. We envy sinners-- those who do not profess faith in Christ
2. We envy compromising Christians-- those who profess faith in Christ but who still live in want of full conformity to the commands of God...

I.e. well x is a Christian and he/she doe it, or has it, etc.
This is the same as envying sinners, especially when it is due to incomplete sanctification that our brother or sister enjoys certain worldly pleasures-- and given the relative immaturity of even the evangelical community, it is no wonder that we see so many so called "Christians" enjoying the worldly life!

We must therefore not avoid this reality-- that sin will have its payoffs!

2. Whereas we acknowledge the worldly pleasures gained by ungodliness-- we are wise not to envy sinners for their worldly pleasure.

Where is your heart? Do you desire, above all, the advantages, riches, and pleasures of this life; or do you set your heart and hope on the life to come? What do you desire? What do you pray for?

How then can we foster the wisdom of not being jealous for the lifestyles of sinners, but rather to walk in the fear of God?
Key: We must contemplate the future
It is quite simply delaying gratification-- like we do in so many other areas of our life
For who hasn't denied themselves that pleasure, so as to have greater pleasure later.

-- you deny yourself a party to study-- because you believe there will be greater gratification later if you graduate
- you deny yourself that piece of food-- because you believe you will be happier to live the few extra years it might bring
-- you deny yourself a new toy-- because you are saving for a greater toy...


What if you really believed in the life hereafter?
When the cross becomes heavy, you have but a little time to bear it! If the way be wearisome, surely the end will abundantly compensate--

Meanwhile, judge not too hastily, by immediate sense or feeling-- hold fast to the promises of God

James 5:11 Indeed we count them blessed who endure. You have heard of the perseverance of Job and seen the end intended by the Lord--that the Lord is very compassionate and merciful.

3. In order to not envy the lifestyle of sin, we need more to contemplate the hereafter--

Jer. 9:11 "I will make Jerusalem a heap of ruins, a den of jackals. I will make the cities of Judah desolate, without an inhabitant.''
John 13:7 Jesus answered and said to him, "What I am doing you do not understand now, but you will know after this.''

How would contemplation of the hereafter change our envy today

1. If we knew that God was preparing us for better and eternal joys by our sufferings today-- then perhaps we would envy those who suffer
2. If we knew that What seems "fun" and "enjoyable" are really the seeds of destruction--

I.e. When we do see the destruction's brought by sin let it serve to remind you of a far greater destruction yet to come...
Consider that when we see in this world the pain of sin-- divorce, premature death, illness-- these things are forewarnings of much more drastic and tragic things to come...
How sad it is, to see people flaunt God all their lives, only to discover the fear of dying.
How sad to see people who for the thrill of sinning, lost their health, their families, their sanity even--

And these things are but the beginnings-- they serve the wise with a divine warning of things to come

Milton wrote of Lucifer in Paradise Lost,
"to reign is worth ambition, though in hell. Better to reign in hell than to serve in heaven."

A wise person knows that nothing could be further from the truth-- to reign in hell is to reign only in destruction...
I.e. The wise person considers the end of seduction, not the beginning

Prov. 5:4 But in the end she is bitter as wormwood, Sharp as a two-edged sword.
The wise person understand that the immediate laughter is not to be unscrutinized in light of its future destiny!
Prov. 14:12 There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death. 13 Even in laughter the heart may sorrow, And the end of mirth may be grief.
Prov. 16:25 There is a way that seems right to a man, But its end is the way of death.

Are you wise? Then you will turn your zeal toward God-- you will desire like nothing else the love of God--such love as has been revealed in Christ for the forgiveness of sins.

Rom. 5:2 through whom we have obtained access to this grace in which we stand; and we boast in our hope of sharing the glory of God.
Eph. 1:12 so that we, who were the first to set our hope on Christ, might live for the praise of his glory.
Col. 1:27 To them God chose to make known how great among the Gentiles are the riches of the glory of this mystery, which is Christ in you, the hope of glory.
Titus 2:13 while we wait for the blessed hope and the manifestation of the glory of our great God and Savior, Jesus Christ.
1Pet. 1:21 Through him you have come to trust in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory, so that your faith and hope are set on God.
