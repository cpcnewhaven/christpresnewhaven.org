Exposition

Setting: The proverbs were addressed originally to young men who were being educated for service to the King.

Again, we must therefore interpret this passage as teaching a principle that is trans-gender, but stated in gender specific ways...

I.e. if wanted to teach against adultery to women, may use langauge fitting to a context of all women.. and vice versa

Therefore, this passage is as much against men adulterers and women adulterers, etc...

Instead of the enticement brought to men by women, we could just as well assume the danger of women being inticed by men beauty, etc...

Note also that of the thirteen verses in this passages, only one (25) contains commands. The other verses describe the protection that the command gives, and the disasters that come to those who refuse God’s protection-- so as to warn us that "sexual love" is not "free"

I. vs. 23: The General value of God's commandments and law as a lamp/light to our path to life in conlusion to 1-22.

For a lamp is the commandment and the law is a lamp and the way of life are by reproofs and instruction

Here: "law"(Torah) is used to describe God's Word (in this context consisting of Pentatuch)-- as this is used interchangably in Psm.119 for instance

Pss. 119:105 ¶ Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path.

Just as a "lamp" and light were often symbols for God's guiding presence (Ex.27:20, 1 Sam.3:3, etc) so God's Word is his mediated presense to guide and lead us for protection, preseveration and santification...

Pss. 18:28 It is you who light my lamp; the LORD, my God, lights up my darkness.

So here-- we would expect that this Proverb will seek to apply something taught in the Mosaic Law as it is God's guiding light, his mediated presence to preserve us is applied specifically...

And so-- we see this pattern in this passage-- where it will begin talklilng about God's light/ commandment protects/ keeps(vs. 24) us and this in relation to our not losing our soul/life (another way of talking about the sum total ofo ur life-- both qualitatively and quantitatively speaking(vs.26)

So that whatever this passage is about to apply from God'slaw- it is for the sake of protecting us against self-destruction!

Raises the question-- how might we be destroyed?

II. vs. 24: Being enticed t by a "foreign" woman.

NKJV- (God's law is)to keep you from the evil woman, from the flattering toungue of a seductress

My Lit: To keep you from the foriegn woman, from the smooth tongue of a foreigner.

Translation notes:

1. The hebrew word(evil) is very close to word "foreigner", only being differentiated by a vowel mark which would have been absent...

2. The hebrew word here "seductress" is almost always trans "foreigner"-- it was only by association with being "pagan" that this word would have been trans. "evil"-- but a very forced trans. (I believe influence through post Maccabean period)

In 45 uses, not one is "sedductress"... except here and in other Prov.5:10, 7:5 20:16, 23:7, 27:13 where it could just as well be "foreign wives" and in the case of 27:13 it clearly ought to be...

And in some cases it is explicitly used for "foreign woman"

1Kings 11:1 But King Solomon loved many foreign women, as well as the daughter of Pharaoh: women of the Moabites, Ammonites, Edomites, Sidonians, and Hittites--

1Kings 11:8 And he did likewise for all his foreign wives, who burned incense and sacrificed to their gods.

3. The Grk. OT (sept) trans. the word "foriegn" so as to refer to a "foreign woman (and by implication man)

4. vs. 34-- speaks of a "jealous' husband-- thus giving support to the focus being on a "foreign" wife...

5. This phrase is similiar (see below) to the 10th commandments-- which says "neighbors wife"

I therefore take this proverb to be a warning against being "seduced" by someone other than your spouse basically! This could include an "evil" man/woman or "prostitute" or even a "foreign" man/woman in the sense of those not your spouse

And by the phrase "smooth tongue" it is meant "charm" (not necessarily evil charm) so as to be warned against the temptation to be charmed sin against marriage institution through sexual sin...

I.e. the realism of this proverb is fascinating-- knowing that we are "charmed" by what is not ours.

II. 25, The command:

Do not covet her beauty in your heart and do not be taken in by her eyelashes

A. Do not covet after her beauty in your heart,

Does not prohibit recognizing beauty; God is the author of beauty, and it is appropriate to recognize beauty wherever it is found.

The verb translated in you english "desire"or "lust" is actually the exact same word translated "covet" in the tenth commandment:

Ex. 20:17 You shall not covet your neighbor’s house; you shall not covet your neighbor’s wife, etc

So we see what "commandment" which this proverb had in mind all along--

It is this command that is God's light of protection and guidance...

Intersestingly, it is also the same word that got Eve in trouble in the Gardon

Gen. 3:6 So when the woman saw that the tree was good for food, and that it was a delight to the eyes, and that the tree was to be desired to make one wise, she took of its fruit and ate; and she also gave some to her husband, who was with her, and he ate.

B. Nor let her allure (take) you with her eyelids.

Similarly here, the command prohibits being "allured" by her eyes. The verb literally means "take", and is used in some contexts to mean "marry," or have "sexual relations with...

Lev. 20:14 If a man takes (marries) a woman and her mother, it is wickedness.

These two commands are two ways of saying the same thing:

It is not sinful to recognize beauty; it is sin to desire what is unlawful to desire. Specifically, it is sinful to permit anyone or any thing to make us unfaithful to our commitments and covenants. Since sex is an covenant act of marriage in the Bible-- and since marriage is designed by God to be monogomas and for the duration of life-- to be "taken" by another, foreign woman, is to break covenant and consequently to lose ones soul and life-- even as Adam and Eve lost soul/life when they took of the tree!

Discussion:

This proverb assumes a Biblical knowledge of the meaning of sex in marriage (For the most part, I will assume it as well) But by way of brief review--

1. Sex was treated as both the initiation and consumation of marriage--

2. Consider that marriage as constituted by the covenant act of sex is ultimate an earthly mirror of God himself as a plurality of persons being one in the Trinity!

I.e. Sexual union is an aspect of the created image of the Triune God in relation together. (Gen.1:26, So God created humans in His image, in the image of God He created them male and female he created them.)

Therefore, to distort and pervert marriage as constituted by sex is to distort and pervert the image of God in this world! The world has "less" of God's ihas less of marriage!

2. Marriage itself as constituted by sex is intended to imitate God

a. Re-creation as only in sex between a man and a woman can humans satisfie the ultimate in the cultural commission to "be fruitful and muliply

b. Our communion as only in sex between a man and a woman can we physically become one as the father and Son and Holy spirit are one-- Sex is an act of extending covenant loyalty and unity in faithful companionship

Therefore to distort and pervet marriage as constituted by sex is to distort and pervert the Creation commision for people to "re-create" and re-companionship after the image of God.

Sex itself is a covenant act of fully giving oneself to another in such a way as to be most intimate and most exclusive in its loyalty to another person-- time permits a full description here -- but you will recall that the very words of Adam with respect to his wife Eve are 'bone of by bones, flesh of my flesh"-- an ancient neareastern oath formula used to say-- so important is this covenant that God can destroy me if I pervert IT.

This is the point of this proverb-- that WE are destroyed, as individual people, and as a socieity, when we destroy and pervert marriage as constituted by the act of sex!
