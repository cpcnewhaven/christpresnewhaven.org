Proverbs 1:7
The First Proverb of all other Proverbs

Introduction:
One could argue that there exists a modern phobia against "fear"--

FDR said: "Let me assert my firm belief that the only thing we have to fear is fear itself." (1st inaugural address, 4 Mar., 1933).

Today, we will discover yet the greatest fear of all-- to lack a Godly fear of God!

Exposition:
I. The First Proverb of all other Proverbs

vs. 1: Title and primary author...
2-6: Purpose and nature of Proverbs
7: The First Proverb of all other Proverbs... functions as the most principle theme behind and within all the rest of the themes in this book and all Israel religion.. as the first wisdom saying of all other wisdom sayings

Here-- this first hemstitch used to express the highest principle of the Israelite

Charles Bridges-- "there is not such a wise introduction to be found in all their books (speaking of heathen ethics) as the very first of all in Solomon's which he lays as the ground of all wisdom."

So Job pronounced before:

Job 28:28 And he said to humankind, 'Truly, the fear of the Lord, that is wisdom; and to depart from evil is understanding.'"

So had the wide man's father when David taught his son

Pss. 111:10 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom; all those who practice it have a good understanding. His praise endures forever.

So weighty is that Solomon will conclude in Eccles:

Eccl. 12:13 The end of the matter; after all has been heard-- Fear God, and keep his commandments; for that is the whole duty of everyone.


Lest you think this is a distinctively OC or Jewish principle, or something that NT saints are free from: The lack of fear for God is what caused Paul to conclude that some in Israel had ceased to be true Israel:

Rom.3:18 There is no fear of God before their eyes.
What the writer of Hebrews notes is the summary of all Christian devotion
Heb. 12:28 Wherefore we receiving a kingdom which cannot be moved, let us have grace, whereby we may serve God acceptably with reverence and godly fear: 29 For our God is a consuming fire.
We are commanded to fear God by Paul:
2Cor. 7:1 Therefore, having these promises, beloved, let us cleanse ourselves from all filthiness of the flesh and spirit, perfecting holiness in the fear of God.
Phil. 2:12 Therefore, my beloved, as you have always obeyed, not as in my presence only, but now much more in my absence, work out your own salvation with fear and trembling;
Jude 1:22 And on some have compassion, making a distinction; 23 but others save with fear, pulling them out of the fire, hating even the garment defiled by the flesh.
By Peter:
1Pet. 1:17 And if you call on the Father, who without partiality judges according to each one's work, conduct yourselves throughout the time of your stay here in fear
1Pet. 2:17 Honor all people. Love the brotherhood. Fear God. Honor the king.

It is in this sense that Solomon teaches that the "fear of the Lord" is the "beginning" of knowledge

1. Knowledge paralleled with "wisdom" & discipline-- technical knowledge, ability
I.e. knowledge which shows itself in the skill of living or synonymous with hDmVkDj
So also in Prov. 9:10: The beginning of wisdom is the fear of the Lord and knowledge goes before honor
2. Beginning--first in importance and well as first in sequence... Hebrew root for "head"
Indeed, the "fear of the Lord" function as The First Proverb of all other Proverbs concerning wisdom...

So what is "the fear of the Lord"?

 

II. Antithetical Parallelism so as to be set in contrast to the "fool"

Part 1: The fear of Yahweh (is) the beginning of knowledge,
Part 2: wisdom and instruction/correction fools despise

Observations:
1. "fear of Yahweh" is in contrast to a "fool"

Here: fool = the hard-headed, thick-headed, stupid person...
The person on his/her way to "ruin"
Prov. 10:8 The wise of heart will heed commandments, but a babbling fool will come to ruin.
The person who dies for lack of sense:
Prov. 10:21 The lips of the righteous feed many, but fools die for lack of sense.
The person who troubles his/her household
Prov. 11:29 Those who trouble their households will inherit wind, and the fool will be servant to the wise.

2. "fear of the Lord" apposite of the fools who

despise instruction/discipline-- humility to learn and remain under God's instruction, even as a child under who is under the discipline and training of a parent
Prov. 15:5 A fool despises a parent's instruction, but the one who heeds admonition is prudent.

Lit. Heb-- fear = "to be afraid" so as to be conformed by it
Must not over spiritualize it! "Fear" in turn leads to:
-- reverence
-- obedience
-- worship

III. The Fear of the Lord in Proverbs

1. It is humility-- Opposite of pride and arrogance

Prov. 8:13 The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil. Pride and arrogance and the way of evil and perverted speech I hate.
Prov. 15:33 The fear of the LORD is instruction in wisdom, and humility goes before honor.
Prov. 22:4 The reward for humility and fear of the LORD is riches and honor and life.

2.It is a Hatred for evil

Prov. 8:13 The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil.

3. It Evidences a true Knowledge of God

Prov. 9:10 The fear of the LORD is the beginning of wisdom, and the knowledge of the Holy One is insight.

4. It is not a crippling phobia, but a source of deep confidence as something rewarded by God.

Prov. 14:26 In the fear of the Lord there is strong confidence, And His children will have a place of refuge. 27 The fear of the Lord is a fountain of life, To turn one away from the snares of death.
Prov. 22:4 The reward for humility and fear of the LORD is riches and honor and life.
Job 4:6 Is not your fear of God your confidence, and the integrity of your ways your hope?

5. Fear of the Lord is more desired than all worldly advantages:

Prov. 15:16 Better is a little with the fear of the LORD than great treasure and trouble with it.

6. To "fear God" is to have life itself

Prov. 19:23 The fear of the LORD is life indeed; filled with it one rests secure and suffers no harm.

IV. Definition:

Short Def. -- The reverential subordination of oneself to God--
Long Def: the all directing One God, the Creator and Governor of the world who gave his law unto his people"
Charles Bridges-- "that affectionate reverence by which the child of God rends himself humbly and carefully to his Father's law. His wrath is so bitter, and his love so sweet; that hence springs an earnest desire to please him-- because of the danger of coming short from his won weakness and temptations-- a holy watchfulness and "fear, "that he might not sin against Him." I.e. The opposite of the "fear of the Lord" is to be 'wise in ones one sight" -- To reject the instruction and discipline of the Lord...

David Hubbard-- "reverent obedience"-- although it includes worship... it radiates out from our adoration and devotion to our everyday conduct that sees each moment as the Lord's time, each relationship as the Lord's opportunity, each duty as the Lord's command, and each blessing as the Lord's gift..."

Mainpoint: Reverent subordination and obedience to God is the first Wisdom of all other wisdom.


Why is the "fear of the Lord" The "first" wisdom" and not merely a crippling phobia?

1. It is an intelligent recognition that the greatest danger in the universe for sinners is God unreconciled.

Luke 12:4 "And I say to you, My friends, do not be afraid of those who kill the body, and after that have no more that they can do. 5 But I will show you whom you should fear: Fear Him who, after He has killed, has power to cast into hell; yes, I say to you, fear Him"


I.e. In a world where our only phobia is against phobia itself-- we are taught here that Fear is not a bad thing. Fear is the appropriate response to danger. Although there are many dangers in our fallen world, the gravest of them all is God unreconciled.
To fear humans, who are inconsistent, fickle, and unpredictable, is crippling. To fear God who is unchanging, and therefore predictable, can be liberating.

Prov. 28:1 The wicked flee when no one pursues, But the righteous are bold as a lion.

At John Knox's burial, James Douglas, Earl of Morton said: "Here lies he who neither feared nor flattered any flesh."

2. It is an intelligent recognition that all knowledge is not equal, but that some knowledge is more important than other knowledge-- the knowledge of God so as to know him in "fear" for He is the most important of all knowledge...

I.e. A person is not "knowledgeable" just because he knows something; he is knowledgeable if he knows important things.
(Illustrate: Jerry Lucas memorized the Manhattan telephone directory).
Today-- people spend sometimes a whole lifetime learning obscure principles which actually correspond to a very minute portion of the universe


Nothing is more important to know than that there is a Day of Judgment yet to come, and that God has provided a way to escape that Judgment through Jesus Christ.

3. Fear leads us to Christ:

Some people just can't be pleased. God, however, can be pleased. He is pleased with the perfect sacrifice of Christ; and he is pleased with those who turn from their sins and trust in Christ. God can be reconciled.

Pss. 27:1 The Lord is my light and my salvation; Whom shall I fear? The Lord is the strength of my life; Of whom shall I be afraid?



Do you fear God?
1. You more fear disobedience more than anything else:
--more than immediate gratification
- more than being popular
-- more than getting your own way
-- more than getting ahead in your business, school or home

Eccl. 12:13 Fear God and keep His commandments, For this is man's all.


Do you fear God?
2. You consider God's word as more important than the word or counsel of anyone else:
--more than your husband or wife
-- more than your parents
-- more than your pastor
-- more than your school teacher
-- more than your friends
-- more than your "counselor"
Sure, God can use these-- but what is always most important to you is God's word-- as it alone is the wisdom from God

Do you fear God?
3. You eagerly flee to Christ for he alone can please god with His wisdom so as to enable you to be found pleasing to God by faith in Him!

As we now come to the Lord's Table-- to the wisdom of God itself-- let this be our prayer, that we might fear the Lord

Let us then sing #9, All you that fear Jehovah's name
