Proverbs 16:18-19
Humility

Introduction:

If the "fear of the Lord" is the first proverb of all "other" proverbs concerning wisdom we learn today what is the first of the other proverbs--
That is to say that if God could do anything so as to make you wise, so as to help you "fear the Lord", what would it be?

Exposition:

Prov. 16:18 -- Before destruction(is) pride and before a fall (is) a haughty (high) spirit
16:19--Better to be poor in spirit among the poor(wno) than to divide the (worldly) spoil with those who are proud (vs. 18)


First a brief literary analysis: A two-part proverb--

I. Part One, vs. 18-- synonymous parallelism so as to consist of two poetic clauses-- both saying the same thing

A. First clause: Lit. In the face of destruction is pride-- state principally (whereas the second clause, meaning the same thing-- is stated pictorially)

Destruction:--used to describe the breaking or crushing of bones
Prov. 17:19 One who loves transgression loves strife; one who builds a high threshold invites broken bones.
-- can refer to the emotional or psychological state of depression or "breakdown"
Is. 65:14 my servants shall sing for gladness of heart, but you shall cry out for pain of heart, and shall wail for anguish of spirit.
Prov. 15:4 A gentle tongue is a tree of life, but perverseness in it breaks the spirit.
-- in general it is a word that describe something all together destroyed or broken
Is. 1:28 But rebels and sinners shall be destroyed together, and those who forsake the LORD shall be consumed.

Now the point of this proverb is to say that destroyed lives are the product of

Pride: --
Why are lives destroyed by pride? Ultimately because God hates pride according to Prov. 8:13,
-- and what God hates is under his cursed-- both through the common ways of life put ultimately through the special and eternal judgement in the last days...
Therefore, stated principally, pride leads to destruction...

B. As mentioned already, the second clause is synonymous to the first, but said by painting a mental picture--

before a fall (is) a high spirit
The intent is to create a picture in your mind of someone falling-- and in order to fall, one must first be in a high position to fall-- this is the person who is high minded so as to be a prime candidate to take a fall!

Kids-- perhaps here we think of Humpty Dumpty, I'm not sure of the original intent-- but the graphic picture presented by that rhyme is very much the picture of this proverb reminding us that a proud person is in a dangerous position-- very much like Humpty Dumpty who sat on a wall, and had a great fall resulting in being broken to pieces such that all the kings men could not put humbly together again...

II. vs. 19, Part two: Further states vs. 18 by what is known as a proverbial "Caparison saying"

I.e. Where one condition is compared with a second condition so as to accentuate one condition as better...

16:19--Better to be poor in spirit among the poor(wno) than to divide the (worldly) spoil with those who are proud (adj from of pride, vs. 18)
Note: the two different Heb. words, yet both can denote either
1. material poverty--
2. spiritual poverty-- which is to say "humility"

Most likely, the this proverb originated from an oral tradition where for the sake of memory, the orator made a play on words which stated that-- it is better to be materially "poor"(poor in circumstances) along with spiritually poverty (rich in virtue), than to share all the worldly riches (spoil-- the payoff of a victory in war) with the proud.

Why, because of what vs. 18 just said-- that the proud are bound to destruction!

There is a "poverty" that is not a virtue but a circumstance of misfortune
There is a second "poverty" that is in fact a virtue that is good in the sight of God

so... as poverty of soul is a virtue, it is said in
wno-- humility
Prov. 3:34 Toward the scorners God is scornful, but to the humble he shows favor.
lpv-- low, poor
Prov. 29:23 A person's pride will bring humiliation, but one who is poor in spirit will obtain honor.

Now, having briefly made some observations regarding the literary structure of Prov. 16:vs. 18-19,

III. What exactly is pride as in contrast with humility?

As a clue, not again the play on words--
Especially, the word "poor" of which words for "humility" are closely related-- to be "poor" is to be in a condition of having unmet needs, of powerlessness perhaps-- a condition of dependency-- an estate of weakness...

 

I.e. the opposite of "poor" is "rich"-- a condition that is self-sufficient, independent of needing help, an estate of strength, of having no real want!

 

As these are used to describe our deposition in relation to God, "poor in spirit" or "humility" is when we recognize and act in such a manner as demonstrates our utter dependency upon God

It is in this sense that Christ said "Blessed are the poor in spirit, for theirs is the kingdom of heaven"

Pride, as noted in today's proverb, is set in opposition to humility so in effect to be "rich in spirit" = it is when we think or act out of our supposed strengths so as to think ourselves to be self-sufficient, where we in fact think and act as if having no needs that makes us dependent upon God

Notice then, in the proverbs, how

1. Humility is closely associated with the "Fear of the Lord" so as to be "honored" by God.

Prov. 15:33 The fear of the LORD is instruction in wisdom, and humility (hwno) goes before honor.
(fear of the Lord-- reverent obedience to God since we recognize that God alone is powerful to either curse us or bless us.

2. Likewise, pride is the opposite of the "fear of the Lord" and is said to be "hated" by God

Prov. 8:13 The fear of the LORD is hatred of evil. Pride and arrogance ... I hate.

3. Furthermore, Pride is not honored by God and so the object of God's curse

Prov. 18:12 Before destruction one's heart is haughty, but humility goes before honor.

4. We know for instance that it was pride, or the arrogance of self-sufficiency before God, that brought down the tower of babel:

Gen 11:4 Then they said, "Come, let us build ourselves a city, and a tower with its top in the heavens, and let us make a name for ourselves; otherwise we shall be scattered abroad upon the face of the whole earth." 5 The LORD came down to see the city and the tower, which mortals had built. 6 And the LORD said, "Look, they are one people, and they have all one language; and this is only the beginning of what they will do; nothing that they propose to do will now be impossible for them. 7 Come, let us go down, and confuse their language there, so that they will not understand one another's speech." 8 So the LORD scattered them abroad from there over the face of all the earth, and they left off building the city. 9 Therefore it was called Babel, because there the LORD confused the language of all the earth; and from there the LORD scattered them abroad over the face of all the earth.
Notice especially that it was the strengths of civilization that led to their pride and consequent curse and destruction by God!

5. And again, we know that it was Nebuchadnezzar's political strength and power that led to his being proud so as to be brought down by God -- Neb would eventually concede his dependence upon God...

Dan. 4:37 Now I, Nebuchadnezzar, praise and extol and honor the King of heaven, for all his works are truth, and his ways are justice; and he is able to bring low those who walk in pride.

6. So dangerous is pride, that you will discover the most awful strength of eloquence concentrated upon the ruin of pride by OT prophets

Job 40:11 Pour out the overflowings of your anger, and look on all who are proud, and abase them. 12 Look on all who are proud, and bring them low; tread down the wicked where they stand.

Zeph. 2:3 Seek the LORD, all you humble of the land, who do his commands; seek righteousness, seek humility; perhaps you may be hidden on the day of the LORD's wrath.

Rom. 11:20 ...Do not become proud, but stand in awe.
1Cor. 10:12 So if you think you are standing, watch out that you do not fall.

Which brings us back to our proverb, and its basic meaning:

If the "fear of the Lord" is the first proverb of all "other" proverbs concerning wisdom we learn today what is the first of the other proverbs-- I.e. When we recognize and act in such a manner as demonstrates our utter dependency upon God, we are blessed of God, when we think and live as if we are self-sufficient and independent of God, we are cursed of God...


Notice that stated the meaning in terms of God's blessing and "curse"?
For here again, we must recognize that this proverb is spoken within the context of an Old Covenant worldview-- so that "destruction" and "calamity" were not merely the result of fickle fate, or a blind succession of events run amuck, but rather "destruction" was ultimately a manifestation of God's curse by his almighty power against that which he hates, just as prosperity under the OC terms, was the sign of covenant blessing to those whom God loves--

As the OC is interpreted by the NC, we discover that the material realities of the OC are representative of the greater and eternal realities spoken of by the NC.

I.e. since pride, is at the root of all sin against God, as originally in Adam ( Gen.3), pride is properly the object of God's righteous and covenant curse such that the wise man tells us to expect destruction after pride!

Or, stated positively, this proverb reminds us that all the eternal and covenant blessings of God's are contingent upon our being humble--
Yet immediately, we are faced with the dreadful reality that we have all been predisposed by our first parent, Adam, to such pride and arrogance as would think and act as if we are self-sufficient rather than God dependent!

And you ask, How might we see this pride in ourselves?

Here, I'm reminded of the seven deadly sins, as pride relates to all the others
For example:
3. Pride is in our envy-- persuading us that we deserve more than we have...
I.e. It is pride that makes us want to have more than our neighbors, then, once we get it, making sure it is known, without looking like we are making it known!
4. Pride is in our anger, in which we adopt a position of superiority from which to inflict our scorn and against those who would dare to bother us or disagree with us.
5. Pride is in our avarice-- prompting us to display ourselves so as to draw attention to that which we hold most worthy of attention-- of ourselves.
6. Pride is in our sloth-- a false assurance that we may get by with a minimum effort and find achievement and reward by sluggishness.
7. Pride is in our lust-- in our scant regard for others as people and our being able to dehumanize them and ourselves and still be regarded as human...

Consider also how pride is manifest in our relationships...

Pride makes us take pleasure in ourselves and our own particular identities, but not in what we share in common with all other humanity-- this is the lesson of racism, tribalism, bigotry, and even gossip-- attitudes within us all.
pride is what makes us to withdraw from those whom we feel to be "less" than us in some way--
So, for example,
The Intellect who retreats from the supposed mediocrity's of mass culture
The commoner who retreats from the supposed eggheads of high culture
The Liberal who retreats from those who dream of a bygone past
And the Conservative who retreats from those who dream about progressive future

Pride is detected in our slogans:

Beer commercials that tell us "You can have it all"
Shoe commercials, "Just do it"
Moreover,

Dorothy Sayers reminds us that "the devilish strategy of pride is that it attacks us, not in our weakest points, but in our strongest. It is preeminently the sin of the noble mind."

John Calvin also:
We are naturally prone to attribute everything to our human flesh, we easily form an exaggerated notion of our strength, and we take for granted that, whatever hardships may happen, we will remain invincible. And so we become puffed up with a foolish, vain, and carnal confidence which arouses us to become haughty and proud towards God, as if our own power would be sufficient without his grace

It is our subtle confidence in gifts, attainments, and privileges that is most to our destruction which ought to make us pause

I.e. our pride is revealed most not in our failures, but in our strengths-- be they moral, intellectual, social, or even spiritual-- for nowhere are we more tempted to pride than in what we are most capable of, or most blessed with... for in our strength lies the greatest temptation to deny our human limitations so as to become self-sufficient and think ourselves without need of God!

And the wise man tells us to expect before destruction is pride!

 

Or, stated positively, all the eternal and covenant blessings of God's are contingent upon our being humble--

And struck by the dreadful reality of our pride at every turn of our life... the wise man has led us to our greatest need of all, the need of someone who would himself be humble for us, so as to satisfy this aspect of God's covenant and to secure for us the covenant blessings even as He deflects the covenant curse!


And so, we read about Christ that
Philip2:6 although he was in the form of God, did not regard equality with God as something to be exploited, 7 but emptied himself, taking the form of a slave, being born in human likeness. And being found in human form, 8 he humbled himself and became obedient to the point of death-- even death on a cross. 9 Therefore God also highly exalted him and gave him the name that is above every name, 10 so that at the name of Jesus every knee should bend, in heaven and on earth and under the earth, 11 and every tongue should confess that Jesus Christ is Lord, to the glory of God the Father.

How is this proverb significant to you
Significance:

1. On the one hand, it would be foolish for a proverb on humility to be interpreted in any such way as to suggest that we ought to depend on our own humility as sufficient toward pleasing God's covenant stipulations unto eternal blessing. I.e.-- It would be the ultimate irony that we might interpret this proverb so as to become self-sufficient in humility so as to gain God's favor


This is why the good news of the gospel is that we recognize we are not humble by nature, and in desperate need of someone who is, and who could act humbly for our sakes-- Indeed, we discover yet again that CHRIST IS THE WISDOM OF GOD WHEREBY CHRIST BECAME HUMILITY FOR US, THAT WE MIGHT ENJOY THE RICH BLESSINGS OF GOD'S FAVOR!
If you are not a believer this morning-- please do not hear in this proverb the ultimate moralism, here in it that which would lead you to Christ for the forgiveness of sins...

2. As those who do enjoy a privileged status before God based on Christ's wisdom unto humility for our sakes, we now desire to follow after Christ so as to imitate his wisdom as revealed in his humility... this is the point of Philip-- to have this mind in yourself that was also in Christ...


And toward this end, not the end of earning our salvation by false humility, we ought to seek after humility, not self-sufficient pride.

And having already mentioned many such things that we may want to consider towards this end, let me now end with the one solution to pride that we would be most prone to resist!--
For as John Calvin noted:
The vanity of pride, God cannot better repress than by proving to us from experience not only our folly, but also our extreme frailty. Therefore, he afflicts us with humiliation, or poverty, or loss of relatives, or disease, or other calamities. Then, because we are unable to bare them, we soon are buried under them. And so being humbled, we learn to call upon his strength which alone makes us stand up under such a load of afflictions.

My good brothers and sisters-- if God has chosen to show your weakness through calamity-- be so inclined to call upon him.
Even the best of Christian saints, while saying that they are dependent on God's grace, are all the same more confident in their own bravery or skills than they ought to be-- so let not one of us murmur that God would bring us to a greater understanding of our dependency of him--
For this may well be the way that God will keep you from falling, knowing that before destruction is pride!

All to Thee I Surrender #562



Our Heavenly Father, we come not with faith in our own humility, but in the humiliation of Christ for our sakes-- we come asking that you would give us more faith to believe in Christ, that you would bless these elements so as to strengthen our faith... and confessing our full confidence in Christ for "In him are hid all the treasures of wisdom and knowledge unto salvation." We pray thee, to show forth in these elements your wisdom in humility. Convict us of the shame in human pride, and help us to follow Christ who humble himself even to death. Grant us therefore, gracious Lord, so to spiritually partake of the flesh and blood of your dear Son Jesus Christ, that our sinful bodies may be made clean by his body, and our souls washed through his most precious blood, and that we may always dwell in him, and he in us. Amen
