Introduction:

David Hall in his recent commentary tells the story of a couple of theology students who meet up with a janitor at the gym..

They notice he is reading his bible and ask him, so what book are your reading?

Revelation, says the janitor…

Two theological students, thinking of all the ways they could enlighten their naive brother about the contents of Rev. ask him… what do you think Rev. is saying?

Do you know what it means…

" Jesus wins"

After thinking, the students knew they couldn’t improve his answer

That is the main message, and it shines through loudly and clearly in this book. We may not follow all the charts or identify every political figure with 666, but we can glean the main messages from this. Hall

Isn't it sad that this book, meant to edify, is either avoided from fear of misunderstanding it or reduced to irrelevancy since it is purported to be only about the future… or past… by some…

No doubt-- Many believe that this final NT book is to function almost like an early warning system for the end-times. They look to it to say something about how it will all end as if then it were irrelevant to our present day struggles and situations!

Yet we have seen that the book is as relevant to us today as it was to the 1st century Christian. John has interpreted Daniel's "last days" as present in his day-- as defined by that period between Christ's first coming and his second! What this means is that the millennia reign of the Messiah that Daniel foresaw has already begun although now it exists as a remnant kingdom in the midst of tribulation. In other words, the "millennium" kingdom that we participate in with John and the 1st century church is a kingdom in the midst of great trial and persecution until Christ's second return. John sees the kingdom as a small remnant kingdom, as did Paul, and as a kingdom that walks a straight and narrow path. It is a kingdom whose great and ultimate victory is by means of a great and ultimate irony. The irony is that in his and our humiliation, he and we will be exalted. Jesus wins, and in him, so do we!

The challenge then for this remnant kingdom is to persevere in the faith without compromise to their confession…

 

-- John's first order of business is to direct us to the Trinity, that our confidence might be made sure upon the knowledge of God and his work for our salvation!

And so, the typical letter salutation beginning in vs. 4 identifies the readership which was only implied in vs. 3 and gives further description of the God of these readers who is responsible for their benediction of grace and peace as from the beginning to the end!

 

Exposition:

A. The Church of God (vs. 4a)

John to the seven ecclesiais (assemblies/churches) that are in Asia

Seven churches--

7 historical churches in Asia but the number "7" could hardly have arisen by chance!

7-- favorite # of John in Rev. as derived from OT-- used to denote "fullness", completeness or universality… (some also argue that it directs us to a divine source)

Either way--

Sometimes, #7 can be used both figuratively and literally-- as in Lev. 4-16 where "sprinkling seven times" is both a literal action and a figurative representation of a complete, effective act…

Other times in OT it is used only in the figurative-- e.g. Lev. 26:18-28. God will punish Israel "seven times" … not 7 distinct punishments but a complete chastisement…

Therefore, against this background-- and the use of 7 elsewhere in Rev. the number here must also be figurative as well as literal…

i.e. the 7 historical churches are representative of all the churches in Asia Minor and by extension the church universal… (this is a book written by extension to CPC) --

This makes sense…

    • After chapters 2-3, it is the universal church that is center stage with no further mention of congregations. Also, the letters addressed to particular churches in chapters 2-3 are also said at the conclusion of each letter to be addressed to all the churches--
    • This interpretation of universal identification is found as early as the 2nd century in the Moratoria Canon AD 180 "John, too, in the Apocalypse, though he writes to seven churches yet speaks to all… "

It is the New Covenant language for "shalom" as to mean all the great privileges of being adopted into God's benevolence and the eternal home of his promise-- once foreshadowed by a promised land but only as this directed humanity to a greater land in eternity!

And doesn't the church under trial need to be reminded of this!

WE are blessed! We are the recipients of God's grace and peace!

Even if for a time we will be asked to believe this not by sight but by faith!

"from"-- (3 from clauses… spilling over into doxology as esp. directed to Jesus Christ… )

Notice then the full-blown Trinitarian formula that is now spelled out at the end of our Special Rev. canon: Father, Holy Spirit, and Son.)

1. from The Father (4c)

the one who is (indicative mood) and who was (imperfect mood) and who is to come (present mid. part)

Confidence in God's sovereign guidance of all earthly affairs instills courage to stand strong… this is the point of the OT expression which lie behind "the one who is and who was and who is coming…

OT context: Exodus 3:14,

-- The threefold clause is a reflection of Exodus 3:14 together with Isaiah 41:4, 43:10, 44:6, 48:12. Which are themselves reflections of Ex. 3:14.

This expression is also expanded in later Jewish tradition such as "I am he who is and who will be" in Targ. Ps.-J Ex. 3:14, or "I am he who is and who was and I am he who will be" (Targ. Ps-J. Dt. 32:39).

Therefore, John is familiar with traditions (see Beale) that expand the clause in Exodus 3:14 to "describe God not merely as present at the beginning, middle and end of history, but as the incomparable sovereign Lord over history" and "is thus able to bring prophecy to fulfillment and to deliver people despite overwhelming odds, whether from Egypt, Babylon or the nations".

("erxomenos"-- is to be understood eschatologically as referring to God's sovereign consummation of history in the future. )

and from the seven spirits

(It is also possible that this is assumed by Is. 11:2ff as well. Note also the 'stairway to heaven' imagery of the "spirits" which represent the Holy Spirit as related to Jacob's vision)

1. Christ persevered as a faithful witness:

.

Christ's resurrection and the coming of His universal kingdom are common themes in the NT, but here they are prefaced with the unique mention of Christ as the faithful witness. This wording suited perfectly the particular situation of these Asia Minor churches and, in particular, the church of the latter days. The following chapters reveal that the church of the latter days will be tempted to compromise its witness because of threatening persecution. Thus, in order to overcome this temptation, the church should model its life as a "faithful witness" on that of Christ.

Our encouragement -- to persevere with Christ is to be raised with Christ

Note: the word Marturia (witness) and the related word "maryr"-- to witness is to "martyr" oneself as a follower of Christ…

"First born" denotes the privileged position that Christ enjoys by means of His resurrection. Because he was the first to be resurrected from the dead, He will inaugurate the new creation. See Rev. 3:14 and Col. 1:18.

OT background for all of this is clear. Psalms 89:27, 37 speaks of Christ as a "faithful witness", the "firstborn", and "ruler of the kings of the earth". The immediate context of the passage is that of David as the anointed king who will reign over all his enemies and whose seed will be established on his throne forever. The NT clearly attributes this "seed" to Jesus Christ.

D. Doxology (5b-8)

Stated 2 times…

1. AS related to the work of Christ

To (by-- date?) the one who loved us and freed us from our sins with/in his blood and made us to be a kingdom, priest to God and his father,

to him be the glory and the power forever and ever, Amen

1. from the phrase "to the one who loves us" and continuing through vs. 6

the phrase "through his blood" (in reference to His sacrificial death and the OT priestly role of sprinkling the blood of sacrificed animals) deals with the atonement that comes from Christ's death as a typological fulfillment of Israel's redemption from Egypt by the blood of the Passover lamb. This interpretation comes from verse 6's allusion to Exodus 19:6, which portrays Christ as both priest and sacrifice. Thus Christ's life and death fulfilled the priestly ministry of the OT. (note Lev. 16:14-19, Ex. 24:8)

the phrase "having loosed us from our sins by means of his blood" may be derived from Is. 40:2 and the related word, "lousanti", which means 'washed'.

Christ's death and resurrection established a twofold office that must be taken up by all believers. In other words, they are to identify with His resurrection and kingdom; they should consider themselves to be resurrected and exercising rule with him. They are to do this 'now and not yet', that is, to rule and be a priest now while awaiting the climax of His kingdom on earth.

The idea of a royal and priestly nation in Exodus 19:6 is reiterated in 1Peter 2:5, which takes what was Israel's purpose (which the OT prophets repeatedly blamed Israel for never fulfilling (see Is. 40-55)) and places it upon the church. This purpose was to mediate Yahweh's light of salvific revelation by witnessing to the Gentiles (see Is.43:10-13).

1Peter also conveys the tacit notion that the church today functions as true Israel, and given the context of Revelations, unbelieving Israelites (ethnically) who claim to be true "jews" belong instead to the synagogue of Satan (2:9) and are "liars" (3:9) …

This then is the high point of vs. 1-6-- the achievement of God's glory through Christ's work and the service of his people as kings and priest.

2. AS related to the person of Christ

Rev. 1:7 Look! He is coming with the clouds; every eye will see him, even those who pierced him; and on his account all the tribes of the earth will wail. So it is to be. Amen.

Rev. 1:8 "I am the Alpha and the Omega," says the Lord God, who is and who was and who is to come, the Almighty.

Vs. 7 is composed of 2 OT citations.

1--Dan.7:13 speaks of the enthronement of the Son of man over all the nations after God's judgment of evil empires. Thus in the NT context, Jesus wins!

2--Zech.12:10ff speaks of the end-time period when God will defeat the enemy nations around Israel, and the Israelites will be redeemed.

Therefore, Christ's 'coming' is understood as occurring both already and not yet; so that our victory is already and not yet.

Thus the phrase "alpha and omega" expresses God's control of history.

Practical Inferences:

1. Against the "anti-Christian" pressures in our world, and in the face of circumstances that seem all but against the survival of Christianity, how can we be sure that Jesus is winning, that ours is the "right" side as it were ?

John's answer is to look not at your circumstances but at God. This answer is exactly why we need a reformation in our worship, both privately and corporately.

One of the most fascinating aspects of the book of Revelations is that most of this book is framed within the context of heavenly worship, or a kind of "liturgy for the end times".

In our passage today, John introduces his liturgical epistle to the universal church with nothing less than a doxology! Why? Because it is doxology/worship that will save us from compromise and encourage us to persevere amidst persecution. Nothing else can!

 

The point is very clear from the framework itself and the letter's salutation; when our faith is weakened and we are tempted to compromise, we are to (in the words of Paul) "set our minds not on the things of this world, but on things above where Christ is seated at the heavenly places (right hand of God)"!

You see, the secret to faithfulness is to keep focused on God, less the circumstances of our lives… what we need is more worship in our lives, not less-- right as everything around us seems to make worship less plausible and our crowded schedules make worship less available we need desperately to worship… and by worship, I mean to encounter God in all His great glory!

And yet, right when we need most to see God today, worship is being crowded out of our lives by the busy schedules that are driven by our capitalism and populism. And when we do finally stop to "worship" (whether in private, corporate, or families), our 'achievement' mentality seeks instead to make aesthetics of worship better, or to learn new and interesting facts, or to make worship more efficient, or to be amused, or to moralize our kids. All of which are things that pale in comparison with a vision for God!
