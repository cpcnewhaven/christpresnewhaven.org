II. The Book and the Oath:

The Book (lit. "little Scroll')

The scroll, at the very least, refers to the contents of 11-16-- what will be the bitter sweet realities of Christ's execution of redemptive history in both judgment and salvation as to the unbelieving and believing people of the world…

It is most likely identified as well with the scroll in Chapter 5 (as was the image of Christ there identified with the "mighty angel" here…) The scroll in chapter 5, which the lamb was given to open, is the contents of redemptive history in the last days leading up to the great and terrible day of the Lord-- a day that will be most bitter for the enemies of God and most sweet to the those who belong to God"

The "little" of the scroll probably is related to the fact that this "scroll/book" is but a portion of the scroll/book of Chapter 5, and yet directs us to what the christians' purpose on a small scale within the greater scale prophecies of the "book" of ch. 5.

Christ sovereignty over history is depicted in his opening of the scroll, and in his stance upon the sea and land--his sovereignty both included his judgments in the world that come upon both the elect and the non-elect. The point then is to show forth Christ who stands as judge over all the earth, both the unbelieving "earth dwellers" and the protection of his people…

It is also important to note the symbolism of the seven thunders--which may be an allusion to that in Psalms 29 where God's thunder of punishment is equated with the "voice of the Lord", which is mentioned seven times. These judgments in Psalms 29 point to a God who "sits as King forever", a phrase used by John here to symbolize Christ's sovereignty over history as its judge and king!

And yet here the prophet is told not to write about the seven thunders-- Why?

This would seem to emphasis the "secret" or "mystery" or "stumbling block" aspects of Christ-- who for a time doesn't appear to the unbelieving world to be reigning, even though he is!

Here is the great secret of redemptive history that will be revealed in the last day of the Lord; although it may seem that the worldly things are in control, that unbelief is winning, that evil is triumphant, and that Christ is but a humble and defeated servant of God, history will prove that Christ's humiliation, which is being lived out by his followers, is a mean towards the end when Christ is shown to rule over every event in order to complete the coming of his kingdom. In other words, the prayer "thy kingdom come" is being answered, albeit ironically in suffering and humiliation for a time, and the kingdom will in the end be triumphant and powerful over Satan's schemes!

Notice then the "oath" taken by the mighty angel here…

The Oath

5 Then the angel whom I saw standing on the sea and the land raised his right hand to heaven 6 ¶ and swore by him who lives forever and ever, who created heaven and what is in it, the earth and what is in it, and the sea and what is in it: "There will be no more delay, 7 but in the days when the seventh angel is to blow his trumpet, the mystery of God will be fulfilled, as he announced to his servants the prophets."

This is a most powerful picture-- where the great and mighty "angel of the Lord" is shown to be raising his right hand and swearing upon God himself, that the kingdom of God will be triumphant! That the mystery of God will be fulfilled, in that God's people will be vindicated, Christ honored, God glorified, in the end!

How does Christ take this oath-- it is fulfilled in the images that are given back in Chapter 5 again-- as through Christ death and resurrection, he fulfills the mystery of the last days. That through death, the kingdom of God is inaugurated, and through Christ's resurrection, the first installment of victory is won-- awaiting the final and complete resurrection of the saints with Christ into glory!

Again, the power of this image is also pastoral-- as for those who have lost hope-- the picture is to see Christ, making an oath to God, upon his own death and resurrection, that the things hoped for will in fact be accomplished.

This then leads us to the prophetic call itself

The prophets eating of the scroll signifies his personal identification with the prophecy-- i.e. that he would not only proclaim lamentation and woe, but would to some degree experience it on behalf of the people… It represented for both prophets their total submission to God's will as a prerequisite for being an instrument in Gold's hand…

John likewise is to preach warning to the church (NT Israel) against unbelief and compromise with the idolatrous world even as he also warns the world of unbelievers themselves.

Summary:

Here then we learn one of the great truths about the gospel-- how it "cuts both ways", how it is as a "two-edged sword". The history that is executed by Christ is

Sweet-- in that it will ultimately result in salvation, vindication, blessing. It is bitter because

a. the path to salvation is a path of suffering (Christ's example as we are commanded to follow… be of the same mind…)

b. the path of redemptive history will lead to curse and damnation for some…

This interpretive parenthesis-- perfectly vindicates and explains even how we all feel when we here the whole gospel proclaimed.

Many of you have felt this-- both the joy of knowing God's will will be done, his glory revealed, even if through judgment.

And yet the sadness that this brings, to think that it come first as through the fiery trials of persecution for believers following in the footsteps of Christ and his "cross"

And the sadness that this brings to think that some, perhaps even some people we know and love, will suffer the eternal judgment of God…

"The sweetness refers to God's redemptive grace in the gospel to those believing and the bitterness to the fact that this grace must be experienced in the crucible of suffering . " (Beale)

2Cor. 2:15

For we are the aroma of Christ to God among those who are being saved and among those who are perishing; 16 to the one a fragrance from death to death, to the other a fragrance from life to life. Who is sufficient for these things?

I.e. the content of the mystery of salvation in the last days as told in Chapter 11 is that God's decree for saints to suffer, which leads directly to the judgment of their persecutors and the vindication and ultimate glorification of the saints.

Main point: To the believer who identifies with Christ, the coming of the kingdom of God is "bitter-sweet" in that it will bring blessing and glory as through the crucible of suffering and pain.

I.e. What should we think about, the events being depicted here as related to God's prophetic word about the days leading up to and including the great day of the Lord?

Answer-- We should feel both the sweetness of it and the bitterness of it!

As depicted by the visions of the seven seals and then again by the seven trumpets-- this interpretive vision (parenthesis)-- perfectly summarizes many of our own thoughts doesn't it…

We rejoice that the gospel will bring glory to God, will eventually vindicate the faithful, will show forth God's holiness to the nations. We rejoice that the gospel is God's grace to save some from the penalty of our sin. All of this is "SWEETNESS"

And yet, it grieves us, especially from our human perspective-- that some will not be saved, that some will be the recipients of God's awful wrath… It grieves us also that the way of the cross is the way to glory…

We grieve as we contemplate Christ's suffering at our own hands-- even though it leads to his ultimate vindication and glory..

And likewise, it grieves us that as we follow after Christ, we too will be called to follow the path leading to the cross--

How-- by die-ing to sin… by putting of worldly pleasures, by being marginalized in this world, by suffering mistreatment, misunderstanding and even at times bodily harm simply for our remaining faithful… All of this is "BITTERNESS…

 

We rightly pray-- "thy kingdom come"

When Christ taught his disciples to pray-- could they have ever really imagined how it would be answered…?

I.e. that their prayer would be fulfilled by a cross before a resurrection… that the kingdom of God is such as to come through death into life?

Again and again, this is the message of our Bible-- that those who are poor are rich, those who are rich are poor… those who would be most prone to think themselves on top of the world are at the bottom of God's kingdom…

For those who identify with the kingdom of Christ, identify with his suffering before they identify with his glory!

Thus, the bitter sweet of the gospel-- that it cuts both ways--

Both applied to the believer but also as this is tragically applied to the unbeliever..

 

Believer-- suffering now, glory later

Loose the world now and its worldly advantages, gain the world later and its eternal benefits

Unbelievers-- glory now, suffer later…

i.e. have the world and its powers at your disposal now, but loose the world in the world to come…

APPLICATIONS:

1. Unbeliever-- the fact of "bitterness" ought to warn you even as the fact of "sweetness" ought to encourage you..

Make no mistake about it-- some will be punished-- and not as if God is unfair-- it is the fair and just response of a holy God against sinful and willful disobedience… Think about it… to those who might be thinking-- but why bitterness at all… why not just skip that if God is so loving…?

Answer-- because the bitterness is the evidence of God's remaining holiness. The bitterness demonstrates that outside of God's presence, we are outside of the presence of real life!

Even now, if you live and move outside the presence of God, you may have all the world’s pleasures, but it is a bitter existence to live outside of God. You may begin to feel this now in subtle ways as you live not in submission to him. You may feel it in your relationships, your emotions, your lack of peace, your striving in this world, your depression, etc. There IS a bitterness.

THE GOSPEL CUTS BOTH WAYS; it proclaims grace and sweetness to those who believe, and yet it proclaims bitterness to those who do not. Don't let worldly pleasures cover over this…

Herein lies a principle-- your sweetness proceeds your bitterness-- so turn away from the sweetness of this world that you might have the sweetness of the world to come…

Or share in the bitterness of this world that you might not share in the bitterness of the world to come…

This is just the reverse for the Christian however…

2. Believer-- there is to be expected bitterness before the sweetness.. You’re to share in the life of Christ before his resurrection, that is, to share in the tribulation that God has brought upon this world. In fact you are to share in the persecution that the world brings upon Christ. If you follow after Christ, you will suffer with Christ merely for being faithful. Do not be surprised if a fiery ordeal comes upon you says Peter!

¶ Beloved, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that is taking place among you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you.

And yet, your encouragement as you suffer with Christ is that you will be vindicated with Christ in the last day…

And yet, bitterness is sweetness in God's grace
