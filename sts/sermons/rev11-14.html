Revelations 11:14-19

The Sounding of the Final Trumpet

Thy Kingdom Come!

Introduction:

We hear a lot these days about the ‘Volatile Market’-- where stocks prices fall and rise in a very sharp and extreme manner. In such a context, investors can either play the market hoping to predict these sharp inclines and declines, or like me, just keep out.

Yet in comparison, we live in a volatile time in redemptive history as well, but we don't have the option to just keep out. We need to make decisions and choices that will either put us on the winning or losing track.

So how do we know which side is up and which side is down? This is in large measure what Revelations is all about-- helping those who might not see who is winning and who is losing. Today’s passage tells us what we might not readily see, what is happening in this volatile market we call in spiritual terms the "last days".

 

Exposition:

The parenthesis in verses10:1-11:13 deals with when God's provision for the elect during the last days has ended. Verse 14 begins where verse 9:21 left off: the "second woe has passed..the third woe comes quickly… ".

The first two woes were announcements of God’s partial judgments in the last days leading up to his final and third judgment on the last and final Day of the Lord--the day when in a decisive manner the great King of the cosmos comes and defeats His enemies and rescues his people!

Again, the chronology pertains not to the historical sequence but to the sequence of John's vision…

 

I. The Foreshadowing History of Israel to the Promised Land Spiritually Fulfilled in the Coming of the Kingdom of God

Before we look more closely at our passage-- we should first take note of two very important OT concepts that are relevant to John's vision.

1. The general OT story that this vision assumes is familiar to the reader, i.e. the story of Exodus, wilderness wanderings, and entry into the promised land as most represented by the defeat of Jericho:

Earlier, in chapter 8, we established that the trumpets were OT symbols announcing the coming of God's Kingdom and,especially, His judgment. Trumpets in the OT were primarily used to indicate a warning to repent; an announcement of judgment, victory or salvation; an enthronement of Israel's king, Note particularly their use as an announcement that holy battle was about to begin. Notice then in the story of the fall of Jericho, the sounding of the trumpets announced the impending victory in a holy war. That this use is what is at work in Rev. is evident from the parallelism between the seven trumpets blown by seven priests (Joshua 6) or by seven angels.

John also patterns the first 5 trumpets after the plagues of God against Egypt as related to God's judgments that hardened Egypt’s heart and the final and victorious judgment of God against Egypt, one that freed Israel from Egyptian bondage.

The point of remembering all this context is to notice how we pick up this OT history again

Rev. 11:14-19 tells of the end of the evil world kingdoms and the church's reward. Note that the church’s reward is told in a fashion that recalls the Exodus/wilderness/Jericho pattern in the OT, the pattern God used to bring Israel into the promised land.

Applied here, it would look like this: Christ has freed us from Egypt (bondage to sin), and yet the church is for a time called to a wilderness episode (a "now/not yet" acquisition of the kingdom of God) that constitutes the last days prior to the complete acquisition of the kingdom once the promised land is completely acquired (which was typologically and finally accomplished at Jericho). The Jericho episode represents then, the climatic and final "day of the Lord" in the OT pattern whereby God's people where finally and completely given the promised land:

Joshua 6:2 The LORD said to Joshua, "See, I have handed Jericho over to you, along with its king and soldiers. 3 You shall march around the city, all the warriors circling the city once. Thus you shall do for six days, 4 with seven priests bearing seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark. On the seventh day you shall march around the city seven times, the priests blowing the trumpets. 5 When they make a long blast with the ram's horn, as soon as you hear the sound of the trumpet, then all the people shall shout with a great shout; and the wall of the city will fall down flat, and all the people shall charge straight ahead."

(also, perhaps the six days in the creation account are likened unto the wilderness days which are then likened unto the "last days" of the NT which ultimately lead to the final and last day of the Lord).

2. The Ark:

The climax of today’s passage is found in vs. 19:

Rev. 11:19

¶ Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple; and there were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, an earthquake, and heavy hail.

The ark signals not only judgment but also God's gracious presence with his redeemed community and his provision of grace by atonement. According to the OT, on the day of atonement, the sacrificial blood was sprinkled on the mercy seat of the ark as a covering for the nations' sins, and this sprinkling ensured God's gracious presence among his people.

What is especially noteworthy is that the ark of the covenant is given the central place in the story of Jericho:

Josh. 6:6

¶ Then Joshua the son of Nun called the priests and said to them, "Take up the ark of the covenant, and let seven priests bear seven trumpets of rams' horns before the ark of the Lord.'' 7 And he said to the people, "Proceed, and march around the city, and let him who is armed advance before the ark of the Lord.'' Josh. 6:8 ¶ So it was, when Joshua had spoken to the people, that the seven priests bearing the seven trumpets of rams' horns before the Lord advanced and blew the trumpets, and the ark of the covenant of the Lord followed them… 11 So he had the ark of the Lord circle the city, going around it once. Then they came into the camp and lodged in the camp. Josh. 6:12 ¶ And Joshua rose early in the morning, and the priests took up the ark of the Lord.

Although the ark disappeared during the Babylonian captivity, some Jewish traditions (especially those that originated during the inter-testaments period) expected the ark’s literal return at the end of time, when God would graciously restore Israel and dwell in the midst of its people (2 Macc.2:4-8, 2Bar.6, Josephus Ant. 18:4.1). I find this tradition is still continued today by some Christians (dis esp) as was applied then to the restoration of a literal Israel.

However, the OT did not expect a literal reappearance of the ark, but rather the OT looked forward to a reappearance of God's presence in Israel. That divine presence was what the ark had represented.

2Chr. 6:41

"Now rise up, O LORD God, and go to your resting place, you and the ark of your might. Let your priests, O LORD God, be clothed with salvation, and let your faithful rejoice in your goodness.

Pss. 132:8

¶ Rise up, O LORD, and go to your resting place, you and the ark of your might.

Jer. 3:16

And when you have multiplied and increased in the land, in those days, says the LORD, they shall no longer say, "The ark of the covenant of the LORD." It shall not come to mind, or be remembered, or missed; nor shall another one be made.

John's vision here keys upon this OT expectation and shows its fulfillment in the second coming of Christ, who is described in the NT as the Mercy seat of God:

Rom. 3

23 for all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, 24 being justified freely by His grace through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus, 25 whom God set forth as a mercy seat, through faith in his blood, to demonstrate His righteousness, because in His forbearance God had passed over the sins that were previously committed, 26 to demonstrate at the present time His righteousness, that He might be just and the justifier of the one who has faith in Jesus.

 

II. The Kingdom of the World Becomes the Kingdom of God

Rev. 11:14

¶ The second woe has passed/come. The third woe is coming very soon.(quickly)

Quickly-- whenever Christ's return is the topic, it is as related to the coming of the kingdom. With Christ comes the kingdom! Note then, every time "quickly" is used in Revelations, it is concerning the coming of Christ.

Rev. 2:16

Repent then. If not, I will come to you soon and make war against them with the sword of my mouth.

Rev. 3:11

I am coming soon; hold fast to what you have, so that no one may seize your crown.

Rev. 22:7

¶ "See, I am coming soon! Blessed is the one who keeps the words of the prophecy of this book."

Rev. 22:12

¶ "See, I am coming soon; my reward is with me, to repay according to everyone's work.

Rev. 22:20

The one who testifies to these things says, "Surely I am coming soon." Amen. Come, Lord Jesus!

Rev. 11:15

¶ Then the seventh angel sounded the trumpet, and it came to pass that there were loud/great/mega voices in heaven, saying, "The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord (GOD) and of his Christ (annointed one--Messiah), and he will reign forever and ever."

Verse 15 begins with a brief hymn as related to the Messianic reign of God's anointed one. Verse15ff forms the content of the third woe/seventh trumpet, and yet puts it into a hymnal context. The "great voices" may be from angels or more probably from the heavenly multitude of saints (see Rev. 7:9, 19:1, 6).

The Kingdom of God has come because its enemies have all been defeated and judged (see Rev. 11:18). God takes to himself the rule that he had temporarily allowed Satan to invade. The sounding of the seventh trumpet, like the seventh seal, narrates the end of history as we know it, but here it is put into the context of proclamation and praise.

"The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord (GOD) and of his Christ

The words has become translated mean ‘to arrive at being’ or ‘to cease being one thing and be another’ or ‘to change to be.’ Together, they mean to be formed, to come to exist, to happen with the implication that what happens is different from a previous state, to come to be in a place – ‘to come to be, to appear, to be in a place.’

What is especially interesting is the seemingly continuity within the discontinuity when "the kingdom of the world" becomes the "kingdom of our Lord". Is it that the old kingdom is destroyed and a new kingdom established, or the old kingdom that was given over to "the world" is now given over to "our Lord"?

The words"his Christ"-- i.e. the anointed one–denote kingly aspects of the Messiah.

He shall reign forever and ever… -- Christ will reign forever and ever (have eternal reign) (see Isaiah 9).

He will reign--- I am not sure whether the "He" here refers to "our Lord" or "his Christ". Perhaps it refers to both antecedents and thus points to their inseparable unity. More likely, the phrase is speaking about the messianic reign. For instance, the phrase would then be the consummate fulfillment of the long-awaited messianic kingdom prophesied in the OT and the NT:

Luke 1:32

He will be great, and will be called the Son of the Most High, and the Lord God will give to him the throne of his ancestor David. 33 He will reign over the house of Jacob forever, and of his kingdom there will be no end."

Is. 9:6

For a child has been born for us, a son given to us; authority rests upon his shoulders; and he is named Wonderful Counselor, Mighty God, Everlasting Father, Prince of Peace. 7 His authority shall grow continually, and there shall be endless peace for the throne of David and his kingdom. He will establish and uphold it with justice and with righteousness from this time onward and forevermore. The zeal of the LORD of hosts will do this.

In spite of the nations’ rage, God will bring his people into his "habitation and sanctuary" (Exodus 15:17), and "reigns forever and ever". Note also in Daniel 7, the evil kingdoms of the world will be defeated and handed over to the authority of the Son of man, who reigns forever. Rev. 11:7 is alluding to Dan. 7:3, 21. See

Daniel 7:13 As I watched in the night visions, I saw one like a human being coming with the clouds of heaven. And he came to the Ancient One and was presented before him. 14 To him was given dominion and glory and kingship, that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him. His dominion is an everlasting dominion that shall not pass away, and his kingship is one that shall never be destroyed.

Zechariah 14:9

¶ And the LORD will become king over all the earth; on that day the LORD will be one and his name one.

Psalms 22:28

For dominion belongs to the LORD, and he rules over the nations.

III. Kingdom Worship

Rev. 11:16

¶ Then the twenty-four elders who sit on their thrones before God fell on their faces and worshiped God, 17 singing, "We give you thanks, Lord God Almighty, the one who is and the one who was, because you have taken your great power and reigned (aor.act ind)."

In Rev. 17, the fact that the kingdom of God is completed is clear in that all the enemies of God have received their final defeat and judgment (see Rev. 19:6 for the final judgment of Babylon the great).

the one who is and the one who was,

God's name here is a variation of the one that was attributed to him in Rev. 1:4, 8, 4:8–‘who was, who is, who is to come’. This phrase was used in the OT to describe God's sovereignty over history, such as to be an invisible but real, guiding all of history to its intended end.

because you have taken your power

Means that God will break into the world history and end it by overthrowing all opposition to his people and setting up his eternal kingdom.

Twenty-four elders on their throne

This is a reference to the church as represented here by the 24 elders in Rev. 4 and 5.

Rev. 4:9

And whenever the living creatures give glory and honor and thanks to the one who is seated on the throne, who lives forever and ever, 10 the twenty-four elders fall before the one who is seated on the throne and worship the one who lives forever and ever; they cast their crowns before the throne, singing, 11 "You are worthy, our Lord and God, to receive glory and honor and power, for you created all things, and by your will they existed and were created."

Rev. 5:8

When he had taken the scroll, the four living creatures and the twenty-four elders fell before the Lamb, each holding a harp and golden bowls full of incense, which are the prayers of the saints. 9 They sing a new song: "You are worthy to take the scroll and to open its seals, for you were slaughtered and by your blood you ransomed for God saints from every tribe and language and people and nation; 10 you have made them to be a kingdom and priests serving our God, and they will reign on earth."

Note that two groups of people are mentioned in verse 18: saints and prophets. This is a description of the whole church, wherein the prophetic character of the whole church is in view. Even the "prophets" are called "servants" (see Rev. 10:7 where all believers bear the name ‘servants’).

 

Fell on their faces and worshipped God…

Matt. 17:2

And he was transfigured before them, and his face shone like the sun, and his clothes became dazzling white. Matt. 17:6 When the disciples heard this, they fell to the ground and were overcome by fear.

Rev. 7:11

And all the angels stood around the throne and around the elders and the four living creatures, and they fell on their faces before the throne and worshiped God, 12 singing, "Amen! Blessing and glory and wisdom and thanksgiving and honor and power and might be to our God forever and ever! Amen."

Rev. 19:4

And the twenty-four elders and the four living creatures fell down and worshiped God who is seated on the throne, saying,

¶ "Amen. Hallelujah!" 5 And from the throne came a voice saying, "Praise our God, all you his servants, and all who fear him, small and great."

Rev. 11:18 The nations were enraged (passive voice: were made angry),

    • but your wrath has come,
    • and the time for judging the dead,
    • and to give the reward your servants, the prophets and saints and all who fear your name, both small and great,
    • and to destroy those who destroy the earth."

Rev. 11:18 begins and ends with judgment on the ungodly. The middle part emphasizes the reward that God will give to the faithful… that of the saints’ deliverance, a position of reign with Christ and the accompanying blessings and, what is John's emphasis throughout, the saints’ vindication.

The reward to the faithful is sandwiched between statements about judgment, to indicate that part of their reward is the satisfaction of knowing that God has vindicated them by judging their persecutors.

The nations are pictured as being enraged against God and the people (see Psalms 2:2). Thus, God’s punishment "fits" their crime against God and his people--wrath for wrath

Note also, Rev 11:18 mentions those receiving the reward, who are the servants, the saints, those fearing God's name, and thus the verse is a fulfillment of the petition in Rev. 6:9ff. They are also mentioned in Rev. 18:24ff, where God is praised by "all his servants and those fearing him and the great and small… " and in Rev. 11:3, 10, where ‘they’ refers to the whole church.

(Note, Rev. 15-19 is not about God's judgment on Israel in AD 70 as some have said. See Rev 18:7 for the reference to "the time for the dead to be judged.").

IV. The King's Presence

Rev. 11:19

¶ Then God's temple in heaven was opened, and the ark of his covenant was seen within his temple; and there were flashes of lightning, rumblings, peals of thunder, an earthquake, and heavy hail.

The lightning, sounds, thunder, etc. are a repeated indicator of the last act of judgment (see Rev. 4:5, 8:5, 16:1).

In Rev. 11:19, these come from the innermost part of God's heavenly temple… with the sound of the seventh trumpet heralding the opening of the seventh seal and thus the very end of history.

The presence of God, and not a literal re-appearance of the ark at the end of the age, is the point here! This will be expanded in Rev. 21:3, 22.

Rev. 21:3

And I heard a loud voice from the throne saying, "See, the home of God is among mortals. He will dwell with them as their God; they will be his peoples, and God himself will be with them;

Rev. 21:22

¶ I saw no temple in the city, for its temple is the Lord God the Almighty and the Lamb.

Main Point: The sounding of the seventh trumpet here, like the sounding of the seven trumpets at Jericho in the Old Testament, represents the final and concluding act of God in redemptive history. At that time, the kingdoms of this world will be judged and the kingdom of God will be completely and eternally established, and these in turn invoke the church to worship then, as it ought to invoke our perseverance in worship and hope now.

Note, some see this as the reign that was fulfilled at Christ's death and resurrection, that began at Christ's first coming (see Acts 4:25-27, Heb.1:1-5 as then related to Pss 2). But here we have a context of judgment against God's AND the church’s enemies, again describing a ‘now and not yet’ kind of reign, a reign that was partially fulfilled in Christ's defeat over sin and death at the cross and yet will not be fully fulfilled until his second return.

Present Day Implications:

Rev 11:15

"The kingdom of the world has become the kingdom of our Lord (GOD) and of his Christ, and he will reign forever and ever…"

Implicitly here and more explicitly elsewhere, the scripture concedes that for a time, we live in the "kingdom of the world". This means that we live in a world that seems at least to be ruled by virtues and visions other than the virtues and visions of God! It is a "kingdom of the world" , and we feel it everywhere practically, don’t we.

Paul describes it this way:

2Tim. 3:2

For men will be lovers of themselves, lovers of money, boasters, proud, blasphemers, disobedient to parents, unthankful, unholy, 3 unloving, unforgiving, slanderers, without self-control, brutal, despisers of good, 4 traitors, headstrong, haughty, lovers of pleasure rather than lovers of God, 5 having a form of godliness but denying its power. And from such people turn away

Our posture and responsibility in this kind of world order are also made clear to us by Paul: we are to "turn away from it"

And yet this is hard to do; it is hard because the rules of the kingdom of this world determine who gets the benefits of this world. And so, more often than we would like to admit I'm sure, these dispositions, attitudes, and values come into the church and into the lives of the people of the church such as to make the church and its people "lovers of themselves… rather than lovers of God". The people in the church are too often found to be proud, disobedient to partents, unthankful, slanderous against others, headstrong, etc.

This is the "wilderness" we live in the present age--a wilderness that is ruled more often than not by the harsh realities brought upon us by the harsh values of this world.

The good news is this: those who remain faithful, who persevere with faith while in this wilderness, they will inherit the promised land-- a land described here as a kingdom of our Lord. Eventually their pilgrimage will lead to a Jericho episode, a day when the God will turn this world into "HIS WORLD", AND THEN WHOSE SIDE WOULD YOU WANT TO BE ON?

To Adults

the message is not all together different-- but we can fill in some more details…

We hear a lot about falling and rising stock prices and the volatile market these days. To use this as a metaphor for what we have heard today, if God were to say to you, "anytime now, GE stock will crash!", what would you do?

In moral and spiritual terms, we live in volatile times (or the last days) when at one moment we see the kingdom of the world rising and yet in another moment we see it falling. This is exactly what God says would be like in the wilderness age or the last days-- when the kingdom of this world is now and not yet crashed and when the kingdom of God is now and not yet fully risen. But our passage today tells us that the stock of this world is about to crash anytime now.

There is hardly a day that passes when you are not confronted with a myriad of choices that all can be reduced to one single choice: which kingdom will you take your stand on?

Everytime you cave into the things Paul warns against, be it the love of money, of power, of fame, of self; be it haughtiness, pride…

Everytime we in essence say-- I will do it my way for my interest, we stand on the side of a world that is soon coming to an end..

You could say that you are putting your interest in stock that you already know will fall at years’ end. So the point of our passage is to tell you what you might not readily see, that God is winning, that His kingdom stock is going up and the worldly stock is going down!
