Introduction:



Exposition:

WE are in the seventh bowl wherein the judgement of the Anti-Christ is foretold. Part 1-- (Rev. 18)-- exposes the anti-Christ-- I.e. Where to find him in this world, What he does, and How he does it. Before we develop what John would have us understand, i.e. the exposing of the Anti-Christ, let's take a brief moment to understand the parts of the vision.



I. Brief Review of the Vision (verse 1-2) with Interpretation (verse 3-18)

A. Vision:



Rev. 17:1

Then one of the seven angels who had the seven bowls came and spoke with me,



Revelation 17:1 to 19:10 is a large interpretive review of the 6th and 7th bowls, which foretell the judgment of Babylon. First, the angel who reveals the vision of chapter 17 and who interprets it (see Rev. 17:18) is "one of the seven angels having the seven bowls". This is the first indication that Rev. 17 adds to the sixth and seventh bowls.



Come, I will show you the judgment of the great prostitute



The phrase "I will show you" exposes what before was not clear, thus justifying the "amazement" that John expressed. What is exposed is not only that the anti-Christ is judged but also that the anti-Christ is a great prostitute. Moreover, the the vision will expose how "the prostitute" lures people into her seductive bed.



17 But the angel said to me, "Why are you so amazed? I will tell you the mystery of the woman,



Who is the prostitute exactly? The prostitute = "great city".



18 The woman you saw is the great city that rules over the kings of the earth.



"great city" = Babylon in Rev. 16:19. Note also Rev. 17:5's "Babylon the Great, the Mother of Harlots". And yet this Babylon is described as sitting on seven hills. A city built on seven hills describes Rome.



9 the seven heads are seven mountains on which the woman is seated



Already, we see that the "anti-Christ" is less a person or any one government and more a world system that is expressed in many contexts and in many governments even. Moreover, the "system, world order" is one who "sits on many waters". To be seated means to have authority. This prostitute, this great city, this world system is "sitting" which connotes "sovereignty", and thus is a rival to Christ's sovereignty. Note Rev. 18:7 when the prostitute says, "I sit as a queen", thus justifying the label "anti-Christ" given to her. The phrase "many waters" represents in this vision the multitudes, the nations, and the languages who are lured into submission under the authority of the prostitute/anti-Christ.



Rev. 17:15

And he said to me, "The waters that you saw, where the whore is seated, are peoples and multitudes and nations and languages.

Note also that the prostitute/Babylon "who sits on many waters" is taken directly from Jeremiah's judgment against Babylon in Jer.51:13, foreshadowing the ultimate judgment of the anti-Christ.



Jeremiah 51:11

Make the arrows bright Gather the shields The Lord has raised up the spirit of the kings of the Medes. For His plan is against Babylon to destroy it, Because it is the vengeance of the Lord, The vengeance for His temple. 12 Set up the standard on the walls of Babylon; Make the guard strong, Set up the watchmen, Prepare the ambushes. For the Lord has both devised and done What He spoke against the inhabitants of Babylon. 13 O you who dwell by many waters, Abundant in treasures, Your end has come, The measure of your covetousness.



What is particularly important to observe from Jer. 51 is that Babylon with its sea-based economy is described as "dwelling on many waters" and "abundant in treasures." This gives us a hint on how this vision will point us in the understanding of the seductive nature of idolatry itself.



Rev. 17:2

with whom the kings of the earth have committed fornication,

Submission to the anti-Christ is described as being in an adulterous relationship! Fornication = covenant infidelity. According to the marriage covenant, to commit adultery with a prostitute is to abandon the marriage and to violate the marriage covenant. The same is true with one's covenant with God: to commit idolatry is to abandon one's covenant faithfulness to God. Therefore, this word "fornication" refers to the anti-Christ's intoxicating and immoral influence over the nations (see also Rev.18:3). It does not refer to "fornication" in the literal sense (although it might involve this) but figuratively. That is, the nations are accused of accepting the religious and idolatrous demands of an ungodly earthly order. Thus "all those dwelling on the earth became drunk from the wine of her fornication "(Rev. 17:2), leading them to spiritual infidelity.

The nature of such spiritual fornication is described as being intoxicating like the numbing caused by too much alcohol. Thus there is something about idolatry that numbs us into submission to the Anti-Christ!



This is an amazingly common depiction in scripture: intoxication leading to spiritual unfaithfulness.

Lam. 4:21

Rejoice and be glad, O daughter Edom, you that live in the land of Uz; but to you also the cup shall pass; you shall become drunk and strip yourself bare.

Rev. 18:3

For all the nations have drunk of the wine of the wrath of her fornication, and the kings of the earth have committed fornication with her, and the merchants of the earth have grown rich from the power of her luxury.



Rev. 17:8

And the inhabitants of the earth, whose names have not been written in the book of life from the foundation of the world, will be amazed when they see the beast, because it was and is not and is to come

The beast=Satan who is to be destroyed. The "earth dwellers" in Revelation are those who have committed their trust in the world system and thus puts them at odds with Christ. Here in this verse , they are those who follow the beast and whose names are not written in the book of life, thus they are covenant idolaters who are not the elect who live by faith in Christ.



Well, very briefly , we have dissected the main parts of the vision. Now let's put it all together with more detail and in a redemptive historical context so that the Anti-Christ will be more clearly exposed!



II. The Anti-Christ Exposed. 3 Questions answered.

A. Who is the anti-Christ?

The anti-Christ is that world order, that was, is and will be, and wherein Satan lures people into spiritual infidelity! Any institution or facet of culture that is characterized by pride, economic overabundance, persecution and idolatry is part of Babylon. Ironically, it would be ideal from Satan's point of view if we were to look for any one government/person to be the anti-Christ while he is busy seducing us with the things that pervade our everyday life! The nature of the anti-Christ is such as to cause our amazement of its seductiveness, its proximity, and its pervasiveness. The "prostitute" in the vision is the world system itself, the "city." We have already seen in Revelation that the "city" is identified with Egypt, Babylon, and Rome. This would argue against thinking of the "anti-Christ" as any one single person or government.

13 These are of one mind



It is not necessary to interpret this phrase as "one world government" in a literal sense as some have wanted to do. Rather notice that the "one mind" refers to what Babylon, Egypt, Rome in particular and all of us in general by nature want to do, and that is to give power and authority to the beast and be used by him to seduce the kings, the multitudes, and many languages. For instance, Rev. 17:13 describes the "ten horns" as representing the complete world order (not literally 10 governments) that is given the work of rivaling Christ's authority on behalf of the anti-Christ. Thus these "10 horns" will "make war with the Lamb" (here, Christ).

Therefore, who is the Anti-christ?

Answer: He's anywhere and everywhere within the world system where Christ's exclusive authority is rivaled.



B. How does he rival Christ?

He acts like a prostitute (see verse 1, "the great prostitute"). A prostitute "rivals" or "threatens" the true marriage bed by her offering an alternative bed.

One more further note about the nature of the "lure" that John has in mind:

The connection between economic factors and spiritual idolatry is well attested elsewhere in the Apocalypse and first century Asia Minor. Customarily, each trade guild had patron gods to which members had to pay homage as well as paying homage to the Roman emperor. If Christians did not participate in such homage, they were economically ostracized and prevented from practicing their trade (much like today when a Christian doesn't deny some Christian conviction or practice for the "good of the company" then he/she is prevented from vocational advance.)



6 And I saw that the woman was drunk with the blood of the saints and the blood of the witnesses to Jesus.



Those who do not submit to the idolatrous requirements of economic prosperity will be persecuted by the economic system itself, by Babylon the Great. John expresses this by portraying the woman as "drunk with the blood of the saints and from the blood of the witnesses to Jesus." The persecution of the saints took the form of ostracism from trade. If a Christian had too high a profile in rejecting all forms of idolatry, then the state might persecute either through exile (see Rev. 1:9), imprisonment (see Rev. 2:10), or capital punishment (see Rev. 2:10-13).
