Exposition:

First angel-- vs. 17:1-- I will show you-- exposes the Anti-Christ

Part 1: The Anti-Christ is exposed as he's anywhere and everywhere within the World system that Christ's exclusive authority is rivaled. And Like the Prostitute that lures people into her bed by her seductive beauty, so the Anti-Christ seduces his followers into the bed of his idolatry by intoxication with worldly power, prestige and privileges-- especially as related then to economic related luxuries/gain.

Second angel, vs. 18:1-- Pronounces judgment upon the Anti-Christ… (with great authority)

The judgment promised by the first angel is only mentioned once in ch.17 (where the rest is devoted to exposing the anti-Christ), yet in ch.18 the promise to show forth judgment is fulfilled by the second angel. -- thus, Ch. 17 focuses on the guilt of the anti-Christ and 18 on the judgment of the anti-Christ… Thus a continuation of the vision began in 17:1.

The logic of the passage moves progressively in the declaration of Babylon's coming punishment is the basis successively for exhorting saints to escape Babylon lest they be judged with her (1-8) for Babylon's allies to lament because they perceive their own demise (9-19) and for the saints to rejoice (20-24) and for the climactic glorifying of God as just (1-10)

Main Point: The judgment against the Anti-Christ will be in demonstration of God's Holy Justice, Power and Grace as then cause now for warning, reflection and rejoicing in anticipation of the great wedding feast of the Lamb!

 

Having looked at some of the details, we should not miss that our text is driven by one over arching theme-- Christ's love for His bride!!!!

This whole judgement has been framed in the context of adultery on the one hand, and a bride busying herself for the great wedding day on the other.

Christ loves his bride, and will vindicate and glorify her in the end.

1. True Believers are encourage to persevere in the faith, remaining true to her husband, putting their hope in that which will last vs. that which will not last. The anti-Christ is compared to a fleeting and short-lived "fling" with disastrous consequences in the end.

Illustration: When we hear about unfaithful husbands or wives-- we think of all that they are giving up. How their "fling" will destroy years of building trust, and a future that cannot be had with anyone else. All the shared memories, all the ways that sticking it out drove us to discern something deeper and more meaningful about life itself and the idea of love in particular--something that then couples will share together in the end. And so the danger of literal adultery-- how it is that we throw away for a fling, for a fleeting momentary pleasure, something that can't be had

The assurance to the church comes from the assurance that God, acting as her great husband, will judge her persecutors (see 18:6, 20, 24: 19:2-3)

True Believers are encouraged to know that God will defend them, that their suffering will not be in vain, that they will be, in the end, the exalted and glorified bride of Christ, fully honored as it should be, even though for a while the bride suffering many hardships and stress due to the workings of the anti-Christ.

2. Great warning to those who would disrespect or bring pain to Christ's bride!

One of the major themes of Rev. has been the visions that depict God's answer to the cry of his bride.

Bottom line-- it is a dangerous thing to stand/act against the bride of Christ.

Such revelations make us more aware as to how we treat Christ's bride-- how Christ is offended by this and how Christ will ultimately come to the rescue-- to stand against the bride of Christ, to do anything a that causes suffering upon the Bride of Christ is to be in opposition to Christ himself, the great king of kings and lords of lords…

I.e. to do anything that brings harm to the church is to stand against Christ!

Thus, the warning against slander, gossip, actions that defy or bring schism to the church.

Thus, the warning to those who wold act in league with the anti-christ-- often from within and often without knowing it even, as described in the book of Jude for instance and 1-2 Tim… where some would entice as from within.

Remember that Satan almost always presents himself in religious garb!!!!

Christ's rivals-- where not the "sinners" but those who hated sinners! Those who felt that Christ was mingling with the sinners, or those who wanted Christ to be at war with Rome

Be careful-- to stand against Christ church will look no different-- you will make enemies of sinners rather than the spirit of the anti-Christ, you will want the church to be at war with a modern Rome vs. to be about the business of saving sinners from their sins!

It should cause great concern that Jesus' persecutors condemned him for being too worldly and too lackadaisical with Rome, even as they condemned him for proclaiming forgiveness to tax collectors and prostitutes.
