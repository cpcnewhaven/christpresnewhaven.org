The General Context: Inclusion theme in Romans

Paul uses the expression "there is no distinction" (Romans 3:22 and 10:12) in reference to the character of God's last days redemption and the inclusion of both the Jews and Gentiles into the covenant context that had been previously reserved for the Jews.

Paul's point has been that one of the most significant manifestation of the change from one redemptive

epoch to another regards the long awaited inclusion of the Gentiles into the covenant-- ("all people, both Jews and Greeks" 3:9)

Therefore, the "Jew-Gentile" combination is found in Romans first in reference to the universal nature of

sin (2:9-1, 3:9) and second in relation to the same Lord such that both are included in the historical-redemptive plans of God equally (3:29, 9:24; 10:12). Just as "all have sinned and fallen short of the glory of God" (both those under the law and those outside of it) so too is "the righteousness of God through faith to all who believe."

Sixty- five times in the letter to Romans, Paul uses "all" to demonstrate the equality of God's redemptive economy. Such examples include:

1. references to power of faith to all who believe unto salvation,

2. the universality of sin-- both Jew and Greek respectively,

3. the inclusiveness of Abraham's seed to include both Jew and Gentile by faith,

4. the condemnation of all who would pass judgement against the weak

5. , the inclusion of all who are in Christ as the true Israel, etc.

In other words, as clearly stated in the theme of the

epistle itself, Paul has made it clear that indeed, there is no distinction between the Jew and Greek with respect to the gospel. He writes:

For I am not ashamed of the gospel: it is the power

of God for salvation to every one who has faith, to

the Jew first and also to the Greek. Romans 1:16

Significance--

And so to recieve one another in the context of Romans is to recieve the nations into the covenant context of God's redemptive family... Israel.

Jews-- insiders-- those familiar with the law and the prophets

Gentiles-- outsiders- those not familiar but more significantly-- those socially "black-balled" from the covenant community.

Rom. 12:5 so we, who are many, are one body in Christ, and individually we are members one of another.

III. Why a Multi-national Welcome-

Passage is clear-- the clause immediately following the command "welcome one another" is a comparative clause-- "just as Chrsit welcomed you...

Here-- the same word 'welcome" is used to decribe Christ's ministry ...

Why ? Because Christ does!

I.e. As a church whose head is Christ-- we welcome those who Christ welcomes-- pure and simple!

If we were to make someone feel unwelcome who Chrsit has welcomed-- we cease to be Christ's church don't we! (which is why we will always treat any kind of discrimination so seriously)

But notice carefully Paul's further explanation of what it means that christ welcomes us--

1. He became a "servant"

Because I say to you that Christ has become a servant

The original carefully sets up two categories of people to whom Christ became a servant...

a. of the Jews-- insiders

of the circumcized (Jews) on behalf of the truth (faithfulness) of God in order that He might confirm the promises of (with respect to, given to, in the possession of) the fathers (patriarchs)

b. of the nations-- outsiders

and/yet of the nations on behalf of mercy in order to glorify God

the nations-- the word is used 6 times in th following four verses... so as to underscore the point-- that the nations are to be welcomed and the nations (part two) are to be reached under the NC vision.

c. God's plan all along-- which is Paul's point in quoting from the OC

Perhaps what is most striking though about Paul's die-hard commitment to the inclusion theme is that He clearly understood the welcoming of the Gentiles into the covenant family as in fulfillment to God's former promises in the OC... as to be realized in the last days...

What is especially significant is that Paul from all three parts of OT-- law, prophets and narratives

2Sam. 22:50 Therefore I will give thanks to You, O Lord, among the Gentiles, And sing praises to Your name.

Deut. 32:43 "Rejoice, O Gentiles, with His people; For He will avenge the blood of His servants, And render vengeance to His adversaries; He will provide atonement for His land and His people.''

Is. 11:10 ¶ "And in that day there shall be a Root of Jesse, Who shall stand as a banner to the people; For the Gentiles shall seek Him, And His resting place shall be glorious.''

Mainpoint:-- A truely Apostolic church has not only an apostolic doctrine but an apostolic vision and practice-- here so as to have a multi-national welcome!

Key Point: If our desire is to be an "apostolic church", it is not enough to have apostolic doctrine-- we must also have an apostolic vision! That vision is clearly the Great Commission as unto all the nations.
