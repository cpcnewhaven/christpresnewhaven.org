Text: Romans 3:21-26

Historical Context: Church growth by Jewish and Gentile conversions. (Two groups of people that had been moral and ethnic rivals.)

The distinguishing mark of the Jew (that which distinguished them as a people of God over against all "other" people):

The law (Mosaic Covenant, Torah)

Consequently, of crucial importance to the early church became the question:

What is the Christian's relationship to the Mosiac law?

-Profound implications both in terms of how Jewish and Gentile converts ralated to each other in the church.

-Also, profound theological implications with respect to salvation.

Jewish Answer:

1. Righteousness revealed in the law.

2. Therefore, we are declared righteous by keeping the law.

Consequently, should require Gentile converts to be circumcized, etc.

Paul's Response:

3:20, "For no person will be justified in His sight by deeds prescribed by the law, for through the law come the knowledge of sin."

3:21: "But now", God's righteousness (the satisfaction of his holy standard of justice) has been revealed. apart from the law, yet is attested to by the law.

Paul's basic point:

Then (under the adminstration of the law)

1. We were made mindful of our sin such that God's righteousness is justifiably revealed in his punishment of all people since all have sinned.

2. While the law couldn't save you, it did point to a way where God could remain just in punishing sin yet also provide a way to save sinners from it.

Discussion:

How does the Lords Supper function as a a "sign"?

1. By the words This is my body... this is my blood of the new covenant which is poured out for many for the forgiveness of sins,

Christ clearly wants us to remember the cross where his body and blood were sacrificed.

2. Christ clearly wants us to identify his death on the cross as accomplishing that which was pre-figured in the Sacrificial system attested to by the law on the Day of Atonement.

Mk. 15:37-38, Then Jesus gave a loud cry and breathed his last. ANd the curtain of the temple was torn in two, from top to bottom...

Therefore, the Lord's Supper Signifies:

1. That Christ was attested to by the law. (Not a new idea)

-Depicted by the tearing of the curtain in scripture upon his death.

2. That by Christ death on the Cross, God's righteous anger has been satisfied. God proves himself to be righteous, even as provision is being made for our be declared "not guilty."

3. That through faith in Christ, our sins are forgiven.

I.e.

Lord's Supper Signifies the Doctrine of Justification :

1. Living enactment of God's proving himself to be righteous in his anger against sin.

2. Living enactment of God's justice being served, (not waved) by means of a ransom.

3. Living enactment of God's free, unmerited grace, that gift of being esteeemed as righteous in God's sight, together with all the benefits of eternal life in God's blessed favor and blessing.

WCF Justificatin is described as follows:

Christ, by his obedience and death, did fully discharge the debt of all those that are thus justified, and did make a proper, real, and full satisfaction to his Father's justice in their behalf. Yet, inasmuch as he was given by the Father for them; and his obedience and satisfaction accepted in their stead; and both, freely, not for anything in them; their justification is only of free grace; that both the exact justice and rich grace of God might be glorified in the justification of sinners.

Lord's Supper elludes to an ironic profundity:

God's holy anger and righteous indignation against sin is shown to coexist with God's gracious love and benevolence toward the sinner.

At the cross as depicted in the Lord's supper, God's wrath and love co-exist and are shown to be eternally compatible!

Such a doctrine ought to cause our souls to swell up with thankfulness to God in Christ. Listen to some examples:

What does it mean then that Christ told us to "Do this in remembrance of me:"?

As a cemmemoration are we to remember?

a. That God's justice has not been waived, but satisfied by a costly payment.

b. Remembe the great sacrifice that was made for you!

Note: Elements don't signify two but one reality.

b. That the benefits of the new covenant are the forgiveness of sins because of the sacrifice of Christ as our legal substitute.

2. By telling us to Do this in rememberance of me: we are given the primary purpose for our meeting together on the Lord's Day.

It was intended to be the focal point of Christian worship.

Indeed, this was reflected in the practice of the early church.

Acts 2:42, in their assemblies, "they devoted themselves to the apostles teaching and fellowship (collection), to the breaking of bread and prayers."

Note: Not just what they happened to do, but what they were devoted to doing. Lord's supper was what they were "devoted"to do in worship

Acts 20:7, On the first day of the week, when we were gathered together in order to break bread, Paul talked with them..."

Note: "Breaking bread" is regarded as the point of their gathering.

1 Cor.11:20, "When you meet together, it is not the Lord's supper that you eat...

Again, Paul, as he corrects the Cor. abuse of the Lord's supper, affirms that corporate worship is characterized by the Lord's supper being administered.

Point: Christ's commandment to "do this in rememberance of me" was taken seriously by apostolic church and was what characterized their worship!

It's distinguishes our worship as Christian like nothing else that we do.

Modern Difficulties:

While wanting to eccentuate the value of the Lord's supper, we have devalued it as an distinguishing mark of worship.

1. We have tended to undermine certain distinguishing marks of Christian worship by relegating them to a special season or time.

Devalued as a distinguishing mark of worship in exchange for a distinguishing mark of a season, etc.

2. Have practiced it less frequently in order to make it more special.

Yet same logic is never applied to prayer, preaching, etc.

Affect: Made it into a non-essential element of worship.

Corrective logic:

If the Lord's supper and what it signifies ought to characterize Chrisitan worship, then what would stop us from partaking of it weekly as an essential element of Chrisitan worship?

Therefore The Lord's supper (and that which it signifies) ought to so characterize christian worship that we would think it odd that we would have worship without it, like we would worship without prayer, preaching etc
