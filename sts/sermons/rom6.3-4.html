Romans 6:3-4

Christian Baptism

Introduction:

1. Last week we looked at the OC sacrament of circumcision and how it is perpetuated even today under the NC context through baptism. We say that it had both a physical and spiritual reality in so far as circumcision, as means of grace, was intended to direct us to a deeper and more everlasting reality in so far as God's work for our salvation included both justification and regeneration. Much interest was raised to understand this more thoroughly as from the NC context and Christian baptism. And so I want to continue last weeks sermon today as we turn to a New Covenant text of scripture..

2.Furthermore, as it happens in God's providence, we have today before us two children who await their baptism into the church, which again suggest the occasion to clear up some lingering questions as to the function and meaning of Christian Baptism.

So the question before us today, as from last weeks sermon and because of what awaits us in this service of worship is this:

What is the truth about Baptism as to what it should direct us to with respect to what is being offered through the gospel to the person being baptized?

What reality is being proclaimed by this event? What does it direct us to?

Is it but a family ritual to celebrate the birth of a new member in the family-- NO

Does it work some saving magic such that there is a necessary and direct relation between the act of baptism itself and a persons eternal destiny in heaven-- NO

I.e. getting baptized in itself doesn't save a person-- nor does should it be used like a an insurance policy…

For as we will see a personcan be baptized without truly being baptized?

And our text today-- Rom. 6:1-4-- is the perfect passage to better understand this…

Exposition:

Context:

For Paul has just decribed the free grace of God that results in our justification in chapter 5.

The apostle now pursues the objection most commonly laid against the preaching of divine grace-- namely, that free (vs. earned) grace would result in a licencious and "free" lifestyle with respect to moral rightousness?

About Chapt 6-- Calvin states:

the apostle maintains that those who imagine that Christ bestows free justification upon us without imparting newness of life shamefully rend Christ asunder… The truth is rather that believers are never reconciled to God without the gift of regeneration. Indeed, we are justified for this very purpose, that we may afterwards worship God in purity of life…

And then with reference to the true meaning of Christian Baptism, Paul shows the kind of change which ought to follow justification. Vs. 3-5

I. Baptism signifies Union with Christ through judgment resulting in justification:

3)Do you not know that all of us who have been baptized into Christ Jesus were baptized into his death? 4) We ere burdied therefore with him by baptism into death…

Observations:

A. Baptism is associated with judgment in this passage:

2 times-- "baptized into his death… baptism into death"

1. We know that the penalty of sin is death-- death respresents not only the fact that we physically die-- but that we die without ever being restored to God and all the benefits that God's promised Adam upon humanities completion of the probation period in the creation covenant…

Rom 6:23-- For the wages of sin is death

Physical death is both a reality of the curse against sin but also became a symbol that represents all that misery that is the result of sin and God's judgment against it…

Today, we suffer many miseries because of our sin-- this is part of God's plan such as to drive us back to him.

Rom. 1:18ff-- decribes God's judgment as delivering us over to our sin and with this of course the pain and misery that result..

Think about all the miseries in this life--- they are all reminders of God's judgment that should lead us to his grace…

So that where there is an eternal "judgment" which death refers to, there is also that present judgment that "death" refers to.

So in some way, Paul states that baptism directs us to Christ's death and subsequently to the issue of God's judgment itself

But how does Baptism signfiy this judgment and our passing through it into a justification?

2. Key is the Water used in Baptism-- if understood within a redemptive historical framework, we discover that water came to represent judgment in that its use as a symbol originating from historical judgments…

1 Peter 3:18-22, For Christ also suffered for sins once for all, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring you to God. He was put to death in the flesh, but made alive in the spirit, … the days of Noah, during the building of the ark, in which a few, that is, eight persons, were saved through water. 21 And baptism, which this prefigured, now saves you--not as a removal of dirt from the body, but as an appeal to God for a good conscience, through the resurrection of Jesus Christ, 22 who has gone into heaven and is at the right hand of God, with angels, authorities, and powers made subject to him.

1 Cor.10:1-2, baptism = passing through Red Sea with Moses

I do not want you to be unaware, brothers and sisters, that our ancestors were all under the cloud, and all passed through the sea, 2 and all were baptized into Moses in the cloud and in the sea,

The water then reminds us also of the judgment that awaits all people!

2Pet. 3:5 They deliberately ignore this fact, that by the word of God heavens existed long ago and an earth was formed out of water and by means of water, 6 through which the world of that time was deluged with water and perished. 7 But by the same word the present heavens and earth have been reserved for fire, being kept until the day of judgment and destruction of the godless.

Therefore, as by these historic "judgment" ordeals in redemptive history-- water itself became symbol of divine judgment such as to pass through it was to pass through this judgment…

Paul hear recognized the use of water as such as symbol even as it had been recognized earlier in redemptive history.

So for instance,

Just as blood also represented God's judgment (in to take blood was to take away life such as to invoke the image of death) so then it was used ceremoniously to in purification rites to symbolize that a person could pass through God's judgment into a new life that was not longer under that judgment..

The use of "water" became a vivid picture likened by Peter unto the waters of the flood and Red sea which signifies the coming of a great judgment ordeal of which all must pass.

For the adversaries of Noah and Israel, these waters enacted God's terrible curse and condemnation.

We therefore discover that "water" like "blood" came to be associated with judgment such that it was used ceremonially even in the OC Church to signify God's providing a way to pass through judgment into new life… a kind of "purification" rite of passage… a passage through judgment…

Blood:

Ex. 24:6 And Moses took half the blood and put it in basins, and half the blood he sprinkled on the altar.

Ex. 24:8 And Moses took the blood, sprinkled it on the people, and said, "This is the blood of the covenant which the Lord has made with you according to all these words.''

So also, it might surprise some of you to discover, that water was also used in purification rites-- again like blood, used to symbolize a passing through judgmet into a new life no longer under God's curse of death! This is clearly refered to both by Ezekiel and the writer of Hebrews:

Water:

Ezek. 36:25 "Then I will sprinkle clean water on you, and you shall be clean; I will cleanse you from all your filthiness and from all your idols.

Heb. 10:22 let us draw near with a true heart in full assurance of faith, having our hearts sprinkled from an evil conscience and our bodies washed with pure water.

Therefore, the symbol of water in baptism, like the symbol of blood, was used to direct us to that "new life" that was no longer under God's judgment curse of death…

It for this reason, that Paul, being familiar with the historical redemptive framework of the scriptures-- related baptism with its use of water as a purification rite-- to Christ death! For in Christ's death, the curse of God's judgment was diverted from us to him so that by faith in Christ-- we may as the cremonial rite directs us-- pass through judgment into a new life no longer under God's curse against sin…

It is for this reason that Paul will conclude this chapter that

Vs. 22-- the wages of sin is death but the free gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord!

2. Moreover-- the words "have been baptized are in a form that indicates an action completed in the past. -- once and for all

Vs. 10-- The death he died he died to sin, once for all,

The grammer puts the recipient in a passive condition which indicates that it was "his" death that accomplished this for us-- not anything that we ourselves have done-- which explains the phrase baptised into his death!

In this way, baptism doesn't save us, but directs like a sign to that which does save us-- namely the death of Christ on our behalf!

Illus-- Stop sign doesn't do much good unless it directs us to that which will…

Summary: The truth about Baptism is that it signifies a passage through judgement resulting in our being restored to God forever and ever!

Again, it is this aspect of grace that Paul has already developed in Chapter 5 as well.

And in Chapt 4, you might also recall that this was the meaning of circumcision as well-- a ceremony directing us to God's promise to justify some so as to no longer be under the legal curse of God's judgment against sin!

But remember that Paul's concern is to respond to the objection that such grace leads to licentiousness-- so he now connects this objective work of Christ as represented by baptism with yet another and subjective work of Christ that is also represented by baptism and is inseperably connected.

So that if baptism directs us toGod's work for our justification, it ought also to direct us to God's work in regeneration…

II. Baptism signifies a union with Christ that results in regeneration:

So that as Christ was raised from the dead by the glory of the Father we too might walk in newness of life

Clearly here, by the use of walk, Paul is speaking of a subjective aspect to the meaning of baptism

Walk-- is used here to describe not a person legal status with God (justification) but rather a persons actual manner of living before God-- this would include our spiritual disposition, our affections and our actions.

Rom. 8:4

so that the just requirement of the law might be fulfilled in us, who walk not according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.

2Cor. 5:7

for we walk by faith, not by sight.

newness of life-- Paul is talking about our subjective natures that are "born again" so as to be new creatures… we live a new life even as we are no longer under God's legal curse!

Paul is clearly affirming that the baptism rite ought to direct us to a new life as indicated by conversion (repentance and faith) and sanctification (that manner of life that is more and more not according to sin (flesh) but according to the spirit (rightousness)

See then Paul's logic for this--

He in effect says that even as baptism directs us to Christ's death-- that Christ didn't remain in the grave but was raised from the dead…

Was raised-- (aor. Pass) once and for all past as accomplished for us

So that we might walkin… This is then followed by the result clause (aor.act subj)-- which puts us in the action of walking in newness of life…

I.e. Christ's resurrection was for the purpose that we might also walk in a new way! A resurrected life…

This is Paul's point in Rom. 8-- when he argues that the same spirit that was at work to raise Christ from the dead is now at work in those who God has justified!

THE TWO ARE INSEPERABLE SO THAT NOT ONLY DOES OUR LEGAL STATUS WITH GOD CHANGE BY FAITH-- BUT ALSO WE CHANGE AS WELL-- THIS BEING ATTRIBUTED TO REGENERATION (NEW LIFE)

Calvin: The truth is rather that believers are never reconciled to God without the gift of regeneration. Indeed, we are justified for this very purpose, that we may afterwards worship God in purity of life… By using an adverb to denote future time, Paul shows the kind of change which ought to follow justification.

This is further argued by Paul in vs. 5 which helps us understand more about the nature of our "new life"

Vs. 5-- "For if we have been united with him in a death like his, we shall certainly be united with him in a resurrection like his… we know that our old self was crucified with him so that the sinful body might be destroyed and we might no longer be enslaved to sin.

Paul is saying that our objective union with Christ is such that we have legally been "judged already (through Christ's death) and legally have entered into a new standing with God (through Christ's resurrction.

He then states that this legal union has a corresponding and necessary subjective union in so far as

1. We mortify (put to death) that part of us that sins

2. Even as we live to that new life in us that is rightous

He used this word:

Destroyed-- I.e. destroyed, make ineffective, be from an association with something, abolished, set aside.

So as to say that sin no longer enslaves us to itself, but rather rightousness does.

vs. 6-7: No longer its slave--

I.e. Don't have to serve it but now have natures such that we serve rightousness.

Vs. 22-- you have been set free from sin and have become slaves of God, the return you get is sanctification and its end, eternal life…

Paul's fundamental point to those who would accuse his doctrine of grace with licentiousness is to respond that that there is a subjective aspect to divine grace as well.. that even as the free grace of God justifies us so as to no longer be under the legal judgement of God-- that this same free grace regenerates us and in this present world will enable us more and more to walk in newness of life!

I.e. Paul is saying that to understand free grace only in the objective sense but not also in the subjective sense is to severely limit the extent and effacacy of free grace.

So inseperable are the two, the objective and subjective aspects of free grace--- that to the objection, "doesn't free grace (as related to justification) encourage us to continue to sin?" Paul response in no way! (vs. 2)

And of all things, he uses the ceremonial rite of baptism to illustrate this-- assuming hear that his readers already understood what baptism represented.

As Baptism directs us-- The two, justification and Regeneration, are inseparable such that you will know the one by the other!

Calvin: Paul rightly passes from the fellowship of Christ's death to the sharing of His life. Because these are inseparably connected, our old man is destroyed by the death of Christ, so that His resurrection may restore our rightousness, and make us new creatures.

There is put one more issue could be raised at this point-- Paul's language here links justification and regeneration leading to sanctification so necessarily, that it has mislead some to suspect that a person who still struggles with sin might not be justified -- thus the doctrine of perfectionism in this life as evidence to justification.

We should notice the obvious at this point-- that Paul will take his argument and exhort believers to live godly lives-- which itself presupposes that Paul understood something about the present redemptive age such that while we are expected to persevere in progressing toward holiness, we are not yet made fully holy-- such that the free grace of God is evidence not by our perfection yet, (this awaits us in glory) but is evidence by a life that peseveres in dieing to sin and living to rightousness more and more as the day of glorification draws near!

Vs. 11-- Why else would Paul exhort believers to walk in newness of life unless in fact there was still some walking yet to be done?

So you also must consider yourselves dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus, Let not sin therefore reign in your moral bodi9es to make you abey their passions. Do not yeild your members to sin as instruments of wickedness… etc. for sin will have no dominion over you since you are not under the law but under grace.

Mainpoint: Baptism ought to direct us to the free grace of God that both sets us free from the legal curse against our sin (justification) AND sets us free from the bondage of sinning even in this present life so that we might walk in newness of life in rightousness (regeneration unto sanctification).

 

Application:

Have you been baptized?

1. One way you could answer is by thinking about the ceremony itself-- the sprinkling of water whether as child or an adult-- and if so you could say yes.

But, as we have seen this ceremony of baptism, like circumcision, is applied to those who enter the church since the church, with its ministry of word, sacraments and pastoral care-- are means of grace that God's instituted in order to direct you to something more eternal, more abiding, more spiritual than even membership in a that church that is visible in the present age…

2. So this raises the question as to whether you have been baptized with that kind of baptism that your physical baptism was intended to direct you to-- a spiritual baptism that is unto justification and regeneration unto salvation.

Are you justified-- saved by free grace from God's curse against you as a sinner?

Well since Paul has shown that justification is inseperably conneccted to regeneration-- this question could just as well be asked

Are you regenerate? And how would you know.

Well Paul just taught his readers that "faith" is in fact instrument through which free grace comes to us personally.

Faith-- which is to depend on God alone for these things promised in justification and sanctification-- is in fact the first evidence of regeneration.

Therefore, have you put your faith on Christ alone so that you might be set free from God's curse against sin and that you might even now have the power of the Holy Spirit working in you that you might even walk in newness of life?

Calvin: We know that whatever the Lord offers by the visible symbol is confirmed and ratified by their faith. In short, he teaches us what the truth of baptism is when rightly received. Thus he testifies that all the Galations who had been baptized into Christ had put on Christ (Gal.3:27) We must always use these terms while the institution of the Lord and the faith of Believers corrspond, for we never have naked and empty symbols, except when our ingratitude and wickedness hinder the working of the divine beneficence…

 

Therefore, if you have been baptized ceremonially-- this should all the more direct you to that spiritual baptism that is first revealed by faith..

Mark 1:8

I have baptized you with water; but he will baptize you with the Holy Spirit."

And if you wonder about whether you have faith or not-- you are to examine yourself to see evidence of God free grace in so far as it is enabling you more and more to walking in newness of life!

There is then this haunting reality for every man, women and child that sits here today-- that you could be "baptized" without really being baptized!

If you are the elect, though you will see in yourself a continued struggle against sin-- it will have the effect in you to BOTH

1. Bring you to humble reliance upon God everyday for the forgiveness of sin based on his free, unmerited grace!

2. It will also bring you to those means of grace that God has provided in order that you might more and more find victory in this life even over the sins-- since you have been told already that they no longer "enslave" you as they once did!

Let us then come to this baptism even as we examine ourselves as to whether we have been truly baptized by the Holy Spirit, that same spirit that raised Christ from the dead that even now works in you in newness of life!
