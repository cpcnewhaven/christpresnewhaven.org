ROMANS 9:1-18
THE JUSTIFICATION OF GOD
Part One: Did God Fail In His Promise?

Introduction:
Whenever things don't work out the way we think it should, or perhaps in accordance to our prayers-- it is tempting to have one of two responses

1. "God isn't fair"
2." God failed"

This attitude is revealed at many levels-- both by those who are not Christians, who look at a world that seems to be out of control... Or by Christians whose supposedly Christian expectations are met by a sad reality!
Both call to question the realibility of God!
Both call for the "Justification or vindication of God! (Now some will say that God needs no justification for his actions-- this is true and in fact one of the ways that Paul will in fact defend God against those who would doubt his fairness or power)

As you will see, this was exactly what was at issue for the people to whom Paul was writing 2 thousand years ago.

Exposition: (only part 1)
I. Problem Exposed:
What seemed to be two mutually exclusive truths:
1. "No Distinction":
a. No distinction regarding who is rejected by God

Rom. 3:9 What then? Are we any better off? No, not at all; for we have already charged that all, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin, 10 as it is written: "There is no one who is righteous, not even one;


b. No distinction regarding who is blessed by God

Rom. 3:21 But now, apart from law, the righteousness of God has been disclosed, and is attested by the law and the prophets, 22 the righteousness of God through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction, 23 since all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; 24 they are now justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,


c. Redifinition of who is a real Jew:

Rom. 2:28 For a person is not a Jew who is one outwardly, nor is true circumcision something external and physical. 29 Rather, a person is a Jew who is one inwardly, and real circumcision is a matter of the heart--it is spiritual and not literal. Such a person receives praise not from others but from God.


2. Nothing can seperate us from the covenant love of God:

Rom. 8:39 nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord.


First Century Jewish dilemma:
"No distinction? No privileged status for ethnic Israel?

J. Dunn p.647
"The bulk of Paul's Jewish contemporaries had made the mistake of understanding their status as God's chosen people in terms of works, in terms of national customs and ritual acts which defined their identity as God's holy people.


Therefore, to say that Israel, as an ethnic, national people, are no longer regarded as the people of God-- is as to say that God had failed! For didn't God promise Israel an eternal covenant status and blessing as His holy people?

Therefore, what is at stake for Paul is nothing less than the Rightousness and justice of God under scrutiny?
Either:
1. God has Failed-- making promises he couldn't keep
2. God is UnJust: Saying one thing but then doing something else
That is, assuming Paul's Gospel! God's goodness, fairness and even morality are called into question.

These are the two questions being answered in Chapter 9
vs.1-13: Did God's word fail? (vs.6) No!
vs.14-29: Is God Unjust? (vs.14) No!

He will ultimately Conclude his argument in Chapter 11,

Rom. 11:1 I ask, then, has God rejected his people? By no means! I myself am an Israelite, a descendant of Abraham, a member of the tribe of Benjamin. 2 God has not rejected his people whom he foreknew. Do you not know what the scripture says of Elijah, how he pleads with God against Israel? 3 "Lord, they have killed your prophets, they have demolished your altars; I alone am left, and they are seeking my life." 4 But what is the divine reply to him? "I have kept for myself seven thousand who have not bowed the knee to Baal." 5 So too at the present time there is a remnant, chosen by grace. 6 But if it is by grace, it is no longer on the basis of works, otherwise grace would no longer be grace.


II. The Justification of God Part 1: DID GOD FAIL?
A. 9:1-5: The Problem:
Question due to a seeming contradiction between the promised privileges to Israel and the Plight of Paul's Jewish Kinsmen.
1. Notice Paul's sorrow for Jewsih kinsmen according to his gospel, vs. 2

great sorrow- affliction, pain of mind"
so much so, that He would prefer to be cut off from God himself if this would mean the salvation of those in whom he is sorrowful.
I.e. Willingness to become (lit) an anathma -- cut off from God!
Point: Assumes that these people are in fact cut off from God such that Paul is willing to be their substitute if he could!


2. Notice carefully WHO Paul is talking about. vs.3

RSV: kinsmen by race
Literally: "my brothers, kinsmen according to the flesh"
Therefore: his ethnic or "family" kinsmen


3. Notice carefully the advantages acknowledged by Paul on behalf of his kinsmen by race (4-5)

Note: Series of 6 words that rhyme ("aye" endings) implies that the meaning of list lie more in the whole than in each particular.
THEREFORE, the whole list describes a people who in some sense enjoyed a privledged status with God.
a. "adoption as sons"

Ex.4:22- "And you shall say to Pharaoh... Israelis my first born son, and I say to you, let my son go that he may serve me..."

b. "glory" = "shikinah glory",

Refers to the OT theophanies of God, especially early wilderness experience. Theophany- manifestations of God to people.
Point: Always associated with God's act in saving Israel. Ex. Burning Bush (Ex.16:7,10; 24:16; 29:43...)

c. "Covenants" (plural)

Adamic, Noatic, Abrahamic, Mosaic, Prophetic.
Especially the Prophetic promises to restore Israel
Is.59:20-21 as qouted by Paul in 11:26-27: "And this is my covenant with them, when I takeaway their sins."

d. "giving of the law"

Law was the most sacred possession of Jew and brought salvation with it. (Ex.19:6; 29:45, 33:19)

e. "service of worship"

-In Grk. OT: 3 times Passover, 1 time to sacrifices.
I.e. Sacrificial provisions in the OT which were the ordinance through which God mediated His salvation.

f. "promises"

g. "Theirs belong the partriarchs"Abraham, Isaac and Jacob"

Through whom God preserved a redemptive line.

h. "Human ancestry of Christ"

Linked to prophecies linking the coming delivererwith the seed of Jesse, etc.

Point: These Privileges are indicative of a special status with God-- one laden with salvation implication-- so it would seem

So, how is it that Paul's gospel, one that does not acknowledge covenant privileges for ethnic Israel, could be true...
According to Paul's Gospel-- has God simply failed?
For according to Paul, their plight is as horifying as their privileges were glorious.


B. Question Raised: 6a: In light of promises, has God's word failed.
Obvious 1st century question:

C. 9:6-13: Question Answered:
vs.6b: "not all those from Israel are Israel"

Implicit: You misunderstood God's word!
They had misunderstood the place of ethnic Israel in redemptive history!
Key: Ethnic/national Israel was intended all along to be symbolic of a deeper and true covenant people.
Israel was typolological of the covenant people

vs. 7: not all are children of Abraham because they are his descendants-- but through Isaac shall your descendants shall be named

I.e. "through" which means-- from out of


Therefore, God has not failed. God's redemptive purpose have been exclusive all along as pertaining to the elect.(8:33) AND NOT ALL ISRAEL ARE ELECT.

Explanation:
Romans 8:33: Who are the "elect" to whom this hope of never being seperated from the love of God holds true?
2 Israel's:
1. Ethic Israel-- temporal, national and ethnic descendents of Abraham, Isaac, Jacob and Moses...
2. Remnant people of God in whom the national Israel was always symbolic in its temporal context!


Then note vs. 8: contrast between--

1. Natural children (tekna of sarx) are not the children of God.
2. but the childrne of the promise are reckoned as descendents (sperma) of promise"
Rom.9:29- "sperma" = "remnant"
Quote from Is.10:20, "though....only remnant will be saved"
I.E. according to the OT: Purifying judgment would render forth aholy remnant. Is.5:16


Therefore, not all "Israel" is the "remnant Israel"-- and only the "remanant Israel" enjoy the eternal promises of God in the gospel!

D. 2 Proofs: of an "elect" Israel from out of an "ethnic" Israel-- of which the true church is today the "elect" Israel... Case studies in Redemptive History
vs.7-9: Proof # 1: , Isaac vs.Ishmeal

vs. 7: From a Human point of view, a arbitrary choosing of Isaac over Ishmeal.
You will remember that this was even in spite of Abraham's objections on behalf of Ismeal...


vs.10-13: Proof # 2: Jacob vs. Esau.

Key: vs. 11, Choice of Jacob over Isau before birth. "and had done nothing good or bad..." in order that God's purpose of election might continue, not because of works but because of his call...
Intentionality with respect to God's choice
Irrespective of anything accomplished by either Jacob or Isau, and irrespective of Isaac's personal intention to bless Isau the true firstborn...
Is grounded only in the fact that God choose one and not the other!

Both case studies point to the fact that not all Isreal are the "true Israel" since exclusion of some and inclusion of others has been the pattern of redemption since the very beginning...


1. That the "true Israel" consist of those elected by God is clear to see from these two case studies in redemptive history
2. But the point is that not all Israel is the people that are blessed by God

Paul's point is that just as Isaac was chosen over Ismeal, and Jacob over Esau, so too some Gentiles, are chosen over some Jews as the "true Israel" who in fact will never be seperated from the love of God.

Paul's point is that having an ethnic or family affiliation with national Israel does not make you necessarily blessed in God eternal coveant.

Conclusion: God's word has not failed!-- not if we understand it properly.

Paul's gospel-- that the remnant people of God are characterized by faith in Christ...
That some Jews are not true Israel since they do not believe in Christ is not anything new-- since it has always been the case that some are chosen by God as indicated by thier faith, others are not...
I.e. God's purposes versus human purpose should be our standard.
It was always his purpose that He would select from out of Israel a remnant who would enjoy the full priviledge of the gospel-- Paul's gospel is only to expand the notion that these "remnant people" are not exclusively from out of Israel, but from out of the nations as well!

Mainpoint: God is justified against the claims that he has failed according to the Gospel. Paul's point was to reveal a false expectation that was rooted in falsely understanding the place of Israel in God's historical-redemptive plan!
APPLICATION:

1. Clarification in Our Hermenetic-- especially in anticipation of Reading Proverbs

National Israel, in the redemptive historical plan of God- is typological as symbolically representative of the the elect

Today-- The true Church = Israel!

Therefore, as we read the Old Covenant-- we must remember that the Mosaic law promised temporal blessings to a temporal nation-- but these blessings are now to be interpreted as representing God's covenantal blessings for the true remnant people or the true Church!

When we interpret the promises of God in the Old covenant:
If they are temporal, we must remember that they pertain to national Israel as symbolic of the spiritual blessings which are enjoyed by the remnant people.
Just as it was wrong in Paul's day to presume that national or temporal prosperity necessarily means covenant blessing, so today-- it is NOT TRUE that to be blessed materially is to be blessed covenantally with everlasting life! We must never forget that today, those who have received Christ genuinely are the true Israel of God!

2. Comfort from this passage:

Has God failed when something bad happens?
No-- Because God's purposes are not always our purposes...
THERE IS GREAT COMFORT IN KNOWING THIS!

When we receive bad news-- news that seemingly is evil-- we imediately ask-- where was God?
Here we discover that God's purposes are always kept-- and are always for good, to those who are the elect-- those who are never seperated from his love (8:33), those who are characterized by faith in Christ!

Have we lost a theology that is big enough for God's soeverignty in our lives?
Illus: Casey at Bat-- (tell the story-- the late 19th century story of the great baseball player who struck out-- later the "vindication of Casey" was printed in the New Yorker where Casey hits a home run)
Could we modern people write a story like "Casey at Bat" -- do we have room in our theology for failure under the providence of God-- even failure that in fact is good!

3. Warning from this passage:

There is an election into God's privileged family that transcends all human affiliations

To be affiliated with the outward means of grace that are provided through the visible assembly of God's people IS essential to salvation-- but SALVATION IS NOT ESSENTIAL TO THE MEANS OF GRACE...
I.E. Membership in the church IS NOT the same as being a member in the true church!

Even if you have professed faith in Christ and are now a communicant member of the church-- this does not necessarily make you a member of the true Church.

Only those who genuinely live accordingn to faith in Christ are members of the true church

Just as Paul could say-- "not all Israel is Isreal"
so we can say, not all church is the church"
