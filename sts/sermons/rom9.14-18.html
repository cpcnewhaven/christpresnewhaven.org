Romans 9:14-29
The Justification Of God Part 2, Is God Fair?

Introduction:
Have you ever throught or asked-- Is God Fair?
This question is often the result of when we are somehow confronted with the sovereignty of God over us-- when we are subject to a crisis or tragedy, or when we hear about election into salvation,or when it is perceived that some prosper while others don't-- for what seems to be no human reason..
Is God fair?

Exposition:
I. Problem (reviewed)

A. Security in ethnic/national heritage so as to insure God's favor. As ethnic/national Jews-- they were "better off" with God... at least so they thought.

B. Gospel:"No Distinction":

a. No distinction regarding who is rejected by God
Rom. 3:9 What then? Are we any better off? No, not at all; for we have already charged that all, both Jews and Greeks, are under the power of sin, 10 as it is written: "There is no one who is righteous, not even one;

b. No distinction regarding who is blessed by God
Rom. 3:21 (salvation-- favored status with God) through faith in Jesus Christ for all who believe. For there is no distinction, 23 since all have sinned and fall short of the glory of God; 24 they are now justified by his grace as a gift, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus,


C. Then did God fail since he promised to never forsake Israel?

Answer vs.1-13: (vs.6) No! "It is not as though God's word Failed
Why? because
vs.6b: "not all those from Israel are Israel"
I.e. vs. 7: not all are children of Abraham because they are his descendants-- but through Isaac shall your descendants shall be named

Proofs:
vs.7-9: Proof # 1: , God elected Isaac-- not Ishmeal although both where sons of Abraham
vs.10-13: Proof # 2: God elected Jacob-- not Esau, although both were sons of Isaac


Therefore, God is shown to have always elected some out of Israel to be the "true Israel." God exerts his sovereign right as creator to determine who does and does not have salvation from their sins. God did not fail his promises to Isreal, but fulfills then in the true Israel-- those remnant people who according to Paul's gospel put their faith in the Messiah!


II. SECOND QUESTION AGAINST GOD: WELL THEN IS GOD FAIR?
Problem Stated: vs.14: Is there injustice with God?

Whereas before the ability (power) of God to keep his promises was being questioned, now the justice of God is what's under scrutiny. If you listen carefully, it's a question we have all asked-- Is it fair that God chose this X (husband, child, circumstance, trouble) for me and not my friend? Or how many people have criticized the Christian God for allowing natural distructions in the world-- is God fair-- we ask? Is is fair that that "good" person lost their child? Is is fair that that God allowed that civilization to be persecuted? Is it fair that the tornado hit "x" city and not "y" city? Is if fair that my husband/wife is not like my friends husband/wife? etc. etc.

And then it is concluded-- NO, so how could I serve and worship a God who is unfair?
1. Notice carefully the exact nature of the question.

unjust = a construction of the word "not rightous"
I.e. Did God Fail a standard of what is right?
And if so, then whose standard?
Note: What is it about us, that we rise up and stand in judgement against God-- imposing OUR standard of right and wrong upon him so as to measure him by it-- and then, when He is viewed as failing that standard, to conclude that God is not God at all!

Point: the very question "is God fair" assumes a standard of what is right-- but is OUR standard consistent? Paul then tests the presuppositional standard that is being used to condemn God as "unfair."

2. Notice Paul's reponse to the Justice of God under scrutiny:
vs.14b. by no means

Why? Implied-- justice presupposes a standard of right-- whose standard? According to God's standard, unconditional election doesn't deny God's justice and righteousness, it suppports it.

3. vs 15: Supporting Argument: Consider Exodus 33:19
Context:
a. Exodus 33:1- Moses is comand to leave Sinai and go to the land promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob
b. Ex.33:2- promise to send angels before them for protection
c. Ex.33:3- 5 God determines not to go with them for he says, "you are a stiff-necked people; if for a single moment I should go up among you, I would consume you... "

(vs.7-11- describes in what sense God had been willing to co-inhabit Israel up until now-- via the "tent of meeting" where God's imediate presence was represented by the "pillar of cloud" that would discend upon the tent. ) It is in this sense, God is unwilling to go with Israel!
For Moses, this was like taking away the only security Israel had! It was the pillar of cloud that represented to both Israel and the world that God was with them-- he didn't want to lose this special ordinance! (like taking away the Lord's table, but more--since God miraculously endwelled the tent of meeting)

d. Therefore, Moses pleads with God to not forsake Israel-- appealing to God's promise of favor to Moses:
vs. 12-13: "Yet you have said, "I know you by name, and you have also found favor in you"
appealing to God's promise to inhabit Israel:
vs.14: "Consider too that this nation is your people, He said, "My presence will go with you and I will give you rest."
appealing to God's reputation among the nations if he were to forsake Israel:
vs15-"How will anyone know you are pleased with me and with your people unless you go with us? In this way we shall be distinct.

e. vs. 17: God agrees to do the very thing Moses asks

**F. Ex.33:18: "Now show me your glory"
Why this request?
Mose's Delimma: After what God had already said concerning the condition of the people, how could Moses trust that God wouldn't consume the people?
Moses prays then for God to reveal his character such as would not consume Israel!
That this is the reason for Mose's request is made evident by the Lord's response.
Response: vs. 19 (quoted by Paul)
"And the Lord said, "I will cause all my goodness to pass in front of you, and I will proclaim my name, the Lord, in your presence I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy, and I will have compassion on whom I will have comassion."

a. Grammer: formula in which the action is left unspecified such that the force is to emphasis the freedom of the subject to do what he pleases.
Examples:
"Bake what you will bake, boil what you will boil" (Ex.16:23)
"The went about where they went about" (2 S 23:13)

In this case, God is free to show mercy even in spite of the fact that he would ordinarily destroy "stiff-necked" people!

God's Assurance to Moses is that God works independant of human circumstances or conditions. There are no stipulations outside God's own counsel or will which determine the disposal of his mercy and grace! In other words, in God's election of a people for salvation, God is acting out of a full allegiance to his own character and resputation out of an esteem for his glory. ( Piper, Justification of God, p.161)

It this "fair"?== thankfully, not according to human standards or God would have destroyed a stiff-necked people. But God"shows mercy to whom he shows mercy" after the standard of his own purposes for glory!

Therefore, the standard for justice is measured by a divine Standard--

Calvin: God's will is, and rightly ought to be, the cause of all things that are. For if it has any cause, something must precede it, to which it is.... bound; this is unlawful to imagine. For God's will is so much the highest rule of righteousness that whatever He wills, by the very fact that He wills it, must be considered righteous." Institutes, 3.23.2


From Moses's point of View: The independence of God's activity from the human condition is Good News!

God is saying that he will not destroy, not because of Israel but because of his will.
Ezek. 36:22f- Therefore say to the house of Israel, This is what the Soverign Lord says: It is not for your sake, O House of Israel, that I am going to do these things, but for the sake of my holy name...
I.e. Ex.3:14- "I am who I am"
Also, the same lesson that Paul refers to in the case of Pharoh
Rom.9:17, I have raised you up for the very purpose of showing my power in you, so that my name may be proclaimed in all the earth..


"The exercise of God's sovereign freedom, mercy and in hardening, is the means by which he declares the glory of his name!" Piper, p.161

Conclusion: God acts independently of human condition and circumstances after the standard which is in order to reveal his own glory.

Is God fair?
That is, Does God act according to what is right?

If your standard is your own glory, your own convenience, your own preferences, your own prosperity-- No!

But IF you make God's standard your own, which is to reveal his glory to the nations-- Yes

For the Jews in Paul's day-- they had forgotten that God's promise to bless them was always conditioned upon the standard that it brought glory to God! Yet if it brings glory to God for some of national ethnic/Israel to be chosen, and some not-- then this is "Fair" after the standard of God's glory!

Justice is relative to God NOT mere mortal! God is righteous in that he is acting in a way consistent with the Divine purpose,

"Justice of God is his unswerving commitment always to bring his actions into accord with the reality of his infinitely worthy glory." Piper p.73


Application:
1. If you are a unbeliever-- I hope you recognize the good news here.
It doesn't take a genious to realize that the human race is in some sense morally flawed!

Nor does it require much of a theologian to understand that if God is perfect in his holiness, that God could do anything short of condemning sin.

Therefore, if left to this, we would all "fall short of the glory of God without distinction, and be subject to God's holy justice against us!

However, If for the sake of God's glory, he chosed to "show mercy on whom he shows mercy, compassion on whom he shows compassion" then we, like Moses, ought to appeal to God that he might so chose to show us mercy and campassion, even though we don't deserve it.

This is to say that the doctrine of election into salvation is GOOD NEWS.

Perhaps for the self-rightous--those who think that they are in themselves without sin, or little enough sin so as to not condemn them to God's judgment-- the doctrine of election is not good news. But for those who are humble (which I find many unbelievers to be) then for them, the good news of the gospel is that God can bless you even in spite of yourself-- because God gives mercy on whom he pleases, irregardless of your sins!

How would you know if he does this to you
1. You will desire it-- as evident through prayer
2. You will want to turn away from any known sin, and want God to make you more and more like Him in holiness...

That is to say that one evidence that you are "chosen by God" for mercy is that He will give to you a new dispostion-- one that in inclined to follow after him-- one who has a changed orientation in life such that God's glory and preferences are now your own!

2. Children: Story of the flunked student! Once there was a student who turned in his paper late to the teacher and received back a "C." The student complained because he thought it was not fair that his paper was dropped two letter grades for only one day late. The teacher read the course policy which stated that under no circumstances would the teacher receive late papers. Then the teacher asked to have the paper back, and said, you want "fair"-- the teacher then put a big "O" on the paper and gave it back to the student-- the student then cried out for "mercy" not "Justice."
So it is this way with God-- justice would demand that we are declared "guilty" under the law-- but God's mercy condemned Christ for us s o as to satisfy divine justice, and to declare us "not guilty" for Christ's sake so as to satisfy divine mercy-- Indeed, God says, "I will have mercy on whom I will have mercy."

3. Believers--
Israel was called a stiff-necked people. Even with all the ordinances of God, even with God's "pillar of cloud" around them, they murmured against God at every turn of the way when things were somehow not in accordance to their own preferences or plans!

We must be careful not to be a stiff-necked people. When life doesn't work after our plans, we must resist questioning the "fairness of God."

God always is fair-- in that he always acts in accordance with the standard which is according to his own glory!
For those who remember that they are not their own, but belong to their faithful savior Jesus Christ for his glory-- yes, God is fair! For we know that God causes all things to work together for good to those love him and ARE CALLED ACCORDING TO HIS PURPOSE-- A PURPOSE WE UNDERSTAND NOW TO BE HIS OWN GLORY...

For believers then, we must heed the advice of Paul to his Jewish brethren, lest we too fall short of persevering in the faith!

Rom.9:19, Paul says, "You will say to me then, Why does he still find fault? For who can resist his will? But who are you, a man to answer back to God? Will what is molded say to its molder, "Why have you made me thus? has the potter no right over the clay to make out of the same lump one vessel for beauty and another for menial use? What if God, ... in order to make know the riches of his glory for the vessels of mercy"


Or in Rom11where Paul ultimately Concluded his argument:

O the deptyh of the riches adn wisdom and knowledge of God! How unsearchable are his judgements and how inscrutable his ways! For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been his counselor?... For to him and through him and to him are all things. To HIM be the glory for ever. Amen


Indeed, God is vindicated as fair always according to his own standard for his own glory

This was also the conclusion of the great hymn, "It is well with my soul", written amidst the turmoil of having received news of his child's death
