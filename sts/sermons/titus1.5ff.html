Titus 1:5-2:2

Elder Qualifications

Intro.

Expostion:

I. An Apostolic Mandate:

vs. 5: left you behind in Crete for this purpose... to put in order what remained to be done... to appoint elders in every city.

The phrase "set in order what remains... is answered gramatically to appoint elders."

As if the gospel had been preached, people had been converted, but still something remained to be done!

(an idea that is perhaps foreign for many today)

Supporting evidence:

1. Acts 14:23 and after they had appinted elders for them in every city...

2. "every city"

3 "just as I commanded" (ordered) Non optional

Clearly, the gospel is not complete without the appointment of elders!

A church isn't a apostolic church without elders

By inference, a church isn't a church without that which the elders function, I.e. oversight, discipline

If an essential element of the apostolic mandate, what then are the qualifications and duties?

A sared office with sacred qualifications.

(Divinely appointed with divine characteristics)

II. Qualifications:

A. By Implication: One who can do the job!

3 titles used in passage to decribe the same office:

vs. 5: "elders: (Presbuteros) speaks to his status in the faith (not age)

1Tim. 4:12, Let no one despise your youth.

Samuel Miller

"The term from a very early period come to be a mere title of office without any respect to the years of the individual who bore it... It may be remarked however that although neither scripture nor the constitution of the Presbyterian Church prescribes any absolute rule with respect to the age of those who may be considered as condidates for the eldership; yet it is very manifest that those who are either minors in age or novices in Christian character and profession ought by no means in ordinary circumstances to be elected to his office. In the church of Scotland, the rule is that no one can be chosen an elder who is not 21 years of age...

vs. 7: "overseer" (episcopos) speaks to his function as a shepherd

vs. 7: "God's steward" (oikonos) speaks to his relation to the church as a manager and governor

This office is one of dignity and usefulness. The man who fills it has in Scripture different titles expressive of his various duties. As he has the oversight of the flock of Christ, he is termed bishop or pastor. As it is his duty to be grave and prudent, an example to the flock, and to govern well in the house and Kingdom of Christ, he is termed presbyter or elder. As he expounds the Word, and by sound doctrine both exhorts and convinces the gainsayer, he is termed teacher. These titles do not indicate different grades of office, but all describe one and the same office. BCO 8-1

Who then can do the job of elder, overseer and steward?

Key Phrase: someone who is blameless (vs. 6, 7)

Qualifications are introduced with this general and overal quality such that all other qualifications are intended to clarify this one general qualification.

1 Tim. 3:2, "now an overseer must be blameless"

Meaning:

I.e. Unquestioned integrity with respect to the areas required. , beyond the reproach of others

Not flawless but unquestioned.

Said positively: Exemplary

Key: Not one of these qualifications are any different from what all Christians should desire, simply ought to embrace them in such a way as to be a good example...

They are to be a model in Christian faith

They are to be such as to be able to promote true discipleship.

Three general areas:

1. Exemplary in Family relations

a. With Respect to wife: husband of one wife

loyal to her

(not that he must be married, but if married, ought to be faithful to her and committed to her best interests

See Eph. 5

(Note: nothing is said about the wife's godliness)

b. With Respect to children:children having faith

Two options:

1. Children that are believers

2. Children that carry on within the context of faith

1 Tim. 3:4-5, He must mangae his own household well, keeping his children submissive and respectful in every way for if a man does not know how to manage his won household, how can he care for God's church.

Idea Is clear from 1 Tim. An elder ougt to be exemplary in home management.

1. Wife: Eph. 5. Love wife as Christ loved church

2. children:

Eph. 6:4, Fathers, do not provoke your children to anger, but bring them up on the discipline and instruction of the Lord.

Point: Elders ought to demonstrate

1. Ability to discipline children and keep them within the realm of faith. (They are required in so far as they remain in the home to live after Christian priorities, etc.

2. Ability to instruct children in the Christian faith.

Note again: All fathers are exhorted to do this, elders ought to be exemplary at it!

2. Exemplary in Personal Character

Absence of Negatives:

1) not arrogant (self willed), a self loving, arrogant, headstrong or rebellious person

2) not quick-tempered: (lit. Prone to anger) (under control and temperate)

3) not addicted to any substances (drunkard), Temporate and Controled by the Holy spirit

4) not violent: (not contentious and eager to "fight" but a lover of peace

4) not greedy (1 Tim. 3: not a lover of money) but gracious and giving

b. Presence of Postives:

1) given to hospitality: gladly opening his home to the needy and strangers. (includes benevolence)

2) A lover of good- both "good things" and "good people" being devoted to that which attracts and promotes the kingdom of God rather than being attracted to those things which work against the kingdom of God.

3) sober-minded: temperate, master of self

4) righteous: God's law is standard for living or "just" (as in fair and judicious)

5) Holy: unpoluted but given to conduct that is in the image of Christ.

6. Self-Controled: His appetites (hobies, interests, etc. ) are under control. Able to keep ones strengths from being abused.

3. Exemplary in Orthodoxy (sound doctrine)

holding fast to the sure word according to the teachings (the word in greek for "doctrine")

RSV: The sure word as taught

Note: Already, there was various uses of the Word such that the "sure word" was to be measured against the apostolic tradition.

Confessionalism is already functioning

1 Tim. 3:14, But as for y ou, continue in what you have learned and have firmly believed, knowing from whom you learned it...

2 Reasons: in order to be competent

1. To instruct in "sound doctrine

"sound" = "healthy"

Doctrine can become unhealthy in one of two ways.

Addition of doctrinal tumors

Substraction of doctrinal limbs.

Key: What is not in scripture is as important as what is in scripture.

Point: The elder ought to be a deligent student of God's word being careful to interpret it after the apostolic tradition as recorded in orthodox creeds.

2. To refute opponents.

Notice context vs. 11ff

whole families being led into harms way by teachings which claimed to be according to the word.

If even before the Canon was closed such heresies and false teachings abounded, then not surprised that today the elder must be theologically prepared to also recognize and refute such harmful teachings.

Why examination is essential in order to determine a man's theological ability to be exemplary. (not just that he doesn't hold heresy, but that he can identify, instruct and refute heresy)

Elder must be prepared to do both!

Mainpoint: The office of elder is essential to the gospel and ought to be men that are exemplary in Christian faith and practice.

1 Tim. 4:16, Take heed to yourself and to your teachings; hold to that for by so doing you will save both yourself and your hearers.

Children: By "exemplary" = hero

Hero is someone you want to be like

Someone you respect

someone who has proven himself to be worth knowing., etc.

An elder is a man that can be a human hero since he follows Christ the ultimate hero.

A Sacred Office: (vs. secular office) therefore sacred qualifications vs. secular...

The elder's character speaks more loudly than his tongue. This consideration is immeasurably more weighty in the case of the sacred than of the secular... If he is evidently intelligent and shrewd, but of doubtful integrity, the plausibility of what he advances will be felt; but the more ability he shows, themore will the people fear to commit themselves to his opnions; for they have no guarantee of moral principle that he is not employing these forces of his genius, manifestly so powerful, to entrap and injure them instead of to benefit them. ... Eloquence may dazzle and please; holiness of life convinces. R. L. Dabney , Sacred Rhetoric

Exemplary vs. Mediocre:

it is compounded of a deep dissatisfaction with what is and a clear grasp of what could be. It begins with indignation over the status quo, and it grows into the earnest quest for an alternative...

1. Exemplary theology:

Your competent knowledge and good judgment must be such a soundness of mind as will command the respect of all men, with a real mastery of the theology of redemption. A frivolous, weak, illogical mind will detract from the weight of all that you could say for religious truth. ... I so far admit this statement as to avow that a fool has no business in the sacred office, whatever may be his zeal or his opportunities for training... To do this you only need constant and faithful study of your own department and this, I repeat, it is your clear duty to bestow.

2. Exemplary Character:

The Elder must rise far higher; he must exhibit a symmetry and elevation of Christian character, an exaltation above all carnal ambitions, which will make him venerable and lovely in the eyes of the flock. Such a character clothes his instructions with a weight and sweetness which no talent or learning can give.

3. Exemplary Love for Saints:

The third quality, good-will, must rise above the humanity and the benevolence of the good citizen to an ardent love for souls. The elder should be recognized as one who affectionately hungers for the spiritual good of his charge

The Real Thing! (No counterfeit will do!)

Illus: Whitefield

War: Mock battles vs. terrible reality

In the former there are marchings and counter marchings, there are all the pomp and circumstance of war, there are clouds of suphurous smoke, and the ear is astounded with the thunders of artillery and the rattle of small arms. The young soilder is not a little excited; he pants with toil, he thrills with ardour, he is eager to see his party repuls their pretended adversaries. But still he is conscious that it is only a splendid farce!

Actual battle, again he marches and retires, bodies again wheel and manoeuvre before him, aids gallop with orders, the guns roar... but he also sees plain proofs that these are no longer blank cartridges which are fired. The earth is ploughed and the forests are cut with bullets and as he glances along the line, he sees here and there a comrade who drops his musket. This war is in Reality! Now again he is excited, he pants , he is ardent for victory, he thrills with passion, but it is a terrible reality! Such was the conviction, such was the awakening of the men who fell under the spell of a Whitefield's sacred eloqauence!
