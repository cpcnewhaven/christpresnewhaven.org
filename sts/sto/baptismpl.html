The Privilege of Christian Baptism

I just received a very special letter from Singapore. It clearly depicts one of the exciting privileges God gives to our church in the Yale/New Haven community.

Elaine, like Bim, are students at Yale from Singapore. Both have ,for the first time, been exposed to the discipleship ministry of a church and desire to publicly profess their faith in Christ through Christian baptism. Yet both incurred significant opposition to their new found faith and were forbidden by their Buddhist parents to be baptized. As is commonly the case, much of the opposition is related to family loyalties and tradition rather than "truth claims" per se.

Understandably perhaps, the Buddhist parents are concerned that their children are merely discarding the very culture and values which they hold to be dear. And admittedly, Elaine and Bim have discovered that their belief in Christ has put them in conflict with various rituals and values of their home culture. Yet they have also discovered that to be "in Christ" ought also to mean that they are more profoundly "pro-family" and "pro-culture" if what is meant by this is "family" and culture" as God meant them to be. And so, when Elaine asked to speak with me about being baptized, I found myself in a difficult situation. One the one hand, I knew that Elaine was called to follow after Christ, even if this might mean getting baptized without her parents consent-- risking even the possibility that she would be rejected by her family. (This even more the case with Bim) Yet I also felt that her parents might not yet have enough information, about what Christianity is, and is not, so as to be able to have an honest evaluation.

We decided to delay baptism until after the summer break so as to do everything possible to honor her parents. Elaine's last words to me before leaving for Singapore were cheerfully spoken. She said, "either way, I can't wait to be baptized in the Fall." A few days ago, I received the following note from Elaine.

Greetings from Singapore in the name of Christ, our Savior!... I have the most excellent news: my parents have given their consent to my baptism!! To God be the glory, great things He has done. Sincere thanks to you and people at Christ Presbyterian who have been so kind with your encouragement and prayers; the Lord has answered mightily.


Elaine's eagerness to follow Christ, even if this meant severe persecution from a family that she dearly loves, has made a profound impact upon our whole congregation. We have all come to appreciate more profoundly the meaning of following after Christ as His disciples, and the privilege of Christian baptism.
