Blessed are the Humble

 

Sitting yesterday afternoon in my study with Andy, I again was convinced in the power of the gospel to save! It was our third meeting together-- and only three weeks ago Andy had prayed to receive Christ in the same chair he was sitting in yesterday. Having come from a very difficult past, he was one of those, "highly unlikely" types to receive Christ-- but then again, those are in fact the types that I have so often witnessed God's saving power. Unlike those who might have grown up in a church, Andy was in no doubt that he was a sinner-- he did, however, wonder if God could and/or would love him and forgive him. As we discussed Christ's conflict with the Pharisees over against His friendship with sinners, Andy was deeply encouraged to discover that the gospel was not for the religious, self-righteous types, but for sinners like himself-- for those, convinced of their own (rather than everyone else's) need for forgiveness. Andy related how our church and especially our worship seemed so friendly and accessible and even "easy" for him-- hmmm! (Andy hates to read and is not one for "education", but he found our service "easy." I marvel at this, those I would expect to find our sermons the easiest-- the educated and religious types-- find them the most difficult, and here, a person who has never really gone to church, a person who is not your typical "yalie" type-- is in fact saying that our 1 1/2 hour service is "easy to understand, friendly, and keeps my attention"-- hmmm!) Now, Andy's testimony is of a growing young Christian, whose honest questions and eager heart to learn about Christ and the teachings of scripture are changing his life in some major ways, effecting his lifestyle, his relationships and even prompting him to be a witness at work. BLESSED ARE THE HUMBLE, FOR THE SHALL INHERIT THE KINGDOM OF GOD! Please pray for this new brother, and praise God with us for his new life in Christ!

Oh, but don't forget Chuan Yi and Hai Hong-- both recently converted to Christ through our church and are now forming the core for a new investigative study for some Chinese people in our city. Pray as I write some materials for this study and as Ruth, one of our graduate student interns who is somewhat fluent in Chinese will be taking these lessons and adapting them to this group. Pray also for Chuan-Yi's atheist husband as he shows some interest in his wife's new faith. Pray also as these precious young Christians consider Christian baptism, as this is perhaps more significant for them given their eastern orientation-- as they properly understand that a conversion to Christ is a conversion into the living presence of Christ as he is mediated within a communal context-- something we "westerns" in our prepositional way of thinking have often negated. Pray also for Ruth as she meets with these new Christian women in helping them to be rooted and grounded in the faith.

These stories and others encourage me to press one with a trust not in demographics but in the power of the gospel. It is true, I confess, that when I survey the demographic trends in our region I am tempted to despair. Our city continues to decrease in population at 4% even as our state decreases at .4%. (We will loose a congressional seat this year) We continue to see a relatively flat economy in these parts, with the only movement away from our region rather than into it. And so we would appreciate your prayers as we consider some very creative solutions to our demographic reality. And yet, join us in giving Him thanks--in part for the gracious support like yours of people outside of this region that sustains our ministry here that is reaching lost people for Christ. In other words, it is when I survey the cross rather than demographics that I see a kind of religion where we are blessed even amidst suffering and hardship-- I see the power of the gospel to save in spite of even the most adverse circumstances. I am therefore encouraged to press on by stories like Andy, Chuan Yi and Hai Hong. Moreover, we are delighted to welcome Tom and Teri Morrison to our team-- much needed help in especially reaching the nations that have congregated at Yale. Their coming is again largely supported by those outside of this region who realize the potential of this economically depressed city for reaching and even changing the world for the glory of Christ. I want to especially welcome those of you who have through the Morrison's joined our "team." We are gratefully and affectionately yours in Christ!
