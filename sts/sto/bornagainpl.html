Even In Our Dying, We Can Be Born Again!

Since after the death of Jeanette's husband, some ten years ago, Jeanette had expressed her strong doubts to her daughter (a member of CPC) regarding the Christian faith. At the time that I first met with Jeanette to discuss her spiritual concerns four year ago, I remember a women who was clearly struggling to understand matters of faith and religion. It is true that she had raised her children within the Christian tradition, and in many ways exemplified the things characteristic of a Christian wife. But as she now lived alone in her apartment, it was as if she had now embarked upon a new found freedom to question and explore matters of faith for herself-- even in so far as it led her to doubt. She couldn't help but think that a large part of what had been passed on to her was merely sentimental superstition. Looking back, it is easy to detect that she was a person attempting to distinguish between that kind of religion that was of human imagination, and that which was genuinely of God's own design. Until you have pastored some of the people raised within the Roman Catholic tradition, it is hard to grasp just how burdensome many of the practices and rituals can be to the thinking and sincere mind!

In subsequent years, Jeanette's health began to slip. In recent months, her daughter spent hours reading scripture to her beloved mother and praying constantly for her salvation. My next pastoral contact came several weeks ago on March 26th, when Lillian informed me that her mother was dying and wanted to meet with me. (The doctors at the time were concerned that Jeanette might not even last the night.) That night, we read the scriptures to Jeanette, especially Romans 8:1-4, 31-39, John 11:17ff, John 14:1-7 and perhaps most significantly Ephesians 2:7-10.

Even as her body was ready to quit living, it was as if Jeanette was still not ready to go. I remember saying to Jeanette: "You know that you are dying don't you." She indicated that this was true. We asked here if she would like for Jesus to receive her into heaven. Again, she indicated that this was her desire. We explained that entrance into heaven was God's gift to her, if only she would put her trust and faith in Jesus-- how Jesus alone could save her from the penalty and guilt of her sins, and that she needed nothing more than to put her faith in him for the forgiveness of sin and life everlasting. When we asked her if this was her desire, to trust in Christ so as to be with him in heaven, she again indicated that this was her desire.

That evening, we left Jeanette's hospital room still not sure she would last the night, but feeling more assurance that she might be prepared to die, to be with the Lord in heaven. It was perhaps God's grace that he enabled Jeanette to return to her room at her retirement home several days later, where we again spoke with her about her faith, and where she again seemed to indicate in even clearer ways that she had understood and received the gospel-- that we are saved by grace through faith, and not by our own works, this faith being the free gift of God. And it was then that we became even more assured that EVEN IN OUR DYING, WE CAN BE BORN AGAIN!

Your English translations may call it many things-- new birth, regeneration, or even the often maligned phrase "born again"-- in the Greek-- but its one single idea, whether it happens gradually from childhood, or whether in a moment, or perhaps even at ones last dying moments, it is the idea that our eternal destiny and relation to God is very much related to our having the gift of new life while on earth, even as this is evidenced by putting our faith in Christ alone, as our only mediator between God and humanity, our deliver, our substitute in judgment and the first among many to be raised from the dead. This "new birth" is what is clearly spoken of in 1 Peter 1:3-9 that even in our dying, even as our bodies are wasting away, our inner person may be reborn through faith with such consequences as a new and living hope and a heavenly inheritance.

Early Friday morning, we reflected upon these things as I read 1 Peter 1:3-9 with Lillian and her daughter at the foot of Jeanette's bed, moments after Jeanette passed from this life into her heavenly inheritance. Yesterday, three of Jeanette's twelve granddaughters read the scriptures that were so significant to Jeanette her last days on earth even as I reflected upon 1 Peter1:3-9, and our hope unto eternal life!

O what a serious thing is it to die! It is a passage out of the swift river of time, into the boundless and bottomless ocean of eternity. They that did see the continual revolutions of days and nights, passing away one after another, will then be fixed in the perpetual NOW... Then how surprisingly glorious will heaven be to believers! For a soul to be now here in the body, conversing with people, living among sensible objects, and within a few moments to be with the Lord-- this hour on earth, the next in heaven, this moment viewing this world, the next among an innumerable company of angels and beloved ones now made perfect! O what a change is this. What, but wink and see God! To be lifted from a bed of sickness to a throne of glory! To leave a sinful, troublesome world, a sick and pained body, and be in a moment perfectly cured, and feel Yourself perfectly well, and free from all troubles and despair! John Flavel
