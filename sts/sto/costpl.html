The Cost of Discipleship


If you are reproached for the name of Christ, you are blessed, because the spirit of glory and of God rests upon you... if one suffers as a Christian, let him not be ashamed, but under that name let him glorify God. 1 Peter 4:14,16


I confess that never, until just a few moments ago, have I really known anyone that could really understand the true meaning of this passage. Sure, I have often quoted these verses when I endured some relatively insignificant inconvenience because of Christ. And I have often heard such themes sometimes callously blazoned in Christian conferences, songs and literature. But never had I even vicariously felt the true impact of these words-- at least not until just a few moments ago when I discovered them imprinted with tears upon the face of a man that really understood. For here was a young man who was committed to following after Christ even though it would mean the loss of his father and mother.

The conversation began by Bim asking to borrow my Bible. He then turned to Matthew 10:34-39. As he read the words, for I have come to set a man against his father and a daughter against her mother, tears began to run down his face. I knew then that his parents had made it clear that to become a follower of Christ meant the loss of family relations. The Chinese traditional beliefs of Bim's parents reflects a mixture of Taoism, Buddhism and Ancestral worship. Their objection to Bim following Christ into Christian baptism is due to the belief that when a member of a family defects to another religion, they should expect the wrath of the gods to come upon the family. Therefore, Bim would not be allowed to return home if he professed faith in Christ.

As I talked with Bim, I quietly reflected upon the privilege of being a follower of Christ. Here was a man who loved his parents, who was deeply traumatized by the impending loss of his parental relations, but who all the same counted himself fortunate to have been called out of this world into the kingdom of Christ. I told him that I too felt honored with the privilege of sharing with him in his suffering-- if but only from a distance. I also encouraged him regarding the promises of God for those who remain steadfast in the faith.

Would you pray for Bim. By the end of our meeting, today, we agreed to make one more effort at getting his parents consent before Bim's baptism. Pray for a miracle! Also, thank you for sharing in the building of this church. Clearly God has positioned us to have a world-wide impact. Would you continue to pray that our impact would be faithful to Christ and His gospel? This will be an important year of leadership development and expansion. Thank-you!
