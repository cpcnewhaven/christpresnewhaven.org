The Fresh Presence of God

 

It was an all too familiar struggle. I was driving to a house for a pastoral visit and was struggling to get "up" for the visit, even though I should have been more eager. I was meeting with a couple who had been visiting our church with some friends for almost 6 months off and on. They attended our evangelistic drama event during the Easter season last spring. Through an amazing set of events, Victoria had met Anita and had come to see Anita who played the leading part in the play. And then, several months later, there they were on the front row during our worship service. Chris was legally blind and found it easier to see what was going on near the front. They began to attend our "conversations with Jesus" class this fall during our School of Discovery every Weds. evening which also included a fellowship dinner. Two of their four kids came every Sunday with them and seemed to love our "kids club" every Weds. evening as well. And so I thought it about time to give them an already overdue visit. When I mentioned it to them they seemed both delighted and anxious.

And so why was I having a hard time getting up for the visit? Sin, laziness, obsession with more tangible goals–all familiar struggles. But I have also found that these struggles seem to intensify in moments when God is doing a special work in the lives of his people. When I arrived, my "goal" was to ask them what they knew about the gospel and talk with them plainly about their personal faith. It turned out to be the very thing they wanted to talk about but were afraid to ask. As I described the gospel that day, tears swelled up in Victoria’ s eyes. All of this was her first encounter with the Christian faith. God was clearly doing a work in her soul such as she seemed ready to embrace Christ for the forgiveness of sin and eternal life! Chris had been raised a Catholic and was just beginning to see the difference between the gospel we preached and the gospel of the Catholic church. For the first time he began to understand that it was not by keeping the ten commandments or being a relatively good person that a person could hope to be saved since even our best attempts to satisfy God’s law would fail miserably. It was explained how we are saved by Christ’s works rather than ours and Chris too was ready as this all seemed to him "Good news"! As it turned out, their first step of "repentance" was to admit they were not married and to discuss plans to that end. Both had children from previous relationships as well as they seemed eager to tell them of their new life and plans in Christ.

Having prayed with them and scheduled a follow-up visit, I found myself wanting to stay. The fresh presence of God in that place had overcome my laziness and sin. I was again renewed in knowing that God was working in our city. People were being prepared by the regenerating work of the Holy Spirit to receive Christ. We even discussed the possibilities of having a Bible Study in their home to reach their neighborhood–onethat is not yet represented in our church. Vision was being exited. Truly, this is what it means to plant a church in the city of New Haven. For if it means nothing else, it means getting into the homes and neighborhoods of our city–albeit the college neighborhood at Yale, or the neighborhood such as this of people living in the city–and talking with folk about what it means to embrace Christ!

I hope the story of Chris and Victoria will encourage you to pray for us-- that we would be "out in the neighborhoods" of our city, eager to talk about the first things concerning faith in Christ. Pray as we look to begin a second worship service that will be located both geographically and stylistically in the neighborhood of Chris and Victoria. Pray too as we are in serious need to begin our "phase two" of our facility project toward the completion of a worship center. While our current worship space has been very much appreciated, it will not be available in a little over a year. We are hoping for the Lord to provide enough funds that we might be able to add a modest size worship space to our existing study center. And always know that your gifts are greatly appreciated, even as I hope you find them to be wise investments in the Kingdom of God.
