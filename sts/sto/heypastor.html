Hey Pastor…And then it came!

 

 

It seemed odd that "J" would be so abrupt. "Hey pastor" she said in the study center hallway near my office door, "are you sure about this sovereignty doctrine?" I was in a hurry. "It's a busy time of year" I thought to myself, "and I have a gillion things on my mind--surely I don't have time for a frivolous chit-chat about God!" So I replied, "sure I'm sure" while thinking to myself, "quickly, let me get into the safety of my office where I can get down to my work." I justified my secret thoughts remembering that we had already discussed this issue before over the past two weeks. So you can understand why my feet froze in mid motion when "J" replied, "I was just wondering because I just found out that I am HIV positive."

It might not have been the most sensitive way to break the news to me-- but life can be messy and with it our way of communicating. And so we talked-- even though briefly. But I should first give you some history. Several weeks ago, "J" happened upon our sign in front of our study center in the center of town and wondered in thinking (in her words roughly) "these seem to be some thoughtful Christian people that I would like to talk with." Over the course of the next several weeks, she talked with both Tom and myself while spending hours reading in our study center library. She was filled with questions about faith, questions about God, questions about suffering and questions about death. Some of her questions were precipitated by the anticipated death of her father. (Which happened last week.) But also, as a social worker, she was finding that the ordinary cliches of religion were insufficient toward finding rest for her homeless soul. In short-- she needed someone to give her some real answers to some very tough questions-- never realizing that the answers would soon be directed to a tough situation concerning herself. Her crisis of faith was in God's plan only to be followed by a crisis of life!

Sometimes it is easy to think that what we do in the church is marginal to the real stuff of life. But how do you respond to a person in the late stages of HIV so as to offer "grace" if we don't first talk about a theology of God-- how He causes all things…? What would "J" hope in if not that God was doing something that would prove to be good in her life-- at least as concerns her eternal life? For if God didn't cause it, then what did? Fickle fate? What hope is there in that? Was it a triumph of evil in a great cosmic spiritual warfare? Again, "J" would need to fear. So I explained to "J" the very same things that I have taught in theology class many times and for that matter, the things I taught even this week in our intern training sessions. I explained that God was perfect in his being, contingent upon nothing for all his actions lest we admit to Him being imperfect in being. I explained that God, in an ultimate sense, causes all things. How if this was not true, we could have no hope and "grace" would no longer be "grace" since it too would be contingent upon something other than God. In short-- that regardless of all the circumstances that might surround "J" being infected with HIV (a tragic story in its own right) that God caused it. And it is precisely this that gives us hope and comfort--knowing that if God caused it, then He caused if for some good!

"Yes" she said, "this does comfort me." Isn't it strange how something so disquieting as the sovereignty of God can be so quieting? You can of course pray for "J." As she begins her treatment, but as she finds her home with Christ!

And "J" is but one example of how God is at work here. She has been bringing her city kids to our study center and we hope to see them join our Weds. Evening "Kid's Club" together with the other kids in our church. We begin our "School of Discovery" at our Study Center next week, together with our "Reformed Fellowship" meetings on campus this week. We will launch a ministry to "Pre-School moms" and several other aspects of ministry here in New Haven. We are excited about the four student interns we are privileged to train for lay-ministry in the church. We will begin a "ESL" ministry (English as a second language") where International students will be introduced to the Christian faith as well. Our schedule is packed-- but please pray that we will have many conversations about the real stuff of faith in Christ with many more people this year looking to find a home in Christ! And of course, your part in this ministry is greatly appreciated. We really could not be doing this without God's gifts through you!
