The New Society
July 19, 1996

Preston Graham, Jr.

Hanging around either academic or political places, it is customary to hear the chatter about how this world is going to be a better place once this next education program is in place, or after this next political platform is elected into office. In so many words, the chatter is about a "new society"-- a place where racism, terrorism, cheating and one-up-manship will be more or less abolished.
But then I read the paper and think, who are they kidding. Just this week, I was jolted with the awful and tragic news of Flight 800. Just this week, a man got mad at another for cutting him off in his car, so he got out of his car and shot him dead right in the middle of Interstate 95 in New Haven. Just this week, I saw a little league coach blatantly lie and cheat-- using a kid dishonestly to win an all star game by over 10 points. Just this week, I counseled with a couple whose marriage is breaking up, another person who is crying out for help in a cocaine addition after loosing his family and business. Where is the "new society" that our political and educational schemes have promised us?

But then again, I reflect on our new church in New Haven. Just this week, a local physician who had been brought to our church and followed up with pastoral visits prayed to receive Christ-- surrendering his life to the shepherd of souls so that Christ can make him over as a new man. Just this week, another man was referred to me from one of the faithful members of our church-- and has asked to become a Christian. Moreover, this is the season where I do regular pastoral visitations with our members. And I have been genuinely encouraged to discover that our ministry is really making a difference in peoples lives. I learned of two children-- children of a women who became a Christian in our church not too long ago and is presently married to a unbelieving man-- who have both discovered the real meaning of Christian discipleship and are self-consciously wanting to follow after Christ. Just recently, I discovered that our ministry had probably saved another marriage. Just this week, our study center was filled with Yale students who are learning that to follow Christ means to be made over into a new person as this impacts every facet of our life-- from our ethics in school and work, our way of being married and even the way we ought one day to coach a little league baseball team. Just this week, our children are learning about the "Dangerous Journey"-- that is, the worldly dangers of following after Christ on the narrow path after the great book The Pilgrims Progress -- in our weekly community outreach for children. And so, just this week, it dawned upon me that God IS making a "new society." It is His Church. I don't mean dusty and outdated "good ole fashion religion." I mean modern people who are loosing their life to Christ and seeing Christ make them into new people and are now joining together as a new society.

We have discovered a new resolve to let the world chit- chat about the "new society" even as those who are in Christ find renewed confidence in the power of the Gospel to save and change lives! It may not be political correct to apply Ephesians 5 to marriages, but it does save marriages. It may not be convenient to hold your child out of that sporting event so as to honor the Lord's Day, but it teaches him/her that Christ is the way to real happiness, not sports. I suspect that we are learning at Christ Presbyterian Church to make room in our theology for a "mustard seed" kingdom of God-- one that may not at first capture the attention of pop culture or pop media and may even evoke the snears of our neighbors-- but all the same a kingdom that really changes lives and is recorded in the heavenly newspaper where the angels are said to rejoice when even one sinner turns from their formal ways to follow after Christ.

Please continue to support our ministry here through your prayers and financial assistance. Even as we continue to see spiritual and even numerical growth, our obstacles are both spiritual and financial-- the first because it is the nature of Christian ministry itself. The second because we happen to be in a region which is suffering economically, and are reaching a people-- many of whom are from all around the world but who do not have much to give in terms of financial giving. I would especially ask that you pray for us as we raise support for Mel Sensenig who will replace George Levesque in helping us to minister to the college community. (We are excited that George has taken a job at Yale where he will be able to continue to serve in our church as a lay leader and have a "insiders" opportunity to share the Gospel.)

Also pray for our new church homepage. We have students and former members who are presently in regions of the world where Biblical teaching is scarce. Our church homepage, which includes a "study center," has made available sermons, Bible studies, pamphlets, papers and even our prayer letters so as to assist our international friends in following after Christ.

Again THANK YOU for your help to us as we continue to plant a "new society" in New Haven and around the world!
