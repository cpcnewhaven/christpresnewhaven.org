<P ALIGN=left>
The body of man is not more capable of being really the subject of love or hatred, joy or sorrow, fear or hope, than the body of a tree, or than the same body of man is capable of thinking and understanding.  As it is the soul only that has ideas, so it is the soul only that is pleased or displeased with its ideas.  As it is the soul only that thinks, so it is the soul only that loves or hates, rejoices or is grieved at, what it thinks of... (Quotes taken from <U>A Treatise Concerning Religious Affections</U>, Part 1, "Concerning the Nature of the Affections and their Importance in Religion", Section 1)
</P>
</BLOCKQUOTE>

<P ALIGN=left>
Our conviction is that the very most central core of true "religion" resides in our religious affections.  The great American theologian, Jonathan Edwards, defined "affections" by the "will and inclination" which govern our choices.  It pertains to the things we "love" or "cherish" that makes us who we are in Christian faith and practice -- and we love only what we can know in some reasonable sense.  Therefore, Edwards further explained that the "proper seat of the affections is but the mind only." 
</P>
